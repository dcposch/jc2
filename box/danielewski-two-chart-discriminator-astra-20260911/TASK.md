## Exact all-degree target

Let T=Spec C[x,t,Z]/(t^2-1-x^2 Z). Its two affine-plane charts are

    T minus D_-: t=1+x^2 y, Z=2y+x^2 y^2;
    T minus D_+: t=-1+x^2 v, Z=-2v+x^2 v^2,
    v=y+2/x^2 on the overlap, D_±={x=0,t=±1}.

The global nonvanishing symplectic form is omega=dx wedge dt / x^2,
equal to dx wedge dy and dx wedge dv on the respective charts. Thus
O(T)=C[x,y] intersect C[x,y+2/x^2] inside C[x,x^-1,y].
An arbitrary regular pair H,G on T with dH wedge dG=c*omega, c nonzero,
would restrict to a polynomial Keller counterexample on either chart:
if one restriction were an automorphism, its polynomial inverse composed
with (H,G) would extend that chart's inverse to all of T, contradicting
the actual pole of y along D_- (or v along D_+).

This is BROADER than the previous quotient surface
S=Spec C[A,U,Z]/(U^2-A-A^2 Z), with q(x,t,Z)=(x^2,xt,Z).
Do NOT impose the involution (-x,-t,Z), homogeneous coordinates, finite
total degree, or a sparse-family ansatz. Symmetry is unnecessary for this
sufficient counterexample target. Do not assert that every JC2 counterexample
extends to this fixed T, or that this surface is a newly discovered route.

constant-Jacobian identity yields a uniform obstruction or an explicit regular
pair, using the transition v=y+2/x^2 itself. In concrete terms seek all-degree

    J_(x,y)(P,Q)=c nonzero;
    P(x,v-2/x^2), Q(x,v-2/x^2) belong to C[x,v].

usual missing-polynomiality gap is not a new closing mechanism. A useful
