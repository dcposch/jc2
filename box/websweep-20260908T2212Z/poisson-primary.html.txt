An Explicit Counterexample to the Rank-Two Poisson Conjecture
Christopher D. Long
galizur@gmail.com
(July 21, 2026)
Abstract
Let
𝒫2=ℂ​[x,q,p,z]\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathbb{C}[x,q,p,z]
carry the canonical Poisson bracket determined by
{p,x}={z,q}=1\{p,x\}=\{z,q\}=1
and by the vanishing of the other brackets between distinct generators.
Here and throughout, “rank two” means two canonical pairs in the standard
indexing of the canonical Poisson algebras; thus there are four polynomial
generators and the Poisson tensor has geometric rank four. We give explicit
polynomials
R,T,D,S∈ℚ​[x,q,p,z]R,T,D,S\in\mathbb{Q}[x,q,p,z]
satisfying
{D,R}=1,{S,T}=1,{R,S}={R,T}={D,S}={D,T}=0,\{D,R\}=1,\qquad\{S,T\}=1,\qquad\{R,S\}=\{R,T\}=\{D,S\}=\{D,T\}=0,
while
R=x​(2−3​x​q).R=x(2-3xq).
Consequently, the assignment
(x,q,p,z)↦(R,T,D,S)(x,q,p,z)\mapsto(R,T,D,S)
defines a Poisson endomorphism of 𝒫2\mathcal{P}_{2} that is not an automorphism.
This disproves the Poisson Conjecture for two canonical pairs, and hence for
every number of canonical pairs at least two. The associated polynomial map
of 𝔸4\mathbb{A}^{4} preserves the canonical symplectic form, has Jacobian
determinant one, and has an explicit fiber consisting of exactly three points.
The proof uses a polynomial source coordinate system in which the symplectic
identity reduces to three displayed coefficient identities. A separate
appendix uses the same four output polynomials and their
Hamiltonian duals to construct an explicit nonautomorphic endomorphism of the
fourth Weyl algebra.

1 Introduction and rank convention
For a positive integer nn, write
𝒫n=ℂ​[x1,…,xn,p1,…,pn]\mathcal{P}_{n}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},p_{1},\dots,p_{n}]
(1)
with canonical Poisson bracket
{f,g}=∑i=1n(∂f∂pi​∂g∂xi−∂f∂xi​∂g∂pi).\{f,g\}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{i}}\right).
(2)
Thus {pi,xj}=δi​j\{p_{i},x_{j}\}=\delta_{ij}. In this paper, we use rank nn
to mean the index nn in (1), equivalently the
number of canonical pairs. This differs from the geometric rank of the Poisson
tensor, which is 2​n2n. In particular, the title’s “rank two” means two
canonical pairs, four polynomial variables, and a Poisson tensor of rank four.
This is also the indexing convention of Adjamagbo and van den Essen, whose
canonical Poisson algebra 𝒫n\mathcal{P}_{n} has 2​n2n polynomial generators
[1, Section 1].
The assertion PC​(n)\mathrm{PC}(n) is that every Poisson endomorphism of 𝒫n\mathcal{P}_{n} is
an automorphism. Recall that JC​(3)\mathrm{JC}(3), the three-variable Jacobian
Conjecture, asserts that a polynomial map 𝔸ℂ3→𝔸ℂ3\mathbb{A}^{3}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{A}^{3}_{\mathbb{C}} with
nonzero constant Jacobian determinant is a polynomial automorphism
[6]. The present construction begins with an explicit
counterexample to this three-variable assertion. The rank-two Poisson
endomorphism is obtained by adjoining a polynomial source coordinate and a
Hamiltonian correction, and every Poisson identity is then verified directly.
The construction below grew from the three-variable polynomial map
Π​(X,Y,W)\displaystyle\Pi(X,Y,W)
=(1+X​Y)3​W+Y2​(1+X​Y)​(4+3​X​Y),\displaystyle=(1+XY)^{3}W+Y^{2}(1+XY)(4+3XY),
Σ​(X,Y,W)\displaystyle\Sigma(X,Y,W)
=Y+3​X​(1+X​Y)2​W+3​X​Y2​(4+3​X​Y),\displaystyle=Y+3X(1+XY)^{2}W+3XY^{2}(4+3XY),
ℛ​(X,Y,W)\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X,Y,W)
=2​X−3​X2​Y−X3​W.\displaystyle=2X-3X^{2}Y-X^{3}W.
(3)
This map was announced by Alpöge, whose post thanks Akhil Mathew for asking
about the problem and Fable for working on it [2]. Buzzard’s
contemporaneous account reports that Mathew suggested the problem to Alpöge
and that Fable found the counterexample [4]; the Ulam research note
similarly credits Mathew for asking the question and Fable for the work leading
to the example [8, Introduction]. The Ulam note independently
verifies the determinant and the three-point collision and studies the fibers
and global geometry [8, Theorem 3.1 and Section 4]. Its
Jacobian determinant is −2-2, and the three points
(0,0,−14),(1,−32,132),(−1,32,132)\left(0,0,-\frac{1}{4}\right),\qquad\left(1,-\frac{3}{2},\frac{13}{2}\right),\qquad\left(-1,\frac{3}{2},\frac{13}{2}\right)
(4)
have common image (−1/4,0,0)(-1/4,0,0). The standard cotangent lift
(X,Ξ)⟼(F​(X),JF​(X)−𝖳​Ξ),F=(Π,Σ,ℛ),(X,\Xi)\longmapsto\bigl(F(X),J_{F}(X)^{-\mathsf{T}}\Xi\bigr),\qquad F=(\Pi,\Sigma,\mathcal{R}),
is polynomial because detJF=−2\det J_{F}=-2, preserves the canonical one-form
Ξ𝖳​d​X\Xi^{\mathsf{T}}dX, and is noninjective on the zero section. Thus the
announced map already gives a Poisson counterexample on three canonical pairs.
The purpose of the present construction is to reach two canonical pairs.
The three-dimensional core used here is the determinant-one normalization
G=(ℛ,−Π/2,Σ).G=(\mathcal{R},-\Pi/2,\Sigma).
(5)
The four-dimensional Poisson endomorphism is obtained from this core by a
polynomial change of source coordinates and a Hamiltonian correction. Every
identity needed below is proved directly, so the proof does not depend on an
external verification of (3).
The main result is the following.
Theorem 1.1 (Main theorem).
There are explicit polynomials R,T,D,S∈ℚ​[x,q,p,z]R,T,D,S\in\mathbb{Q}[x,q,p,z] such that
Φ:𝒫2⟶𝒫2,Φ​(x)=R,Φ​(q)=T,Φ​(p)=D,Φ​(z)=S,\Phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2},\qquad\Phi(x)=R,\quad\Phi(q)=T,\quad\Phi(p)=D,\quad\Phi(z)=S,
(6)
is a Poisson endomorphism but not an automorphism. Its associated point map
Φpt=(R,T,D,S):𝔸ℂ4⟶𝔸ℂ4\Phi_{\mathrm{pt}}=(R,T,D,S):\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies
detJ​Φpt=1,\det J\Phi_{\mathrm{pt}}=1,
and the fiber over (0,1/8,0,0)(0,1/8,0,0) consists exactly of
(0,0,124,−18),(1,23,24796,−8964),(−1,−23,24796,−8964).\left(0,0,\frac{1}{24},-\frac{1}{8}\right),\qquad\left(1,\frac{2}{3},\frac{247}{96},-\frac{89}{64}\right),\qquad\left(-1,-\frac{2}{3},\frac{247}{96},-\frac{89}{64}\right).

Consequently, PC​(2)\mathrm{PC}(2) is false.
The formulas have rational coefficients and therefore define the same
counterexample over every field of characteristic zero. We work over ℂ\mathbb{C}
to match the standard formulations of the Poisson and Jacobian conjectures.

[δi,fj]=δi​j[\delta_{i},f_{j}]=\delta_{ij}
as differential operators on ℬ\mathcal{B}. The multiplication operators
fif_{i} commute. Hamiltonian derivations satisfy

(77)
and every Poisson bracket between the Hamiltonians in
(74) is constant. Hence
[δi,δj]=0[\delta_{i},\delta_{j}]=0. The images in (75) satisfy all
defining Weyl relations, so Φ^\widehat{\Phi} is an endomorphism. This
Hamiltonian lift is the present specialization of the construction in the
proof of [3, Theorem 7(2), equation (5)]; all relations needed here
have just been verified directly.

order and write
δi=∑k=14ai​k​∂k,𝖠=(ai​k).\delta_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{4}a_{ik}\partial_{k},\qquad\mathsf{A}=(a_{ik}).
(78)
Let JfJ_{f} be the Jacobian matrix of (f1,f2,f3,f4)(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}) with respect to
(X1,X2,X3,X4)(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4}). Equation (76) is the matrix identity
𝖠​Jf𝖳=I4.\mathsf{A}J_{f}^{\mathsf{T}}=I_{4}.
(79)
Since detJf=1\det J_{f}=1, the matrix 𝖠\mathsf{A} is invertible over ℬ\mathcal{B}.
The same Jacobian identity also implies that the fif_{i} are algebraically
independent. Indeed, a nonzero relation of minimal total degree would, after
differentiation and multiplication by Jf−1J_{f}^{-1}, give lower-degree

order filtration, let σm\sigma_{m} denote the degree-mm principal-symbol map,
and put
ηi=σ1​(δi),ξ=(ξ1,…,ξ4)𝖳,η=(η1,…,η4)𝖳.\eta_{i}=\sigma_{1}(\delta_{i}),\qquad\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{4})^{\mathsf{T}},\qquad\eta=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{4})^{\mathsf{T}}.

(80)
one has η=𝖠​ξ\eta=\mathsf{A}\xi. Since 𝖠∈GL4​(ℬ)\mathsf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathcal{B}),
ℬ​[η1,…,η4]=ℬ​[ξ1,…,ξ4].\mathcal{B}[\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{4}]=\mathcal{B}[\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{4}].
In particular, the monomials ηβ\eta^{\beta} are linearly independent over
ℬ\mathcal{B}, and
σ|β|​(fα​δβ)=fα​ηβ.\sigma_{|\beta|}\!\left(f^{\alpha}\delta^{\beta}\right)=f^{\alpha}\eta^{\beta}.
(81)

it to the standard Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis shows that
{fα​δβ:α,β∈ℕ4}\{f^{\alpha}\delta^{\beta}:\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{4}\}
(82)

g=∑α,βcα​β​fα​δβ.g=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}f^{\alpha}\delta^{\beta}.
Suppose

∑|β|=m(∑αcα​β​fα)​ηβ=0.\sum_{|\beta|=m}\left(\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha\beta}f^{\alpha}\right)\eta^{\beta}=0.

ℬ=ℂ​[f1,f2,f3,f4].\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{C}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}].

Remark B.2 (Why the fourth Weyl algebra appears).
The main construction has four algebraically independent output coordinates
f1,f2,f3,f4f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}. Pairing them with the four Hamiltonian derivations in
(74) produces four multiplication–derivation pairs,
which is precisely the generator count for 𝒜4\mathcal{A}_{4}. Thus the same
polynomials naturally connect the rank-two canonical Poisson algebra—where
“rank two” counts two canonical pairs—with the fourth Weyl algebra, whose
index counts four polynomial coordinates and their four conjugate
derivations.
