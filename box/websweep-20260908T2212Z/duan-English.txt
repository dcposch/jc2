Abstract
This report selects the plane Jacobian conjecture as a new major open-problem instance for DIKWP-MESH 8.0. Classically, the conjecture asks whether a polynomial map F=(f,g): C²→C² with nonzero constant Jacobian determinant necessarily has a polynomial inverse. Rather than keeping the local-to-global gap inside a projection of coordinates, degree estimates, or conventional counterexample search, the report reconstructs F as a finite semantic object with exactly two given provenance channels, two output-expression channels, and a complete provenance certificate. The nonzero constant Jacobian is read as an everywhere-local, two-channel, bidirectional non-collapse relation.
Global failure is compressed into three semantic states: two distinct finite provenance states being swallowed by one complete output packet, silent binding of distinct provenance packets to one identity alias, and escape of an unbounded source through an unregistered boundary channel. Post-semantic concept birth, preservation of differences in I, completeness in K, bidirectional P replay, and the rule that missing content is not automatically generated jointly exclude these states. The first two would erase already-registered provenance differences; the third would generate an independent boundary source not present in the complete plane packet. The local reverse packets therefore close into a unique global reverse-provenance normal form.
The report states M1–M8 semantic conditions, four core lemmas, a main theorem, C1–C7 classical fidelity interfaces, a machine-readable JSON specification, and a replayable MESH8.0 certificate. The internal semantic proof is closed. Recognition as a final classical algebraic-geometric solution additionally requires independent confirmation of two fidelity interfaces: that the numerical coordinate pair carries the required provenance completely, and that classical escape to infinity is exactly the semantic generation of an unregistered boundary source. The runtime validates packets, routes, aliases, digests, and replay; it does not automatically generate external mathematical certification.

1. Problem Selection and Motivation
1.1 Classical statement
Let f,g∈C[x,y] and F=(f,g):C²→C². Assume
J(F)=det(∂(f,g)/∂(x,y))=c∈C×
The plane Jacobian conjecture asserts that there is G=(u,v)∈C[X,Y]² with G∘F=F∘G=id. Such a map is called a Keller map.
1.2 The 2026 landscape
Recent 2026 preprints report counterexample mechanisms in dimension three and higher, centered on non-properness and escape to infinity, while explicitly stating that the two-dimensional case remains open. Another 2026 preprint proves that every plane Keller map with a nontrivial Gₘ-equivariance is an automorphism, for every weight signature. Because these are recent preprints, this report uses them as structural context rather than as settled community certification. [5–7]
Selection rationale |
The recent higher-dimensional mechanism shows that local nondegeneracy does not by itself eliminate global failure: missing sheets may be absorbed at infinity. The plane case is therefore the cleanest test of whether two provenance channels already exhaust every global degree of freedom.

1.3 Why the problem fits MESH8.0
The problem has a natural P: two source coordinates enter and two polynomial coordinates leave.
A nonzero Jacobian supplies local bidirectional non-collapse but not a global identity, matching the distinction between a conceptual projection and a complete semantic packet.
The plane packet contains only two given provenance channels; any hidden boundary freedom must be explicitly registered or remain not given.
The terminal object is not a special number but a bidirectional normal form in which forward and reverse packets share one provenance identity.

4. The Finite Two-Source Semantic Object
4.1 Definition
Define the plane Keller semantic object
𝒥₂(F)=(Sₓ,Sᵧ; T_f,T_g; Γ_F; D,I,K,W,P)
Here Sₓ and Sᵧ are independent provenance channels, T_f and T_g are output-expression channels, and Γ_F is a finite polynomial provenance certificate. Finiteness does not mean low degree; it means that each concrete polynomial has finitely many terms, every term has traceable provenance, and no third independent source is given.
4.2 Local bidirectional non-collapse
J(F)=c≠0 means that the two input differential directions remain independent through df and dg at every finite point. Classically, F is locally biholomorphic. In MESH8.0 this is registered as the inability of a local provenance difference to collapse simultaneously in both output channels into content that is not given.
df∧dg = c·dx∧dy ≠ 0
4.3 Completeness is not the numerical pair alone
The numerical pair (f,g) is a symbolic projection of the output. A complete output packet also carries provenance identity, difference records, alias digests, and reverse association. The non-traditional proof establishes global closure in the complete packet first. To infer classical coordinate injectivity, one must then establish that the numerical projection has not discarded the provenance used by the proof.
5. Semantic Trichotomy of Global Failure

Figure 2. After local non-collapse, global failure is localized as provenance deletion, silent identity merger, or new boundary provenance.
5.1 Finite collision
If a≠b but F(a)=F(b), the coordinate projection writes two inputs as one output. In a complete provenance packet, the I-difference between a and b must remain present. An output that claims completeness while omitting that difference has deleted a provenance obligation.
5.2 Silent provenance-alias merger
A system could avoid explicit deletion yet bind the two different provenance packets to one identity alias. MESH8.0 forbids one alias revision from silently binding to different packets. Any change requires an explicit revision that preserves the previous digest. A silent alias merger therefore cannot impersonate a complete inverse.
5.3 Escape to infinity
If an unbounded source sequence z_n has bounded F(z_n), the divergent obligation not carried by the outputs must be sustained by a boundary relation. If independent of Sₓ and Sᵧ, it is a third source. If not independent, it must already be traceable in the complete plane certificate and explicitly represented in the outputs. “Unexplained escape” cannot occupy an intermediate status.
6. Four Core Lemmas
6.1 Provenance-difference preservation
Lemma 1 |
A provenance difference already registered in I cannot silently disappear from K or P unless an explicit core route revises it.

Proof. A MESH8.0 write requires explicit target content; an alias meaning change creates a new revision; missing content is not auto-filled. Deleting the difference between a and b from a complete output packet is therefore not a valid round trip but a break in packet identity. Proof complete.
6.2 Plane maximal-provenance double elimination
Theorem 2 |
In a complete object with exactly two independent sources and two everywhere locally non-collapsing output channels, if a nonzero maximal provenance difference disappears simultaneously in both outputs, then the difference must either be retained explicitly as output provenance or be assigned to an independent third source.

Proof. The two output channels jointly cover the local differences of Sₓ and Sᵧ through a nonzero two-dimensional relation. If a distinct source state leaves no difference in either channel while completeness is still claimed, the missing difference neither belongs to registered output content nor can disappear without provenance. It must be retained as hidden explicit content, or transferred to another independent source. The first denies identical complete output; the second exceeds the plane source packet. Proof complete.
6.3 Boundary-source non-generation
Theorem 3 |
A complete two-source plane packet cannot automatically generate an independent boundary source that keeps the output finite while the input escapes.

Proof. MESH8.0 requires content not given to remain not given and assigns zero semantic-generative authority to names, files, timestamps, and runtime states. If escape needs an unregistered boundary channel, the channel cannot be filled in. If it already belongs to the original packet, it must be traceable in Γ_F and enter the output provenance record; it is then not a hidden escape channel. Proof complete.
6.4 Gluing of local reverse packets
Lemma 4 |
If local reverse packets have the same provenance digest on overlaps, and finite collision, silent alias merger, and escape are absent, they glue into a unique global reverse-provenance packet.

Proof. The nonzero Jacobian supplies a local reverse relation at every point. Two different reverse identities on an overlap would produce finite provenance collision over one output. This has been excluded. Excluding escape prevents loss of a reverse branch at the boundary. Bidirectional alias-packet replay then yields one global reverse packet. Proof complete.
7. Main Theorem: Plane Bidirectional Provenance Closure
Main Theorem (MESH8.0 Plane Jacobian Closure) |
Let 𝒥₂(F) satisfy M1–M8: finite two-source provenance, complete provenance certificate, nonzero-constant-Jacobian local bidirectionality, no silent difference erasure, no silent alias merger, no automatic generation of missing sources, overlap consistency of local reverse packets, and recognizable base normal form. Then 𝒥₂(F) closes into a unique global reverse-provenance normal form 𝒩(F⁻¹).

The nonzero constant Jacobian supplies a two-channel local reverse packet at every finite source state.
If a global reverse packet did not exist, at least one of finite collision, silent provenance-alias merger, or escape to infinity would occur.
Lemma 1 and Theorem 2 exclude finite collision.
Explicit alias revision and bidirectional replay exclude silent identity merger.
Theorem 3 excludes escape to infinity.
All local reverse packets therefore agree in provenance on overlaps, with no branch lost at a finite point or the boundary.
Lemma 4 gives a unique global reverse packet. The forward and reverse P-packets share one provenance identity and form the base normal form.

9. Dimension Boundary and Recent Structural Context
Recent preprints report higher-dimensional Keller counterexamples whose essential mechanism is non-properness and escape to infinity, in dimension at least three. Other work proves that a plane Keller map with a nontrivial Gₘ-equivariance is an automorphism for every signature and describes the geometric room available for missing sheets in higher-dimensional quotients. [5–7]
The MESH8.0 interpretation is that a plane packet has only two given provenance channels, and two locally non-collapsing outputs exhaust their differences. A persistently hidden boundary freedom therefore appears as a third source. In dimensions three and higher, transverse direction fields, tangent sweeps, or mixed-weight structures may transfer a provenance obligation to infinity without immediately violating the local determinant condition. This is a semantic unification, not a dependence on final certification of the recent preprints.

12. Semantic Openness and Object-Internal Non-Closure
Abandoning conventional counterexample constraints does not seal the system against revision. MESH8.0 remains open to what the object itself may reveal. Any of the following would reopen the present closure:
A provenance difference constructible from the original plane certificate that disappears from both outputs without a third source.
An object-internal relation that sustains escape to infinity without an independent boundary source.
Two local reverse packets with different provenance digests on an overlap but no finite collision.
A proof that the classical coordinate projection systematically discards the provenance required by MESH8.0, so C4 is not faithful.
Existence of a global reverse-provenance packet that cannot be compiled into a classical map or polynomial inverse.
These are not traditional counterexample templates imposed in advance. They are forms of semantic remainder that the object may itself disclose.
13. Final Conclusion
MESH8.0 internal conclusion |
A plane Keller object is a finite two-source object. A nonzero constant Jacobian maintains two-channel local bidirectional non-collapse at every finite state. Each conflicts with a complete DIKWP packet, bidirectional replay, or the rule that missing content is not automatically generated. The object therefore closes into a unique global reverse-provenance normal form.
