## 1. The normalized infinity problem has a unique polynomial solution candidate

Put `q=2t+1`, `alpha=3/(4y^2)`, and use F1--F3. The leading coefficient simplifies:

    omega = alpha/(6d+3),     3d^2=t+1.

Indeed `4t+1=3(2d-1)(2d+1)`. None of `d`, `2d+1`, `alpha`, or `omega`
vanishes for t>=2, factorwise. Write `E` for the left side minus right side
of F3, and `W=sum_{j=0}^q w_j x^j`, with `w_q=omega`, `w_0=-B`.

Fix *arbitrary* monic C of degree t-1 and arbitrary b. There is a unique
pair `(W,B)` with these constant and leading conventions for which

    [x^n]E=0 for every q<=n<=2q.

This is a polynomial construction in the coefficients of C and b, over
the scalar field factor, and imposes no lower row. First solve for
`w_{q-1},...,w_1`, in that order. The coefficient `[x^(q+m)]E`, m>=1,
is affine in w_m with scalar diagonal

    L_m = 2*((q+m-1)*omega+alpha)
        = 2*omega*(m+2t+3+6d).

Every other coefficient of W entering this row has larger index. Terms
involving B, eta, r, and K have degree at most q here, except D*W and A*D,
whose dependence on W also has index larger than m because deg D=t<q.
The norm of the last factor is

    (m+2t+3)^2-12(t+1)>0

for t>=2, m>=0. Thus the recursion is valid on both field factors, including
the split indices. The leading `x^(2q)` row is the normalization identity.

After these choices, the coefficient `[x^q]E` has B coefficient

    -((2q-1)*omega+alpha) = -2*alpha*d,

a scalar unit. This solves B. The lower rows `0<=n<q` remain an exact
necessary and sufficient residual for F3. Rows 0 and 1 vanish identically
by the definitions `w_0=-B`, `eta=w_1`; no lower row has been promoted.

## 2. A uniform symmetry exclusion, including repeated roots

Write

    C=x^(t-1)+sum_{j=1}^{t-1} c_j x^(t-1-j).

The construction in Section 1 commutes with the weighted action

    c_j -> lambda^j c_j,  b -> lambda^(t+1)b,
    W(x) -> lambda^q W(x/lambda),
    B -> lambda^q B,  eta -> lambda^(q-1) eta.

This follows either row by row from the recursion or by substituting
`C_lambda(x)=lambda^(t-1) C(x/lambda)` in F3. Every term in F3 scales by
`lambda^(2q)` after changing x to x/lambda.

**Theorem (nontrivial stabilizer exclusion).** For every t>=2 and both
field factors, a normalized F3 solution with B*eta nonzero has trivial
stabilizer under this multiplicative action on the input `(C,b)`.

**Proof.** Suppose a nonidentity lambda fixes C and b. Uniqueness in
Section 1 gives `B=lambda^q B` and `eta=lambda^(q-1) eta`. If B and eta
were both nonzero, these equalities would give both lambda^q=1 and
lambda^(q-1)=1, hence lambda=1. Contradiction. No root has been divided
out or assumed distinct. This includes the case C=x^(t-1), b=0, whose
stabilizer is the whole multiplicative group. QED.

Equivalently, if an integer ell>=2 divides every j for which c_j!=0,
and either b=0 or ell divides t+1, then B*eta=0 for every normalized
solution. A primitive ell-th root supplies the nontrivial stabilizer.
Examples include all b=0 supports

    C=x^(t-1)+c*x^(t-1-ell),    2<=ell<=t-1,

and more generally `C=x^m P(x^ell)` with P monic. Root collisions are
unrestricted. This is a coefficient-sensitive uniform partial, not a
proof on the complementary support strata: on B*eta!=0 the nonzero
weights among `{j:c_j!=0}` and `{t+1:b!=0}` must have gcd 1.

For b!=0 and a nontrivial stabilizer of order ell, ell divides t+1;
then `q=2(t+1)-1` is coprime to ell, so the theorem actually forces B=0.
For b=0 and ell arbitrary, B and eta transform with the consecutive
weights q and q-1, so at least one vanishes.

### Stronger numerical-semigroup version

The recursion in Section 1 consists only of polynomial operations and
division by the displayed scalar units. Consequently B and eta are
polynomials in the input coefficients, homogeneous of weights q and q-1.
For a support set

    Sigma={j:c_j!=0} union ({t+1} if b!=0),

let N*Sigma denote the additive semigroup generated by Sigma, including 0.
If q is not in this semigroup then B=0; if q-1 is not in it then eta=0.
Thus every point on B*eta!=0 must have *both* consecutive integers 2t
and 2t+1 in N*Sigma. This is stronger than the gcd condition, is valid
for every t>=2 on both factors, and keeps arbitrary multiplicities.

For a binomial C with a single nonzero gap j>=2, and arbitrary b,
Sigma is contained in `{j,t+1}`. Since q<2(t+1), membership gives

    q in <j,t+1> iff j divides (2t+1) or j divides t,
    q-1 in <j,t+1> iff j divides (2t) or j divides (t-1).

Here `<j,t+1>` is the enlarged semigroup; when b=0, N*Sigma may be a
strict subset. Thus these memberships are only necessary conditions for
B*eta!=0, never sufficient conditions for a solution.

Both can hold only when `j|t` or `j=3 and t=1 mod 3`:
the two alternatives pairing j|(2t+1) with j|(2t) are incompatible,
and pairing j|(2t+1) with j|(t-1) forces j|3. Conversely these listed
conditions do give both memberships. Therefore all binomial-C strata

    C=x^(t-1)+c*x^(t-1-j),  2<=j<=t-1,

with arbitrary b satisfy B*eta=0 unless `j|t` or `j=3,t=1 mod 3`.
For example at the first nontrivial split index t=11, every such j from
2 through 10 is excluded uniformly in c and b, on both d=+2 and d=-2.
The same holds for every prime t=2 mod 3. This is an exact support
argument, not a finite computation or an extrapolation from t=11.

The cyclic family `C=x^r(x^ell-a)^m`, r+ell*m=t-1, ell>=2, b=0
(or ell|t+1), is covered as well, with each nonzero root having the
unrestricted multiplicity m and zero having multiplicity r.

## 3. Exact classification on the full monomial-C stratum

When C=x^(t-1), the high recursion is explicit, for arbitrary b:

    W=omega*x^q + k*b*x^t - B,
    k=-1/(2y(3d+2)).

The only nonzero lower positive coefficient allowed by the high rows is
w_t. In its row, `L_t=6*omega*(t+2d+1)` and
`[x^t]D=3b/(2y)`. Since `t+2d+1=d(3d+2)`, solving this row gives k as
displayed. Every other lower positive w_m is zero. The x^q row forces
B=0. In particular eta=0 for t>=2.

The coefficient x^(t-1) of the remaining E is

    b^3*k*(2-t)/2.

Since k is a scalar unit, t>=3 forces b=0. Thus the *only* normalized
solution on C=x^(t-1) for t>=3 is

    b=B=eta=0, W=omega*x^q.

The x^(2t) coefficient vanishes when b=0 and the leading equation then
verifies the entire F3 identity. For t=2 the cubic obstruction vanishes;
the separate x^4 coefficient retains the known exceptional-factor
behavior and must not be inferred from the t>=3 assertion.

### Converse classification when b=B=0, arbitrary C

For every t>=2, **any** normalized solution with b=B=0 has
`C=x^(t-1)` and `W=omega*x^q`. This is stronger than assuming monomial C.
Here F3 reads `2xWW'=W^2-2AW+A^2/3`. If rho!=0 were a root of W,
substitution would make rho a root of A, of even order 2m because
`A=alpha*x^3*C^2`. Put s=ord_rho(W)>0. The left side has exact order
2s-1. If s<=2m, every term on the right has order at least 2s, impossible.
If s>2m, the A^2 term gives exact right-hand order 4m, incompatible with
the odd order 2s-1. This includes s=2m and arbitrary root multiplicities.
Thus all roots of W lie at 0, and W=omega*x^q. The equation now becomes

    (A-3*omega*x^q)^2=(6*d*omega*x^q)^2.

The fixed leading coefficient selects `A=alpha*x^q`, hence monicity gives
C=x^(t-1). This is a proof using the linked square, although it concerns
a stratum where B*eta already vanishes and does not close the open chart.

## 4. Shifted coincident roots: exact split-index slice certificates

For b=0, C=(x-a)^(t-1), a!=0, weighted scaling reduces to a=1.
The driver `rigidity_single_root.py` implements Section 1 using rational
pair arithmetic in `Q[d]/(3d^2-t-1)`, so split factors remain present.
It normalizes `W=alpha V`, `B=alpha beta`, `eta=alpha e`, removing y.

The driver checks every high row exactly, reports nonzero lower rows,
and checks their norms. A nonzero norm proves a residual is nonzero on
*both* split factors; this is not a modular computation. At t=3 and
t=11 the x^2 residual has nonzero norm and therefore excludes this
single-root stratum. A uniform closed formula or sign proof for this
residual has not been found. This finite observation is not extrapolated.

The stronger driver `rigidity_coincident_allb.py` keeps arbitrary b and
normalizes

    V=W/alpha, beta=B/alpha, e=eta/alpha, u=b*y,
    a=x^3*C^2, delta=2u*x*C, K=x^2*C-u.

The exact normalized residual is

    E = 2*x*V*V' - V^2-beta*V +2a*V-delta*V
        -a^2/3+a*delta/3+beta*(a-delta)
        -(2u^2/3)*V' +(2u/3)*e*K +beta*e*x
        -(2u^2/3)*a/x +(4u^2/3)*(beta+V)/x.

The quotients are polynomial. Every positive coefficient of V, as well
as beta, is affine in u: its weight is at most q=2t+1<2(t+1). Every lower

## 5. Remaining obstruction and a resonance caution

The preceding uniform results retain all multiplicities but do not
exclude input supports with gcd 1. They therefore do not close
OPEN[K16-8.1-RADICAL] for general t, nor promote theorem (T).

The formal infinity diagonal would vanish at Laurent index

    m_*=-2t-3-6d.

This is negative for both d and all t>=2. It is integral at the split
indices d an integer, where `m_*=-6d(d+1)-1`. This observation can locate
a negative-power recursion resonance; it supplies neither generic-index
nonexistence nor split-index existence, because the polynomial recursion
uses only m>=0 and is uniformly nonresonant. A proof would have to use
the remaining lower rows, not the absence of this formal resonance.
