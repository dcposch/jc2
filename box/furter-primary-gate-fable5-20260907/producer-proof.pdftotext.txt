     Abstract. Let 𝑎, 𝑏 ∈ ℂ[𝑧] be polynomials with 𝑎 ∘ 𝑏(0) = 0 and (𝑎 ∘ 𝑏)′ (0) = 1. We
     prove that either 𝑎 ∘ 𝑏 = 𝑧 or
                              ord0 (𝑎 ∘ 𝑏 − 𝑧) ≤ deg 𝑎 + deg 𝑏 − 1.

     The proof is dynamical: scalar multiplication splits a degenerate fixed point into
     attracting fixed points without changing the number of distinct critical values, attracting
     basins are separated by critical values, and critical values are subadditive under compo­
     sition. The bound is sharp for every pair of degrees. It proves the rigidity conjecture
     𝑅(𝑚, 𝑛) of Furter for all 𝑚, 𝑛 ≥ 1, previously known only for min(𝑚, 𝑛) ≤ 2. Conse­
     quently the composition-coefficient map 𝔸𝑚+𝑛 → 𝔸𝑚+𝑛 is finite and flat of degree
     ( 𝑚+𝑛                                                                        2
        𝑚 ), the closure of every length-two multidegree stratum of Aut(𝔸ℂ ) is the one
     predicted by Furter, and the Strong Factorial Conjecture of Edo and van den Essen holds
     for all polynomials 𝑋1 ⋯𝑋𝑚 (𝜇1 𝑋1 + ⋯ + 𝜇𝑚 𝑋𝑚 ).


1. Introduction
For a nonzero convergent power series 𝑢 at the origin, ord0 𝑢 denotes the exponent of its lowest-
order term. For a nonconstant polynomial 𝑓 ∈ ℂ[𝑧] let
                               CV(𝑓) = {𝑓(𝜁) : 𝜁 ∈ ℂ, 𝑓 ′ (𝜁) = 0}

be the set of distinct finite critical values of 𝑓; multiplicities are not counted, so # CV(𝑓) ≤
deg 𝑓 − 1.
  In [1] Furter introduced the following statement about polynomial composition.
Conjecture 1.1 (𝑅(𝑚, 𝑛), Furter). Let 𝑚, 𝑛 ≥ 1 and let
                                      𝑚                          𝑛
                         𝑎(𝑧) = 𝑧 + ∑ 𝑎𝑖 𝑧𝑖+1 ,       𝑏(𝑧) = 𝑧 + ∑ 𝑏𝑗 𝑧𝑗+1
                                      𝑖=1                       𝑗=1

be polynomials with complex coefficients. If 𝑎 ∘ 𝑏(𝑧) = 𝑧 + 𝑂(𝑧𝑚+𝑛+2 ), then 𝑎 = 𝑏 = 𝑧.
Furter proved 𝑅(𝑚, 𝑛) when 𝑚 ≤ 2 or 𝑛 ≤ 2 [1, Theorem A], verified it by Gröbner basis compu­
tations for 𝑚 ≤ 8 and 𝑛 ≤ 5, and showed that 𝑅(𝑚, 𝑛) for 𝑑 = (𝑚 + 1, 𝑛 + 1) determines the
Zariski closure of the set of plane polynomial automorphisms of multidegree 𝑑 [1, Theorem B]. Edo
and van den Essen [2] showed that the family 𝑅(𝑚) of inverse-coefficient statements equivalent
to the 𝑅(𝑚, 𝑛) is a special case of their Strong Factorial Conjecture. Lewis, Perry and Straub [3]
proposed an algorithmic approach to the multidegree closure problem and recovered the known
cases. The case 𝑅(3), equivalently 𝑅(3, 𝑛) for all 𝑛, remained open.
   The main result of this paper is a contact bound for compositions from which 𝑅(𝑚, 𝑛) follows
for all 𝑚, 𝑛.

2. Attracting fixed points require distinct critical values
The following is the polynomial case of a theorem of Fatou; see [4, Section 8] for rational maps.
We include the short proof.

Lemma 2.1. Let 𝑃 ∈ ℂ[𝑧] have degree at least two. The number of distinct attracting fixed points of
𝑃 is at most # CV(𝑃 ).
Proof. Let 𝑝 be a fixed point with multiplier 𝜆 = 𝑃 ′ (𝑝), |𝜆| < 1. Its basin
                                     𝐵𝑝 = {𝑧 ∈ ℂ : 𝑃 ∘𝑛 (𝑧) → 𝑝}

is open, since it is the union of the preimages of a small disk around 𝑝 on which 𝑃 is a contraction,
and bounded, since for an escape radius 𝑅 with |𝑃 (𝑧)| > 2|𝑧| whenever |𝑧| > 𝑅 every point
outside the disk of radius 𝑅 escapes to infinity.
   We claim that 𝐵𝑝 contains a critical point of 𝑃 . If 𝜆 = 0, then 𝑝 itself is critical. Assume 0 <
|𝜆| < 1 and suppose that no critical point lies in 𝐵𝑝 .
   Choose a disk 𝐷 = 𝐷(𝑝, 𝜌) ⊂ 𝐵𝑝 . Every point of 𝑃 −𝑛 (𝐷) lies in 𝐵𝑝 . The derivative of 𝑃 ∘𝑛 does
not vanish on 𝑃 −𝑛 (𝐷): a zero would mean that some intermediate iterate 𝑃 ∘𝑖 (𝑧), 0 ≤ 𝑖 < 𝑛, is a
critical point of 𝑃 whose orbit later enters 𝐷, hence lies in 𝐵𝑝 . Since a polynomial is proper, the
restriction
                                          𝑃 ∘𝑛 : 𝑃 −𝑛 (𝐷) → 𝐷

is a proper unramified holomorphic map. On each connected component of 𝑃 −𝑛 (𝐷) its image
is open and, by properness, closed in 𝐷, hence equal to 𝐷; a proper local biholomorphism onto
the simply connected disk 𝐷 is a covering, hence a biholomorphism on each component. The
component containing 𝑝 supplies an inverse branch

                 𝑢𝑛 : 𝐷 → 𝐵𝑝 ,     𝑢𝑛 (𝑝) = 𝑝,    𝑃 ∘𝑛 ∘ 𝑢𝑛 = id𝐷 ,     (𝑢𝑛 )′ (𝑝) = 𝜆−𝑛 .

All 𝑢𝑛 take values in 𝐵𝑝 ⊂ {|𝑧| ≤ 𝑅}. Cauchy’s estimate on the circle of radius 𝜌/2 about 𝑝 gives
|(𝑢𝑛 )′ (𝑝)| ≤ 2𝑅/𝜌 for every 𝑛, contradicting |𝜆|−𝑛 → ∞.
    Choose a critical point 𝜁𝑝 ∈ 𝐵𝑝 . Its image 𝑃 (𝜁𝑝 ) is a finite critical value lying in 𝐵𝑝 , because 𝐵𝑝
is invariant under 𝑃 . The basins of distinct fixed points are disjoint, since an orbit cannot converge
to two points. Thus distinct attracting fixed points give distinct critical values.                       □

7. Closures of multidegree strata
Let 𝒢︀ = Aut(𝔸2ℂ ) be the group of polynomial automorphisms of the complex affine plane, let 𝒜︀ ⊂
𝒢︀ be the affine subgroup and ℬ︀ ⊂ 𝒢︀ the triangular subgroup of automorphisms (𝑥, 𝑦) ↦ (𝛼𝑥 +
𝑝(𝑦), 𝛽𝑦 + 𝛾) with 𝛼𝛽 ≠ 0. By the theorem of Jung and van der Kulk [7], [8], 𝒢︀ is the amalgamated
product of 𝒜︀ and ℬ︀ over 𝒜︀ ∩ ℬ︀. Every 𝑔 ∈ 𝒢︀ ∖ 𝒜︀ can be written as 𝑔 = 𝑎0 ∘ 𝑏1 ∘ 𝑎1 ∘ ⋯ ∘ 𝑏𝑙 ∘ 𝑎𝑙
with 𝑎𝑖 ∈ 𝒜︀ and 𝑏𝑗 ∈ ℬ︀ ∖ 𝒜︀, and the sequence (deg 𝑏1 , …, deg 𝑏𝑙 ) of integers ≥ 2 depends only
on 𝑔. It is the multidegree of 𝑔 [1], [9], also called the polydegree [3]; affine automorphisms have
multidegree ∅. For a multidegree 𝑑 let 𝒢︀𝑑 ⊂ 𝒢︀ be the set of automorphisms of multidegree 𝑑. The

For a nonzero polynomial 𝑓 let 𝑁 (𝑓) be its number of monomials. The Factorial Conjecture asserts
that 𝐿(𝑓 𝑘 ) = 0 for all 𝑘 ≥ 1 forces 𝑓 = 0. Edo and van den Essen [2] formulated the stronger
statement below and connected it to Furter’s conjecture.
Conjecture 8.1 (Strong Factorial Conjecture, [2]). Let 𝑓 ∈ ℂ[𝑚] be nonzero. For every 𝑛 ≥ 1 there
is 𝑘 with 𝑛 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛 + 𝑁 (𝑓) − 1 and 𝐿(𝑓 𝑘 ) ≠ 0.
Let 𝐸 [𝑚] = {𝑋1 ⋯𝑋𝑚 (𝜇1 𝑋1 + ⋯ + 𝜇𝑚 𝑋𝑚 ) : 𝜇1 , …, 𝜇𝑚 ∈ ℂ}. Theorem 2.25(5) of [2] states that
Conjecture 8.1 holds for every 𝑓 ∈ 𝐸 [𝑚] if and only if 𝑅(𝑚′ ) holds for all 𝑚′ ≤ 𝑚. Corollary 5.1
therefore gives the following.
Corollary 8.2. For every 𝑚 ≥ 1 the Strong Factorial Conjecture holds for every polynomial in 𝐸 [𝑚] :
if 𝑓 = 𝑋1 ⋯𝑋𝑚 (𝜇1 𝑋1 + ⋯ + 𝜇𝑚 𝑋𝑚 ) is nonzero, then for every 𝑛 ≥ 1 there is 𝑘 with 𝑛 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛 +
𝑁 (𝑓) − 1 and 𝐿(𝑓 𝑘 ) ≠ 0.
