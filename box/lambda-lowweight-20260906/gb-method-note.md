# Exact weighted runs: implementation and interpretation

`run_truncated.py` streams coefficient polynomials into Singular 4.3.2 over
Q. The variable order is the production metadata order, followed by `Z,ss`
(or `T,Z,ss` in the Keller variant). There is exactly one global `wp` block.
The weight of `h_b_a` is `K-a`, that of `Ai_b_a` and `Bi_b_a` is `i*K-a`,
and that of `c` is `n+m-1`. Every original parameter has positive weight.
The added variables have weight 1.

The source TSV is scanned in full and SHA-256 hashed. Complete original
rows, rather than low-weight parts of rows, are retained precisely when
`n+m-1-K*h_power-y_power <= B`. Every monomial in every retained row is
checked to have bidegree `(x_power+1,n+m-1-K*h_power-y_power)` using the
printed coefficient grading. Source polynomials are not copied into a
retained artifact tree. Independent audit `leader-bidegree-audit.json`
checks every monomial of all ten exact canonical h-adic leaders to have
bidegree `(0,W)`; the compressed formulas remain the actual input.

The affine localizer is represented homogeneously by

    Z*lambda - ss^(W+1).

The cutoff is `B=N*W+3`, for N=1,2,3. The script sets `degBound=B`, runs
`std`, and computes the normal form of `ss^B`. It records basis size,
maximum basis weight, whether the normal form is zero, and whether it is
exactly the original monomial. A completed nonzero normal form excludes a
unit certificate of homogeneous weight at most B in this presentation.
This margin covers `N*(W+1)` for all three powers. It is not a direct
inhomogeneous positive-wp cutoff on `Z*lambda-1`.

The baseline ideal is the Jacobian coefficient ideal I_J, prior to
`T*c-1`. The distinct `.keller` variant adds `T*c-ss^(n+m)` whenever that
generator's homogeneous weight is at most B. For smaller B the truncated
systems agree after adjoining an unused variable T. A baseline properness
claim must never be transferred to the untruncated Keller system.

The distinct `.charge0` variant computes the exact first-charge-zero
component relevant to the target `ss^B`. It retains only the localizer.
This is valid for this target because every Jacobian generator has strictly
positive first charge, every multiplier has nonnegative first charge, and
the localizer and target have first charge zero. Thus the charge-zero
component of `(I_J,Z*lambda-ss^(W+1))` equals that of the principal
localizer ideal. This variant is explicitly distinguished from completion
of the full weighted standard basis. Its principal basis has no nontrivial
critical pairs; nonzero `lambda` gives a nonzero target normal form at every
tested weight. It proves a fact about the necessary enlargement, not
attainment by a polynomial pair.

The first full probes use a declared 240-second wall cap. Larger solo
retries allow 900 seconds total and a separate 240-second cap after the
INPUT_READY marker. Final C455 Keller probes allow 900 seconds total and
60 seconds after INPUT_READY, with the lane's 03:40Z hard shutdown applied
if earlier. Every cap is at most the user's 40-minute limit. A cap hit is
`OPEN_WALL_CAP`; its requested cutoff is not a completed degree. A zero
remainder is only `UNIT_CANDIDATE_NEEDS_COFACTORS` until an original-ring
cofactor identity is extracted and verified. No global dimension or
survivor inference is made from a truncated output. Singular's warning
that a truncated object is not a full standard basis is retained in logs.

Resource custody: early single-job runs used a 16-GiB RSS threshold. Before
parallel execution, the old runner and its active child received an
inherited hard address-space limit of 8 GiB. The second runner received a
7.75-GiB hard address-space limit and a 7.5-GiB RSS threshold. Future
parallel subprocesses use 7.75-GiB address-space and 7.5-GiB RSS caps. At
most two Singular jobs run concurrently. Large solo retries instead use
15.8-GiB address-space and 15.5-GiB RSS caps; no second CAS is launched
during them. Peak process RSS is reported by `/usr/bin/time`; for
group kills, the 0.2-second process-tree monitor supplies the observed peak.
Temporary receiver rows live in `/dev/shm` and are removed before sealing.

The frontier variant first computes a truncated basis of original rows
of weight at most F=12. Every original prefix generator is checked to
reduce to zero in that basis. Higher retained rows are replaced by their
normal forms modulo this prefix after resetting degBound=0; the difference
has explicit standard reduction cofactors in the prefix ideal. This
preserves the ideal generated by selected rows. Only the final augmented
std at B can complete the requested target weight. A FRONTIER_READY marker
does not include the characteristic localizer. C341 full-memory frontier
runs did reach the final std, then exceeded their RSS caps.

Packed rings change exponent storage, not the ring or ideal. Before any
polynomial input, ringlist/maxExp creates an identical Q/wp ring with a
smaller exponent bit mask. Explicit coordinate-map, coefficient-field,
variable-count and every-weight checks precede the input, together with
the product ss^B*ss^B=ss^(2B). Mask511 has usable exponent capacity255 and
covers every 2B<=186 used here. Mask255 has capacity127 and is used only
for the new C455 Keller probes, where 2B<=114. Independent controls also
compare full small standard bases under the ring map. This mechanism is
not an exponent quotient or monomial deletion.

The distinct Keller+UX variant adds proven necessary linear attainment
constraints from UX.json. These are polynomial consequences of the
characteristic upper-coefficient/scalar-leader equations U, as proved in
UX-note.md and checked independently. The complete original variable
ring is retained. Each eligible UX coordinate z is a generator. Every
row f and the global canonical lambda are replaced by their monomial
remainder after setting these generator coordinates to zero. For each
discarded monomial the driver records its quotient by a deterministically
selected z; consequently f-filter(f)=sum(z*C_z) exactly. This establishes
ideal equality after adjoining UX, without assuming an arbitrary slice.

To avoid repeated Python filtering, seven unique full row sources also
have temporary UX caches. Each cache receipt binds the original and
filtered SHA-256 hashes, all original/retained term gradings, and the
deterministic rational cofactor partition. The driver refuses a cached
case unless --ux is set and every required coordinate generator is
retained at the cutoff; it verifies the cache receipt SHA and records
original-source custody in each run receipt. Its second filter then
removes no further Jacobian terms; lambda still enters from the full
canonical formula and is checked against the exact UX-reduced hash.
C455's Jacobian shrinks only 22,470,719 to 20,539,571 terms (about9%),
despite its leader shrinking 2,672 to79 terms. Cache bytes are temporary
in /dev/shm; the retained receipts and reconstruction driver suffice.

R003 and R002 have mechanically identical ordered polynomial
presentations, including every source row and decompressed canonical
leader byte. R003 receives explicit same-map aliases for expensive full
probes, with the source receipt/hash/status preserved. All three R003
charge-zero tests were nevertheless executed individually. The final
best-results index selects authoritative receipts without deleting the
historical attempts. No OPEN receipt supplies its requested B as an
actually completed weight.
