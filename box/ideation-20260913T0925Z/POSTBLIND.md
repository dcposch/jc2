## 3. The proposed one-hour ring test has a direct answer

For tau(x,y)=(-x,2/x^2-y), set

    T: t^2-1=x^2 Z, sigma(x,t,Z)=(-x,-t,Z),
    i_-(x,y)=(x,x^2y-1,x^2y^2-2y).

The image U of i_- is T minus {x=0,t=1}; sigma(U) is T minus
{x=0,t=-1}. These two open charts cover T. A tau-invariant polynomial
P(x,y), viewed as a rational function on T, is regular on U and, by
invariance, on sigma(U). It is therefore globally regular on T and
sigma-invariant. Conversely every invariant global function pulls back to
an invariant polynomial. The already banked two-chart/invariant-ring
calculation gives

    C[x,y] intersect C(x,y)^tau
      = i_-^* O(T)^sigma
      = C[x^2, x^3 y-x, x^2 y^2-2y].

For the last equality, write each T-function uniquely a(x,Z)+t b(x,Z):
invariance makes a even and b odd in x. Its generators are x^2,xt,Z,
with (xt)^2=x^2+x^4 Z. This is the SAME S ring, at every polynomial
degree, not an expanded candidate space. It is a direct attachment of
the existing DS-INV-1 charts, not a new scientific computation. No one-hour
CAS/invariant-generator experiment is warranted.
