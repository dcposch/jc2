KELLER MAPS AND HOLONOMIC D-MODULES

arXiv:2609.10180v1 [math.AC] 9 Sep 2026

WENLIANG ZHANG

A BSTRACT. Let k be a field of characteristic p and R = k[x1 , . . . , xn ]. Let D denote the ring of
k-linear differential operators on R. Let ϕ : R → R be a k-algebra endomorphism whose Jacobian
is a unit in R. We show that there is a unique k-algebra endomorphism Φ : D → D such that
Φ|R = ϕ and that the restriction of scalar via Φ preserves holonomicity. We also construct an
example of a k-algebra endomorphism of D that does not preserve holonomicity.

1. I NTRODUCTION
Let k be a field of characteristic 0 and R = k[x1 , . . . , xn ] be a polynomial ring in n variables.
Let ϕ : R → R be a k-algebra endomorphism. ϕ is called Keller (or Jacobian by some authors)
if det(Jϕ ) ∈ k× where Jϕ denotes the Jacobian matrix of ϕ. The Jacobian conjecture ([Kel39])
asserts that every Keller endomorphism on R is an automorphism. This conjecture has been
investigated from a viewpoint of D-modules (cf. [Dix68, BCW82, Bas89, Bav05, BKK07]),
where D stands for the ring of k-linear differential operators on R (more details on D can be
found in §2). Let ϕ̃ denote a k-algebra endomorphism on D such that ϕ̃ |R = ϕ (the existence
of ϕ̃ is discussed in detail in §2). Let ϕ̃ R denote the D-module that agrees with R as an abelian
group with the (left) D-structure given by θ · r := ϕ̃(θ )r for each θ ∈ D. If ϕ̃ R remains a simple
D-module, then the Jacobian conjecture holds true (Remark 2.4). The recent counterexample
to the Jacobian conjecture ([Alp26]) implies that ϕ̃ R may not be a simple D-module. From the
D-module theoretic viewpoint, ϕ̃ R being a finite-length D-module is the best one can hope for.
Indeed, a remarkable result of Bavula ([Bav01, Theorem 1.3]) shows that if M is a holonomic Dmodule then so is ϕ̃ M; in particular ϕ̃ R has finite length as a D-module. (The notion of holonomic
D-modules is discussed in detail in §3.)
Recently there have been considerable developments and interest in D-modules in prime characteristic p, including the notion of holonomic D-modules. It is natural to ask whether [Bav01,
Theorem 1.3] can be extended to prime characteristic p, which is the motivation behind this article. The ring of differential operators behaves vastly differently in characteristic p from characteristic 0: in characteristic 0, D is has finitely many k-algebra generators {x1 , . . . , xn , ∂x1 , . . . , ∂xn }
and consequently an extension of a Keller map in Endk (D) is determined by its image on these
finitely many generators.However, in characteristic p, the ring D is no longer finitely generated

Our first main result, proved in §2, is the following:
Theorem 1.1 (=Theorem 2.11). Assume char(k) = p > 0 and R = k[x1 , . . . , xn ]. Let ϕ ∈ Endk (R)
be a Keller map. Then there is a unique Φ ∈ Endk (D) such that Φ|R = ϕ.

This is surprising and drastically different from characteristic 0. In characteristic 0, a Keller
map may admit infinitely many extensions in Endk (D) (Example 2.6). Moreover, in characteristic p, if ϕ is not Keller (e.g. the Frobenius endomorphism on R), then it may also admit infinitely
many extensions in Endk (D) (cf. Example 2.12).
To finish our extension of [Bav01, Theorem 1.3], we prove in §3 that:
Theorem 1.2 (=Theorem 3.8). Assume char(k) = p > 0 and R = k[x1 , . . . , xn ]. Let ϕ ∈ Endk (R)
be a Keller map and let Φ be its unique extension in Endk (D). Then the restriction of scalar via
Φ preserves holonomicity; that is, if M is a holonomic D-module, then so is Φ M.
If ϕ ∈ Endk (R) is not Keller (e.g. the Frobenius endomorphism), then ϕ may admit an extension in Endk (D) that does not preserve holonomicity. In §4, we construct such an example
(Example 4.1). Hence in characteristic p:
(1) if ϕ ∈ Endk (R) is Keller, then it admits a unique extension Φ ∈ Endk (D) and the restriction of scalar via Φ preserves holonomicity, or
(2) if ϕ ∈ Endk (R) is not Keller, then it may admit an extension ϕ̃ ∈ Endk (D) such that the
restriction of scalar via ϕ̃ does not preserve holonomicity.
Nonetheless, combining Theorem 1.1, Theorem 1.2 and [Bav01, Theorem 1.3] produces the
following characteristic-free statement:
Theorem 1.3. Let k be an arbitrary field and R = k[x1 , . . . , xn ]. Assume that ϕ ∈ Endk (R) is
Keller and let ϕ̃ be any extension of ϕ in Endk (D). Then the restriction of scalar via ϕ̃ preserves
holonomicity; that is, if M is a holonomic D-module, then so is ϕ̃ M. In particular, ϕ̃ R has finite
length in the category of D-modules.

Remark 2.4. Assume char(k) = 0. The functorial extensions of Keller maps are closely related
to the Jacobian conjecture. Let ϕ : R → R be a Keller map. Let ϕ̃ be the functorial extension
of ϕ to Endk (D). We claim that, if ϕ̃ R is a simple D-module, then ϕ must be an automorphism
(hence the Jacobian conjecture holds true). To see this, we consider the image of ϕ. Since
ϕ̃(θ )(r) = ϕ(θ (r)) by functoriality, the image ϕ(R) is naturally a D-submodule of ϕ̃ R. If ϕ̃ R is
a simple D-module, then ϕ(R) = R. Since R is noetherian, surjectivity of ϕ forces injectivity;
this shows that ϕ is an automorphism.

However, the recent counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture implies that ϕ̃ R may not be a
simple D-module. Hence, in terms of D-modules, the finite length property of ϕ̃ R is the best one
can hope for.
