**1. What Moh's D1 statement actually gives.** Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic zero and put t=x^(-1). The descended pair is polynomial and monic in y, of y-degrees n'=eK and m'=qK, with K=gcd(n',m'), and J(P,Q)=c x^ell, c≠0. Prime marks are labels. Moh's approximate-root definition on printed p.148 and Theorem 1.1 on p.149 distinguish a polynomial approximate root from arbitrary matching leading terms. Theorem 1.2, printed p.149/PDF page 10, imposes y-degree <K on expansion coefficients and order at least j times the root accuracy. Its hypotheses include a common coherent complete system and the corresponding quasi-approximate root. It does not impose total degree ≤jK. Nor does it state that a y-monic polynomial of degree K has x-degree ≤K−V2. Frozen `order-basis-full-gpt55-20260903.md:38` correctly describes the result as a necessary order lower bound, but the later inventory also imposed caps that do not follow from that lower bound.

For a CENTERED generic D1 root σ1=π t^(δ1), where π is transcendental over k, define

    D_i = {(b,a) in Z_{≥0}² : 0≤a<K,
                 b≤floor(a δ1 − i B)},
    B = B_safe = V2 δ1 + (K−V2) δ_s.

Then the full raw D1 h inventory is D_1:

    h = y^K + Σ_{(b,a) in D_1} h_ba x^b y^a.

Every listed coefficient is independently free in the over-approximation. The expansion uses

    P = h^e + Σ_{i=1}^e α_i h^(e−i),
    Q = h^q + Σ_{i=2}^q β_i h^(q−i),
    supp(α_i), supp(β_i) ⊆ D_i,   deg_y α_i,deg_y β_i<K.

The β1 block is zero because h is the unique qth approximate root of Q. This is not an optional coefficient gauge. Constants in the terminal α_e and β_q blocks may subsequently be removed by explicit translations of P and Q. No scalar shear is used on these twelve fibres: the relevant full α_(e−q) block is never just the scalar line.

Here is the elementary support proof, conditional precisely on the centered root. Substitution sends x^b y^a to π^a t^(aδ1−b). At the smallest occurring t-exponent, different a give different powers of π; for fixed a the exponent determines b. Thus cancellation cannot raise the minimum. Consequently

    ord_t F(t^(-1),πt^(δ1)) = min_{F_ba≠0}(aδ1−b).

The order floor ≥iB is therefore equivalent to the displayed support inequality. Monicity isolates y^K, whose weight Kδ1 is ≥B because δ1≥δ_s. For h, root multiplicity V2 inside D1 and K−V2 outside it give an order floor B: each inner factor contributes δ1 at the generic point, and every outer separation is at least δ_s. This is the weak safe floor, not the tighter and generally unjustified replacement δ_s→δ2. Negative upper bounds in the floor loop contribute no monomial; nonnegative upper bounds include BOTH endpoints. There is no residual infinite support: a ranges over K integers and each b range is finite.

The old code also imposed

    b+a≤K,   b≤max(K−V2,0).

Those are respectively a total-degree cap and an x-width cap. The source explanation about a y^V2 factor on the degree-K face cannot impose either condition on every lower coefficient, and cannot establish that degree K is the highest total-degree face. This is exactly the FALLACY-v2 distinction between an order floor and attainment/exhaustiveness.

**4. Source-support theorem using the outermost common disc.** The following argument applies to every class here, including s'=4. It assumes exactly the descended monomial-Jacobian datum and its effective characteristic tower; it does not assume two points at infinity for the descendant, a partition of h, or a second descent.

Moh explicitly extends Proposition 4.6 to J=x^ell in the remark on printed p.171/PDF page 32. Its modified order equation replaces −1+δ by −1−ell+δ. Proposition 5.1, pp.173–175, constructs the minimal disc containing all roots of g and its relevant characteristic polynomials, and Definition 5.1's note on p.179 identifies it as D_s. In particular it contains the roots of P=g and Q=T1 in the descended datum. For the last effective characteristic entry M_s, where M_s≠n'−1 after the p.174 convention, the terminal equation is

    δ_s = (δ_s−(ell+1))/(n'−M_s),
    δ_s = −(ell+1)/(n'−M_s−1).

A nonzero c has valuation zero and changes no order equation. Alternatively, the polynomial scaling x→a x with a^(ell+1)=c^(-1) reduces literally to J=x^ell over algebraically closed k, preserving y-monicity and root orders. The child terminal M is independently licensed: Prop.6.3(2) scales the y-degrees of the corresponding characteristic polynomials by u_s/d_s; Moh p.154 recovers characteristic data from those degrees, and the homogeneous auxiliary identities on p.150 give M_i′=(u_s/d_s)M_i. The outer radius therefore uses n′, M_s′ and ell, with no copied inner V root-count assumption. Raw V labels are used only to retain the frozen D1 coordinates. All twelve have δ_s<0, and the p.174 deletion is a no-op on their enumerated effective data. Put d=−δ_s>0.

Let Q be monic of y-degree N=qK and let

    η(x) = −[y^(N−1)]Q(x,y)/N ∈ k[x].

This is the mean of the Q roots. If the outer disc is {z:ord(z−A)≥δ_s}, the average of roots in it also lies in it: division by the nonzero integer N has valuation zero and the ultrametric inequality applies. Every P root and Q root ρ therefore satisfies ord(ρ−η)≥δ_s. The explicitly allowed polynomial automorphism is

    φ(x,y)=(x,y+η(x)),       φ^(-1)(x,y)=(x,y−η(x)).

Its determinant is one. The pair P∘φ,Q∘φ remains polynomial and monic with the same y-degrees, and its Jacobian remains cx^ell because x is unchanged. The roots in the new y-coordinate are ρ−η. Pairwise root contacts, and hence disc radii, are unchanged. This is the group element required by FALLACY-v2. A denominator in η is only the constant N, a unit in characteristic zero; there is no localization at a parameter or discarded branch.

Give x weight 1 and y weight d. For a monic polynomial F of y-degree L all of whose roots have valuation ≥−d, its coefficient of y^a is an elementary symmetric sum of L−a roots. Its t-order is ≥−d(L−a). Since that coefficient is polynomial in x=t^(-1),

    deg_x [y^a]F ≤ floor(d(L−a)),
    weighted_degree F ≤ dL.

This applies simultaneously to the translated P and Q. It allows a possibly smaller subfamily than the raw D1 inventory in some directions and a larger one in others. It will be contained in our final UNION support; no equality with raw D1 is claimed.

Take h to be the unique qth approximate root of the translated Q, monic of y-degree K. Write

    h=y^K+Σ_{j=1}^K H_j(x)y^(K−j).

Matching the coefficient of y^(qK−j), for j=1,…,K, in Q−h^q recursively solves qH_j as that coefficient of Q minus a polynomial in earlier H_i whose products have total index j. Induction gives deg_x H_j≤floor(dj): the degree of each such product is at most the sum of its bounds, hence at most floor(dj). Division by q is by a constant unit. This proves polynomiality and the necessary h support directly, without assuming a common inner-disc quasi-root theorem:

    G_1 = {(b,a):0≤a<K, 0≤b≤floor(d(K−a))}.

Moreover deg_y(Q−h^q)<(q−1)K, so the β1 coefficient in the h-adic expansion is exactly zero. That construction is compatible with all coefficient specializations over k; it introduces no nonvanishing hypothesis beyond c≠0.

Monic division by h preserves the weighted bounds. If weighted_degree F≤dL, a leading-y monomial L_r(x)y^r produces quotient L_r(x)y^(r−K) of weight ≤d(L−K). Its product with h has weight ≤dL; subtracting it cannot introduce a term of larger weight. Repeating terminates in the y-degree, including when a leading coefficient vanishes under specialization. Iterating the division gives the displayed P,Q expansions with

    G_i = {(b,a):0≤a<K, 0≤b≤floor(d(iK−a))}

for α_i and β_i. All divisions are monic, over the declared polynomial coefficient ring; there is no hidden fraction field or pivot saturation.

It follows that the FINAL inventory in block i may be

    S_i = D_i ∪ G_i.

Use S_1 for h, S_i for α_i, and S_i for β_i with i≥2. Every coordinate in the union is free. A trace-normalized actual pair is represented by its G_i coefficients and zero values for the other coordinates. This gives a source-to-chart map even if its D1 center is nonzero, and simultaneously retains all 76 previously omitted D1 h coordinates. The finite enlargement is deliberately an over-approximation: additional points do not certify a source or a Keller pair, but UNIT excludes every source in the fibre.
