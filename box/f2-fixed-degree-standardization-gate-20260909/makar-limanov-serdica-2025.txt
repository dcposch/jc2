     ON THE SHAPE OF A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE
      TWO-DIMENSIONAL JACOBIAN CONJECTURE∗

                                Leonid Makar-Limanov

                           Communicated by A. Giambruno


                         To Vesselin Drensky with best wishes


      Abstract. Polynomials f, g ∈ C[x, y] is a Jacobian pair if the Jacobian
      J(f, g) = 1. The Jacobian conjecture (JC) formulated by O. H. Keller states
      that then C[f, g] = C[x, y]. In this paper further information on the shape
      of the Newton polygon of f if the pair f, g is a counterexample to JC is
      obtained.

        Introduction.

        History
        This question makes sense for polynomials with any number of variables
and it was asked in 1939 by O. H. Keller (see [14]) though for polynomials with
integer coefficients. He checked it for a birational map.
        The case n = 1 is trivial, the first published proof for the case n = 2
appeared in 1955 (see [9]), and several other proofs, even for arbitrary n were
published a bit later ([24, 25, 26, 10]).
        The question still commanded some attention apparently because it has
a very simple algebraic formulation and some subtlety: it is certainly wrong if
the polynomials are replaced by rational or analytic functions but an answered
question is not as attractive as an open problem.
        Then all the proofs with exception of Engel’s became wrong and at least
for n > 2 the problem became open.
        Finally it was Engel’s turn. Apparently Abhyankar knew about mistakes,
but it seems that only after Vitushkin’s talk (see [29]) it became official.
        Say, L. A. Campbell, who was first to show the if C(x)/C(f ) is Galois
extension then it is birational, (see [6]) wrote the paper assuming that Engel’s
paper has no mistakes and added in proofs a remark with attribution to Vitushkin
that it has (at least) two.
        The paper [5] of Bass, Connell, and Wright which appeared in 1982 in
the Bulletin(New Series) of the American Mathematical Society about ten years
after JC became a problem again, contains a snapshot of what was known about
JC including interesting historical remarks.
        Thanks to this paper JC became very popular and now-days even has
its own code 14R15 in MSC2020. Also Smale (see [27]) included it in his list of
problems for the 21st century.
        Usually there are proofs of JC on the arXiv which are never published.
The last published wrong proof appeared in 2022 (see [4]).

         What is done in this paper
         There are different restrictions on potential counterexamples to JC (see
[1, 2, 3, 7, 12, 13, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 28]). Among them are
restrictions on the shape of the Newton polygons of potential counterexamples
and the quasi-homogeneous forms which are supported by their edges. Here a new
restriction on these forms is obtained. This gives some information on Newton
polygons of potential counterexamples.
         The Newton polygon of a polynomial in two variables is the convex hull
of degree vectors of all monomials which appear in this polynomial, i.e. if xi y j is
a monomial of a polynomial in question, the point with coordinates (i, j) belongs
to its Newton polygon.
