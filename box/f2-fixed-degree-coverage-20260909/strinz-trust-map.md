## Evidence classes

| Class | What it supports | What it does not support |
|---|---|---|
| Written proof | Theorem A, using identified published inputs and the finite-witness transfer | Kernel certification of the Newton-polygon literature or the whole reduction |
| Exact checked | Carrier classifications, hostile fixtures, Gate-B identities | Any stronger hypothesis or actual-source attachment not encoded by the checker |
| Kernel checked | The local K5 polynomial divisibility theorem in `formalization/Challenge.lean` | The carrier derivation, Theorem A, Gate B, or a `(75,125)` exclusion |
| Modular reconnaissance | Search and pricing information retained in the campaign archive | Characteristic-zero membership, nonmembership, or theorem status |

## Two complementary publication audits

Palomar asks whether a small, ordinary Lean statement is exactly what the
proved Solution establishes, under an allowed axiom set, and independently
replays the exported proof. The selected theorem is a short, essentially
single-exceptional-term divisibility lemma. It passes that local pipeline; the
badge would certify alignment and replay, not theorem depth or the carrier
derivation.

Grand Portage asks whether the broader publication portrait has coherent
source observations, claim scope, evidence custody, replay closure, and priced
open leaves. Its audit is derived-only (`graph_effect = NONE`): it reports
release readiness but grants no mathematical theorem authority.

Neither system composes Theorem A with the carrier model. The missing arrow in
the diagram is therefore visible rather than hidden by either verification
surface.
