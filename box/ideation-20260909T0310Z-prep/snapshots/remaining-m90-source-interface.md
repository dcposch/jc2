## Complete guarded B0 acceptance theorem

Work over Q, later passing to an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. Use ordinary source coordinates `(u,v)` and auxiliary `(X,Y)`. The injective homomorphism is

`Phi(u)=X^5`, `Phi(v)=Y+X^(-1)`.

Its inverse on its image is untranslation `Y -> v-X^(-1)`, followed by replacing `X^(5i)` by `u^i`. Equivalently, after adjoining `X^5=u`, set `Y=v-X^(-1)`. A Laurent polynomial in `X` and polynomial in `Y` belongs to this image exactly when **after untranslation** all X exponents are nonnegative multiples of 5 and all v exponents are nonnegative. Requiring trivial X characters before untranslation would be the wrong test. The chain rule is

`J_(X,Y)(Phi(P),Phi(Q)) = 5 X^4 Phi(J_(u,v)(P,Q))`.

Thus literal bracket `X^4` gives original Jacobian `1/5`, with positive sign. Now put `t=XY`, `z=Y^(-1)`, whose rational inverse is `X=tz`, `Y=z^(-1)` and whose Jacobian in `(X,Y)` is `-z`. In this chart,

`Phi(u^i v^j) = t^(5i-j) (1+t)^j z^(5i-j)`.

This is a polynomial-source image, not a free Laurent coefficient. Inverting auxiliary X or Y to check an identity does not introduce an original affine denominator: the source polynomials are independently defined before these changes, and the homomorphisms are injective.

Define the finite source supports by `i,j>=0`, `i+j<=D`, `ell=5i-j<=U`, with `(D,U,h)=(75,15,3)` for P and `(125,25,5)` for Q. On each ell,

`max(0,ceil(ell/5)) <= i <= floor((D+ell)/6)`, `j=5i-ell`.

Write `p_ell(t)=t^ell sum_i alpha_(ell,i)(1+t)^(5i-ell)` and similarly q with beta. The terminal halfspace `5a-17b<=h` is exactly the vanishing of the first

`r_ell=max(0,ceil((5ell-h)/12))`

Taylor coefficients of the sum. Its rows are binomial coefficients; the initial falling-factorial Vandermonde rows have full rational rank. The five fixed linear normalizations in the inspected source are

`[t^4]p_3=1`, `[t^21]p_15=1`, `[t]q_1=-1`, `[t^18]q_13=-3`, `[t^35]q_25=-9/5`.

### An exact smaller coefficient indexing, without a build

There is no need to expand the ramified bracket to obtain the same full ideal. Let `H=J_(u,v)(P,Q)-1/5`. Multiplication by `5X^4` after Phi and the `(t,z)` substitution is an injective **Q-linear** map on the finite-dimensional space containing H. Its coefficient matrix has a Q-linear left inverse on its image. Consequently the ideal generated by every transformed coefficient is **identical** to the ideal generated by every ordinary coefficient of H, over the polynomial parameter ring. The jet rows, normalizations and degree guard can be retained unchanged. This is ideal equality, not merely radical or point-set equality; there is no parameter pivot or new localization.

The left inverse is explicit, not an uncomputed matrix inversion. If `c_(e,L)` are the coefficients of the transformed residual, untranslate `X^e Y^(e-L)` after division by `5X^4`. For `L=5I-J+4`,

`[u^I v^J]H = (1/5) sum_(e>=L+J) (-1)^(e-L-J) binom(e-L,J) c_(e,L)`.

The sum is finite over the existing residual support. Its Y powers are nonnegative because every active binomial contribution has `e>=L`. Conversely each c is the fixed binomial expansion of `5X^4 Phi(H)`. These two literal transformations establish both ideal containments.
