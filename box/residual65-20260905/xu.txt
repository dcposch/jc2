                                                                                            1. Introduction
                                              Through out this paper, K denotes an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.
                                              Let (f (x, y), g(x, y)) be a Jacobian pair. The Jacobian condition fx gy − fy gx = J is an expression
                                           of two terms. We take a root of f (y)fy (y) in the Puiseux field K ≪ x−1 ≫ and plug in the
                                           expression. The expression becomes one term, therefore the orders of the factors of the expression
                                           are calculable in conditions.
                                              Moh in [Moh83] first introduces the concepts of π-root, major root and minor root. He gets
                                           plentiful results for major roots and a few for minor roots. For minor roots, Moh proves that any
                                           minor roots are not split before order 1. We introduce the concept of final roots. We find some
                                           relationship between intersection numbers and the split of final minor roots. On the other hand,
                                           I(fξ , g) can be expressed by only final major roots while I(fξ , fy ) can also be expressed by total
                                           split π-roots. These formulas and inequalities of final major roots and final minor roots provide
                                           some constrains for possible counter examples for Jacobian Conjecture.
                                              With the help of quasi-approximate roots and Abhyankar-Moh planar semi-group theory, we
                                           show that for any case, the principal minor roots are not splitting at order 1 for all effective quasi-
                                           approximate roots. This provide us a tool to further study the split possibilities for the principal
                                           minor roots.
                                              In the last section, we discuss the split possibilities for case (99, 66) that Moh does not discuss
                                           in details and find an unknown possible split and suggest case (99, 66) is open.

Corollary 4.8. Let (f, g) be a Jacobain pair. The following are equivalent.
    (i) K(f, g) = K(x, y).
   (ii) K[f, g] = K[x, y].
  (iii) I(f, g) = 1.
  (iv) f has no minor roots.
   (v) I(f, fy ) = degy f − 1.

Corollary 4.9. ([Xu93]) Let f (x, y) be a polynomial, monic in y. If f is an embedded line, then
I(f, fy ) = degy f − 1. In other words, the curve f has degy f − 1 vertical tangent lines.
Proof. By Abhyankar-Moh’s Epimorphism Theorem ([AM75]), there exits polynomial g(x, y) such
that Jacobian J(f, g) ∈ K∗ and K[x, y] = K[f, g]. By above corollary, I(f, fy ) = degy f − 1.

                                                  6. Applications
  We apply Corollary 5.3 to special cases. We show that these formulas work in some cases but
are not effect in other cases.
6.1. Case (75, 50). We take case (75, 50) as an example. According to [Moh83], page 202, there
are two sub cases [For (75, 50) second sub case, δ1 = 1/3 should be δ1 = 2/3]. We only show the
second one here, as it provides more splitting possibilities, therefore more complicated. i.e. We
have
