## Statement

Over C set

    F(x,y)=(P,Q)=(x+x^2*y, y+x*y).

This polynomial map is quasi-finite, of generic mapping degree2, but

    min lambda1(alpha F beta) = 1+sqrt(2) > 2,

where alpha,beta range over ALL polynomial automorphisms of A2. The same
minimum holds if both automorphisms are required to have determinant1.
Here lambda1(G)=lim_n deg(G^n)^(1/n), with ordinary total degree.
The proof below establishes the limit for the maps used, without a general
dynamical compactification theorem.

The example is NOT Keller: JF=1+x+(2x+x^2)y is nonconstant and vanishes
at(-1,0). Thus this refutes only the proposed implication

    quasi-finite F => min lambda1(alpha F beta)<=generic degree(F).

It neither refutes a Keller-specific lowering theorem nor resolves JC2.

## 1. Every fiber is finite; generic degree is two

Fix a target value(s,t). If x!=-1, then y=t/(1+x), and P=s becomes

    (1+t)x^2+(1-s)x-s=0.                         (1)

This polynomial in x is never identically zero: its constant term would
give s=0, while its linear coefficient would then be1. Thus there are
at most two points of this part of the fiber. On x=-1, the equations
require t=0 and give the unique possible point y=s+1.

For t=0, (1) factors as(x+1)(x-s). Excluding x=-1 leaves at most one
point, so the exceptional x=-1 point does not increase any fiber above
two. Every fiber is therefore finite, including exceptional target values;
the finite-type morphism is quasi-finite.

Generically t is different from0,-1 and the discriminant

    (s+1)^2+4st

is nonzero. Equation(1) then has two distinct roots, neither equal to-1
because its value at-1 is t. Each root gives exactly one y. This proves
dominance and generic mapping degree2. The point(1,-1) has no preimage,
so the map is not finite; quasi-finiteness is not being confused with
properness. Direct differentiation gives the Jacobian stated above.

## 2. Two-sided changes reduce to left changes

For any fixed polynomial automorphism beta, the degrees of H^n and
beta H^n beta^-1 differ by bounded multiplicative factors: composition
gives the bound deg(beta)*deg(beta^-1) in both directions. Consequently
their exponential degree growth rates agree whenever one exists.

For H=alpha F beta,

    beta H beta^-1 = (beta alpha) F.

Hence the two-sided value set equals the left-composition value set
{lambda1(gamma F)}. The same argument works within determinant-one
automorphisms. It does NOT assert invariance under independent changes.

## 3. A corner-monomial calculation

Call x^a y^b a corner of a polynomial if its coefficient is nonzero and
every other monomial has exponent vector coordinatewise <=(a,b), with at
least one strict inequality. Equivalently it is the unique leading
monomial for every strictly positive real weight(s,t).

Suppose both components of G have such corners with strictly positive
exponents, the two rows of a matrix M. Substitution multiplies the corner
matrices. Indeed, lowering either outer exponent lowers the weight after
substitution because both inner components have positive weighted degree.
The unique outer corner and unique inner corners have nonzero multiplied
coefficient; no other term ties them. Inductively G^n has corner matrix
M^n. In particular

    deg(G^n)=max_i sum_j (M^n)_{ij}.              (2)

For the positive matrices below this has exponential rate equal to their
positive eigenvalue of largest modulus. For a rank-one matrix v w^T,
one can simply use M^n=(w^T v)^(n-1)M. No asymptotic cancellation or
unstated algebraic stability is assumed.

## 4. All nonlinear automorphisms have a common pure-variable leader

Let U,V be independent variables, with positive weights p,q satisfying

    q<p<2q.                                     (3)

For EVERY nonaffine plane polynomial automorphism gamma, the two leading
monomials throughout this cone are nonzero scalar multiples of T^a,T^b,
where T is either U or V, a,b are positive integers, and max(a,b)>=2.
The choices T,a,b depend on gamma, not on the point(p,q) of the cone.

Here is a derivation covering all polynomial degrees and translations.
Write gamma as a shortest alternating word of affine maps and nonaffine
upper-triangular maps. Inner affine factors cannot be upper-triangular,
since then neighboring triangular factors combine and shorten the word.
Every other affine factor is B1*tau*B2 with B1,B2 affine upper-triangular
and tau(U,V)=(V,U), by the elementary two-cell Bruhat decomposition of
GL2; translations can be included in a B factor. Absorb these B factors
into their neighboring triangular maps. Their nonlinear degrees stay>=2.
After absorbing an end swap into an affine end factor we obtain

    gamma=L H_k ... H_1 A,                      (4)
    H_i(U,V)=(b_i V+c_i, a_i U+R_i(V)),
    a_i*b_i!=0, deg R_i=m_i>=2, k>=1,

where A,L are affine automorphisms. This uses generation, not a conjugacy
classification of gamma.

The two components of A have weights in{p,q}, and at least one has
weight p. A component containing U has unique leader a nonzero multiple
of U, since p>q; otherwise its leader is a multiple of V. At H_1, the
term R_1(A_2) strictly dominates a_1 A_1: its weight is at least2q>p.
Thus the new two leaders are powers T,T^(m_1) of the SAME variable
T=U or V selected by A_2, with unequal exponents1,m_1.

At each subsequent H_i, the second coordinate has strictly larger
weight before the step. Its m_i-th power dominates the old first
coordinate. The exponent pair changes from(r,s), s>r, to(s,m_i*s).
Both remain powers of the same T. Finally L combines two distinct
weights. In each output either the larger-weight term has nonzero
coefficient, or only the smaller-weight term remains. They cannot cancel
one another; invertibility of L ensures that at least one output retains
the larger term. This proves the assertion, including arbitrary end
translations. All comparisons are strict throughout(3).

## 5. Exact minimum for F

For every positive source weight(s,t), F has unique leaders

    in(P)=x^2*y,  in(Q)=x*y,
    p=2s+t,      q=s+t.

They satisfy(3). Apply section4 to any nonaffine gamma. Substitution has
unique leaders throughout all positive(s,t), so these are actual corners
of gamma F. There are two possible corner matrices:

    T=U: M=[[2a,a],[2b,b]], lambda1=2a+b>=4;
    T=V: M=[[a,a],[b,b]],   lambda1=a+b>=3.

The inequalities use a,b>=1 and max(a,b)>=2. Thus EVERY nonaffine left
composition has dynamical degree at least3.

It remains to check all affine gamma, not just coordinate permutations.
Any affine output containing U has leader P; any output without U has
leader Q. Invertibility prevents both outputs from lacking U. The three
possibilities and their matrices are

    (P,Q): [[2,1],[1,1]], lambda1=(3+sqrt(5))/2;
    (Q,P): [[1,1],[2,1]], lambda1=1+sqrt(2);
    (P,P): [[2,1],[2,1]], lambda1=3.

Nonzero scalar multiples of these leaders do not affect(2), and the
affine constants are lower terms. These cases exhaust all affine maps.
The smaller value is1+sqrt(2), achieved by gamma(U,V)=(V,-U), whose
determinant is1. Together with section2, this proves both stated minima.
