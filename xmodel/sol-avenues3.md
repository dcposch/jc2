There are four distinct objects in play.

1. `directionb_core23_p105337.ms` is the 87-variable unspecialized modular

2. `directionb_row22compat_p*.ms` is 72 variables and 54 equations, but

   the `3*3*2*2=36` radical fibers.
3. `directionb_row22red_p*.ms` is the exact 29-variable pivot-eliminated form
   of that same fixed radical fiber.  The 72v compatibility variety is
   isomorphic to the 29v row22red variety times the recorded `A^21` free
   factor; the 22 eliminated pivots reconstruct uniquely.  Neither object is
   equivalent to the unspecialized 87v object.
4. `directionb_fiber_gb_p105337.out.txt` is the reduced grevlex GB of D21 on
   that representative radical fiber.  The tracked local artifact exists only
   for `p=105337`.

The phrase “fiber GB” is easy to misread.  It is **not** a GB at one fixed D21
base point.  It describes the whole 13-dimensional D21 locus after the finite
radical coordinates have been specialized.  Conversely, it is not a
parametric GB whose 13 directions are coefficient parameters: those directions
are ordinary quotient-ring variables.  It is also not a 397-dimensional
vector-space basis and not a list of 397 points.

The exact banked ring is


in precisely that grevlex variable order.  The 397 polynomials contain
282,304 terms.  `A1,A2` and the ratios `HWi/Wi` have been specialized, while
`W1,W2` remain symbolic and invertible through `uWi*Wi-1`.  The basis pins
quartics for `W1,W2`; therefore it covers all 16 pole-scale root branches over

Consequently, a current `[1]` would be important but fiber-local.  It would not

## 3. The NF quick shot: exact result and best software move

### 3.1 Which rows can be reduced

The six raw rows of the 72v compatibility object do not live in `R0`: they
still contain the 22 pivot coordinates.  Reducing them directly against the
397-element basis is a wrong-ring operation.

The correct targets are equations 24--29 (zero-based) of
`directionb_row22red_p105337.ms`.  They live in

```text
S = R0[x16,x17,x24,x25,x32,x37,x69].
```

The banked GB embeds canonically in `S` and remains a GB because the extended
grevlex order restricts to the banked order on `R0`.  The seven new variables
must remain polynomial variables; treating them as a coefficient field would
silently invert nonunits and change emptiness.

### 3.2 What a constant NF would prove

Let `I21=<G>` and let `c_i` be one correct post-pivot compatibility row.  If

```text
NF_G(c_i) = lambda,       lambda in F_105337^*,
```

then `c_i-lambda` lies in `I21*S`, so

```text
I21*S + <c_i> = S.
```

Thus even one nonzero constant NF would prove that the compatibility
intersection is empty on the **entire representative fixed-radical D21
locus**, uniformly in all seven auxiliary variables and all symbolic `W`
branches.  A zero NF would say that row is redundant.  A nonconstant NF says
nothing alone: several nonconstant remainders may jointly generate 1, or may
have a large common zero set.

Even a constant would not prove the full D23 kill for the scope reasons in
Section 2.  Repeating it at three primes would still be modular evidence, not
an exact characteristic-zero identity.

## 5. FC5 versus the Sigray mass bound

### 5.1 The exact interface

Let `s=alpha+beta`.  At a Sigray pole the repository's entry formulas give

```text
M=b,       kbar=a*s,       rho=a/b,
w=(kbar-rho)/nu.
```

Therefore the pole mass has the exact state expression

```text
             w*M^2
ab/nu = -----------------.                         (5.1)
          s*M - 1
```

Call the right side `Phi_s(w,M)`.  This is the best possible scalar bridge
from the FC5 state to TD-BOUND.  It is substantially better than comparing
`w` informally with `ab/nu`.

### 5.2 Why it does not telescope

`Phi_s` is not monotone across the admitted merge arithmetic.

- Residue A has `s=5`, two incoming `(a,b,nu)=(1,1,2)` poles, hence
  `w=2` and total incoming mass 1.  Its IIa merge has
  `(r,l,nu_merge)=(2,1,3)`, so `M'=2`, `w'=3/2`, and

  ```text
  Phi_5(3/2,2)=2/3;       incoming - outgoing = +1/3.
  ```

- As an arithmetic/printed-tier negative control, the admissible type-`(3,4)`
  three-pole packet with three `(1,1,3)` poles has `s=7`, `w=2` and total
  incoming mass 1.  The IIa data `(r,l,nu_merge)=(3,1,2)` give
  `M'=3`, `w'=8/3`, and

  ```text
  Phi_7(8/3,3)=6/5;       incoming - outgoing = -1/5.
  ```

The second packet is not claimed realized by a Keller map; it is enough to
refute a proof from FC5/MP6 arithmetic alone.  Any valid telescope needs an
extra signed merge defect, branch history, or proximity contribution.  FC5
forgets precisely that history when it emits only `(w',M')`.

My rating is therefore:

- direct scalar FC5 proof of `sum ab/nu<=1`: **3–5%**;
- FC5 as bookkeeping inside a new proximity/intersection invariant:
  **20–25%** chance of a useful lemma;
- full G5 closure from this route alone: **<=2%**.

### 5.3 The larger G5 correction

Even a proof of

```text
sum_F a_F*b_F/nu_F <= 1
```

would imply only

```text
td <= alpha*beta
```

for the selected type.  The cited GGV chain enumeration is finite only after
an input degree bound; it is not an absolute finite catalog of possible
`(alpha,beta)`.  Thus `alpha*beta` can still run without a proved bound.  A
second theorem bounding the selected pair's type/degree is required to close
foundation G5.  `TDBOUND.md` currently overstates the finite-ladder payoff.

What remains genuinely valuable is the exact resolved-pencil identity

```text
td/(alpha*beta)
  = sum_F a_F*b_F/nu_F
  = (1/2) * sum_p (r_p/alpha-s_p/beta)^2,

Delta^2 = -2*td/(alpha*beta).
```

The target `Delta^2>=-2` is a Keller/proximity theorem at divergence centers,
not a consequence of the exceptional lattice alone.  In particular, support
on an ADE tree is insufficient: already on `A1`, `(2E)^2=-8`.  Any ADE route
must prove that `Delta` is an actual root (and integral), not merely supported
on a root lattice.

### 5.4 New G5 experiments

**CONJECTURE P (proximity-source law).**  On a common resolution, put
`u_p=r_p/alpha-s_p/beta` and change from total multiplicities to proximity
increments `q=P^{-1}u`.  The constant-Jacobian condition forces `q` to be a
sum of at most two primitive source packets, one for each infinity direction,
with squared norm at most 2.  Then `Delta^2>=-2` follows.  FC5 should be tested
as the local slope/flux update for these packets, not as the potential itself.

This is conjectural.  Its first falsification test is to compute `q` for
residue A and for the sign-reversing three-pole packet above.  It is sharper
than “the remaining graph is ADE” because it names the missing source/root
assertion.

A cheap theorem-discovery gate is to enumerate the symbolic MP6 merge schemas
and solve for a low-degree augmented potential

```text

```

whose total is monotone.  The `+1/3` and `-1/5` examples are mandatory

### 7.2 Exact Euler-characteristic identity — new global lane

Let `S=A(F)` be the nonproperness curve and `U=A^2\S`.  For a Keller map, the
restriction

```text
F^{-1}(U) -> U
```

is a finite etale cover of degree `td`.  Additivity and multiplicativity of
compactly supported Euler characteristic give the exact identity

```text
1 - chi_c(F^{-1}(S)) = td * (1 - chi_c(S)).          (7.1)
```

**CONJECTURAL USE E.**  Jelonek's rational one-place components, together
with the Sigray dicritical maps and their intersections, make both Euler
characteristics in (7.1) computable or sharply bounded from the pole tree;
the resulting divisibility/sign constraints bound `td` or kill residue A.

The identity is exact; the claimed computability and leverage are
conjectural.  I rate a useful obstruction at **10–15%**.  Its advantage is
that it sees the whole nonproperness curve rather than one fixed generic
fiber passport.  The first pilot should use the already enumerated residue-A
B/x component menus and must include component intersections and exceptional
preimages, not just count components.
