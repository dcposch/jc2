## 3. New mechanism: the order-2j divisibility lever depends only on 2m−n (UNVERIFIED heuristic, not NO_NEW_MECHANISM)

Status: this seat's manual heuristic from the frozen 16d/15z/16f texts. It is a candidate mechanism-level reason that the F9 pipeline could be uniform along its table row; it is NOT a theorem and must be checked by the discriminator in Card 1.

Setting (16d, 15z): A=a(R)+F, B=b(R)+q(R)F+G, ρ=b′−a′q, and the exact identity [A,B]=[R,T]+[F,G] with T=a′G−ρF−(q′/2)F². For monic references of R-degrees m<n (16d has m=3, n=5, R of combined degree 7):

- a′(K)=m·K^(m−1) and the leading term of q′(K) is a nonzero scalar times K^(n−m−1).
- Since ord G ≥ 2j and ord F = j, the order-2j slice of T is m·K^(m−1)·G_2j − (scalar)·K^(n−m−1)·F_j² plus terms carrying explicit s^7-shifted factors, and the order-2j bracket equation forces that slice into ker[K,−]=k[K] (weight-forced, with the exceptional pure-K-power orders as in 16d).
- Hence the lever is K^(m−1) | K^(n−m−1)·F_j², i.e. **K^(2m−n) | F_j²**, whenever 2m−n>0. For m=3, n=5 this is exactly 16d's K | F_j².

Consequence if correct: on every GGHV row with (m,n)=(j+2, 2j+3) — printed rows F2, F9, F22 — one has 2m−n=1 for EVERY j, so the first-contact divisibility K | F_j² is available for the whole row, not only at j=1. On rows with 2m−n ≤ 0 (for example the (j+2, 4j+7) rows F7/F14/F18/F20 and the (j+2, 7j+13) row F13) the lever is vacuous, because K^(m−1) already divides the F_j² term. This is a structural dichotomy across the printed table that the campaign has not recorded.

What it does NOT give: the multiplicity cover. With the general normal form K^(m−1) ∤ F_j, the cofactor C may contain K up to m−3 times, so the line-factor consumers would need multiplicities up to 2m−3 at double lines and m−2 at simple lines, and the consumer inequalities (product of initial degrees times the balance r, against the target order 7(m+n)−5) must be re-derived for each (m,n). The 16f margins at (3,5) were 91/2, 195/4, 45 against 51; whether analogous strict margins exist at (4,7) against 72 is unknown to this seat and is exactly the cheapest decisive question.
