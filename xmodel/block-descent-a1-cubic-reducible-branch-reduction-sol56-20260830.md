## 3. Irreducible `B` is impossible

Assume that `B` is irreducible.  The promoted normalization/forest theorem
makes the normalization map point-bijective; unibranch singularities do not
alter the analytic topology.  Hence `B` is topologically contractible.
Lin--Zaidenberg gives algebraic coordinates in which

```text
B={x^p-y^q=0},       gcd(p,q)=1.                       (3.1)
```

If `min(p,q)=1`, then `B` is smooth, contradicting the already frozen smooth-
branch exclusion.

Suppose `p,q>=2`.  Then (3.1) has exactly one affine singularity, the origin.
Positive real weighted scaling

```text

```

preserves the complement and scales `x^p-y^q` by `r^(pq)`.  Using a weighted
norm, every orbit meets a weighted three-sphere once.  This gives a radial
deformation of the global complement onto the torus-knot link complement;
the same construction in a small ball gives the local complement.  Thus

```text

```

The completed rank-three algebra at the origin has the decomposition (2.1).
Therefore the image under cubic monodromy of the right side of (3.2) lies
in one companion-fixing group `S2`.  By (3.2), so does the whole global
image.  This contradicts the already proved global image `S3` and proves
(0.1).

## 5. The discriminant-double-cover reformulation

Let `q` be a squarefree equation of the reduced curve `B` and put

```text
Z={w^2=q} -> A2.                                             (5.1)
```

Because `B` is reduced and has only finitely many singular points, (5.1) is
a normal hypersurface: it is regular in codimension one and Cohen--Macaulay.

Take the `S3` Galois closure of the connected cubic cover over
`A2 minus B`, and normalize over `A2`.  Quotienting by `A3` gives exactly
the sign double cover (5.1).  At every affine point over `B`, the promoted
local monodromy/decomposition group is contained in, and generically equals,
a transposition subgroup.  Its intersection with `A3` is trivial.  Hence
`A3` acts freely on every geometric point of the normalization over `Z`, and
the quotient map

```text
Xhat -> Z
```

is a connected finite etale cyclic cover of degree three.  This proves the
necessary condition

```text
survivor => H^1_et(Z,Z/3) is nonzero.                         (5.2)
```

The familiar cusp warning is correctly typed here.  For an irreducible
cusp the punctured double plane can have a cyclic-three local cover, but it
ramifies at the double-plane singular point.  The full local companion split
forbids exactly that failure of etaleness.

No converse to (5.2) is asserted.  A cyclic-three cover of `Z` would still
need the deck involution to lift by inversion and would still need all the
pseudo-plane, first-leg, and finite-flat cubic data.
