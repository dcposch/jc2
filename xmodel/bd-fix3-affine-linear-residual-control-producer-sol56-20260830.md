## 1. Source boundary and Miranda algebra

The affine-linear partition was posted, with a proof sketch but no cited

```text

```

The MathOverflow post is treated as a lead, not authority.  Every deduction
used below is reconstructed.  Miranda's primary theorem confirms the
trace-zero rank-two module and multiplication table.

Put `A=C[u,v]`.  A finite-flat integral cubic algebra splits by trace as

```text
B=A direct-sum E,       rank_A(E)=2.
```

Quillen--Suslin makes `E` free.  In a trace-zero basis `(z,w)`, multiplication
has the exact form

```text
z^2 = 2(a^2-bd) + a z + b w,
zw  = -(ad-bc) - d z - a w,
w^2 = 2(d^2-ac) + c z + d w,
```

and the associated binary cubic is

```text
Phi(X,Y)=b X^3-3a X^2Y+3d XY^2-c Y^3.
```

For affine-linear `a,b,c,d`, write

```text
Phi=P0+u P1+v P2
```

with fixed binary cubics `P0,P1,P2` over `C`.

## 2. Exact nonmonogenic translation

The broad block obstruction says that `B/A` cannot be monogenic.  In
Miranda coordinates this has an exact binary-cubic form.

For `t=r z+s w`, the trace-zero coordinates of `t^2` are

```text
(a r^2-2d r s+c s^2,
 b r^2-2a r s+d s^2).
```

Therefore the determinant of `(t,t^2)` relative to `(z,w)` is

```text
b r^3-3a r^2s+3d r s^2-c s^3 = Phi(r,s).
```

Since `1,t,t^2` span the algebra generated by `t`, and adding an element of
`A` to `t` changes neither generated algebra nor trace-zero part, one gets:

> **Lemma 2.1.**  `B` is monogenic over `A` if and only if there exist
> `r,s in A` with `Phi(r,s) in C^*`.

Thus every proper cubic Keller block must satisfy

```text
Phi(r,s) not-in C^*       for all r,s in A.
```

This is stronger than checking constant directions, but the constant cases
already remove one whole row of the affine-linear partition.

## 3. Reconstruction of the affine-linear partition

### 3.1 A common zero of `a,b,c,d`

If the four affine-linear coefficients have a common base point, translate
it to the origin.  Integrality makes it unique and gives

```text
Phi=u P1+v P2,       gcd(P1,P2)=1.
```

The standard incidence resolution can be written directly on the total
space of `O_(P^1)(-3)`: with fibre coordinate `tau`,

```text
(u,v)=tau*(-P2,P1).
```

Away from `tau=0` this is the incidence surface, while the zero section is
contracted to the unique non-Gorenstein point above the origin.  The
degree-three map `[-P2:P1]` gives

```text
Y minus {y0} isomorphic to O_(P^1)(-3)^times,
Cl(Y)=Z/3,       O(Y)^*=C^*.
```

The ramification support is nonempty.  In the localization sequence

```text
O(Y)^* -> O(U)^* -> direct-sum_i Z[R_i] -> Cl(Y),
```

a nonzero free abelian group cannot inject into the finite group `Z/3`.
Hence `O(U)^*/C^*` is nonzero.  Pullback along the dominant first leg would
turn such a unit into a scalar unit of `C[x,y]`, contradicting injectivity.
The common-zero case is therefore excluded.

### 3.2 No common coefficient zero: incidence torsor

Now the Miranda cover is the incidence hypersurface

```text
Y={P0(X,Y)+uP1(X,Y)+vP2(X,Y)=0}
  subset A^2 x P^1.
```

There is no direction `q in P^1` at which all three `Pi` vanish: otherwise
every generic binary cubic has a fixed linear factor, contradicting that
`K/C(u,v)` is a field.

Define

```text
S_X={q in P^1 : P1(q)=P2(q)=0, P0(q)!=0}.
```

If `q=[r:s]` lies in `S_X`, then `Phi(r,s)=P0(r,s) in C^*`; Lemma 2.1
makes the algebra monogenic.  Thus a proper block has

```text
S_X=empty.
```

This also disposes of `W=0`.  Indeed, in characteristic zero
`W=P1 P2'-P2 P1'` vanishes identically only when the nonzero `P1,P2` are
proportional.  Their common zeros then lie in `S_X`; the case
`P1=P2=0` makes the generic cubic constant and nonintegral.  Hence any
candidate has `W!=0`.

With `S_X=empty`, `(P1,P2)` is basepoint-free and defines a degree-three
morphism

```text
rho=[P1:P2]:P^1 -> P^1.
```

Projection `q:Y->P^1` is an affine-line torsor under the kernel of

```text
O^2 --(P1,P2)--> O(3),
```

namely `O(-3)`.  Zariski-local affine-line coordinates show

```text
O(Y)^*=C^*,       Cl(Y)=Pic(Y)=Z,
```

with generator `q^*O(1)`.  The surface is smooth because the two partial
derivatives with respect to `u,v` are `P1,P2`, which never vanish together.

### 3.3 Reducible ramification

If `R` has at least two irreducible components, then the boundary group
`direct-sum_i Z[R_i]` has rank at least two and cannot inject into
`Cl(Y)=Z`.  Localization again produces a nonconstant unit on `U`, excluded
by the source unit group.  Thus only irreducible `R` survives.

### 3.4 The irreducible residual

On `P^1 minus Crit(rho)`, the incidence equation and its derivative in the
`P^1` direction form a nonsingular two-by-two affine-linear system in
`u,v`.  They define a unique ramification point, so the horizontal
ramification is a graph.  At a critical direction the two rows become
dependent.  Compatibility would produce the entire affine-line fibre as a
vertical ramification component, contradicting irreducibility.  Hence the
system is inconsistent there and

```text
R isomorphic to P^1 minus Crit(rho).
```

Since `rho` has degree three, Riemann--Hurwitz gives at least two distinct
critical directions.  Thus `H_c^1(R,Z)` has rank
`#Crit(rho)-1 >= 1`.

The fixed-sheet theorem says generic cubic inertia has type `(2)(1)`, so
the ramification divisor is generically reduced.  With irreducible support
it equals `R` as a divisor.  Adjunction in `A^2 x P^1` gives

```text
omega_Y = q^*O(-2+3)=q^*O(1).
```

Hurwitz therefore makes `[R]` a generator of `Pic(Y)=Z`.  The localization
sequence now computes, rather than merely bounds,

```text
O(U)^*/C^* = 0,       Cl(U)=0.
```

If `r=#Crit(rho)`, constructible multiplicativity and additivity give

```text
chi_c(Y)=chi_c(P^1)*chi_c(A^1)=2,
chi_c(R)=2-r,
chi_c(U)=r >= 2.
```

Thus `U` is not `A^2`, but this does not obstruct a nonproper etale map
from `A^2` into it.

## 4. What `Cl(U)=0` does and does not buy

Let `V=g1(A^2)`, an open dense subset of `U`.

**Lemma 4.1.**  `U minus V` has no divisorial component.

If an irreducible divisor `C` were missed, `Cl(U)=0` would give a rational
function `r` with `div_U(r)=C`.  Normality makes a rational function with no
pole at any height-one point regular on `U`.  Its pullback to `A^2` would be
a nowhere-zero polynomial, hence a scalar.  Dominance makes
`O(U)->C[x,y]` injective, forcing `r` itself to be scalar, a contradiction.
Therefore

```text
V=U minus Sigma
```

for a finite set `Sigma` of closed points.

This still does not make `g1` finite or a covering of `U`.  If it were
finite etale over `V`, Euler characteristic would give

```text
1=chi_c(A^2)=d1*chi_c(V)=d1*(r-#Sigma),
```

impossible for `d1>=2`.  Hence any actual first leg in the residual case is
forced to remain nonfinite even over its almost-surjective image.

Strong Zariski Main embeds `A^2` openly into the finite normalization `Z`
of `Y` in `C(x,y)`.  The missing boundary of `Z` may map onto curves already
covered by other source sheets; `U minus V` having no divisor does not rule
that out.  The elementary control

```text
(G_m minus {1}) x A^1 -> G_m x A^1,
(z,t) |-> (z^2,t)
```

is etale, quasi-finite, surjective, and nonfinite: the removed boundary
`{z=1} x A^1` maps onto the already-covered curve `{1} x A^1` through the
remaining sheet `z=-1`.  Its source is not `A^2` and has nonconstant units,
so it is not a candidate; it only falsifies the Hartogs/covering shortcut.

The exact remaining first-leg obstruction is therefore to exclude such
redundant boundary sheets for a source that is specifically `A^2`, not to
prove again that the target image misses no divisor.

## 5. An explicit faithful residual control

Take

```text
Phi=u X^3+X^2Y+XY^2+vY^3.
```

In Miranda notation this is

```text
b=u,       a=-1/3,       d=1/3,       c=-v,
```

so all four coefficients are affine-linear.  Let

```text
pi:Y={Phi=0} subset A^2_(u,v) x P^1_[X:Y] -> A^2_(u,v).
```

Every fibre is a length-three divisor in `P^1`; `pi` is finite flat of
degree three.  Projection `q:Y->P^1` has affine-line fibres because
`(X^3,Y^3)` never vanishes simultaneously, so `Y` is integral.  It is
smooth since the ambient partial derivatives `partial_u Phi=X^3` and
`partial_v Phi=Y^3` never vanish together.

Here

```text
P0=X^2Y+XY^2,       P1=X^3,       P2=Y^3,
rho=[X^3:Y^3],      Crit(rho)={0,infinity}.
```

On the chart `Y!=0`, put `lambda=X/Y`.  Then

```text
v=-u lambda^3-lambda^2-lambda,
Y_chart=Spec C[u,lambda].
```

The ramification equation is

```text
q0=3u lambda^2+2lambda+1=0.
```

It has no point over `lambda=0`, and on the other projective chart the
corresponding equation is `q_infinity=1+2mu+3v mu^2`, which has no point at
`mu=0`.  Solving gives

```text
u=-(2lambda+1)/(3lambda^2),
v=-lambda*(lambda+2)/3,
```

so `R isomorphic to G_m` and is irreducible and reduced.

The two charts of `Y` are affine planes.  Their intersection has unit group
`C^* lambda^Z`; the chart unit groups are only `C^*`.  The two-chart Picard
sequence therefore gives

```text
O(Y)^*=C^*,       Pic(Y)=Cl(Y)=Z.
```

On the overlap one computes

```text
q_infinity=-lambda^(-1)*q0.
```

Thus `[R]` is a generator (up to sign).  Localization yields

```text
O(Y minus R)^*/C^*=0,       Cl(Y minus R)=0.
```

Since `R=G_m`,

```text
H_c^1(R,Z)=Z,
chi_c(Y minus R)=2-0=2 != chi_c(A^2)=1.
```

The generic branch polynomial is

```text
u T^3+T^2+T+v.
```

At the ramified root `T=lambda`, its third root is

```text
mu=-lambda*(lambda+2)/(2lambda+1).
```

Generically `mu!=lambda`; local inertia is a transposition fixing this
third sheet.  At the isolated parameter `lambda=-1` the three roots meet,
producing the familiar cusp phenomenon but not changing generic divisorial
inertia.  Hence the fixed-sheet theorem is met exactly.

Because `[R]` is nonzero, the surface is nonfactorial and the cubic algebra
is nonmonogenic.  It also passes the two stronger filters derived in the
independent `BD-GAL` review:

```text
S1: Z[R] -> Cl(Y)=Z sends 1 to a generator and is injective;
S2: the tame different divisor is R, hence the different is nonprincipal
    and omega_(Y/A^2) is not a free O(Y)-module.
```

Equivalently, in the Delone--Faddeev dual-functional formulation, the
associated ternary norm cubic represents no unit.  Thus this example is a
positive cover-side answer to every finite-form filter currently proposed
for `BD-FIX3`: normal domain, simple transposition discriminant, infinite
class group, injective ramification-component lattice, nonprincipal
different, and nonmonogenicity all coexist.

It is emphatically **not a sandwich candidate**.  What is absent is precisely
a dominant everywhere-etale quasi-finite map `A^2->Y` missing `R`; supplying
one would be Keller-map work, not a cover-side construction.

## 6. General cubic constraints gained

The desk analysis gives the following exact restrictions for any proper
cubic block, without the affine-linear hypothesis:

1. the cubic field `K/C(f,g)` is non-Galois, so its Galois closure has group
   `S3` in the natural three-sheet action;
2. every divisorial inertia group acts by a transposition and fixes the
   third sheet;
3. over every branch component `D` the geometric divisorial pullback has
   the form

   ```text
   g2^*D=2R_D+T_D
   ```

   generically, with `R_D` ramified and `T_D` etale over `D`;
4. the discriminant has valuation one at every branch component, hence its
   divisorial support polynomial is squarefree up to a scalar;
5. the free group on ramification components injects into `Cl(Y)`, so all
   those classes have infinite order; the different is nonprincipal and
   `omega_(B/A)` is nonfree;
6. the normalization is nonfactorial and nonmonogenic; in Miranda data the
   latter is exactly `Phi(r,s) not-in C^*` for every `r,s in C[u,v]`; and
7. the quadratic discriminant field in the `S3` Galois closure cannot lie
   inside `C(x,y)`, since that would be a forbidden proper Galois
   intermediate field.

These are genuine restrictions, but the explicit control shows that items
1--6 are mutually consistent on the cover side.  Item 7 concerns the
unavailable source-field embedding and supplies no contradiction by itself.
