## 3. Attack on the determinant / double-branched-cover observation

### 3.1 The valid core, reconstructed

Assume:

- `B` is a reduced irreducible affine plane curve with `Norm(B)=A1` (one place at infinity), so `K_infty` is a knot;
- `rho: pi1(A2-B) -> S_d` sends every generic meridian to a transposition and is transitive;
- the charged infinity-to-affine quotient `G_infty ->> G_aff` (Zariski--van Kampen versus the closed-braid product relation) holds, so `rho` pulls back to the knot group.

Transpositions generate a connected graph on `{1,...,d}`. A transitive subgroup of `S_d` generated by transpositions is `S_d`. Thus `G_infty -> S_d` is surjective.

Sign `S_d -> C2` sends every transposition to the nontrivial element. Abelianization of a knot group is `Z`, generated by a meridian, so this is the unique mod-two abelianization. The corresponding double cover of `S3` branched along `K_infty` therefore satisfies: `m^2` is already trivial in `S_d`, and the restriction of `rho` to `ker(sign)` descends to a surjection

```text
pi1(Sigma_2(K_infty)) -> A_d.                            (3.1)
```

For `d=4`, `A4^{ab} = C3`. Abelianizing (3.1) yields a surjection `H1(Sigma_2) -> C3`. For a knot, `Sigma_2` is a rational homology sphere and `|H1(Sigma_2)| = |det(K)| = |Delta_K(-1)|`. Therefore

```text
d=4  =>  3 divides Delta_K(-1).                          (3.2)
```

Equivalently: a meridian-transposition `S4` representation induces a Fox 3-coloring. This is the same 3-torsion that the double-plane Kummer sequence sees at infinity, not a new torsion class.

For `d=3`, (3.1) is a surjection onto `A3=C3`, recovering ordinary 3-colorability of the trefoil. That case is already closed by degree three.

### 3.2 Genus two and the `delta >= 3` floor

`A_d` for `d>=5` is simple and nonabelian, so `A_d^{ab}=1`. Abelianization of (3.1) is then the zero map. **There is no all-degree determinant congruence.** Any all-degree continuation must use the nonabelian homomorphism (3.1) itself, or meridional rank, not `det`. That is a real overread in the overlay if it is taken as a uniform `3 | det(K)` in every degree.

The second clause is narrower, and after correcting the torus-knot genus it is valid in its proper cell. Schubert plus the charged nearby-fibre computation give, for a rational one-place curve,

```text
g_3(K_infty) = delta_affine.
```

The cabling formula in `c61f0ceb...` is `g(C_{p,q}(J)) = p g(J) + (p-1)(|q|-1)/2`. Cables of the unknot are torus knots, with

```text
g(T(p,q)) = (p-1)(|q|-1)/2.
```

Thus `g(T(2,5))=2` and `g(T(3,4))=3`, not 2. Cabling a trefoil cannot produce genus two: `p=2` forces `|q|=1`, and `p>=3` already yields genus at least three. Allowing graph-knot connected sums would add only `T(2,3)#T(2,3)`, which is composite. A one-place algebraic knot at infinity is an iterated torus knot coming from a single Puiseux chain, hence prime, hence not a connected sum.

The only genus-two one-place iterated torus knot is therefore `T(2,+/-5)`. It is 2-bridge, `Delta(-1)=5`, and `Sigma_2` is a lens space with cyclic fundamental group. Both (3.2) and (3.1) fail, and meridional rank two already forbids a transitive transposition image in `S_d` for `d>=4`. **Genus two is empty in the irreducible one-place simple-cover cell.** Combined with the one-node theorem at genus one, that cell requires

```text
delta_affine >= 3.                                        (3.3)
```

This is a floor, not attainment. Equality at three still has to name a knot. The first torus knot of genus three that is not 2-bridge is `T(3,4)` (`det=3`, bridge number 3). `T(2,7)` has genus three but is 2-bridge and dies with `T(2,5)`. Idea A tests whether `T(3,4)` can actually carry the `S4` representation; it does not reopen genus two.

Two-bridge knots are independently excluded for every `d>=4` by meridional rank two and, equivalently, by (3.1): a cyclic group cannot surject onto `A_d` for `d>=4`. That is the same obstruction written twice, not two obstructions. The increment of (3.2) over rank-two is the both-odd torus knots of larger bridge number, such as `T(3,5)` (`g=4`, `det=1`).

### 3.3 Connection to Puiseux pairs, Alexander polynomials, Fox colorings, and affine `Pic[3]`

- **Puiseux pairs.** A one-place iterated torus knot is a cable tower whose pairs are the characteristic Puiseux pairs of `B` at infinity. `T(2,2g+1)` is a single pair `(2,2g+1)`. `T(3,4)` is the pair `(3,4)`. Affine singularities change `delta` and therefore the Seifert genus of the nearby fibre of a good polynomial; Neumann's theorem identifies that genus with `g_3(K_infty)`. The knot type is still the infinity type, but goodness identifies it with the nearby fibre's boundary, so `g_3` is constrained by affine `delta`. A node (`delta=1`) forces genus one, hence a trefoil, which is the charged one-node theorem. Extra affine `delta` raises genus and therefore forces a higher-genus iterated torus type. Do not assign a `(3,4)` infinity pair to a `delta=1` curve: that would be a genus mismatch, not a pole/interior confusion.

- **Alexander polynomials.** For torus knots, `Delta_{T(p,q)}(t)=(t^{pq}-1)(t-1)/((t^p-1)(t^q-1))` and

  ```text
  det(T(p,q)) = p  if q even;
                q  if p even;
                1  if both odd.
  ```

  Consequently every `T(odd,odd)` has `det=1` and is excluded for `d=4` by (3.2), even when bridge number is large enough that meridional rank does not forbid `S4`. That is the one genuine increment of (3.2) over two-generator rank. Examples: `T(3,5)` has genus 4, bridge number 3, and `det=1`. It is a candidate that meridional rank would leave open and (3.2) kills.

- **Fox colorings.** (3.2) is exactly 3-colorability. It is necessary for a meridian-transposition `S4` representation and far from sufficient. The named nodal cubic is 3-colorable and still has `H1_et(D,Z/3)=0`. Fox coloring is the boundary shadow, not the affine torsor.

- **Affine `Pic[3]`.** The sign double plane `D: s^2=q_B` is the affine counterpart of `Sigma_2`. After identifying `Z/3` with `mu_3`,

  ```text
  0 -> O(D)*/O(D)*^3 -> H^1_et(D,Z/3) -> Pic(D)[3] -> 0.
  ```

  A restriction (or residue) map from this group to `Hom(H1(Sigma_2), C3)` sends the forced etale class, in the zero-cusp row, to a 3-coloring of `K_infty`. Vanishing of the target does kill the torsor; nonvanishing does not create one. If the uncharged constant-unit lemma is later promoted, the kernel of restriction is precisely anti-invariant `Pic(D)[3]`, which is the remaining global gate. Localization, not coloring, is the content.

### Idea A. The `T(3,4)` homomorphism discriminator

**Required hypotheses.** Charged one-place Chau parametrization; goodness and `G_infty ->> G_aff` (Opus-provisional); simple connected monodromy; irreducibility of `B`. Do not charge a proper block: the statement is cover-theoretic.

**Required hypotheses.** Provisional function-pair package `2531a89d...`; inverse-Kummer identities `bcc5148...`; Formanek's field-of-fractions theorem; a compactification in which the index divisor meets the line at infinity in a measurable packet. ### Idea D. Class-group localization of the double plane, nodal-control style

**Type.** Active zero-cusp gate. Subsumes Fox coloring.

**Hoped-for implication.** For every reduced one-place polynomial curve `B` with the charged `(2,2)` and no `(3,1)`, the normal double plane `D: s^2=q_B` has torsion-free class group (or at least `Pic(D)[3]^-=0` after involution), hence no connected etale `C3` torsor.

**Required hypotheses.** Promoted etale-torsor gate `f5dbad98...`; factoriality of `C[x,y]`; connectedness of the branch; the one-place hypothesis so that a Nagata chart analogous to `div(u)=2P0+P1` exists. The constant-unit lemma is **not** required: a torsion-free `Cl` kills units cubed and Picard 3-torsion together, as in the nodal control.

**First falsification test.** Take the next one-place curve after the nodal cubic: an immersive polynomial parametrization with one ordinary node and one `A2` (affine `delta=2`, infinity type `T(2,5)`). This curve cannot be an `S4` branch, so it is a method control, not a possible horn member: `det=5` makes the boundary map to `Hom(H1(Sigma_2),C3)` zero, and a surviving anti-invariant 3-torsion class would be purely affine. If `Cl` remains torsion-free, move the live computation to a `delta=3` embedding (the actual remaining cell), not to a larger random family.

**Primary theorem interface.** Kummer sequence (4.1) and nodal computation (5.1)--(5.3) of `f5dbad98...`; Chau polynomial parametrization.

**Score.** 8. Directly the remaining zero-cusp content; the first test is one exact surface, not a family.

**Type.** New connection of avenues 6, 7, 26, 27. Not a listed active quartic gate.

**Hoped-for implication.** A cyclic one-place component of `A(F)` cannot be a coordinate (it has a cycle, so it is not a line). ### Idea F. Quartic Formanek index as a rank-four `DISC8-INDEX`  *(cross-avenue)*

**Type.** New connection of the promoted cubic discriminant/index theorem with the cycle-one monogenic order. Not a restatement of Idea B: B is the Orevkov packet, F is the intrinsic degree/length dictionary.

**Hoped-for implication.** For the free rank-four order `O(V)[t]`, the Fitting/length index `I_t` is basis-independent in the primitive element `t` up to the Formanek choice of coordinate, and its degree on a compactification of `V` is bounded in terms of `deg C0` and `mu` the same way `deg(det M)<=4` was bounded for quadratic cubics. ### Idea G. Seifert/`pi1(Sigma_2) -> A_d` for `d>=5`, after dropping `det`  *(cross-avenue)*

**Hoped-for implication.** For a one-place iterated torus knot, `Sigma_2` is a Seifert fibered manifold (Brieskorn `Sigma(2,p,q)` when `K=T(p,q)`). **Required hypotheses.** (3.1), hence the same provisional topology interfaces; simple connected monodromy; one-place. Do not abelianize. Do not claim a result for reducible links.

**First falsification test.** `T(3,4)` double cover is the Brieskorn sphere `Sigma(2,3,4)` with binary octahedral fundamental group of order 48. A genus-three trefoil-cable remains `OPEN` for `d>=5`.

**Primary theorem interface.** Double branched covers of torus knots are Brieskorn spheres; binary octahedral / icosahedral orders; (3.1).

**Score.** 8. Cross-degree, cheap, and strictly stronger than (3.2) as soon as `d>=5`. It does not reopen genus two; it constrains the first equality case of a floor that is already forced.
