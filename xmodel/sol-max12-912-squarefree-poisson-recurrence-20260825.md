## Setup and homogeneous centralizer lemma

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and write

```text
[A,B] = A_x B_y - A_y B_x.
```

Let `K` be a squarefree binary cubic and normalize

```text
P9 = K^3,   Q12 = K^4.
```

The only homogeneous polynomial of degree `d` commuting with `K` is zero
unless `3 | d`; when `3 | d`, it is a scalar multiple of `K^(d/3)`.
Indeed, choose a projective chart avoiding the three roots of `K` and write

```text
K = y^3 k(z),   W = y^d w(z),   z=x/y.
```

Then `[K,W]=0` is

```text
d k'(z) w(z) - 3 k(z) w'(z) = 0,
```

so `w^3=c k^d`.  Squarefreeness forces `3 | d` and the stated form.  This
elementary homogeneous lemma is the only centralizer input used below.

## Formal truncation lemma

The row calculations below are instances of one stronger graded statement.
This formulation was written after the degree-12 preregistration and remains
provisional pending hostile review.

Localize at `K` and complete for decreasing homogeneous degree.  There is a
unique formal cube root

```text
R=P^(1/3)=K + terms of degree at most 2.
```

The same dehomogenization proof extends the centralizer lemma to homogeneous
elements of the fraction field: a degree-`d` rational function commuting
with `K` is zero unless `3|d`, and is then a scalar multiple of `K^(d/3)`.
Now put `T=Q-R^4`.  Since `P=R^3` and `[P,Q]=c` is constant,

```text
[R,T] = c/(3 R^2),                                    (FT1)
```

whose leading homogeneous degree is `-6`.  If the current leading term of
`T` has degree `d>-7`, the degree-`d+1` part of (FT1) is `[K,T_d]=0`.
The rational centralizer lemma makes it zero unless `3|d`; in the resonant
case it is `a K^(d/3)`, and subtracting `a R^(d/3)` cancels it without
changing (FT1).  Descending through all `d>=0` gives constants
`lambda,mu,nu,delta` such that

```text
Q = [ R^4 +(lambda/3) R^3 + mu R^2 + nu R + delta ]_>=0.   (FT2)
```

Here `[ ]_>=0` means the sum of the nonnegative homogeneous pieces.  The
normalization `lambda/3` matches (5).  The possible resonances occur only at
degrees `9,6,3,0`; they explain the target-shear `lambda`, the `mu` in
(13), and the two later constants.  After removing also the commuting
negative-degree resonances, the first noncommuting remainder can start at
degree `-7`; bracketing it with the degree-nine leading part of `P` is
exactly where a degree-zero constant Jacobian can enter.

Consequently every remaining positive-band equation can be generated by
expanding the four fractional powers in (FT2) and demanding polynomiality.
Original-bracket replays remain valuable independent controls, but a reviewed
proof of (FT2) would replace separate Groebner derivations for all upper
bands at once.  It does not by itself prove that all polynomiality conditions
are inconsistent, that `P` is a cube, or that the final constant row cannot
be one.
