## A changed combined question, not a new genus family

ROOT proposed during cross-examination:

> Let C be a smooth closed irreducible curve in A2 over an algebraically
> closed characteristic-zero field k. Suppose x dy=dH in Omega^1(C), with
> H in O(C), and O(C)^*=k*. Must the smooth projective completion have
> genus zero?

The September 14 [ROOT](exact-plane-curve-genus-swarmHQ-root-20260914.md)
and [Astra](exact-plane-curve-genus-swarmHQ-astra-20260914.md) reports
refute exactness alone, even on every smooth generic fiber of a polynomial
pencil. They do NOT refute this combined question:

- ROOT's curve has x²(1+t^n)=c!=0, so x is a nonconstant unit on the
  literal closed curve.
- Astra's elliptic plane curve has q=x³−x and D=3x²−1 as nonconstant
  units on its literal closed coordinate ring.

No points are silently deleted in either report's actual ring check.
The changed premise is constant units, not a repetition with a different
genus or degree. Neither cross reviewer nor ROOT proved the implication or
found a qualifying counterexample. Bounded history searches found no
existing resolution in their searched scope; no novelty claim follows.

For the smooth completion Cbar and boundary B, the unit condition says

    ker(Div_B^0(Cbar) -> Pic^0(Cbar)) = 0.

Exactness gives an additive de Rham/period constraint. A proof would have
to force a nonzero principal boundary divisor from that constraint and
positive genus, using the plane embedding. No such implication was found.

An equivalent algebraic formulation does not close this gap. If C=V(h)
and H is lifted polynomially, set

    V=(x-H_y)∂x + H_x∂y.

Then div(V)=1 and V(h) is divisible by h. Conversely, a polynomial
divergence-one field preserving (h) gives exactness on a smooth C:
i_V(dx wedge dy) restricts to zero, and
x dy-i_V(dx wedge dy) is a closed polynomial 1-form, hence dH.
The divergence-one construction was already present in the September 14 ROOT report's
construction motivation. It supplies neither completeness of V nor a
constraint forcing a boundary unit. It is KNOWN, not a new proof mechanism.

## One bounded negative control

Sol supplied, and ROOT manually checked, the following standard-embedding
test. For a smooth affine elliptic curve y²=f(x), with f a monic squarefree
cubic, the only removed projective point is infinity, so all units are
constant. Write a putative regular primitive as H=A(x)+B(x)y. The equation
x dy=dH implies

    A'=0,    2f B'+f'B=x f'.

Degree comparison forces B=alpha*x+beta. For f=x³+a*x²+b*x+c, coefficient
comparison yields

    alpha=3/5, beta=-2a/15, b=a²/3, c=a³/27.

Thus f=(x+a/3)³, contradicting squarefreeness. This one Weierstrass
embedding class therefore cannot be the desired counterexample. This is
MANUAL/PRODUCER-CHECKED/UNPROMOTED, not a theorem about arbitrary unit-free
plane embeddings and not a reason to commission a genus/degree ladder.

## Actual-source composition and separate gaps

For a polynomial Keller pair J(f,g)=1, polynomial Poincare integration
gives dS=x dy-f dg. On f=c the stronger ambient identity is

    d(S+c g)-x dy=-(f-c)dg.

The pullback is exact, but curve exactness alone forgets this compatible
first-order condition and the nonsingular whole pencil.

If the combined curve implication were valid over the algebraically
closed geometric-generic field, a positive-genus Keller fiber would have
a nonconstant GEOMETRIC unit. Its finite definition field would then
allow the [reviewed globalization bridge](geometric-unit-globalization-review-swarmHQ-sol-20260916T233000Z.md)
to apply ONLY after verifying its extra hypothesis that EVERY closed
fiber of the chosen f is irreducible. That all-fiber hypothesis is not
asserted for arbitrary Keller components here. Even with it, global
non-base units remain unexcluded. Units over k(f) before extension are
not a substitute for units over its algebraic closure.

Thus the combined question is a conditional necessary-condition bridge,
not JC2 closure. A counterexample to the weaker curve question would
likewise not supply the actual ambient Keller identity.
