## 2. Exact ancestry lemma

For a nonempty finite set `S`, put

```text
A(S)=ker(epsilon:Q[S]->Q)
```

and give `Q[S]` its standard permutation pairing.  Let
`p:S->B` be a surjection of nonempty finite sets.  Pushforward of basis
vectors gives

```text
p_*:Q[S]->Q[B],       e_s |-> e_(p(s)).
```

It preserves total augmentation and restricts to a surjection
`p_*:A(S)->A(B)`.  Let `n_b=|p^{-1}(b)|`, `w=|S|`, and for `y in A(B)` define

```text
T_p(y)=p^*(y) - (sum_b n_b*y_b/w)*1_S.                (2.1)
```

This is the orthogonal projection of `p^*y` to `A(S)`.  For
`x in A(S)` and `y in A(B)`,

```text
<p_*x,y> = <x,T_p(y)>.
```

Thus `T_p` is the adjoint of the restricted pushforward.  It is injective:
if `T_p(y)=0`, then `p^*y` is constant on `S`; surjectivity of `p` makes `y`
constant on `B`, and `y in A(B)` forces `y=0`.

Moreover

```text
im(T_p) = ker(p_*|A(S))^perp,
A(S) = im(T_p) direct-sum ker(p_*|A(S)),              (2.2)
dim ker(p_*|A(S)) = |S|-|B|.                          (2.3)
```

The equality in (2.2) follows from adjointness, injectivity, and dimensions.
Consequently the two-term complex

```text
A(B) --T_p--> A(S)                                    (2.4)
```

has zero kernel and cokernel canonically identified, using the pairing, with
`ker p_*`; its only cohomology has dimension `|S|-|B|`.

The correction term in (2.1) is essential when ancestry-fibre sizes vary.
For fibre sizes `(2,1)` and `y=(1,-1)`, raw pullback is `(1,1,-1)` and is not
augmented.  Formula (2.1) gives `(2/3,2/3,-4/3)`; the orthogonal excess line
is generated by `(1,-1,0)`.  This is an exact nonuniform negative control.

## 3. Naturality and the local excess

The construction uses no ordering or choice of a point in an ancestry fibre.
For every commuting square of finite-set bijections, permutation pullback
intertwines `p_*`, the standard pairings, and `T_p`.  Hence (2.4) is
equivariant for every group acting compatibly on `S`, `B`, and `p`.  This is
the precise algebra needed for root-braid and factor-swap descent once the
geometric ancestry map is known to carry those actions.

At a Section-7 point, substitute

```text
S=S_special,       B=S_generic,
|S|=w_i(z),        |B|=b_i.
```

Then (2.4) has generic rank `b_i-1`, special rank `w_i(z)-1`, and degree-zero
excess

```text
dim coker(T_p)=w_i(z)-b_i.                             (3.1)
```

In the exact isotrivial test, where the special and generic sheet sets are
canonically identified, `p` is a bijection, `T_p` is an isomorphism, and the
cone vanishes.  Thus the corrected constant-Kummer zero test is automatic at
the finite-set type, without counting persistent affine ramification or the
physical pole as excess.

## 4. Exact geometric stop condition

The remaining stalk theorem is no longer “find an injection of the right
dimension.”  It is:

> **ANCESTRY-STALK.** Pull the scheme-theoretically diagonal-marked conductor
> square to a strict-henselian disc at an actual Section-7 exceptional point.
> Prove that its horizontal generic branches and special nearby sheets define
> a canonical surjection `p:S_special->S_generic` with cardinalities
> `w_i(z)` and `b_i`, respectively.  Prove equivariance for inertia/root braid
> and factor swap, and prove that admissible boundary blowups preserve `p` up
> to unique equivariant isomorphism.

If `ANCESTRY-STALK` holds, Sections 2--3 construct the desired local complex,
prove concentration and length (3.1), and supply descent and blowup invariance
formally.  If the conductor yields only an unmarked relation, a correspondence
with missing ancestors, or a path-dependent subset, then the construction
does not apply.  The inequality `w_i(z)>=b_i` alone cannot repair that failure.

Ordinary specialization of geometric points does not supply this map for
free: its evident direction is generic-to-special and it can merge points.
The required special-to-generic ancestry is therefore additional marked
geometry, not a reversal of the usual specialization relation.  Nor can the
cardinalities be interpreted as support cardinalities of one finite flat
scheme without proof.  These direction/type checks are part of
`ANCESTRY-STALK` itself.

The most efficient next test is therefore one literal Section-7 local
normalization: enumerate horizontal versus vertical conductor branches and
ask whether every special nearby sheet has a unique generic-direction
ancestor.  A single ambiguous or ancestorless sheet refutes this version of
the surface card; a canonical surjection completes its entire local linear
layer.
