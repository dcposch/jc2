## 1. Literal rings, map, and potential

Work over C. Let X=Spec R with R=C[x,x^-1,y], and Y=Spec A with
A=C[p,p^-1,q]. Define

    p=x^3,
    q=y/(3x^2)+(4/9)x+2/(3x),
    S=(2/3)xy-x^4/9+x^2/3.

The Jacobian is p_x*q_y=3x^2/(3x^2)=1, since p_y=0. Directly,

    p dq = (x/3)dy + [-2y/3+(4/9)x^3-(2/3)x]dx,
    x dy-p dq = (2x/3)dy+[2y/3-(4/9)x^3+(2/3)x]dx = dS.

In particular S itself extends polynomially over the entire source A2;
q does NOT. Its numerator after multiplication by3x^2 is
y+(4/3)x^3+2x, not divisible by x (restrict x=0 to get y).
Clearing this denominator changes the Jacobian condition, not the scope.

The literal A-algebra map is
