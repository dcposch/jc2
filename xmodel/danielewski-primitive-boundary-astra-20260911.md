## 1. Verdict and elementary geometry

The proposed degree-two map, its exact omitted line, and the divisor-class distinction all check. No exclusion of primitive-boundary omission is obtained here: that universal exclusion is exactly equivalent to the still-sought nonexistence of an etale map T -> A2. A useful torsion-class obstruction applies to S, but not to T.

Throughout work over C. Put

    T: t^2-1=x^2 Z,
    D_+={x=0,t=1}, D_-={x=0,t=-1}, L_-={Z=0,t=-1}.

The defining polynomial is primitive and linear in Z over C[x,t], with coprime coefficients, hence irreducible. Its gradient cannot vanish on T: x=0 would force t=+/-1. Thus T is a smooth integral surface.

On T minus D_-, the function y=(t-1)/x^2 extends as Z/(t+1) where needed. It gives the exact isomorphism

    A2 -> T minus D_-, (x,y) -> (x,1+x^2 y,y(2+x^2 y)).

Similarly T minus D_+ has coordinates (x,v), v=(t+1)/x^2, with t=-1+x^2v and Z=v(-2+x^2v). These two opens cover T. The form omega=dx wedge dt/x^2 is respectively dx wedge dy and dx wedge dv, so extends everywhere and is nowhere zero.

## 2. Quotient, open immersion and exact degree-two image

Set S: U^2=A+A^2 Z. The involution sigma(x,t,Z)=(-x,-t,Z) has no fixed point: x=t=0 is incompatible with the equation. Its invariant ring is generated by A=x^2, U=xt and Z, because an invariant monomial has even total x,t exponent and t^2=1+x^2Z. The relation is precisely U^2=A+A^2Z. Thus q:T -> S has the asserted formula.

Here is an all-locus etaleness check, without deleting x=0 or t=0. The opens A!=0 and 1+AZ!=0 cover S. Over the first, q is the quadratic algebra x^2=A, with t=U/x and derivative 2x a unit. Over the second it is t^2=1+AZ, with x=U/t and derivative 2t a unit. The relations recover x^2=A in the second chart. These monic quadratic descriptions prove finite etale, locally free rank two, and surjective. They also identify its geometric degree as two. The involution is free; no global free module basis is asserted.

The map j:S -> T is

    (A,U,Z) -> (X,tau,Z)=(2U,1+2AZ,Z).

It lands on T since tau^2-1=4AZ(1+AZ)=4U^2Z. Its image lies in the open O={Z!=0} union {tau+1!=0}: if Z=0, then tau=1. Conversely the formulas

    U=X/2,
    A=(tau-1)/(2Z) on Z!=0,
    A=X^2/(2(tau+1)) on tau+1!=0

agree on the overlap by X^2 Z=tau^2-1. They satisfy A+A^2Z=X^2/4 and recover both maps. The excluded complement of O is exactly L_-. Hence j is an isomorphism S -> T minus L_-, in particular an open immersion, and S is smooth.

Consequently

    E2=j o q: (x,t,Z) -> (2xt,2t^2-1,Z)

is etale EVERYWHERE, has geometric degree two, and has image EXACTLY T minus L_-. It is nonproper because a proper map has closed image whereas this image is a proper dense open. On the dense open xt!=0 a direct differential calculation gives E2^*omega=2omega; since both forms are global, that identity holds on all T.

The exceptional-looking loci are not discarded. Source x=0 maps to D_+, not D_-. Source t=0 has x^2 Z=-1 and maps onto D_- minus its intersection with L_-. Source Z=0 has t=+/-1 and maps to {Z=0,tau=1}. In particular E2 does NOT omit D_-.

## 3. Divisor classes and units, without homology assumptions

Localizing at x gives C[x,x^{-1},t], a factorial ring with units c x^n. Thus Cl(T) is generated by D_+,D_-: any other prime divisor is principal on this localization and differs from a principal divisor on T only along those two components. A relation supported on D_+,D_- must come from a unit c x^n of the localization. Since div(x)=D_++D_-, these are exactly the multiples of that one relation. Therefore

    Cl(T)=Z[D_-], [D_+]=-[D_-].

The same unit calculation shows O(T)^*=C^*: a global unit c x^n must have zero valuation on both D_+ and D_-, so n=0.

The equation (t+1)(t-1)=x^2Z gives

    div(t+1)=2D_-+L_-.

At the generic point of D_-, t-1 and Z are units and x has order one, so the order is two. At the generic point of L_-, x and t-1 are units and Z has order one. These are all the components; their intersection causes no additional prime divisor. Thus [L_-]=-2[D_-].

Deleting a prime divisor quotients the class group by its class: divisors on the open extend to T, and its principal-divisor relations differ only by multiples of the deleted component. Hence

    Cl(T minus L_-)=Z/2,
    Cl(T minus D_-)=0.

The first open is S by j. Moreover a unit on T minus L_- has divisor n L_- on T. Since [L_-] has infinite order, n=0, and normality then makes it a unit on T, hence constant. Therefore Cl(S)=Z/2 and O(S)^*=C^*, proved algebraically. The primitive chart line D_- and the doubled-class omitted line L_- cannot be interchanged by an automorphism of T, whose action on Cl(T)=Z is multiplication by +/-1.

## 4. Two exact pole controls and noninjective pullback

The rational function

    r=(t-1)/(2Z)=x^2/(2(t+1))

is regular on T minus L_- and has a genuine order-one pole at the generic point of L_-. Nevertheless E2^*r=x^2 is globally regular. Thus etaleness alone does not prevent a rational pole from disappearing under pullback when its divisor is omitted.

By contrast the affine-plane chart coordinate y=(t-1)/x^2 gives

    E2^*y=Z/(2t^2).

The curve C={t=0} has x^2Z=-1, so x and Z are units there; t vanishes to order one. Hence this pullback has a genuine order-two pole along C. The pair (2xt,Z/(2t^2)) is NOT a regular map T -> A2. Omitting L_- does not make the D_--chart coordinate regular.

There is also a direct control against injectivity of etale class-group pullback. Scheme-theoretically E2^{-1}(D_-)=C with multiplicity one: on C, x!=0 and the pulled-back local equation X=2xt has order one. But div(t)=C. Therefore

    E2^*[D_-]=[C]=0,

so E2^*:Cl(T) -> Cl(T) is the zero map although E2 is dominant etale of degree two and its preimage of D_- is nonempty. No finite-map norm argument can silently be applied to this nonproper endomorphism.

## 5. Exact unrestricted implication; what the distinction does not prove

Let i:A2 -> T minus D_- -> T be the chart immersion of section 1. Then the following EXISTENCE statements are exactly equivalent, with no degree or symmetry restriction:

    an etale map h:T -> A2;
    an etale endomorphism f:T -> T whose image avoids D_-;
    H,G in O(T) with dH wedge dG=c omega, c in C^*.

Forward, h gives f=i o h. Reverse, an f avoiding D_- factors through that open and its inverse chart gives h; etaleness is preserved by these isomorphisms/open immersions. Finally a map (H,G) to A2 is etale exactly when its Jacobian relative to the global nowhere-zero omega is a unit. Section 3 proves every such unit constant. This establishes the scalar-pair equivalence, not existence or nonexistence of any such pair.

Consequently proving that NO etale endomorphism omits D_- would resolve this same sought scalar-pair question. The example, primitive/doubled class distinction and noninjective pullback do not supply that missing theorem in this attempt; no logical-independence claim is intended. They precisely prevent misusing E2 as either a scalar-pair construction or evidence that class-group pullback is injective. An arbitrary etale map is open and dominant here, but its image need not be closed or have only finitely many missing points: E2 omits an entire line.

## 6. A valid torsion-class lemma, applied only where its hypotheses hold

Let X be a smooth integral affine variety with torsion Cl(X) and O(X)^*=C^*. A dominant endomorphism phi cannot omit an entire prime divisor D. Indeed choose n>0 and a rational f with div(f)=nD. Normality implies f is global regular (a normal affine coordinate ring is the intersection of its height-one valuation rings). If phi omitted D, the global regular f o phi would have no zero and hence would be a constant unit. Dominance injects function fields, forcing f constant, contrary to its divisor. Etaleness is not needed for this first assertion.

If phi is additionally etale, its image is open. Since it cannot omit a prime divisor D, its image meets a dense open of D. An inverse-image prime divisor over that open has ramification index one. Thus for any rational r with ord_D(r)<0, the pulled-back valuation is also negative. In particular phi^*r cannot be globally regular. This second assertion uses the open-image/local etale property, not merely dominance. For comparison, the dominant nonetale map (u,v) -> (u,uv) on A2 pulls the genuine-pole function v/u back to the regular v; its image meets {u=0} only at one point.

Section 3 verifies BOTH required invariants for S, and section 2 verifies its smoothness, so the lemma applies to dominant etale SELFmaps of S. It does not apply to T, whose class group is infinite cyclic. Nor may it be transported from S to T merely through q or j: they are maps between different varieties, j omits L_-, and a map into the primitive chart is not thereby a selfmap of S. No rational-homology substitute for the class group or units is used.
