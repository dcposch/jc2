## Statement and scope

Let p,u be algebraically independent over C and write

    q=P(p,u)=sum_(j=0)^m a_j(p)u^j,
    a_j in C(p), a_m!=0, m>=2.

Let A=C[p,q], K=C(p,u), and let S be the normalization of A in K.
Then at least one of the following holds:

1. A branch-image curve of Spec(S)->Spec(A) has affine normalization
   different from A1.
2. Every fiber of p:Spec(S)->A1 has a single reduced component isomorphic
   to A1, its multiplicity divides m, and m*Cl(S)=0.

The proof chooses case1 when any critical-value branch of P has a pole
over any finite p=a; otherwise it establishes case2. The alternatives
need not be exclusive as statements about S.

Consequently there is no C-field embedding K->C(x,y) for which the images
of p,q are polynomials with nonzero constant Jacobian on the WHOLE A2.
The embedding may have arbitrary finite degree onto its image. Here m is
the second-leg field degree [K:C(p,q)], not the first-leg degree or a
polynomial total-degree bound.

This extends the accepted Laurent-coefficient donor exclusion to arbitrary
finite coefficient poles and zeros of a_m. It still requires q to be a
POLYNOMIAL in the rational fiber parameter u. It does not cover arbitrary
rational dependence on u, produce an intermediate field for an arbitrary
Keller map, or prove JC2. No literature novelty or exhaustive classification
is claimed.

## 1. Generic normalization and actual branch curves

The degree of the nonconstant polynomial P over C(p) gives
[K:C(p,q)]=m. Choose a nonzero d in C[p] whose zero set contains every
coefficient pole and every finite zero of a_m. The leading coefficient is
then a unit in C[p,1/d], so u is integral over A[1/d]. Hence

    S[1/d]=C[p,1/d,u].                                      (1)

This ring is normal and finite over A[1/d], with fraction field K.
On this open the finite map is ramified precisely where P_u=0.
All irreducible components of that critical divisor dominate the p-line:
P_u has invertible leading coefficient there, so it has no vertical factor.
Their closures in Spec(S) are genuine ramified prime divisors, not divisors
contracted to points by this finite map.

Suppose one critical-value branch has a pole over p=a in C. Restrict its
place from the critical-curve function field to the function field of its
branch image, generated by the RESTRICTIONS of p,q to that curve (not the
two-variable target field). The restricted valuation satisfies

    v(p-a)>0,                 v(q)<0.

It is an infinity place of the affine normalization. If that normalization
were A1, the nonconstant regular function p-a would be a polynomial in its
coordinate and would have a pole at its sole infinity place. This is a
contradiction. The argument uses an actual ramified curve from (1) and all
places over each a, even when critical roots repeat. Thus this case gives
alternative1. From now on assume every critical value is integral at every
finite a.

## 2. Local good reduction without a global cyclic cover

Fix a in C, put w=p-a, and let O=C[p]_(w). Write

    a_m=w^ell b,       ell in Z, b in O*,
    s=-a_(m-1)/(m a_m).

The O-algebra O[BETA]/(BETA^m-b^(-1)) is etale: BETA and m are units.
Choose a localization at one point over w=0, and call it O', with beta the
image of BETA. This is a normal local domain, an essentially etale DVR over
O, with residue field C and uniformizer w. The localization need not be
finite as an O-module; the proof does not require that. Write E=Frac(O').

Set D=O'[t]/(t^m-w). The polynomial is Eisenstein, so D is a DVR with
uniformizer t, residue C, and fraction field E(t) of degree m over E.
Substitute

    u=s+beta*t^(-ell)*v,
    H(t,v)=P(p,s+beta*t^(-ell)*v).

Since a_m beta^m t^(-m ell)=1, H is monic of degree m and centered:

    H=v^m+sum_(j<=m-2)c_j v^j,              c_j in E(t).

The coefficients lie in D[1/t], and the critical values stay integral
after the base extension. The accepted valuative argument yields c_j in D.
For completeness, if a coefficient has a pole, let

    rho=max_j{-ord_t(c_j)/(m-j)}>0.

After a finite valued extension clearing denominators of rho, the polynomial
G(z)=t^(m rho) H(t,t^(-rho)z) is integral, monic, centered, and its reduction
has a nonzero lower coefficient. Roots of G'/m are integral and their
reductions account for all derivative roots, including multiplicities.
Every corresponding G-value has positive valuation, so all critical values
of the reduction vanish. A monic centered characteristic-zero polynomial
with all critical values zero is z^m: if it has r distinct roots, the
derivative has m-r zeros at those roots, forcing r=1; centering places that
root at zero. This contradicts the lower coefficient. A completion may be
used for this valuation argument only; no formal algebraization is inferred.

Thus H belongs to D[v]. The normal ring B=D[v] is finite over O'[q]: t is
integral over O', and H(t,v)-q is monic in v.

## 3. The actual local normalization and special fiber

The action of mu_m on B defined by

    t -> zeta*t,              v -> zeta^ell*v

fixes O', u, and q. It is faithful on t. Since t^m-w is Eisenstein over E,
its fixed field in E(t,v)=E(u,t) is E(u).

Consider S'=S tensor_(C[p]) O'. It is normal by essentially etale base
change. It is a domain, not an assumed selected component: S is torsion-free,
hence flat, over the PID C[p]; therefore S' injects into its generic fiber.
That generic fiber is E[u], by normalizing C(p)[q] in C(p,u) and (1).
Consequently Frac(S')=E(u). Both S' and B^(mu_m) are finite normal
O'[q]-algebras with this fraction field, so uniqueness of normalization gives

    S'=B^(mu_m).                                           (2)

There is one reduced component of the w=0 fiber. Indeed averaging makes
invariants exact, and w is invariant, so

    S'/wS'=(B/wB)^(mu_m),
    (S'/wS')_red=C[v]^(mu_m)=C[v^r],
    r=m/gcd(m,ell).                                        (3)

Here B/wB=C[t,v]/(t^m). Its nilradical is (t), and taking the invariant
quotient followed by reduction gives the invariant ring of C[v]. Thus
(3) is a statement about the NORMALIZED fiber, not just a pure-power
equation in an unnormalized presentation. Since O'/w=C, the fiber of
S' is exactly the fiber S/(p-a), so no descent from a formal fiber is needed.

Its multiplicity is r. At the divisor t=0 in B, the decomposition group
is mu_m; its action on C(v) has kernel of order g=gcd(m,ell). Thus the
valuation ramification index of B over (2) is g. Since ord_t(w)=m,
the normalized downstairs order of w is m/g=r. Equivalently the invariant
field has t-value group gZ. This also covers ell=0, with gcd(m,0)=m and
r=1, and negative ell. Hence

    div_S(p-a)=r_a E_a,       E_a reduced isomorphic to A1,
    r_a=m/gcd(m,ord_a(a_m)),  r_a divides m.                 (4)

An essentially etale extension changes no multiplicity; alternatively the
equality of fiber schemes above gives the same conclusion over the original S.

## 4. Global divisor classes and exclusion

Equation(1) is factorial. For any Weil divisor class of S, its restriction
there is principal. Subtract the same principal divisor on S; the remaining
divisor is supported on fibers over the finitely many zeros of d. By (4)
each such fiber has only one prime component E_a and r_a E_a is principal,
with r_a dividing m. Therefore

    m*Cl(S)=0.                                             (5)

No global polynomial good-reduction coordinates, global cyclic cover of S,
or global A2 normalization have been assumed.

Now suppose K embeds in L=C(x,y), taking p,q to a polynomial Keller pair
f,g in R=C[x,y]. Integrality and normality put S inside R. Thus F factors
as A2->Spec(S)->A2. Its first leg is quasi-finite, and the reviewed local
sandwich argument puts its image in the etale locus of the finite second
leg, including when the first-leg degree is one.

In alternative1 the ramified branch-image curve is a component of the
nonproperness set of F. The accepted Jelonek--Lason polynomial coverage
forces its affine normalization to be A1, contrary to Section1.

In alternative2 choose a ramification prime E if there is one. Equation(5)
gives div(h)=mE for a rational function h. Normality makes h regular, with
zero set exactly E. Since the source image misses E, its pullback to R is
a zero-free polynomial, hence a scalar unit. Injectivity of the field map
then makes h that same scalar in S, contradicting its divisor. If there
is no ramification prime, purity gives a finite etale connected cover of
A2_C, necessarily trivial. That contradicts m>=2. These are the old
source-transfer arguments, now using (5).

## Replay and controls

certificate. All identities above are uniform, not finite-degree evidence.
The following hand checks test the changed local/global step.

- Multiple finite leading zeros: q=p(p-1)u^2. With z=p(p-1)u the full
  normalization is C[p,q,z]/(z^2-p(p-1)q). It is finite, has the right
  fraction field, and is normal: its only singularities are the two points
  p=0 or1, q=z=0; a hypersurface surface regular in codimension one is
  normal. Both reduced exceptional fibers are A1 and each has multiplicity2.
  This is not a single-monomial leading coefficient. No assertion that
  this quadratic example itself is a new frontier exclusion is made.
- Bad-value control: q=(u^2-1)/(p(p-1)) has critical value
  -1/(p(p-1)). Its branch-image curve p(p-1)q=-1 has normalization
  A1 minus{0,1}, so the pole alternative really occurs at multiple places.
- Excluded degree1: q=u/p. The embedding p=x,u=xy gives f=x,g=y, a
  polynomial automorphism. Thus m>=2 is essential.
- Omission of the good-value hypothesis: the known equation
  u^2-p^2-p^3q=0 normalizes through u=pv to v^2=1+pq and has a split
  p=0 fiber. Its critical value -1/p has a pole. One cannot apply (3)
  to an arbitrary unnormalized pure-power special equation.
