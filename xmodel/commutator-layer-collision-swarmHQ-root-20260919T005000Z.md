## Statement and scope

Let A=C<X,Y> be the free associative algebra, c=XY-YX, I=(c),
R=A/I=C[x,y], and B=R tensor_C R=C[x1,y1,x2,y2]. For a polynomial
pair (P,Q) with Jacobian determinant identically 1, the following are
equivalent:

1. There are polynomial lifts U,V in A of P,Q with [U,V]=c modulo I^2.
2. The actual two-point collision ideal
   J=(P(x1,y1)-P(x2,y2), Q(x1,y1)-Q(x2,y2)) equals
   D=(x1-x2,y1-y2) in B.
3. (P,Q) is a polynomial automorphism of C^2.

This is a method discriminator, NOT a proof that these conditions always
hold, a counterexample, or a solution of JC2. It concerns polynomial
coefficients at the first commutator-ideal layer, not an augmentation-adic
formal inverse. A prescribed lift need not work even for an automorphism.

## 1. The full relation module, not its diagonal specialization

Write e for the class of c in I/I^2. Multiplication on the left and right
factors through R because I I is zero in this quotient. The commuting
left and right R actions make I/I^2 a B-module. There is a surjection

    theta: B -> I/I^2,       (p tensor q) -> p e q.

Surjectivity follows because I is the two-sided ideal generated by c.
Representatives of p,q can be chosen arbitrarily in A: changing one by
an element of I changes the product only by an element of I^2.

For injectivity, use the algebra homomorphism into upper triangular
2 by 2 matrices over the polynomial domain B defined by

    rho(X) = [[x1,1],[0,x2]],    rho(Y) = [[y1,0],[0,y2]].

Put dx=x1-x2, dy=y1-y2. Diagonal entries of rho(a) are the two evaluations
of the abelianization of a. Every element of I therefore maps to a
strictly upper triangular matrix, and rho(I^2)=0. Direct multiplication
gives

    rho(c)=-dy E12,
    rho(u c v)=-u_ab(x1,y1) v_ab(x2,y2) dy E12.

Thus theta(f)=0 forces dy*f=0 in B, hence f=0. We have proved

    I/I^2 = B e, freely of rank one.

Equivalently this is the free rank-one R-bimodule, NOT a central
rank-one R-module. For example [Y,c] maps to -dy^2 E12, which is nonzero.
Restricting to x1=x2,y1=y2 erases precisely this left/right information.

## 2. Ordered lifts give the secant determinant

For a polynomial P, take its ordered lift P~ with each monomial written
X before Y. Use subscripts 1,2 for evaluation at (x1,y1),(x2,y2), and set

    a_P = (P(x1,y2)-P(x2,y2))/dx,
    b_P = (P(x1,y1)-P(x1,y2))/dy,
    Delta_P=P1-P2=dx*a_P+dy*b_P.

These quotients are polynomials, including on dx=0 or dy=0: no
localization is involved. Define a_Q,b_Q,Delta_Q in the same way and

    S = [[a_P,b_P],[a_Q,b_Q]],    H=det S=a_P*b_Q-b_P*a_Q.

The upper-right entry of rho(P~) is a_P. This can be checked on X^iY^j:
the upper-right entry of rho(X^i) is (x1^i-x2^i)/dx and multiplication
on the right by rho(Y^j) contributes y2^j. Constants contribute zero.
Consequently

    rho([P~,Q~])_12 = Delta_P*a_Q-Delta_Q*a_P = -dy*H.

The commutator belongs to I. Section 1's injectivity now proves

    [P~,Q~] = H e   in I/I^2.                         (1)

## 3. All polynomial lift corrections

Any polynomial lift of P is P~+F with F in I; write [F]=f e for f in B.
Likewise write [G]=g e for a change of the lift of Q. Conversely EVERY
f in B is realizable this way: write f as a finite sum of p_i tensor q_i
and choose F=sum p_i~ c q_i~ in A. Thus f e is bimodule notation, not
literal multiplication by a four-variable polynomial inside A.

Left multiplication by Q~ on I/I^2 acts as Q1; right multiplication
acts as Q2. Products between F and G lie in I^2. Hence (1) gives

    [P~+F,Q~+G] = (H-Delta_Q*f+Delta_P*g)e   in I/I^2. (2)

Since e is a free generator, a pair of corrected polynomial lifts with
commutator equal to c modulo I^2 exists exactly when

    H-1 belongs to J=(Delta_P,Delta_Q).                (3)

Explicitly, if H-1=alpha*Delta_P+beta*Delta_Q, choose f=beta and
g=-alpha in (2). Necessity follows by rearranging (2).

## 4. For a Keller pair this is the entire collision exclusion

Always J is contained in D=(dx,dy). On the diagonal, a_P=P_x and
b_P=P_y, so under our determinant-one assumption

    H = 1 modulo D.                                  (4)

The secant identity (Delta_P,Delta_Q)^t=S(dx,dy)^t and its adjugate give

    H*dx = b_Q*Delta_P-b_P*Delta_Q in J,
    H*dy = -a_Q*Delta_P+a_P*Delta_Q in J.              (5)

If H-1 belongs to J, subtracting (H-1)dx and (H-1)dy from (5) shows
dx,dy belong to J. Therefore J=D. Conversely J=D and (4) imply (3).
This proves statements 1 and 2 equivalent without a radicality assertion,
cancellation modulo J, set-theoretic replacement, or reducedness theorem.

If J=D, equal P,Q values at two points force dx=dy=0. The map is
injective, so the inherited characteristic-zero injectivity theorem gives
statement 3. Conversely a polynomial inverse expresses x,y as polynomial
functions of P,Q. Difference identities for these inverse polynomials
express dx,dy as B-linear combinations of Delta_P,Delta_Q. Thus D is
contained in J and J=D. This finishes all three equivalences.

The normalized determinant-one scope suffices: a pair with constant
nonzero determinant lambda can first be replaced by (P,Q/lambda).
No uniform existence of corrections is supplied by this normalization.

## 5. Controls and completion distinction

Positive control: P=x+y^2,Q=y has H=1; the ordered lifts X+Y^2,Y
satisfy [X+Y^2,Y]=c exactly, not just modulo I^2.

Prescribed-lift negative control: for the identity abelianization P=x,
Q=y, take U=X+cY,V=Y. Here [cY]=y2 e and (2) yields

    [U,V]=(1-dy*y2)e modulo I^2,

which differs from e in the polynomial domain B. Removing cY restores
the correct lift. Existence for an automorphism must not be confused
with success of every chosen lift, or with diagonal evaluation of it.

There are two different completions. A compatible I-adic formal lift
with polynomial quotients necessarily solves this same A/I^2 problem.
Thus merely allowing higher I-orders does not remove its first-layer
obstruction. We make no theorem about higher-order lifting or termination.

By contrast, H is a unit on the diagonal. In B[1/H], equations (5)
already give J B[1/H]=D B[1/H], hence H-1 belongs to J B[1/H]. The
same is true after completing B at a diagonal point. Those localized or
local-formal coefficients are not polynomial B-coefficients. An
augmentation-adic inverse near one source point cannot be substituted
for the global correction required in statement 1.
