## A. Exact conductor through fixed rational forms

Set A=C[f,g] and L=Frac(R)=Frac(B). Jacobian one makes f,g algebraically independent and Omega_(R/A)=0; hence j is unramified. The relation P has positive W-degree and is irreducible over C[U,V,W]. Characteristic zero and the absence of a relation in U,V imply P_W is nonzero and not divisible by P, so c!=0. No monicity in W is needed.

The hypersurface B is Cohen-Macaulay and Gorenstein. With the chosen ambient volume dU wedge dV wedge dW and defining equation P, hypersurface adjunction identifies its canonical module as

    omega_B=B*eta,       eta=dU wedge dV/P_W

inside the one-dimensional L-space of rational two-forms. The displayed formula holds on P_W!=0 and determines the rational generator globally; it is a dualizing generator, not a claim of a globally regular Kahler form on the singular surface. Since df wedge dg=dx wedge dy, its pullback is eta=omega/c, with omega=dx wedge dy.

The standard finite duality fact used is: for a finite map of equidimensional Cohen-Macaulay schemes, with a maximal Cohen-Macaulay finite source algebra, the source canonical module is Hom_B(R,omega_B). Under the canonical trace, this is an equality of rational canonical modules when the fields agree; the generic field trace is then the identity. Thus it carries no arbitrary invertible or rational twist. Here R is smooth and is maximal Cohen-Macaulay as a B-module: a system of parameters in a B local ring remains a system of parameters in each finite source local ring above it, and those regular source rings are Cohen-Macaulay. Equivalently one can apply the finite duality statement stalkwise. The source canonical module in this fixed rational realization is R*omega.

Every B-linear map R->B extends over L to multiplication by some q in L. Because 1 belongs to R, qR subset B forces q in B; these q are precisely the conductor

    I={q in B:qR subset B}=ann_B(R/B),

also an ideal of R. Therefore finite duality in the fixed rational forms gives

    I*eta = Hom_B(R,omega_B) = omega_R = R*omega.

Substituting eta=omega/c and cancelling the nonzero rational form gives I/c=R, hence I=cR, not merely equality of radicals. An abstract canonical-module isomorphism alone would not justify this cancellation; the generic trace identification above is essential. Multiplying P by a nonzero constant multiplies c by that same constant and leaves the ideal unchanged. Conversely every other generator of this principal ideal differs by a unit of R, and R^*=C*. No nonconstant source-unit ambiguity exists on the whole plane.

## B. Paired Euler identity, with its exact generator

At a generic ordinary double point use the Keller target coordinates U,V on each source branch. The Jacobian condition makes their local volume Omega=dU wedge dV equal to dx wedge dy. Write their graph functions H_1,H_2, delta=H_1-H_2, and

    P=u(U,V,W)*(W-H_1)*(W-H_2),

with other local factors absorbed into the unit u. Let u_i=u(U,V,H_i). Then c_1=u_1 delta and c_2=-u_2 delta, with u_1=u_2 on delta=0. These c_i are the actual restrictions of the same global P_W, not independently normalized local conductor generators.

Push the source E=(x partial_x+y partial_y)/2 to each U,V chart, obtaining E_i. The canonical identity and volume normalization give

    i_(E_i) Omega=alpha_i=dH_i+U dV,
    i_(E_1-E_2) Omega=d delta.

Evaluating the second equality on E_1-E_2 shows (E_1-E_2)(delta)=0. Thus E_1(delta)=E_2(delta), even before restriction to the common curve. On that curve, terms containing delta disappear from the derivative of u_i delta, so

    E(c)(a)=u_1 E_1(delta),
    E(c)(b)=-u_2 E_2(delta),
    E(c)(a)+E(c)(b)=0.

This is the paired Euler identity directly, without a residue-sign convention. Agreement of alpha along the curve does not force either value to be zero. For a local differential control, take H_1=V+U,H_2=V,u=1. Then delta=U, E_1=(1+U)partial_U-partial_V and E_2=(1+U)partial_U. On U=0 the two E(c) values are 1 and -1. This is a local two-branch control, not a global polynomial Keller counterexample. Rescaling local generators independently can destroy their paired normalization; a common global constant preserves the identity.

## C. Reduced conductor valuation and the homogeneous-level exclusion

Here is the required valuation argument, derived from A and the actual local graph factorization. Unramifiedness makes the differential of j into A3 have rank two; locally the source is a smoothly embedded surface branch. Finiteness permits shrinking around a target point so these finitely many source neighborhoods exhaust its branches. Birationality prevents duplicate branches. A target point with only one branch is smooth and normalization is an isomorphism there. Thus a conductor point has at least two branches. Each projects isomorphically to the same local U,V base because J(f,g)=1, so the reduced hypersurface germ has factorization

    P=u*product_(i=1)^r (W-H_i).

On branch i, differentiation and restriction give the exact expression

    c_i=u_i*product_(j!=i) (H_i-H_j).

At a generic point of a conductor curve on branch i, A identifies the conductor valuation with

    ord(c_i)=sum_(j!=i) ord(H_i-H_j),

where factors not vanishing along that curve have order zero. The nonzero terms are positive integers. At a general point of its target image, every companion branch through the image contributes such a positive order; intersections confined to exceptional points are avoided. Therefore valuation one permits exactly one companion and intersection order one. At a generic smooth point of that divisor the difference has nonzero differential, so the two surface branches meet transversely. This proves the ordinary-double reduction instead of silently assuming it for every conductor.

Now suppose I=(h_d-a)R, where h_d is nonconstant homogeneous of degree d>0 and a in C*. By A and R^*=C*, the fixed generator is c=lambda*(h_d-a) for some lambda!=0. Euler gives

    x*(h_d)_x+y*(h_d)_y=d*h_d.

On h_d=a this is nonzero, so the divisor is smooth and reduced, possibly disconnected. Every conductor valuation is one. Choose a generic ordinary double pair a_1,a_2 supplied by the preceding argument. On every point of the conductor,

    E(c)=lambda*d*a/2.

Thus B would say 0=E(c)(a_1)+E(c)(a_2)=lambda*d*a, a contradiction. Such a nonconstant level has points over C, so no empty-divisor exception is involved. We have excluded the exact ACTUAL conductor ideal shape (h_d-a)R under the given source hypotheses. This is not an exclusion based only on the support if its actual conductor multiplicities are larger, and is not a statement about all reduced conductors or arbitrary levels.
