## 2. Shifted Davenport--Stothers identity

Put `E=sum_(ell>=1) r_ell w^-ell`.  Equation (0.1) says

```text
g=w^12-E,                  f^3=w^24.
```

Consequently

```text
g^2-f^3=-2w^12 E+E^2.                                  (2.1)
```

The square `E^2` has strictly negative `w`-order.  Substituting (0.2) into
(2.1), and using `w^8=f`, gives

```text
g^2-f^3=-2mu_4 f-2R_7 w^5+O(w^4),
g^2-f^3+2mu_4 f=-2R_7 w^5+O(w^4).                     (2.2)
```

Since `w=z+O(z^-1)`, the last expression is a polynomial of degree at most
five, with `z^5` coefficient `-2R_7`.  The terminal equation in (0.2) has
nonzero right side, so `R_7` is nonconstant and in particular nonzero.
This proves (0.5) before normalization.

More generally, before imposing any tails, the polynomial

```text

```

has its coefficients in degrees `11,10,9,8,7,6` obtained from

```text

```

by a triangular change of generators with diagonal `-2`.  Indeed (2.1)
has no nonnegative contribution from `E^2`, and each
`w^(12-ell)=z^(12-ell)+` lower powers.  Induction from degree eleven down
to degree six proves equality of the two ideals.  It also gives
