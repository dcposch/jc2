## 1. Primary literature: exact statements and hypotheses

### 1.1 Deligne, Sém. Bourbaki 543 (1979/80)

Acquired: IAS reprint `Number39.pdf` (10 pp.) and Numdam `SB_1979-1980__22__1_0.pdf` (11 pp.). Hashes in §6. Page numbers below are the IAS print (`543-01` … `543-10`).

**Théorème 1 (p. 543-01).** Let `C ⊂ P²(C)` be a plane curve whose only singularities are double points with distinct tangents. Then `π₁(P²(C) − C)` is abelian.

This is the topological fundamental group. Irreducibility is not assumed. Once abelianness is known, homology gives the structure (p. 543-02): if `C` is a union of irreducible components of degrees `d₁,…,d_r`, then `π₁(P² − C)` is `Z^r` modulo the subgroup generated by `(d₁,…,d_r)`.

The hypothesis is printed as “points doubles à tangentes distinctes”: ordinary nodes, tangency excluded. The proof (pp. 543-08–09, Prop. 2.2 + 2.3) makes the distinct-tangents hypothesis load-bearing: at each singular point of a component `D` the two real 2-planes of the branches are taken orthogonal, and the tubular neighbourhood of the normalisation is used to produce a *central* meridian.

**Théorème 2 (Fulton, cited [3], p. 543-01).** Over an algebraically closed field `k`, if `C ⊂ P²` has only double points with distinct tangents, then the *tame* (algebraic) fundamental group of `P² − C` is abelian.

Deligne records that Thm. 1 is a topological recasting of Fulton’s algebraic argument, using Fulton–Hansen connectedness (Thm. 3, p. 543-02) in place of Abhyankar’s algebraic connectedness.

### 1.3 Nori, Ann. Sci. ENS 16 (1983) 305–344

Acquired: Numdam `ASENS_1983_4_16_2_305_0.pdf`, 41 pages (journal 305–344). Hash in §6. OCR of the scan is noisy; statements below were read against the PDF pages, not the OCR alone.

**Weak Lefschetz Theorem (introduction, p. 305; proved §3).** Let `H` be a connected compact complex-analytic subspace of a connected complex manifold `U`, defined by a locally principal ideal sheaf, with `O_U(H)|H` ample and `dim U ≥ 2`. Let `q: U → X` be a holomorphic local isomorphism onto a smooth projective variety, `R ⊂ X` Zariski closed, `h = q ∘ i`. Let `G` be the image of `π₁(U − q⁻¹(R)) → π₁(X − R)`. Then: (A) `G` has finite index; (B) if `q(H) ∩ R = ∅`, the image of `π₁(H)` has finite index; (C) if `dim X = dim U = 2`, the index is at most `(Div h)² / H²`.

This is the local-analytic form the charged report asked for: `U` need not be projective, and Definition 1.8 (“neighbourhood of `h`”) separates branches of a non-irreducible germ by a local homeomorphism `q: U → X`.

**Corollary 2.5 (p. 315).** If `D` and `E` are curves intersecting *transversally* on a smooth surface `X`, `D` is nodal, and every irreducible component of `D` is *smooth* with positive self-intersection, then `ker(π₁(X − D ∪ E) → π₁(X − E))` is central.

**Proposition 3.27 (p. 331) — the `C² > 2 r(C)` theorem.** Let `D` and `E` be curves on a smooth projective surface `X` intersecting transversally. Assume `D` is nodal and `C² > 2 r(C)` for every irreducible curve `C` contained in `D` (`r(C)` = number of singular points of `C`). Then `N := ker(π₁(X − (D ∪ E)) → π₁(X − E))` is a finitely generated abelian group, and the centraliser of `N` has finite index.

For an irreducible plane curve of degree `d` one has `C² = d²` and `2 r(C) ≤ (d−1)(d−2)`, so the numerical bound holds automatically. Setting `X = P²`, `E = ∅` recovers Zariski’s conjecture for *nodal* curves (Nori, p. 306 and Cor. p. 332 via the homogeneous-space argument Prop. 4.1, which does not need the numerical bound).

**Proposition 3.26 (p. 330).** For an irreducible curve `C` with `B(C) > 0` (Nori’s local invariant; for a node, `B(C) = C² − 2 r(C)`), the image of `π₁(tilde C) → π₁(X − R)` has index at most `max(1, C²/B(C))`.

**Proposition 6.5 (p. 336) — other singularities.** For a plane-curve germ, resolve until the proper transform `F` meets the exceptional divisor `G` transversally, and set `s(C;P) = G.(G+2F)`, `F(C) = Σ_P s(C;P)`. If `C² > F(C)`, then `π₁(X − C) → π₁(X)` is a central extension. Remark 6.6: for a node the resulting bound is coarser than 3.27 (`C² > 4 r(C)` in Nori’s comparison). Example 6.7: ordinary cusp has `s = 6`; Zariski’s sextic `f² = g³` attains `C² = F(C) = 36` and has `π₁(P² − C) ≅ PSL₂(Z)`, nonabelian.

**Affine recast, sourced from Neumann–Norbury (arXiv math/0110286, Thm. 2.1), quoting Nori 3.27.** Let `Σ ⊂ C²` be nodal. Let `X` be a blow-up of `P²` resolving the singularities of `Σ` *at infinity*, with `D = X − C²`. If the proper transform of each component satisfies `C̄_i · C̄_i > 2 r(Σ_i)` and meets `D` transversally, then `π₁(C² − Σ)` is abelian (because `X − D = C²` is simply connected, so `N` is the whole group). This is Nori 3.27 with `E` the divisor at infinity, not a new theorem.

Nori contains no theorem whose hypothesis allows tangential double points, and no theorem whose conclusion is abelianness of `π₁(C² − D)` when `D̄` meets `L_∞` non-transversally.

## 2. Caveat (a): projective nodal theorems vs affine `D` with one place at infinity

Identify `π₁(C² − D) = π₁(P² − (D̄ ∪ L_∞))`. Deligne–Fulton apply to this group if and only if `D̄ ∪ L_∞` is a nodal curve in `P²`.

- Affine ordinary nodes of `D` are nodes of `D̄ ∪ L_∞`.
- One place at infinity (normalisation `A¹`) means `D̄ ∩ L_∞ = {Q}` as a set, with `I(D̄, L_∞; Q) = deg D̄ = d`. For `d ≥ 2` this is not a node. Two places (`C^*`) give either two points or one point with two branches; transverse meeting with `L_∞` occurs only if those intersection multiplicities are 1, i.e. `d` points, which is the generic-line case, not one or two places.

Thus Deligne Thm. 1 and Fulton never apply to `D̄ ∪ L_∞` in the boxed class, except the line (`d = 1`), already excluded by Abhyankar–Moh–Suzuki in the charged proof.

Oka’s comparison (ICTP notes, Lemma 2, hashed): there is an exact sequence `1 → N(ω) → π₁(C²_L − C) → π₁(P² − C) → 1` with `N(ω)` the normal subgroup generated by a meridian of `L`. If `L` is *generic*, `ω` is central, the commutator subgroups coincide, and abelianness of the two groups is equivalent. **Remark 3 (Oka):** for non-generic `L` this fails. The standard witness is the cuspidal cubic `Y²Z = X³` with `L = {Z = 0}`: `π₁(P² − C) ≅ Z/3` abelian, but `π₁(C²_L − C) ≅ B₃` nonabelian. So even when Nori 6.5 or Deligne makes `π₁(P² − D̄)` abelian, that does not decide `π₁(C² − D)`.

Nori 3.27 with `X = P²`, `D = D̄`, `E = L_∞` would give exactly `π₁(C² − D)` abelian, *if* `D̄` were nodal, `D̄` met `L_∞` transversally, and `C² > 2 r(C)`. The numerical bound holds for irreducible plane curves; the first two hypotheses fail at infinity.

The Neumann–Norbury recast (blow up until `D̄` meets the exceptional divisor of infinity transversally, then apply 3.27 on that surface) repairs transversality, but still requires the affine curve to be *nodal* and the proper transform to satisfy the numerical bound. It does not cover tacnodes, and it does not apply until the infinite germ has been resolved to a nodal (or smooth) meeting.

Zariski’s flex theorem (Oka ICTP (I) and statement (3); Cogolludo, *Braid monodromy of algebraic curves*, Thm. 2.13, hashed Numdam AMBP 2011): if an irreducible curve of degree `d` has a line `L` with `L ∩ C = {Q}` and `Q` a *smooth* point (flex of order `d−2`, i.e. `I(C,L;Q) = d`), then `π₁(C²_L − C)` is abelian — the local monodromy is `G(d,1) ≅ Z`, all meridians of a generic fibre become equal, so the group is cyclic. Cogolludo’s projective Thm. 2.13 drops smoothness of `Q` but concludes only `π₁(P² − C)` abelian, which is the wrong group (see Oka Remark 3). Oka Prop. 23: a *single* unibranch singularity with a maximal tangent also makes both groups abelian; our `D` may have additional affine double points, so Prop. 23 does not apply.

**Partial sourced YES, `A¹` with smooth infinite place.** A polynomial immersion `γ: C → C²`, `γ(t) = (p(t), q(t))`, is birational onto its image with one place at infinity. If `|deg p − deg q| = 1`, the unique infinite point of `D̄` is a smooth flex of order `d−2` (differential of the compactified parametrisation is non-zero at `∞`; this derivative computation is an inference from the local chart `s = 1/t`). Zariski–Oka (3) then gives `π₁(C² − D) ≅ Z`. This includes every nodal cubic with the flex at infinity, and every polynomial curve of consecutive degrees. It is *not* the boxed lemma: the boxed class also allows `|deg p − deg q| ≥ 2` (unibranch singular infinity) and normalisation `C^*`.

## 3. Caveat (b): tangential double points (`A_{2k−1}`, `k ≥ 2`) vs ordinary nodes

Every theorem in §1 that concludes abelianness of a complement requires *ordinary nodes* (distinct tangents). Nori 6.5 is the only primary statement covering other germs, and its conclusion is abelianness of `π₁(X − C) → π₁(X)`, i.e. of the projective complement of `C`, not of the affine complement.

**Standard nonabelian examples, restricted to the boxed class.**

| Example | `π₁` | Why it is *not* a countermodel |
|---|---|---|
| Line arrangements | typically nonabelian | reducible |
| Two tangent conics | nonabelian | reducible |
| Zariski sextic `f² = g³` (6 cusps) | `PSL₂(Z)` | unibranch cusps `A₂`, not two smooth branches |
| Tricuspidal quartic | metacyclic of order 12 | three `A₂` |
| Affine cuspidal cubic, `L` the flex tangent | `B₃` (Oka Rem. 3) | affine cusp, unibranch |
| Artal torus-type sextics; Degtyarev quintics (`3 A₄`) | nonabelian | unibranch singularities |
| Cogolludo–Elduque affine Zariski pair (arXiv:2306.07359) | one side `F₂` | *reducible*: union of two nodal sextics (two fibres) |
| Oka smooth affine curve with `π₁ ≅ B₃` (Neumann–Norbury p. 4, equation `(x²−y)² y − 4x(x²−y)+4 = 0`) | `B₃` | not verified here to have normalisation `A¹` or `C^*` (degree 5, two points at infinity; genus not computed; fail-closed: not used as a countermodel) |

No sourced irreducible `D` with normalisation `A¹` or `C^*` and only `A_{2k−1}` affine singularities (including the smooth case of no affine singularities, other than closed embeddings already killed by AMS) was found with nonabelian `π₁(C² − D)`. Absence of a countermodel is not a proof.

Orevkov, Math. USSR Sb. 65 (1990) (statement recovered from the author’s posted text at `math.univ-toulouse.fr/~orevkov/fg.pdf`; the PDF itself 504’d, so this sentence is from the HTML abstract and introduction, *not* a hashed PDF): if `K ⊂ C²` has only nodes *and* satisfies the “negativity condition at infinity”, then `π₁(C² − K)` is generated by one meridian per irreducible component, and meridians of intersecting components commute. For irreducible `K` the group is therefore cyclic. Negativity holds for a generic line at infinity and for a general polynomial parametrisation of given degrees; it is exactly the condition our special infinite place can fail. Orevkov’s own degree-(4,6) example that *fails* negativity was computed to have abelian group; that is one example, not a theorem.

Neumann–Norbury: for a *nodal* curve the Orevkov invariant equals `π₁(C² − Σ)`, and is abelian when the link at infinity is a positive braid, or for polynomially parametrised curves with sufficiently negative canonical class on each horizontal curve at infinity. Again: nodes, plus a positivity/negativity condition at infinity.

## 4. Weaker form: Zariski–van Kampen, meridians, braid relations at nodes and tacnodes

Zariski–van Kampen: `π₁(C² − D)` is generated by meridians of a generic fibre of a linear projection, with relations the braid monodromy around the discriminant. Because `D` is irreducible, all meridians of `D` are conjugate.

**Node (`A₁`).** Oka ICTP (II), sourced: local equation `x² − y² = 0` is `G(2,2)`; the monodromy relation is commutation `ξ₁ ξ₂ = ξ₂ ξ₁`. Disjoint transpositions commute, so a transposition-valued `S₄`-representation with disjoint images at a node satisfies the local relation. This is why the charged Thm. 4.3(4) configuration is not killed by ordinary nodes.

**Tacnode and `A_{2k−1}`.** Oka’s model curve `C_{p,q}: y^p − x^q = 0` has `π₁(C² − C_{p,q}) ≅ G(p,q)` (Lemma 4, sourced). The germ of two smooth branches of contact `k` is `y² = x^{2k}`, i.e. `p = 2`, `q = 2k`. **Inference (not a sourced identification in Oka):** the local group of an `A_{2k−1}` is `G(2,2k)`. Feeding Oka’s formulae (R1)–(R2) with `m = k`, `q' = 0` gives `[ξ_j, ω^k] = 1` for `j = 0,1` with `ω = ξ₁ ξ₀`. Equivalently, the link is the torus link `T(2,2k)` with relation `(ξ₀ ξ₁)^k = (ξ₁ ξ₀)^k` in the usual Wirtinger form.

**Inference.** Let `τ = (1 2)`, `σ = (3 4)`. These commute, have order 2, and `τσ` has order 2. Then `(τσ)^k` is `1` or `τσ` according to the parity of `k`, and in both cases `[τ, (τσ)^k] = 1`. Disjoint transpositions therefore satisfy the local `A_{2k−1}` relations for every `k ≥ 1`. Local braid relations at double points of smooth branches do **not** obstruct the weaker form.

Conjugacy of meridians of an irreducible component is compatible with `S₄`: all transpositions are conjugate, and `(1 2)` is conjugate to `(3 4)`. The product of the two local meridians has cycle type `(2,2)`, matching the charged local monodromy at a double point with `a_p = 0`.

**Where the weaker form does die.** If the group is cyclic, it cannot surject onto `S₄`. This happens in the sourced flex case of §2 (`G(d,1) ≅ Z`) and in Orevkov’s negativity+nodes theorem (one generator per component). **Inference:** those two regimes close the weaker form as well as the strong form. They do not cover tacnodes, nor a unibranch singular place at infinity, nor `C^*`.

No further desk-scale relation (infinity braid of a Puiseux pair with `q > 1`, product of fibre meridians, etc.) was sourced that would force two disjoint transpositions to coincide or to fail to generate a transitive subgroup. Completing that computation from a hashed local presentation at infinity would be the remaining group-theoretic step; it was not found as a theorem, and it is not carried out here as an unsourced global van Kampen analysis.

## 5. Verdict

**OPEN.** The boxed lemma is not decided by the acquired sources, and the weaker form is not decided either except on a proper subclass.

**Sourced partial YES (not enough to close B0).**
- `D̄ ∪ L_∞` nodal ⇒ Deligne Thm. 1, abelian. Does not occur in the boxed class for `d ≥ 2`.
- Smooth infinite place (flex of order `d−2`) ⇒ Zariski–Oka, `π₁(C² − D)` cyclic. Covers polynomial curves with consecutive degrees, including nodal cubics with the flex at infinity. Does not cover `|deg p − deg q| ≥ 2` or `C^*`.
- Affine nodes + Nori 3.27 after resolving infinity to a transverse meeting, with `C̄² > 2 r` ⇒ abelian (Neumann–Norbury recast). Covers ordinary nodes only.
- Affine nodes + Orevkov negativity at infinity ⇒ cyclic if irreducible. Negativity is the condition the boxed infinite place can fail.

**No sourced countermodel** in the exact class (irreducible, normalisation `A¹` or `C^*`, affine branches smooth). Every standard nonabelian complement uses unibranch singularities, reducibility, or a generic line at infinity with cusps.

**Single missing local input.** A Nori-type (or Zariski–van Kampen) statement for an irreducible affine curve whose singularities are double points of *smooth* branches, allowing tangency (`A_{2k−1}`, `k ≥ 2`), on the surface obtained by resolving only the place(s) at infinity — alternatively, a single irreducible polynomial or `C^*` curve of that singularity type with nonabelian `π₁(C² − D)` (or with a transposition-valued `S₄`-surjection as in the weaker form). Until that input is supplied, neither form of the lemma may be promoted. Fail-closed: no gap is filled by the nodal theorems, by the flex theorem, or by analogy with torus-type / cuspidal examples.

A YES on that input closes B0 at `N = 4` unconditionally via the charged Thm. 4.3(3)–(4), promotes `A_F = B` at rank four, and completes (M) in both classes. A countermodel of the exact class would leave B0 open and exhibit a possible Keller configuration.
