## New primary source

Remy van Dobben de Bruyn, *Divisors in projective bundles over the projective
line whose complement is affine space*, arXiv:2608.27341v1, submitted
2026-08-27T16:41:51Z, 12 pages:

- primary page: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27341v1>;
- frozen local PDF:
  `refs/vandobben2026_projective_bundle_complements_affine_space_arxiv2608.27341v1.pdf`;
- PDF SHA-256:
  `ad2b2f112a5c61b101758cd45f38339a69a7e3ce6343dfa81e87f784298298c6`.

The main theorem classifies complements of irreducible relative-hyperplane
divisors in a projective bundle over `P^1`.  Such a complement is `A^n` only
when there are exactly two boundary divisors, their fibre-degree classes
differ by one, and their intersection has a very specific reduced one-fibre
shape.  In dimension two the proof reduces to a Hirzebruch surface; an
intersection of multiplicity `k>1` produces a non-linear `D_{k+2}` boundary
dual graph and hence a nontrivial fundamental-group-at-infinity obstruction
to being `A^2`.

The appendix gives a coordinate-free explanation of the recent
three-dimensional counterexample and proves that its source complement is
`A^3` exactly for a tangent-but-not-osculating hyperplane.  It does **not**
construct or claim a plane counterexample.  The obvious dimension-two
symmetric-power analogue has two boundary divisors of the same Picard class,
so it fails the affine-plane recognition criterion rather than transplanting
the three-dimensional construction.
