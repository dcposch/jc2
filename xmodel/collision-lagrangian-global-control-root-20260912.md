## Exact source interface

For a polynomial Keller map F=(f,g):A2 -> A2 with J=1, its full fibre
square is the affine scheme cut out by F(x,y)=F(u,v) in A4. Each projection
is an etale base change of F. The diagonal is open (unramified) and closed
(separated); hence the off factor Z is itself closed and affine in A4.
When nonempty, it is smooth of dimension two. A connected component is
integral: smooth local rings are domains, so distinct irreducible
components cannot intersect; a finite-type scheme has finitely many.
Neither projection of Z is known to be finite or onto all A2.

Put omega1=dx wedge dy, omega2=du wedge dv, alpha1=(xdy-ydx)/2 and
alpha2=(u dv-v du)/2. On the fibre square the two f,g functions and their
differentials agree. Thus omega1-omega2 vanishes on Z, making it an
algebraic Lagrangian in the nondegenerate ambient difference form.
Polynomial Poincare gives dT=alpha-f dg on A2: decompose the closed
polynomial form into homogeneous terms and contract with the radial field,
dividing only by positive degrees. Consequently

    (alpha1-alpha2)|Z=d(T(x,y)-T(u,v)).

Swap has no fixed point on Z and negates this form and its displayed
primitive. The primitive is the restriction of a DIFFERENCE OF ONE GLOBAL
POLYNOMIAL, stronger than merely an arbitrary regular function on Z.
These observations do not prove that Z is empty. The fibre-square and
clopen diagonal are already campaign material, not novelty claims.

## One exact closed affine control

Consider the two-equation surface L in A4:

    u=-x,                  x^2(y+v)=2.

Its coordinate ring is exactly C[x,x^-1,y], since x has inverse
x(y+v)/2 and v=2/x^2-y. This proves smoothness and irreducibility, not
merely parametrization of a dense open. L is CLOSED in A4; the apparent
localization is enforced by its polynomial equation, not an omitted
boundary in the ambient closure. Its two projections are isomorphisms
onto the corresponding x!=0 or u!=0 open planes, hence etale, but neither
is surjective or finite as a map to all A2.

Write sigma(x,y)=(-x,-y+2/x^2). It is an involution and its Jacobian is1:
the derivative matrix has rows(-1,0),(-4/x^3,-1). Thus L is its graph,
omega1-omega2 vanishes on L, and the swap preserves L. It has no fixed
point: u=x and u=-x would force x=0, incompatible with the equation2.

The radial one-form calculation is literal:

    sigma^*alpha=alpha+3 dx/x^2,
    alpha-sigma^*alpha=-3 dx/x^2=d(3/x).

Furthermore H=(3/2)xy is a GLOBAL polynomial on the whole source A2,
and sigma^*(xy)=xy-2/x, so

    H(x,y)-H(u,v)=3/x on L.

Hence even the global-polynomial-difference property of the primitive
does not force diagonal contact. The primitive is a nonconstant regular
unit of L. In fact all units of C[x,x^-1,y] are a*x^n, a in C*, n in Z:
degree in y first forces a unit to be independent of y, then compare
highest and lowest Laurent exponents in x for it and its inverse.

This L also comes from a concrete nontrivial rational Keller map on the
punctured plane, not just an arbitrary Lagrangian immersion. Set

    f=x^2,              g=y/(2x)-1/(2x^3).

Its Jacobian is1; on x!=0 the two points with the same f have u=+x or
u=-x, and equality of g gives respectively v=y or v=-y+2/x^2. Thus L
is EXACTLY its off-diagonal fibre square. Over A=C[f,f^-1,g], x^2=f,
x^-1=x/f and y=2xg+x^-2 prove the source ring is A[x], finite free of
rank2; the derivative2x is a unit, hence etale. Its pole at x=0 is genuine. It does not extend
as this polynomial Keller map on A2.

Important primitive distinction: its radial canonical potential is
T=3/(2x), because f dg=alpha+3 dx/(2x^2). This T is NOT polynomial on
all A2. Nevertheless delta T=3/x equals delta H for the global polynomial
H above; H-T=3fg on the punctured plane. Therefore having a global
polynomial that represents the DIFFERENCE on the collision surface does
not recover the full-plane canonical-potential equation itself.

As a changed-object check, deleting the pole term in g gives
g0=y/(2x), off graph(u,v)=(-x,-y), and lambda=0. That punctured off
graph is NOT closed in A4: its closure meets the diagonal at the origin.
The added pole is precisely why the main control keeps closedness while
still avoiding the diagonal. Neither example is a polynomial Keller pair.
