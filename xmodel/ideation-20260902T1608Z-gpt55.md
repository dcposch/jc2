## Why This Is Structural

The sign issue is visible before any large computation. `FRONTIER-N` rewrites the
degree as a deficiency at the `g`-frontiers. In the tree language, adding
unresolved structure below a known ball increases same-slope contact. Increased
contact subtracts from `N`. Therefore a partial boundary tree naturally gives

```text
N <= U(known tree).
```

To reverse the direction, an argument must upper-bound the amount of contact
that can be hidden below `D_1`. None of Moh's typed constraints does that:
outside `D_1`, minor discs are detectors and contribute zero; along the major
tower the counts are proportional; at `D_1` the recursion stops. The known
mechanisms either count roots, price ramification, or record Noether/genus
identities. Those are all compatible with making the bottom more proportional
and hence making `N` smaller.

This also explains why the `N<=16` filter was destined to invert. A skeleton
with `U<=16` is not a contradiction to `N<=16`; it is a host for it. A skeleton
with `U>16` is not a contradiction either; it just has a wider window. The only
way a boundary filter kills a small-`N` residual is by proving `U<N_min`, and
#16 measured that this never empties a degree through `D<=400`.

What would change this conclusion:

```text
1. a D_1 theorem of the form N|D_1 >= f(D) or >= 17 in the H2 range;
2. a global cover invariant whose output is a lower bound on the permutation
   degree N from the degree/conductor of A_F or E;
3. an arithmetic specialization theorem that turns bounded rank N into an
   impossible trace/conductor identity for large D.
```

Anything else is probably another floor ledger.
