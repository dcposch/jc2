## 1. The precise rational-field implication

Suppose an actual whole-plane Keller inclusion supplies a tower
\[
K=\mathbb C(p,q)\subset K_1=\mathbb C(p,u)\subset L=\mathbb C(x,y),
\qquad m=[K_1:K]\ge2.
\]
Here p,u are algebraically independent, and all inclusions are the literal
source maps. Write \(k=\mathbb C(p)\). If q, viewed on \(\mathbb P^1_k\),
has exactly one **geometric** pole, that point is k-rational: its singleton
geometric support is Galois-invariant and k has characteristic zero.
A k-Mobius change of u sends it to infinity, making q a polynomial in
the new u. Its degree is m. The reviewed
[rational-coefficient donor exclusion](rational-coefficient-donor-swarmHQ-astra-20260915T214600Z.md)
and [different-model review](rational-coefficient-donor-review-swarmHQ-sol-20260915T215100Z.md)

change, not a new source theorem. A single closed point need not be a

The condition m>=2 is the target-leg degree, not the first-leg degree or
a polynomial total-degree bound. Neither the existence of K1 nor the

## 2. A one-place polynomial source fiber need not supply that presentation

Take the dominant polynomial pair
\[
P=y^2-x^5,\qquad Q=x.
\]
This is explicitly NON-KELLER: J(P,Q)=-2y. Over \(k=\mathbb C(P)\), its
generic P-fiber has function field
\[
L=k(x,y),\qquad y^2=x^5+P.
\]
Its smooth projective completion has one k-rational point at infinity,
the only pole of Q=x, of order2. For an explicit infinity chart, put
\(r=1/x\), \(s=y/x^3\). Then
\[
s^2=r+Pr^6.
\]
At r=s=0, the derivative in r is nonzero. Thus s is a local parameter,
\(\operatorname{ord}(r)=2\), and x,y have orders -2,-5 respectively.
This is the single infinity point of the double cover.

The differential dx/y is nonzero and regular everywhere. At finite branch
points, y is a parameter and the equation gives
\(dx/y=2\,dy/(5x^4)\), with x nonzero there. At infinity,
\[
dx/y=-r\,dr/s=-\frac{2r}{1+6Pr^5}\,ds,
\]
so it has a zero of order2. A rational projective curve has no nonzero
regular differential; therefore this generic fiber is not rational.

Moreover, [L:k(Q)]=2. If an intermediate field k(u) existed with
\(k(Q)\subset k(u)\subset L\) and Q polynomial in u of degree m>=2,
then [k(u):k(Q)]=m. Multiplicativity forces m=2 and L=k(u), contradicting
nonrationality. Thus the one-place condition supplies neither rationality
of the actual fiber nor the required polynomial donor presentation.

This controls only the bare inference from one pole to that presentation.
It does not refute a theorem genuinely derived from a constant nonzero
Jacobian. A unique pole of order ONE would be a different condition.

## 3. Two fiber poles do not automatically escape the old donor obstruction

Astra's blind tested the literal rational donor
\[
q=\frac{u^m+h(p)}{u^n},\qquad m>n\ge1,
\]
where h in \(\mathbb C[p]\) is nonconstant and squarefree. Generically
over p this has two poles, at zero and infinity, so it is outside the
polynomial-in-u presentation. Nevertheless it fails the old
missed-principal-divisor mechanism.

The normalization of \(\mathbb C[p,q]\) in \(\mathbb C(p,u)\) is
\[
B=\mathbb C[p,u,q]/(u^m-qu^n+h(p)).
\]
Indeed the defining polynomial, linear in q, is primitive and irreducible
over \(\mathbb C[p,u]\): its two coefficients are coprime because
\(\gcd(u^n,h(p))=1\). The ring is a domain, finite over \(\mathbb C[p,q]\)
by monicity in u, and has the stated fraction field. It is smooth:
a singular point would have u=0 from the q-derivative, h(p)=0 from the
equation, and h'(p)=0 from the p-derivative, contradicting squarefreeness.
Normality now identifies it with that normalization.

For the finite map \(\pi:\operatorname{Spec}B\to\mathbb A^2_{p,q}\),
ramification is cut out by the function
\[
D=m u^{m-1}-nqu^{n-1}.
\]
In its fraction field,
\(D=(m-n)u^{m-1}-nh(p)/u\), so D is nonconstant.
If a dominant rational source substitution made p,q whole-plane
polynomials with nonzero constant Jacobian, the monic relation would
make the substituted u integral over \(\mathbb C[x,y]\), hence polynomial
by normality. It gives a dominant regular map
\(j:\mathbb A^2\to\operatorname{Spec}B\).

Both surfaces are smooth. Since \(\pi j\) is etale, the chain rule makes
\(\pi\) unramified at every image point of j. Therefore j*D is a
nowhere-zero polynomial on \(\mathbb A^2\), hence a constant unit.
Dominance makes the pullback on B injective, contradicting nonconstancy
of D. This is a correct scoped screen by a KNOWN mechanism, not a new
proof strategy, arbitrary rational-donor exclusion or selected successor.
