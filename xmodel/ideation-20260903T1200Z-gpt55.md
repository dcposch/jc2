## 7. Strongest proof attack

The strongest proof attack is not a bigger census. It is a local-to-global
coefficient non-realisability theorem:

For every promoted full-major-tree survivor, the node-local A.3 sibling
coefficient system either has no solution, or any solution descends to a
theorem-(T) monomial-Jacobian system that is impossible.

The first lemma to try is narrower and more promising:

`Danger-clearing lemma.` Suppose a full-tree survivor contains a zero sibling
part at a dangerous parent and the selected route clears danger only by choosing
a nonzero orbit. Then the cyclic quotient A.3 ODE forces a nontrivial
parent character/residue that cannot glue to a later selected zero child.

Why this is the right attack:

- It explains the exact difference between the dead D=105/D=117 rows and the
  first live D=108 rows. The dead rows fail by forced zero major sibling while
  danger remains. The live D=108 group survives by interleaving zero and nonzero
  sibling choices.
- It attacks the local structure before global interpolation creates artificial
  witness columns.
- It is testable on two adjacent nodes, not on a 100-degree global polynomial.
- If it succeeds, it may promote from a D=108 kill to an all-degree rule because
  the pattern is typed by `(P,Q,A,b)` and the danger automaton, not by degree.

The proof skeleton I would hand to a lane:

1. Normalize each node with the variable/ring map declared: coefficient field,
   `pi`, quotient variable `z=pi^A`, zero part `pi^b`, and nonzero orbit
   factors.
2. Write Moh A.3 as a cyclic quotient ODE with all leader vanishings branched.
3. Extract the invariant term that changes when the selected child is a
   nonzero orbit rather than the zero factor.
4. Prove that this invariant is functorial under the child map used by the
   full major tree.
5. Apply it to the shared D=108 top node. If row A and row B demand incompatible
   child invariants, kill the group. If not, keep the invariant as a necessary
   coefficient decoration for the moment engine.

This proof attack respects the fallacy guardrails: it separates flag/place/
series, it treats `REPRESENTATIVE` as a floor only, it does not use pole
identities without vertex hypotheses, it normal-forms in the declared quotient,
and it never turns a lower bound into attainment.
