## 1. What the pinned Moh paper actually is

Moh JRAM 340 is a paper on *Jacobian pairs* `(f,g)` with `f_x g_y - f_y g_x = 1`, and on the tree data of their roots over the Puiseux field `k⟨⟨x^{-1}⟩⟩`. Journal pagination 140–212 is 73 pages of article (74 PDF pages). Section map, from the introduction and the body:

| locus | content |
|---|---|
| Intro, pp. 140–148 | JC; configuration/tree data; approximate roots `T_r^*`; plan of §§1–6 and the two appendices. JC for `deg ≤ 100` is announced as an application. |
| §1, from p. 148 | Roots, discs, `π`-roots. Theorems 1.1–1.2 (approximate / quasi-approximate roots). |
| §2, from p. 149 | *Characteristic data of a pair* `(f,g)`. The pair is viewed as a parametrization *over `k(x)`* of a plane rational curve with parameter `y`, one place at `y=∞`. Data `{M_j, d_j}` along a root of the monic equation of `f` over `k[x]((g^{-1}))`. |
| §3 | Partial derivatives of `T_r^*`; distribution detectors. |
| §4 | Splitting / nonsplitting of discs. Prop. 4.5: at most two points at infinity for `f=0`. |
| §5 | Major discs, towers. |
| §6 | Minor discs; inversion (Lemma 6.2); degree-reduction (Props. 6.2–6.4). Unnumbered Theorem, p. 200: summary of major/minor disc structure under the Jacobian condition. Numerical constraints for a putative JC counterexample of degree `< 100`: `3 ≤ s ≤ 5`, `d_s ≥ 4`. |
| App. I | Polynomial solutions of the ODE `D(a,b,p(π),q(π))=0`. |
| App. II, pp. 207–212 | Computer enumeration leaves four exceptional degree pairs `(64,48)`, `(84,56)`, `(75,50)`, `(99,66)` (table p. 202). Coefficient counts are reduced; the remaining systems are computed. Closing sentence, p. 211: “There is no counter-example of polynomials of degrees less than or equal to 100 for the Jacobian conjecture.” |

The only “polynomial curve” language in Moh is the §2 device of treating a Jacobian pair as a parametrization *over `k(x)`*. That is not FSY’s polynomial curve (a proper map `A^1 → A^2` given by `x(t), y(t) ∈ k[t]`). The two objects share approximate-root vocabulary because both sit in the Abhyankar–Moh theory; they are not interchangeable.

Moh’s theorems are about Jacobian pairs. None is a statement about the existence or non-existence of a genus-0 affine curve with a prescribed `δ`-sequence.

## 4. FSY convention translation: {6,8,3} ↔ our (8,6,3)

The coordinate swap is real, and it is exactly as NR §5 stated. The matching is checked here from FSY’s printed conventions, not from NR’s summary.

**FSY side** (Osaka J. Math. 43, pp. 598–603).

- Def. 1 / Fact 1: for `f` monic in `y`, the `δ`-sequence starts `δ_0 = deg_y f = n`, `δ_1 = deg_x f = m`.
- Thm. 2 (Sathaye–Stenerson converse, FSY’s “Abhyankar–Moh condition”): (1) `δ_0 < δ_1`; (2) `q_k ≥ 2`; (3) `d_{h+1}=1`; (4) `δ_k < q_{k-1} δ_{k-1}` for `k≥2`; (5) `q_k δ_k ∈ ⟨δ_0,…,δ_{k-1}⟩`.
- Def. 6: a polynomial curve is one with a polynomial parametrization.
- Fact 3: it has a *proper* polynomial parametrization.
- Fact 4: if `(u(t), v(t))` is proper for `f(x,y)=0`, then `deg_t u = deg_y f` and `deg_t v = deg_x f`.
- Lemma 1: if the `δ`-sequence is `{δ_0, δ_1, δ_2}`, the second approximate root `g_2` of `f` satisfies `deg_t g_2(u(t), v(t)) = δ_2`.

So FSY’s `{6,8,3}` means, in order: `δ_0=6 < δ_1=8`, `δ_2=3`. Equivalently: `deg_y f = 6 = deg_t x`, `deg_x f = 8 = deg_t y`, and `deg_t g_2(x(t), y(t)) = 3`. The AM numerals check:

```text
d1 = 6,  d2 = gcd(6,8) = 2,  d3 = gcd(6,8,3) = 1,
q1 = 3,  q2 = 2,
q1 δ1 = 24 ∈ ⟨6⟩,     q2 δ2 = 6 ∈ ⟨6,8⟩,
δ2 = 3 < q1 δ1 = 24.
```

All five of Thm. 2 hold. The semigroup is `⟨6,8,3⟩ = ⟨3,8⟩`.

Approximate-root shape, from FSY Thm. 3: `n_2 = n/d_2 = 6/2 = 3`, so `g_2` is monic of `y`-degree 3, and

```text
g1 = y + c1 x + c0,
g2 = g1^3 + a x^4 + (terms of 6 i + 8 j < 24, j < 2).
```

The leading cancellation `a = −1` (monic `x,y`) makes `g_2` a Tschirnhausen improvement of `y^3 − x^4`. Lemma 1 then demands `deg_t g_2(x(t), y(t)) = 3`.

**Our side** (NR §0–§1.1, Galindo–Monserrat Thm. 2.1 / Prop. 2.1, locked in NR). Type `(d,n)=(8,6)`, `Δ=(8,6,c)` with `c` odd, `2c ∈ ⟨8,6⟩`, `c<24`. Conversion `β_1 = d^2/gcd(d,n) − c = 32 − c`. For `c=3`: `β_1=29`, `δ_∞=14`, `δ_aff=7`, `M_∞=30`, `S_aff=⟨8,6,3⟩=⟨3,8⟩` with the seven gaps `{1,2,4,5,7,10,13}` (NR §1.1, re-listed in PB §1 for the sibling types; the `c=3` row of NR is the same listing). Leading form: `c` is the first odd `t`-degree of a remainder of `p^3 − q^4` not in `⟨8,6⟩`.

**The swap.** Set FSY’s `(x,y)` equal to our `(q,p)`:

| | FSY `{6,8,3}` | our `(8,6,3)` after `(X,Y)=(q,p)` |
|---|---|---|
| first coordinate | `x(t)` of degree 6 | `q(t)` of degree 6 |
| second coordinate | `y(t)` of degree 8 | `p(t)` of degree 8 |
| defining polynomial | monic in `y`, `deg_y=6`, `deg_x=8` | monic in `p`, `deg_p=6`, `deg_q=8` |
| extra generator | `δ_2 = deg_t g_2 = 3` | `c = 3`, first odd remainder of `p^3−q^4` |
| semigroup | `⟨3,8⟩` | `⟨3,8⟩` |

FSY Thm. 2 *requires* `δ_0 < δ_1`, so FSY never writes `{8,6,3}`. Our Galindo convention writes `Δ=(d,n,c)` with `d > n`. The two sequences `{6,8,3}` and `(8,6,3)` are the same embedded curve in opposite affine coordinates, not two types.

The identification of `δ_2` with `c` is the approximate-root dictionary: FSY’s `g_2(x,y)` is cubic in `y=p` and leading-equivalent to `p^3 − q^4`; our `c` is the first odd remainder of that same form. Lemma 1 (`deg_t g_2 = 3`) is the same open condition as NR’s `h_3 ≠ 0` after the odd vanishings `h_{23}=⋯=h_5=0`.

**What the translation does *not* do.** The semigroup `⟨3,8⟩` is realised by `(t^3, t^8)`, whose `δ`-sequence is FSY `{3,8}` (and our `(8,3)`), not `{6,8,3}`. Primitive-generator uniqueness (FSY Lemma 3): 6 is not primitive in `⟨3,8⟩`, so it need not appear in every generating `δ`-sequence. Existence of `{3,8}` is not existence of `{6,8,3}`. This is FSY’s own caveat, and it is correct. It is also why a Galindo one-place curve of type `(8,6,3)` (NR §8: local-plus-infinity, possibly positive genus) is not a polynomial curve of that type.

The swap is therefore a bijection of *types of polynomial parametrizations*, and FSY’s `{6,8,3}` is exactly our `(8,6,3)`. If Moh had proved FSY’s sentence, our type would die at the curve level.

## 5. The attributed argument: dependencies, desk status

SS’s reconstruction of what Moh “observed” has two steps.

**(AM).** If a polynomial curve has characteristic `δ`-sequence `(6,8,3)`, then (FSY Thm. 3 + Lemma 1, or SS’s Lemma in §4) there exist `x(t)` of degree 6, `y(t)` of degree 8, and a polynomial `g_2(X,Y)` of the displayed approximate-root shape, such that `deg_t g_2(x,y)=3`. Equivalently in our coordinates: `p` of degree 8, `q` of degree 6, all odd coefficients of `p^3−q^4` of `t`-degree `> 3` vanish, and the coefficient of `t^3` does not. This step is sourced (AM / FSY Thm. 3 / NR §5) and desk-checkable. It is a *reduction to a finite system*, not a non-existence.

**(Calculation).** That finite system has no solution in the proper (gcd-1) locus. This is the step SS attributes to Moh, states is not displayed in [Mo6], and states they rechecked and will not display either because it is still messy.

**Ring-theoretic form of the same system** (NR §5, re-derived). An element `r ∈ ℂ[p,q]` of degree 3 exists. Degrees of `q − α r^2` that are not in `⟨3,8⟩` are gaps `{1,2,4,5,7,…}`, so `q = α r^2 + β r + γ`. Completing the square in `r` and translating `q` gives `q = r^2` without leaving type `(8,6)`. Then `r ∈ ℂ[p, r^2]`, i.e. `H(p, r^2)=r` for some `H`. The locus `r=t^3` (or `B=0` in `r=t^3+Bt+C`) forces a degree-2 cover if `p` is even, and is the `{3,8}` curve `(t^3, t^8)` if `p=t^8`; both are out of class. The remaining identity `H(p, r^2)=r` is the same finite system. No desk identity presents itself (the ROW-SWEEP `(6,4,3)` identity used coprime degrees `(3,4)` after the swap; here the coprime pair is `(8,3)`).

**A bound that does *not* kill.** SS Theorem 1 (Davenport-type): if `x,y ∈ k[t]` are nonconstant and `x^m − y^n ≢ 0`, then `deg_t(x^m−y^n) ≥ (deg x)/n · (mn−m−n)+1`. For `q^4 − p^3` with `deg q=6`, `deg p=8` this is `≥ 11`. The identical-zero case is `p=s^4`, `q=s^3` for `deg s=2`, a gcd-2 cover, out of class. The bound is on the *total* degree of `p^3−q^4`. Our `c` is the first *odd* remainder. Even leading terms of `p^3−q^4` may sit at degree `≥ 12` while an odd remainder of degree 3 survives. Using the bound as a kill of `c=3` would be a raw-remainder-degree substitution. It is not claimed.

**Coefficient count, not a proof.** After the NR §2 normal form (kill `t^7` and `t^6` in `p`; `h_{23}` kills the `t^5` term of `q`), nine coefficients remain and ten odd vanishings `h_{23},…,h_5` are imposed, of which `h_{23}` is already spent. Expected 0-dimensional, not visibly empty. This is FSY’s Gröbner job for `{6,8,3}`, the same shape as their `{6,21,4}` run, and the job NR §5 specified as `I_29`. It is not desk-scale. SS’s “still messy” is the same assessment.

No safe desk replacement for step (Calculation) exists. The step is not in the pinned PDF.
