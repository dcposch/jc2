### 3.1 Step 0 is the wrong category (block-free overclaim)

Opus: `Y` is the normalisation of the target `A^2` in `C(x,y)`; `q`
is finite flat of degree `d` by miracle flatness; `A^2 → Y` is
birational quasi-finite, hence an open immersion by ZMT; this
“reproduces the block-descent architecture with no block hypothesis.”

For a non-proper polynomial map, `C[x,y]` is not finite over
`C[F_1,F_2]`. The integral closure `B` of `C[F_1,F_2]` in `C(x,y)`
need not sit inside `C[x,y]`, so there need not be a morphism
`A^2 → Spec B` at all — only a rational map. ZMT requires an actual
quasi-finite morphism. The standard way to produce a finite model over
the target is to compactify the source, resolve, and Stein-factorize.
That *is* the block-descent architecture: the exceptional data of the
compactification are the block. Calling the affine ZMT factorization
block-free analogizes a compactified Stein factorization to a morphism
that exists in the affine category only in the proper (for étale maps:
automorphic) case.

Consequence: F7 (`CANON-SIEVE/Y`), the LND reopen of row 29 on `C[Y]`,
the free class group `Cl(Y)≅Z^{m_Y}`, and the primitive-branch reach
all inherit Step 0. They are not independent cards. Typed `OPEN` until
`Y` is constructed in a category where `A^2 → Y` is a morphism.

`(M)` as assembled in Steps 1–3 does **not** use `Y`. The identity may
survive the death of Step 0. The block-free slogan and every `Y`-client
do not.

### 3.3 The one-node “control” is one-sided

At `s=1, ν=1`, `(M)` gives `σ - s_p = d-1` against `σ <= d-2`, a
contradiction for all `d>=2`. That shows `(M)` is *strong enough* to
imply a promoted exclusion. It does not show `(M)` is true. There is
no positive control: a polynomial étale non-automorphism of `A^2` is a
Keller counterexample, and none is on file. Reproducing a known empty
set is necessary, not sufficient. Banking `(M)` on this test would
promote an identity because it is strong, which is the floor/attainment
error applied to a formula.

A real control would have to live outside the étale-nonproper class:
either a known finite covering of `A^2` minus a curve where every term
of `(M)` is independently computable, or a proof of the local lemma
`#F^{-1}(p)=#Fix(H_p)` that does not pass through `(M)`. Opus names
Step 2 as the expected failure point and then does not test it.

### 3.4 The Euler assembly silently uses a covering that was parenthetically discarded

Additivity is for `χ_c`. Opus says `χ_c` agrees with ordinary `e` for
complex algebraic varieties. That is true for smooth complex varieties
(even real dimension, Poincaré). `D` and `F^{-1}(D)` are typically
singular curves. Using topological `e` on them without a written
`χ_c` lemma is the same covering-space Euler shortcut the harvest
rejected, even if the *constant-fibre-cardinality* shortcut is avoided.

The formula
`e(F^{-1}(D)) = σ(e(D)-s) + Σ s_p`
needs `F^{-1}(D\Sing D) → D\Sing D` to be a finite covering of degree
`σ`. Opus discards “every component of `F^{-1}(D)` is finite over `D`”
as false, then claims `(M)` independent of it. Non-finiteness supported
only at `Sing(D)` is compatible with a covering over `D\Sing`.
Non-finiteness along `D\Sing` (a sheet escaping while the image point
stays smooth) would drop the fibre cardinality below `σ` and kill
`#F^{-1}(p)=σ` on the smooth locus. So *either* the discarded claim is
false only over `Sing(D)`, in which case it should be restated as a
lemma, *or* the Euler formula is missing horizontal pieces. Parenthetical
discard of the dangerous claim is not a proof that the formula avoids
it. Typed `OPEN` on the covering lemma.
