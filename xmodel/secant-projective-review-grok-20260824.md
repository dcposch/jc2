## 1. Projective setup — CONFIRMED

Let `I=(f_1,f_2)` in `k[x,y,u,v]` with `f_1=P(x,y)-P(u,v)`, `f_2=Q(x,y)-Q(u,v)`. Homogenize each generator at its total degree, obtaining homogeneous `H_P,H_Q` in `S=k[X,Y,U,V,Z]`, and set `K=(H_P,H_Q)`.

Standard homogenization (Cox–Little–O’Shea, *Ideals, Varieties, and Algorithms*, Ch. 8 §4, Theorem 4): for **any** generating set,

```text
I^h = (f_1^h, f_2^h) : Z^∞ = K : Z^∞.
```

The projective closure of `V(I) ⊂ A^4 ⊂ P^4` is `Proj S/I^h = Proj S/(K:Z^∞)`. The naive scheme `Proj S/K` contains every component of that closure, and may contain extra components supported on `Z=0`, namely the `Z`-torsion of `S/K`. Dehomogenizing `K` at `Z=1` recovers an ideal with the same radical as `I` (in the examples below, recovers `I` exactly), so the extra components are invisible in the affine chart.

If `H_P,H_Q` form a regular sequence, `K` is a complete intersection of height two in a five-variable polynomial ring: the affine cone has dimension three and `Proj S/K` has dimension two. A projective complete intersection of dimension at least one is connected (Hartshorne connectedness: a complete-intersection ring of dimension at least two remains connected after deleting a closed subset of codimension at least two; equivalently, components of a projective CI of dimension `≥1` meet in codimension one). That theorem applies to `K`, not to `K:Z^∞`.

Two independent reasons the connectedness does not pass to the honest closure as an obstruction:

1. **Wrong scheme.** Saturating by `Z` deletes (or thins) associated components contained in `Z=0`. Those components can be the only gluing between affine pieces, and they can exist even when the affine scheme is irreducible (the tame automorphism below). Connectedness of `Proj S/K` is then a statement about artificial infinity surfaces, not about off-diagonal sheets of `F`.
2. **Wrong hypothesis.** `K:Z^∞` need not be generated by two homogeneous elements, so it need not be a complete intersection. The Hartshorne hypothesis is lost. Even when the saturation happens to remain a complete intersection, it is a **different** complete intersection, with a different set of components.

If the two homogenized equations fail to retain height two (a common factor, or a drop to height one), even the naive object is not a complete intersection and the connectedness citation never starts. The producer states this caveat; the displayed controls all have height two.

The affine idempotent of the parent, even if granted, is not a degree-zero regular function on a connected projective closure: its homogenization is a section of `O(d)` for `d=deg(det A)>0` and can vanish on `Z=0`. Affine clopen pieces may therefore have closures that meet on the boundary. That is the correct projective language for known nonproperness, not a new discriminator.

This `P^4` with a single homogenizing variable is one compactification of `A^4`. A graph compactification in `P^2×P^2`, a resolution of the closure, or Jelonek’s asymptotic set `A(F)` is a different object. The producer does not identify them.

## 2. Identity and two-step tame controls — CONFIRMED

### 2.1 Identity

`F=(x,y)`. The difference equations are already linear homogeneous:

```text
K_id = (X-U, Y-V).
```

This is prime of height two (quotient a polynomial ring in three variables). Independent `sat_with_exp(K_id,(Z))` returns the same ideal with exponent `0`. The projective scheme is a plane `P^2 ⊂ P^4`. No degree loss, no `Z`-torsion, no off component. This is the no-degree-loss control.

### 2.2 Homogenization and the two identities

Campaign automorphism `Q=y+x^2`, `P=x+Q^2`. Expanded,

```text
P = x^4 + 2 x^2 y + x + y^2     (degree 4),
Q = x^2 + y                     (degree 2).
```

Independent Jacobian: `det J=1`. Homogenizing at those degrees and subtracting the two points gives exactly the producer’s pair

```text
q_1 = X^2 + Y Z,     q_2 = U^2 + V Z,
H_Q = q_1 - q_2,
H_P = X Z^3 + q_1^2 - U Z^3 - q_2^2.
```

Both displayed identities are polynomial, with no division:

```text
H_P - (q_1+q_2) H_Q = Z^3 (X-U),
H_Q - (X+U)(X-U)     = Z (Y-V).
```

Independent expansion over `Q` and independent Singular reduction both give remainder `0`. Consequently

```text
Z^3 (X-U) ∈ (H_P, H_Q),
Z^4 (Y-V) ∈ (H_P, H_Q)
```

because `Z^3 H_Q - (X+U)·Z^3(X-U) = Z^4(Y-V)`. Thus the **exhibited** saturation exponent for the diagonal is at most `4`, and

```text
(X-U, Y-V) ⊂ (H_P, H_Q) : Z^4.
```

The reverse inclusion is immediate: `H_P` and `H_Q` vanish identically on `U=X`, `V=Y`, so `(H_P,H_Q) ⊂ (X-U,Y-V)`, and the right-hand side is prime, hence `Z`-saturated. Therefore

```text
(H_P, H_Q) : Z^∞ = (X-U, Y-V).
```

### 2.3 Infinity surfaces

```text

H_P = (X^2+U^2) H_Q.
```


in `P^4`. These are absent from the affine automorphism (the dehomogenized unsaturated pair at `Z=1` is already `(x-u,y-v)`). They supply the naive complete intersection with two extra projective surfaces at no mathematical cost.

## 3. Characteristic-three Artin--Schreier — CONFIRMED

Over `F_3`, `F=(x-x^3,y)`. Independent Jacobian matrix `diag(1-3x^2,1)` has determinant `1` in `F_3`. Homogenizing at degrees `3` and `1`:

```text
E   = Z^2 - X^2 - X U - U^2,
H_P = (X-U) E,
H_Q = Y-V.
```

Both the factorization and the identification of `E` with the homogenized telescoping determinant are exact over `Z`, hence over `F_3`. In `F_3` one has the further identities

```text
(X-U)^2 = X^2 + X U + U^2,
E       = Z^2 - (X-U)^2 = (Z - (X-U))(Z + (X-U)),
```

so `E` is square-free but reducible over `F_3`.

Independent `sat_with_exp((H_P,H_Q),(Z))` returns the same ideal with exponent `0`. The pair is already the homogenization of `I`. Dimension three in five variables, height two: a complete intersection.

Intersection decomposition, independent ideal equality:

```text
(H_P, H_Q) = (X-U, Y-V) ∩ (E, Y-V) = D ∩ O.
```

This is the producer’s displayed decomposition and is exact. It is an **intersection** decomposition. It is not a primary decomposition of `O`, because `O=(E,Y-V)` is reduced but not prime over `F_3`. Independent `primdecGTZ` of the pair returns three height-two primes, all reduced:

```text
(X-U, Y-V),
(X-U+Z, Y-V),
(X-U-Z, Y-V).
```

The last two are the primary decomposition of `O`:

```text
O = (Z-(X-U), Y-V) ∩ (Z+(X-U), Y-V).
```

Calling `O` “the honest off closure selected by the secant determinant” is correct as a scheme. The word “primary” in “primary/intersection decomposition” is slightly loose; the intersection statement (3.2) is exact. Non-blocking.

Scheme-theoretic meeting:

```text
D + O = (X-U, Y-V, E) = (X-U, Y-V, Z^2)
```

over `F_3`, because `E|_D = Z^2 - (X-U)^2 = Z^2`. Independent ideal equality holds. `Z` does **not** lie in the meeting ideal (`reduce(Z, D+O)=Z`); `Z^2` does. The radical is the reduced line `(X-U,Y-V,Z)`. Affine-cone dimension of the meeting is two, so in `P^4` one has a doubled projective line at infinity. In the affine chart `Z=1`,

```text
(D + O) + (Z-1) = (1):
```

the components do not meet affinely.

Marked affine collision: `F(0,0)=(0,0)=F(1,0)`. The point `(X,Y,U,V,Z)=(0,0,1,0,1)` lies on `O` (`E=H_P=H_Q=0`) and not on `D` (`X-U=-1`). The swapped point `(1,0,0,0,1)` likewise lies on `O`. This is a genuine affine Keller collision whose projective closures meet only at infinity, with nilpotent structure `Z^2`.

## 4. Characteristic-zero rejection and the scope of constant Jacobian — CONFIRMED

The same polynomials over `Q` have

```text
det J_F = 1-3x^2,
E|_D    = Z^2 - 3 X^2.
```

Both identities are exact. The pair `(H_P,H_Q)` is already `Z`-saturated of exponent `0`, with intersection decomposition `D ∩ O` into two primes (here `E` is irreducible over `Q`, so `O` is prime). The meeting is

```text
D + O = (X-U, Y-V, Z^2-3X^2).
```

In the affine chart `Z=1` this is `1-3x^2=0`, the critical locus of `J_F`. The polynomial `E|_D|_{Z=1}=1-3X^2` is not the zero polynomial, so the meeting is not infinity-only. Reducing the same meeting modulo `3` sends `3X^2` to `0` and moves the whole scheme onto `Z^2=0`.

**What constant Jacobian contributes, and what it does not.**

- **Affine non-contact is general.** If `det J_F` is a unit, then `F: A^2 → A^2` is étale. The target is separated, so the diagonal `A^2 → A^2 ×_{A^2} A^2` is a closed immersion; étaleness makes it an open immersion. The diagonal is therefore a clopen subscheme of the affine self-fiber product, and an off component cannot meet it in `A^4`. This uses smoothness of the **affine map** (Jacobian criterion), not smoothness of any compactification, and it does not need reducedness of components. It also does not need the parent idempotent, though the parent’s `e≡1` on the diagonal is the same fact in coordinates.
- **Boundary non-contact is not general.** The characteristic-three Artin--Schreier collision is Keller and meets only at infinity. That single example refutes any characteristic-free implication “`J=1` ⇒ diagonal and off closures are disjoint in a proper compactification” and any claim that `J=1` forces an intersection multiplicity incompatible with a doubled line `Z^2=0`. It does not exhibit a characteristic-zero Keller collision; existence of one is the injectivity endpoint.
- **No hidden projective purity.** The producer does not assume that `K:Z^∞` is equidimensional, Cohen–Macaulay, reduced, or still a complete intersection in general. The tame saturation **removes** components; the Artin--Schreier meeting is **non-reduced**. The negative statement is that constant Jacobian plus naive connectedness does not prohibit this mechanism. A descendant that wanted an intersection-number contradiction would have to **prove** purity/CM/CI of the honest closure plus a specified normal bundle; that is listed as missing data, not as a present hypothesis.
