## 5. Characteristic-zero separator

```text
16 L^3 h15
  = 12 L^2 (A0 E1 + A1 E0)
    - 6 L (B1 A0^2 + 2 B0 A0 A1)
    + (5/2) L^2 (k1 A0^2 + 2 k0 A0 A1)
    - A0^3.
```

Every term except `-A0^3` is visibly divisible by `L`, so `Num15 ≡ -A0^3 (mod L)`. Owner-v2 reports this identity as a Singular reduction; it is an identity of polynomials and does not require an engine.

Source-row vanishing implies vanishing of the first seven `z`-Laurent coefficients of `h15`. By the degree count of §4, `L^3` divides `Num15` in `k(p,k0,...)[z]`. Reduction modulo `L` then gives `L | A0^3`.

On `D(p)`, `L = z^2 + p/2` is squarefree of degree two.

- If `L` is irreducible over the base, it is prime, so `L | A0`. Then `deg A0 <= 1 < 2` forces `A0=0`.
- If `L` splits, `L = (z-r)(z+r)` with `r^2 = -p/2 ≠ 0`, two distinct roots. Then `A0^3` vanishes at both, hence `A0` vanishes at both. A polynomial of degree at most one with two distinct roots is zero.

A candidate `A0` supported at only one root of `L` fails `L | A0^3` because the other linear factor is missing. Grade-fourteen root allocations and the tangent coefficients `A1,B0,B1,E0,E1,k1` contribute only `L`-divisible terms to `Num15` and cannot cancel `-A0^3` modulo `L`. Characteristic zero is used honestly: it inverts `2,3,5,16` in the binomial coefficients and in the clearing factor.
