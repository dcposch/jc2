## 1. An exact global functional, with no cohomology classification import

Work in R=C[x,t,Z]/(t^2-1-x^2 Z), P=t^2-1, with
omega=dx wedge dt/x^2 and {f,g}=x^2(f_x g_t-f_t g_x) on x!=0.
The two polynomial charts make omega globally regular and nonvanishing.
Write f=sum_m x^m p_m(t). Regularity at both boundaries requires
P^ceil(-m/2)|p_m for m<0; for m>=0 there is no divisibility condition.
Define the C-linear polynomial-antiderivative functional

    Lambda(f)=integral_-1^1 [x^1]f dt.

This is formal polynomial integration over C, not numerical integration.
Lambda(1)=0 and Lambda(x)=2. I claim only that Lambda annihilates exact
global two-forms divided by omega; no assertion about the entire H^2 is needed.

Indeed every global one-form is a sum f dx+g dt+h dZ, since the surface is
affine and its Kahler differentials are generated by dx,dt,dZ. The dense-chart
calculation for f=x^m p(t) gives

    d(f dx)/omega = -x^(m+2) p',
    d(f dt)/omega = m x^(m+1) p,
    d(f dZ)/omega = x^(m-1)(2m t p+2P p').

The weight-one contributions require respectively m=-1,0,2. In the first,
P|p so integral p'=p(1)-p(-1)=0; the second coefficient is zero; the third
coefficient is 2(Pp)', whose endpoint values vanish. This proves the claim.

If H,G are regular with {H,G}=c!=0, then for all a,b>=0,

    d(H^(a+1) G^b dG/(a+1))=c H^a G^b omega,
    hence Lambda(H^a G^b)=0.                         (1)

These are consequences of the FULL scalar identity, not additional
independent equations or an implication in the reverse direction.

## 2. Why the original construction target makes every moment automatic

The involution sigma(x,t,Z)=(-x,-t,Z) has invariant functions A=x^2,
U=xt,Z, satisfying U^2=A+A^2 Z. Its volume form is invariant, but

    Lambda(sigma*f)=-Lambda(f),

because the weight-one coefficient transforms to -p_1(-t). Every invariant
f therefore has Lambda(f)=0. In particular (1) holds for EVERY pair H,G in
C[A,U,Z]/(U^2-A-A^2 Z), independently of its Jacobian or algebraic dependence.
This is a new attachment of the already-known moment failure, not a new
general moment theorem. It gives no leverage on the original quotient target.

Moreover ker Lambda is proper and contains1. It cannot be a Mathieu subspace:
the defining property applied to all powers of1 would put every element,
including x, in the kernel. A shifted Laurent-coefficient functional is not
automatically a multiplicative transport of a Laurent constant-term theorem.
No Mathieu, image or SU(2) conjecture is imported as true here.

## 3. One finite control passes the combined proposed filters

Put w=t^2=1+AZ and R0=A+Z. Define actual invariant regular functions

    H=R0^2+U+w,
    G=R0^3+Z(5/2-2w)-UZ.                            (2)

Their Laurent blocks, wt(x)=1,wt(t)=0,wt(Z)=-2, are exactly

    H_4=x^4, H_1=xt, H_0=3w-2, H_-4=x^-4 P^2;
    G_6=x^6, G_2=3x^2 P, G_-1=-x^-1 tP,
    G_-2=x^-2 P(w-1/2), G_-6=x^-6 P^3.

The negative blocks obey the full regularity divisibilities. Their intervals
are [-4,4] and [-6,6], so both genuinely have positive and negative weights,
and weight1 occurs. The top blocks are powers x^4,x^6; the bottom blocks
are powers Z^2,Z^3. Thus both extremal brackets vanish, with ratio3/2 at
both ends. All mixed moments vanish by section2, without a finite-to-infinite
inference or power expansion.

For the ENTIRE weight-zero bracket coefficient only (m,n)=(1,-2),(0,-1)
occur. Using m p q'-n p' q, these contributions are respectively

    2w(2w-3/2)+2P(w-1/2)=1+6wP,
    (6t)(-tP)=-6wP.

Their sum is identically1 as a polynomial in t, not just at t=+/-1.
But weight8 has the unique contributing pair (m,n)=(1,6), giving

    [{H,G}]_8=-6x^8.                                (3)

So the bracket is nonzero and nonconstant. In particular H,G are
algebraically independent in characteristic zero, yet not a Keller pair.
At x=0,t=+/-1,Z=0 their bracket equals1. On Z=0, t=epsilon=+/-1, direct

## 4. Classical overlap, history and disposition

A one-sided coordinate is already excluded by the classical line theorem.
If H has only nonnegative weights, its plus-chart restriction to x=0 is
the constant p_0(1). If it has only nonpositive weights, its restriction
to y=0 is p_0(1): all negative-block polynomials vanish at t=1. In a
stipulated Keller pair, the Jacobian restricted to that coordinate line is
a product of its normal derivative and the mate's tangential derivative.
A nonzero constant product forces the latter to be a nonzero constant.
The pair is therefore injective on that line.

after a target automorphism, must retain both signs in each coordinate.
