## 1. Executive decision

The campaign should now spend less time proving another local emptiness and
more time transporting the strongest existing local certificate through an
honest source/receiver map.  V43C5 V2 provisionally gives the unusually clean
identity

```text
a1^628 = sum_(25 literal rows R_i) M_i R_i
          in Q[t,X19_total],       t = rho^2.
```

Because the coefficient is `1`, this has none of G4's `t^6` ambiguity.  If the
review passes, any *typed* ring map taking those 25 rows to a source or terminal
receiver ideal and taking `a1` to a unit immediately makes that receiver ideal
the unit ideal.  The smallest serious proof task is therefore a
certificate-sized commuting square, not a fresh whole-presentation
elimination.  This remains strictly weaker than global `G2-PSC`, `G2-BD`, a
landing theorem, or a degree ceiling, but it attacks the receiver wall with a
much smaller obligation.

In parallel, the Matysiak claim deserves rapid primary-text acquisition and a
dependency-first audit, but not a reallocation based on its abstract.  There
is a sharp preliminary distinction that can be settled without the paper.  If

```text
A = Q[u,v] -> B = Q[x,y],    u |-> P, v |-> Q,    J(P,Q) in Q^*,
```

then this map is étale.  Consequently, for every square-free `h in A`,
`B/hB` is étale over the reduced ring `A/hA`, hence is reduced.  Thus
`h(P,Q)` is square-free in `B`.  Any condition `5''s` meaning only
“square-free polynomials pull back to square-free polynomials” is automatic
from the Keller hypothesis and supplies no extra leverage by itself.  A useful
condition must instead control *factor descent*—for example, irreducible
factors of `h(P,Q)` lying in the image subring—or prove a noncircular passage
from reduced pullbacks to factorial closure, integrality, or degree one.

This is both the fastest audit test for the external claim and a new internal
avenue: the failure of factors to descend records splitting of pullback
divisors, hence sheet monodromy and the asymptotic/boundary data that the
campaign already computes.

### Card B — Reduced-divisor versus factor-descent audit

**Mechanism.**  Split the phrase “square-free condition” into three formally
distinct predicates for `A=Q[P,Q] subset B=Q[x,y]`:

1. `h` square-free in `A` implies `h(P,Q)` square-free in `B`;
2. every irreducible factor of `h(P,Q)` descends to `A`;
3. `A` is factorially closed/integrally decisive in `B`.

Predicate 1 follows automatically from étaleness as explained in Section 1.
Predicates 2 and 3 are much stronger and encode divisor splitting.  For an
irreducible target curve `h=0`, the factors of `h(P,Q)` are the affine
components of its pullback; their permutation is a sheet-monodromy datum and
their missing closures meet the asymptotic/boundary tree.  This connects the
external claim and row 31 to rows 7, 25, and 2.

**Dependencies.**  None for the étale lemma.  For adjudicating Matysiak,
lawfully acquired, hashed bytes of SSRN 7229358 first and 7229458 second.

**Cheapest test.**  Before reading conclusions, transcribe the exact `5''s`
definition and place it in predicates 1--3.  If it is predicate 1, check where
the proof imports extra information.  If it is predicate 2/3, isolate and
independently prove the first implication from the Keller hypothesis.  In
parallel, build a bounded symbolic control using generic target lines and the
first named boundary/asymptotic divisor, recording factor orbits rather than
only square-freeness.

**PASS.**  A nonautomatic, noncircular lemma from factor descent to degree one
would immediately become a top proof route.  **FAIL.**  An automatic-predicate
substitution, assumed image-UFD/normality, hidden properness, or circular
injectivity closes the advertised proof step without judging unrelated
lemmas.  **Stop.**  Stop at the first unrepaired implication; do not infer from
the abstract.  **Rollback.**  External failure does not remove the internal
factor-splitting invariant.  **Information gain:** extremely high and mostly
desk-scale.
