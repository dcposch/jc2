## Independent recomputation

### 1. Well-definedness — CONFIRMED

Unimodularity of the row `(P_x,P_y)` is exactly the existence of a polynomial vector field `V=A ∂_x+B ∂_y` with `V(P)=1`. If `V'` is a second such lift, `W=V'-V` satisfies `W(P)=0`, i.e. `P_x u+P_y v=0`. Over any commutative ring, a unimodular 2-row `(a,b)` with Bézout coefficients `A,B` (`aA+bB=1`) has syzygy module free on `(b,-a)`: the coefficient is `k=uB-vA`, and

```text
u = k b,     v = -k a.
```

Thus `W=h D_P` with `D_P=P_y ∂_x-P_x ∂_y`. Mixed partials give `div(D_P)=P_{yx}-P_{xy}=0`, and the product rule is `div(h D_P)=D_P(h)`. Therefore

```text
div(V')-div(V) ∈ D_P(R).
```

The images of `D_P` and `X_P` coincide because they are negatives, so the additive quotient is independent of that sign. It is a `C[P]`-module because `D_P(P h)=P D_P(h)` (`D_P(P)=0`). It is not an ideal quotient.

The second engine checked `div(D_P)=0`, `D_P=-X_P` as operators, `div(h D_P)=D_P(h)`, and the reconstruction `k=uB-vA` on the family Bézout lifts plus added syzygies.

Hypothesis that is actually used: `(P_x,P_y)=R`. Without it there is no lift `V(P)=1`, and the Koszul syzygy need not generate. Characteristic zero is not required for this claim.

### 2. Mate equivalence, including signs — CONFIRMED

**Forward.** If `[P,Q]=1`, set `V_Q=Q_y ∂_x-Q_x ∂_y`. Then

```text
V_Q(P)=Q_y P_x-Q_x P_y=[P,Q]=1,
div(V_Q)=Q_{yx}-Q_{xy}=0,
```

so `kappa(P)=0`. Independently verified on all four displayed controls and on `(y,-x)`.

**Converse, correction sign.** If `kappa(P)=0`, write `div(V)=D_P(h)` and replace `V` by `V-h D_P`. The new field still sends `P` to 1 because `D_P(P)=0`, and

```text
div(V-h D_P)=div(V)-D_P(h)=0.
```

Section 4.3 of the gate equivalently *adds* `h D_P` and solves `X_P(h)=div(V_0)`. These differ by `h ↦ -h`. They cut out the same condition `div(V)∈ im D_P=im X_P`. Not a sign error.

**One-form and Poincaré.** For `V=A ∂_x+B ∂_y` and `Ω=dx∧dy`,

```text
α = i_V Ω = A dy - B dx.
```

Then `dα=(A_x+B_y)Ω=div(V)Ω`. If `div(V)=0`, `α` is closed. Over a field of characteristic zero the polynomial Poincaré lemma supplies a polynomial primitive

```text
Q(x,y)=∫_0^x (-B)(t,y) dt + ∫_0^y A(0,s) ds,
```

so `A=Q_y`, `B=-Q_x`, and `V(P)=[P,Q]=1`. The second engine recovered every displayed mate from its `V_Q` by this homotopy (up to a constant, which does not change the Jacobian). Characteristic zero is required here: the homotopy divides by integer exponents. The gate works over `C`.

Hence, exactly, `kappa(P)=0` iff `P` has a polynomial Jacobian mate. Universal nonvanishing of `kappa` on noncoordinates is JC2 in equivalent form, as the gate states.

### 5. Family `P_n=x+x^n y` — CONFIRMED

Fix an integer `n>=2` and `Z=x^{n-1}y`. Then `P_x=1+nZ`, `P_y=x^n`, and

```text
(1-nZ)(1+nZ) + n^2 x^{n-2} y^2 · x^n = 1 - n^2 Z^2 + n^2 Z^2 = 1.
```

The exponent `n-2` is nonnegative, so the Bézout field

```text
V_0=(1-nZ)∂_x + n^2 x^{n-2} y^2 ∂_y
```

is polynomial. Direct differentiation:

```text
div(V_0)=-n(n-1)x^{n-2}y + 2 n^2 x^{n-2}y = n(n+1)x^{n-2}y.
```

Checked for every `n=2..12`. Also `div(D_{P_n})=0`. The zero fibre is reducible (`x(1+Z)=0`), so `P_n` is not a coordinate. The no-mate statement below does not use JC2.

### 6. Weight exhaustiveness and the general-`n` obstruction — CONFIRMED

Weight `w_n(x^i y^j)=i-(n-1)j`. For every monomial,

```text
X_P(x^i y^j)= j x^i y^{j-1} + (n j-i) x^{i+n-1} y^j.
```

Both output monomials have weight `w_n(i,j)+(n-1)`. Verified on a `12×12` grid for `n=2..10`, including the axes `j=0` and `i=0`. The operator is homogeneous of shift `n-1` for this weight, so only input weight `1-n` can produce the target `1` (weight 0). Other weight components of a putative polynomial `Q` map to other output weights and cannot cancel a weight-0 terminal. Kernel elements (`X_P` annihilates polynomials in `P`; `w_n(P)=1`) live in nonnegative weights, not in weight `1-n`.

Nonnegative integer solutions of `i-(n-1)j=1-n`:

```text
i=(n-1)j+1-n=(n-1)(j-1).
```

For `n>=2`, `n-1>=1`, so `i>=0` forces `j>=1`. Setting `k=j-1>=0` gives exactly

```text
M_k=x^{k(n-1)} y^{k+1},    k>=0.
```

This is a complete parametrization of the semigroup, not a bound-40 scan. A rectangle `0<=i,j<=199` contained no extras for `n=2..10`. Directly,

```text
X_P(M_k)=(k+1) Z^k + (n+k) Z^{k+1}.
```

A polynomial slice can use only a finite initial segment `sum_{k=0}^N c_k M_k`. Coefficient comparison with `X_P(Q)=1` forces

```text
c_0=1,
c_m=-(n+m-1)/(m+1) c_{m-1}=(-1)^m (n)_m/(m+1)!
   =(-1)^m binom(n+m-1,m)/(m+1).
```

Every coefficient is nonzero in characteristic zero. The truncation ends with the uncancelled monomial `c_N(n+N) Z^{N+1}≠0`. Contradiction for every finite `N` and every integer `n>=2`.

The same arithmetic with input weight `-n` solves `X_P(h)=div(V_0)` along `N_k=x^{(n-1)k+n-2} y^{k+2}`, with `b_k=(-1)^k binom(n+k+1,k+2)` and terminal `b_N(n+N+2)≠0`. Equivalent obstruction, second gauge.

Displayed `n=3` coefficients `1,-3/2,2,-5/2,3,-7/2` and kappa coefficients `6,-10,15,-21,28,-36` match both closed forms. For `n=2` the direct coefficients are `1,-1,1,-1,...`, the Broughton no-polynomial-slice control in this gauge.

The replay’s bound-40 equality of enumerated and parametrized sets is a software sanity check. The proof is the Diophantine lemma above. Finite truncation checks through cap 12 (direct) and cap 10 (kappa) for `n=2..10` all held, with every terminal nonzero.

### 7. Rational / formal slice — CONFIRMED

The generating function of `c_k M_k` is

```text
Q_∞ = y (1-(1+Z)^{1-n}) / ((n-1) Z).
```

The binomial expansion of this rational function reproduces `c_m` exactly (checked `n=2..7`, order 10). Clearing the denominator as `Q=N/D` with

```text
N=y((1+Z)^{n-1}-1),     D=(n-1) Z (1+Z)^{n-1}
```

reduces `[P,Q]=1` to the polynomial identity `X_P(N)D-N X_P(D)=D^2`, which held for every tested `n` including 12 and 15. At `n=2` this is `y/(1+xy)`, and the bracket is elementary:

```text
(1+2xy)/(1+xy)^2 + x^2 y^2/(1+xy)^2 = 1.
```

Nonpolynomial: `(1+Z)^{n-1}-1` is divisible by `Z` once and not by `1+Z` (it equals `-1` at `Z=-1`). In the quotient `C[x,y]/(1+x^{n-1}y)`, which is an integral domain, the class of `N` is `-y≠0` (`y` is not in the principal ideal: comparing `y`-degree). The class of `D` is 0. So `D` does not divide `N` in `C[x,y]`. After cancelling the simple factor `Z`, the denominator still contains `(1+Z)^{n-1}` with `n-1>=1`, and the remaining numerator is nonzero along `Z=-1`. Hence `Q_∞` is rational, not polynomial. It is a formal/rational control for the obstruction, not a Keller survivor.
