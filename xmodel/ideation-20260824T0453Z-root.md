### Card A — `DIFF-LATTICE-UNIT`: stable differential lattice versus boundary units

- **Target claim (proposed theorem).** Let `A=C[u,v]`, let `L/Frac(A)` be a
  finite separable extension, and let `Cbar` be the normalization of `A` in
  `L`. Suppose `B` is a finitely generated normal factorial `C`-subalgebra of
  `L`, `Frac(B)=L`, `B^*=C^*`, `Spec B -> Spec Cbar` is the Zariski-Main open
  immersion, and both extensions of `partial_u,partial_v` preserve `B` and
  form a nowhere-degenerate frame there. Then the boundary is empty. The
  Keller specialization would make `B` finite over `A`, hence finite etale
  and therefore an automorphism.
- **Avenues:** 16, 29, 31, with 7 and 33 as boundary receivers.
- **Novelty / connection.** This joins the hitherto vague D-module lane to the
  exact normalization/open-immersion object and the elementary but unused
  facts `Cl(B)=0`, `B^*=C^*`. It avoids the refuted premise that the local
  different determines coordinate moments.
- **Evidence used.** Exact characteristic-zero Keller identities and standard
  finite normalization/Zariski-Main structure; the confirmed
  `DIFFERENT-INSUFFICIENT` result is a design constraint, not a premise.
- **First proof program.** Choose a finite `Cbar`-lattice spanning `L`, take
  its saturation under the two commuting derivations inside `B`, and track
  pole-order vectors along every boundary divisor. On the determinant line,
  flatness makes the polar divisor closed under both coordinate derivations.
  Prove the dichotomy: pole orders stabilize, giving a coherent integrable
  `A`-lattice and hence finiteness; or an extremal pole vector yields a
  boundary-supported principal divisor whose restriction is a nonconstant
  unit of `B`.
- **Cheapest discriminator.** Prove the statement first in dimension one and
  for toroidal/SNC boundary charts, with explicit negative control
  `A=C[t] subset C[s,s^{-1}]`, `t=s^e`. That control is derivation-stable but
  has the forbidden unit `s`. Then test all two-boundary toric cones and the
  tame automorphism suite symbolically. The exact gate is whether factoriality
  plus constant units closes the determinant-line dichotomy.
- **Outcomes.** A proof on arbitrary SNC charts gives a candidate global lemma
  for hostile review. A normal factorial constant-unit countermodel kills the
  theorem at its smallest missing hypothesis and is itself a blueprint for a
  Keller counterexample surface. A need for target-divisor pairing hands a
  precise field to the global receiver, rather than restarting local moments.
- **Cost / stop / information gain.** Six to twelve reasoning hours for the
  local theorem and countermodel census; stop on the first constant-unit
  countermodel or on an unbounded lattice invariant with no canonical
  determinant. Expected information gain: very high, because either branch
  resolves whether the canonical normalization can carry a genuinely new
  global obstruction. Resurrection: a theorem controlling boundary class
  groups, regular singularities, or affine extensions of `A^2`.
- **Strongest hostile attack.** `B^*=C^*` may prevent only a boundary divisor
  from being principal, while the determinant recurrence may give merely a
  nontrivial class in `Cl(Cbar)`; factoriality of `B`, not `Cbar`, may then say
  nothing. The one-dimensional proof may fail exactly because surfaces allow
  several boundary components whose classes cancel without producing a unit.
