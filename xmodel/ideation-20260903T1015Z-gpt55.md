## 3. Q3 -- Reframing The All-Degree Program

Disposition over the candidates:

* Q3(a), D-module / Picard-Fuchs / differential Galois: raise and retype. The
  right invariant is not "rank grows, therefore degree bounded" in the abstract.
  It is the obstruction to representing the Gelfand-Leray form
  `dx wedge dy / dg` by a polynomial primitive of `y`-degree `< m`. Equivalently,
  the global Lagrange interpolant of `integral dx/g_y` must lie in `C[x,y]` with
  `deg_y < m`. The cofinal quantity to test is the irregular part at infinity
  of this connection restricted to the `(1)-(13)` tower. If the irregularity
  supplies at least `n-m` independent high-coefficient conditions per order
  after quotienting Galois orbits, this becomes a proof engine.
* Q3(b), lattice plus bottom dessin tower: raise as a coupling receiver, not as
  the main proof. `ORTHO-DEFECT` says `N` is a squared non-proportionality
  defect; `STAR-ABC` says each bottom defect is a rigid Belyi/Davenport-Stothers
  star. The new target is a "dessin tower" representation: a Galois orbit of
  identical `(2,3,V_2)` or general `(d,e,V_2)` stars glued through the same
  higher Puiseux tower. This can reduce the global interpolation matrix by
  characters and expose missing conditions. I do not expect it alone to bound
  `D`, because the proportional chain still stores unpriced length.
* Q3(c), characteristic `p` / Cartier: retype as a diagnostic and a possible

  lifting has already warned that finite-field passes do not lift to polynomial
  characteristic-zero maps. Use Cartier to produce rank certificates for the
  interpolation matrix modulo good primes, not as an independent proof slogan.
* Q3(d), semigroup at infinity / approximate roots: raise highest. This is the
  best candidate for `OPEN[MOH-PROGRAM]` (bounded 652 excess rows at `n<=100`).

  approximate roots, minor-disc transforms, and polynomial cancellation. The

  order mod-`p` or Newton lift on group A at `N=9..12` would be the first real

  datum not stored in the skeleton: global interpolation, approximate-root
  polynomiality, periods, or source/target realisation.

New avenue: monomial-Jacobian approximate-root compilation. Instead of trying
to solve `[f,g]=1` in raw coefficients, transform the skeleton to Moh's

New cross-connection: the same matrix should serve three roles that are

polynomiality conditions on a Puiseux/approximate-root expansion.

## 6. Bottlenecks And Attacks

Proof bottlenecks, reranked:

1. Uniform realisability obstruction for `(1)-(13)` skeletons with integral
   pinned `N >= 6`. This is the main theorem slot. It must consume global
   interpolation or approximate-root polynomiality; the boundary has already
   spent its value.
2. Recovery of Moh's missing bounded program. The bounded target is 652 excess
   rows at `n <= 100`; a second reader must distinguish "forgotten printed
   theorem condition" from "Appendix-II normal-form computation".
3. Branch-orbit/global coupling. `(UNI)` is not a theorem, but the global
   interpolant decomposes under the same Galois orbit data. Orbit characters
   are compression, not a kill by themselves.
4. A cofinal analytic invariant: Picard-Fuchs irregularity, Brieskorn module
   regularity, or a period-rank statement bounding the possible polynomial
   primitive. This is the only place I see a legitimate all-degree ceiling-like
   theorem.
5. Reducible/nonproper block. Delta (g) says reducibility lives where `N`
   contributes zero. A proof still needs a floor or obstruction on the
   nonproper block's horizontal degree.

Disproof bottlenecks, reranked:

1. A high-order formal solution of a real `(1)-(13)` survivor after global
   interpolation, preferably `D=105` group A at `N=9..12` or a nearby `D=108`
   high-freedom row.
2. Galois-consistent gluing of bottom dessins through the higher Puiseux tower.
   A single bottom Belyi star is cheap; a tower of conjugate stars is the first
   meaningful construction.
3. Modular lifting of the exact interpolation system. Work modulo good primes
   only after the characteristic-zero ring map is printed; use it to find
   rank dependencies or p-adic formal branches.
4. Raw coefficient search. It is now strictly dominated by the skeleton-guided
   interpolation/approximate-root normal form.

Strongest proof attack: prove that for every `(1)-(13)` skeleton with integral
`N >= 6`, the global interpolation matrix has a nonzero cokernel obstruction at
some bounded order depending only on the skeleton's divisor chain, and then
show this order is controlled uniformly by the approximate-root semigroup. This
would generalize Moh Appendix II from four degree pairs to all degrees. The
first nontrivial claim should be machine-checked on all 658 `n <= 100` rows and
reviewed against the six printed rows.

Strongest counterexample attack: do not try to build a polynomial map first.
Build a Galois-equivariant formal solution of the global interpolation equations
to high order on a survivor where the first-order count is not overdetermined:
`D=105`, group A, `N=12`, `k=24`. If it survives exact rank tests and a mod-`p`
Newton lift to, say, 40 declared exponents while the same engine kills Moh's
Appendix row, it becomes the first credible positive signal above `D=100`.

Decisive experiment: run the first global interpolation matrix for `D=105`
group C (`M=[40,103], V_3=4, N=9, k=17`). It is small and almost square:
`34` bottom-kernel unknowns versus `35` high-y equations before shared-variable
corrections. A full-rank result kills the group at the first global order. A
rank dependency identifies the character/isotypic component that all later
software should preserve.
