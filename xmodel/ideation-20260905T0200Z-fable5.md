### 1.2 What structurally forces it — the honest answer, and the new lever

Nothing LOCAL forces it (Astra: formal solutions at x = 0 with Bη ≠ 0 exist for every t and merely fail
to truncate). Nothing at the TOP forces it (the leading balance is an identity on the ray). Nothing
DEGREE-ONLY forces it (17(ppppp) DEGREE-BLIND). So the forcing must be a GLOBAL identity among the
2t−1 generators. Two observations narrow what that identity can look like.

(a) **It is an exact square, not a radical, at every banked index.** Astra records the direct exact
exponent 2 at t = 3, 4 (τ² ∈ I_+, τ ∉ I_+). If τ_t² ∈ I_{t,+} for all t, the statement is a
Nullstellensatz CERTIFICATE τ_t² = Σ_k a_k T_{t,k} with a_k weighted-homogeneous of weight
2(4t+1) − wt(T_{t,k}). Certificates of this kind on the ray have so far always had closed forms in t
(the spine substitutions, RESIDUAL-ZERO, TOP-TAIL-UNIT). The mechanism I propose is therefore
"prove the SQUARE identity uniformly from its cofactor structure", and the first thing to learn is
the SUPPORT of the cofactors: if only the tail rows T_{t,t..2t−1} appear, the identity lives in the
tail, whose structure is already uniform (TOP-TAIL-UNIT); if the low rows appear, the identity is a
genuinely full-ideal statement and the tail route is dead. Either outcome is decisive for how Astra's
lane should spend its time.

(b) **The geometric meaning fixes what τ ≠ 0 would be.** From the banked identity
J_{γ,π}(Q,P) = −cz + τπ (z = π − γ, c = −yg): a cone point is a polynomial pair whose Jacobian is a
NONZERO LINEAR FORM, and (8.1) says that form is rigidly proportional to z. Moh's Prop 6.3(3), read
from the page image (p.197), says the descent of a Keller pair has Jacobian EXACTLY −(u_s/b)·γ^ℓ in
the descent variable γ (the ray is u_s = 1, d_s = 4, v_s = 3, so ℓ = 1 — a linear form on the nose).
So a τ ≠ 0 point is a "tilted-critical-line" pair: same shape, same Newton polygon, Jacobian a
linear form whose zero line is not the descent axis. This is the k = 1 monomial-Jacobian receiver of
row 1 with a rotated critical line. It gives a second, structural route: show that for pairs of the
ray's Newton shape (monic in π, deg_γ[π^k] ≤ 3k), a linear Jacobian must vanish on the γ-axis. I do
not have that theorem; §6 Card A gives the cheapest test of whether it is even true at the one place
where an explicit family exists (t = 2, y = 1/5).
