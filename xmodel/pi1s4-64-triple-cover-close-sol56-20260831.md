## 0. Scope, frozen inputs, and verdict

Before any input was used or report file was written, the three frozen-input
SHA-256 values were checked and matched; the first report write was the
required header skeleton.  Exact receipts are recorded at the end.  All statements
about the `(6,4)` row are conditional on the provisional row geometry and
routing in the coordinator input.  The outcome is asymmetric: the
row-specific cancellation lemma is proved, but freeness does not imply
global monogenicity and the row-specific monogenicity question remains open.
Thus the result below is a conditional monogenic-resolvent no-go, not a
uniform kill of the row.

## 1. What global monogenicity actually asks

Put `R=C[x,y]` and let `B=pi_*O_X` be the finite-flat cubic algebra proved in
the charged r2 report.  Since `3` is invertible,
`B=R direct-sum E`, where `E=ker(Tr)`; Quillen--Suslin makes `E` free of
rank two.  Choose a trace-zero basis `z,w`.  In Miranda's notation the four
multiplication coefficients give the binary cubic (the index form)

```text
I(S,T)=B_0 S^3-3A_0 S^2T+3D_0 ST^2-C_0 T^3.          (1.1)
```

For `theta=sz+tw`, direct expansion in Miranda's multiplication table gives

```text
det_R(1,theta,theta^2)=I(s,t).                         (1.2)
```

Any generator can first be translated by one third of its trace; this does
not change the generated algebra and puts it in `E`.

Consequently the exact criterion is

```text
B is generated by one element over R
  iff I(s,t) is in R^*=C^* for some s,t in R.          (1.3)
```

Indeed, a unit in (1.2) says that `1,theta,theta^2` is an `R`-basis.  It also
forces `(s,t)` to be a unimodular column, so it extends to a matrix in
`GL_2(R)`; in that basis the corresponding coefficient of `S^3` is a unit.
Conversely a unit `S^3` coefficient gives a generator.  Translating a
generator by one third of its trace then gives a monic depressed equation
`Z^3+aZ+b`; its polynomial discriminant is the algebra discriminant (up to a
constant square), hence

```text
4a^3+27b^2=kappa F,  kappa in C^*.                    (1.4)
```

This corrects the proposed “global linear factor” criterion.  A unimodular
linear factor of `I` cuts out a section *inside* the cubic divisor of bad
generators in `P(E)`; (1.3) asks for a section avoiding that divisor and
taking a unit value.  These are different conditions, and factorization
cannot replace (1.3).  In fact, if the generic algebra is a cubic
field, `I` has no linear factor over `Frac(R)`: such a factor gives a nonzero
trace-zero nongenerator, whereas every nonzero trace-zero element of a
degree-three field generates it.  The algebra may nevertheless be
monogenic.

The discriminant and Hessian do not repair this.  They are covariants of the
`GL_2(R)` action, not a classification of its integral orbits, and neither
solves the nonlinear equation `I(s,t) in C^*`.  Freeness of `E` merely makes
that equation global.  Thus the correct Miranda residual is a unit-
representation problem, not a projective-module or factorization problem.

Primary source: Miranda, *Triple Covers in Algebraic Geometry*, Theorem
2.7.1 and Remark 2.8.1 (multiplication and a selected generator), Proposition
3.3 and display (3.4) (binary cubic), Theorem 3.6/Definition 3.7 (global
classification), and Lemma 4.5/Proposition 4.7 (branch discriminant).  This is
exactly the distinction already isolated at charged r2 lines 86--139.

## 2. A genuine UFD obstruction, and its exact scope

The missing implication is false even after adding most of the geometric
features present here.  Over `R=C[x,y]`, take

```text
I(S,T)=xS^3-3S^2T+3ST^2+y^2T^3.                       (2.1)
```

This is Miranda data `(A_0,B_0,C_0,D_0)=(1,x,-y^2,1)` and hence, by Miranda
Theorem 3.6(d), defines the associative free algebra
`R direct-sum Rz direct-sum Rw` with
`z^2=2(1-x)+z+xw`, `zw=-(1+xy^2)-z-w`, and
`w^2=2(1+y^2)-y^2z+w`.  Its Miranda branch polynomial is

```text
G=x^2y^4+6xy^2+4x-4y^2-3
 =(1+xy^2)^2-4(1-x)(1+y^2).                           (2.2)
```

In the displayed basis, `Disc(I)=disc(B/R)=-27G`.

It is irreducible.  With `Y=y^2`, (2.2) is the quadratic
`x^2Y^2+(6x-4)Y+4x-3`, whose discriminant over `C(x)` is
`16(1-x)^3`, not a square.  Substitution `Y=y^2` cannot introduce a
factor: comparison of two quadratic factors of an even quartic says that
either this quadratic already splits or `(4x-3)/x^2` is a square in
`C(x)`; neither is true.  Thus `G` is a reduced irreducible sextic.

The algebra is connected and generically `S_3`.  Indeed Miranda's selected
generator has polynomial
`Z^3+3(x-1)Z+(3x-2+x^2y^2)`.  It is irreducible: over `C(x,Z)`, viewing it
as a quadratic in `y` would require the negative of
`Z^3+3(x-1)Z+3x-2` to be a square, impossible by its odd `Z`-degree.
It is therefore irreducible in `C(x)[y,Z]`; primitivity in `Z` and Gauss's
lemma give irreducibility in `C(x,y)[Z]`.
The selected polynomial has discriminant `-27x^2G`, whose square class is
`G`, hence is not a square.  Since the free algebra injects into its generic
fiber, that generic field also makes it a domain.  It is normal: the algebra is
free over the regular ring `R`, hence `S_2`; at a height-one prime away
from `G` it is etale, while at the prime `(G)` its discriminant valuation
is one.  The index-square formula over that DVR makes the order maximal,
so it is `R_1`.

Nevertheless it is not globally monogenic.  Give `x,y` weights `2,1`.
The cases `s=0` or `t=0` are immediate.  Otherwise let `p,q` be their weighted degrees and `s_p,t_q` their
top parts.  If `p>q`, the unique top term of `I(s,t)` is `xs_p^3`; if
`q>p`, it is `y^2t_q^3`.  If `p=q`, the top part is

```text
xs_p^3+y^2t_p^3,
```

which cannot vanish because `-y^2/x` has `x`-valuation `-1` and so is not
a cube in `C(x,y)`.  Hence `I(s,t)` is never a nonzero constant.  This is
a genuinely global failure: (2.1) is primitive at every maximal ideal
(the middle coefficients are nonzero constants), and over the infinite
residue field one can choose a residue vector on which it is nonzero;
the algebra is therefore locally monogenic everywhere.

This example disproves any theorem deduced only from “UFD, all projectives
free, locally monogenic, connected normal cubic algebra, irreducible reduced sextic
discriminant, generic group `S_3`.”  It is not a counterexample to the
charged row: the leading form of (2.2) is `x^2y^4`, so its projective
closure has both `[1:0:0]` and `[0:1:0]` at infinity, whereas the row has
one place at `[1:0:0]` with the specified `(2,15)` germ.  Thus the exact
row-specific unit-representation question remains open; the proposed
general UFD classification cannot settle it.

## 3. Why the four-coefficient bypass does not close

For reference, the normalized Hessian of (1.1) is

```text
H_I=(A_0^2-B_0D_0)S^2-(A_0D_0-B_0C_0)ST
       +(D_0^2-A_0C_0)T^2,                            (3.1)
```

and `disc(H_I)` is the Miranda branch polynomial (up to the fixed numerical
normalization); equivalently `disc(I)=-27 disc(H_I)`.  This records the same
invariant after a twisted `GL_2(R)` action.  It does not decide whether the
cubic represents a unit: example (2.1) has irreducible reduced branch but no
unit value.

There is also an exact reason that selecting an arbitrary element cannot
bypass (1.3).  For `theta=sz+tw`,

```text
disc(charpoly(theta))=I(s,t)^2 disc(B/R).              (3.2)
```

If `I(s,t)` is nonzero, translating `theta` makes the left side
`-(4A^3+27B^2)` (up to the harmless naming/sign convention), and (3.2)
makes it `kappa F I(s,t)^2`.  In the nonmonogenic case the index square is
nonunit.  It creates extra apparent discriminant components and changes both
the Newton polygon at infinity and the Bezout count used in charged r2
sections 4 and 6.  Removing it without proof would simply assume the desired
unit value.  If `I(s,t)=0`, then `theta` is not even a generic generator.

Nor can one run those sections on the five-term four-coefficient
discriminant directly.  The four coefficients have no degree bounds after a
trivialization of `E`, and

```text
B_0^2C_0^2-3A_0^2D_0^2+4A_0^3C_0+4B_0D_0^3
 -6A_0B_0C_0D_0=kappa F                              (3.3)
```

allows cancellations among all five terms.  Local depression over completed
local rings is available and is already reflected in the smooth-branch and
node models of charged r2 section 1, but those local basis changes need not
patch to `GL_2(R)`.  At a node they reproduce the allowed
`Rhat direct-sum Rhat[t]/(t^2-uv)` model rather than a contradiction.  At
infinity, without coefficient pole bounds, (3.3) merely pulls back the
discriminant hypersurface to the prescribed `(2,15)` germ; the four-case
bounded-degree exhaustion in r2 section 4 no longer applies.

Thus a direct Miranda-jet proof would be a genuinely new lemma, not a
rephrasing of the completed depressed calculation.  No such exhaustion is
proved here.  The safe residual is the twisted `GL_2(R)` orbit of (1.1) with
branch `kappa F` and no unit value, subject to the connected, normal and
specified local-monodromy conditions.

## 4. What Davenport--Mason actually implies

Assume now, and only in this section onward, that a global power basis has
been supplied.  Rescale by nonzero complex constants and write its exact
branch identity as

```text
A^3-B^2=F,       deg F=6,                              (4.1)
```

where `F` is the irreducible row equation.  If the leading terms do not
cancel, (4.1) immediately gives `deg A<=2`, `deg B<=3`.  If either bound
fails, equality of the two top degrees is necessary:

```text
deg A=2k,  deg B=3k,  k>=2.                            (4.2)
```

For pairwise coprime one-variable polynomials `P,Q,H` with `P^3-Q^2=H`, the
Mason--Stothers theorem gives

```text
6k <= deg rad(PQH)-1 <= 2k+3k+deg H-1.                (4.3)
```

Equivalently Davenport's inequality is
`deg(P^3-Q^2)>=k+1`.  With `deg H=6`, (4.3) yields only `k<=5`.
Therefore the exact numerical alternatives before using row geometry are

```text
(deg A,deg B)=(4,6),(6,9),(8,12),(10,15).             (4.4)
```

This corrects the dichotomy in the charge.  Only `(10,15)` is a
Davenport-extremal (DS order-five) case, since only there `deg H=k+1`.
The first three pairs are the remaining nonextremal numerical bands and are
not classified away by the Stothers--Zannier equality families.  Shioda's
displayed order-five identity proves sharpness: its degree-six remainder is
squarefree; pulled back to `C[x,y]` through a linear form, its zero set is a
union of parallel lines and hence is not the row.

For completeness, (4.3) is the direct polynomial-abc calculation applied
to `P^3+(-Q^2)+(-H)=0`: pairwise coprimality makes the radical degree at
most `2k+3k+6`, while the largest summand has degree `6k`.  Thus no
classification assertion is hidden in the inequality.  Equality at `k=5`
forces all three radicals to have their maximal degrees and gives the
three-critical-value/Belyi description used by Stothers and Zannier, but a
normalization of such a one-variable triple is allowed to vary with the
chosen line.  Zannier's Proposition 2 encodes the equality cases by weighted
trees (its order-five count is `D_0=4`), rather than by a single
“Chebyshev-like” family.  Promoting any of those linewise normalizations to a
fixed two-variable family would be invalid.

## 5. Generic lines and the row-specific cancellation lemma

The specialization used in section 4 is uniform on a nonempty Zariski-open
set of affine lines.  The coefficient of the top power of the line parameter
is the leading homogeneous form evaluated at its direction, so avoiding the
three corresponding zero sets preserves the degrees of `A,B,F`.  A further
open condition makes the line transverse to the reduced sextic and avoids
its three nodes, hence `F|_ell` has six simple roots.

There is no hidden gcd failure.  If `A(p)=B(p)=0`, then `F(p)=0` and `p` is
singular.  The quadratic tangent cone of `A^3-B^2` is the square of the
linear part of `B` when that part is nonzero; if it vanishes, the
multiplicity is at least three.  Such a point is never an ordinary node.
The row has no other affine singularities, so `V(A,B)` is empty.  Therefore
the restrictions, and each of them with `F|_ell`, are pairwise coprime.
This proves all hypotheses of (4.3) on one common open set and makes the
degree-semicontinuity step airtight.

The row geometry now eliminates all four bands in (4.4).  Let
`C=V(F)`.  The images of `A,B` in `O(C)` have no zero, hence are units.  On
`C`, `B^2=A^3`, so

```text
t=B/A,       t^2=A,       t^3=B.                      (5.1)
```

The projective normalization of `C` has exactly one point over infinity.
The divisor of the pulled-back unit `t` is supported at that one point and
has degree zero, so it is zero; a nowhere-zero regular function on the
projective normalization is constant.  Thus `t=c in C^*`, and for some
`P,Q in R`,

```text
A=c^2+FP,          B=c^3+FQ.                          (5.2)
```

In particular `F` divides the Jacobian `J(A,B)`.

Suppose (4.2) holds.  Introduce the polynomial one-form

```text
omega=2A dB-3B dA.                                   (5.3)
```

Differentiating (4.1) and using `B^2=A^3-F` gives two exact identities:

```text
B omega=3F dA-A dF,        d omega=5 dA wedge dB.     (5.4)
```

The coefficients on the right of the first identity have degree at most
`2k+5`.  Hence, if `omega` is nonzero, its coefficients have degree at most
`5-k`.  For `k<=4` the second identity gives
`deg J(A,B)<=4-k<6`; for `k=5` the coefficients of `omega` are constant, so
the Jacobian is zero.  If `omega=0`, it is zero directly.  Since the
irreducible degree-six polynomial `F` divides this Jacobian, in every case

```text
J(A,B)=0.                                              (5.5)
```

In characteristic zero, (5.5) implies that `A,B` are algebraically dependent
by the criterion with Kähler differentials.  Use the polynomial Lüroth
lemma: two algebraically dependent polynomials in `C[x,y]` lie in `C[r]` for
a polynomial `r`.  Here is the needed short
justification.  Normalize `C[A,B]` in its fraction field.  A general line
in `P^2` maps nontrivially to the smooth projective model of that field, so
Riemann--Hurwitz makes the model `P^1`.  The normalization is therefore the
coordinate ring of `P^1-S`.  It lies in the integrally closed ring
`C[x,y]`: every element integral over `C[A,B]` is also integral over
`C[x,y]` and belongs to its fraction field.  Hence all units of the
normalization are constants.  The set `S` is nonempty because this is a
positive-dimensional affine curve (if `S` were empty its ring would be
`C`).  If `S` contained two points,
`P^1-S` would have a nonconstant unit with divisor supported on those two
points.  Hence `S` is one point and the normalization is `C[r]`, with
`r in C[x,y]`.  Thus
`A=U(r), B=V(r)` and `F=W(r)=U(r)^3-V(r)^2`.  Because `C` is algebraically
closed and `F` is irreducible, `W` must be linear; otherwise its linear
factors give a nontrivial factorization of `W(r)`.  Hence `r` is affine in
`F` and `A,B` belong to `C[F]`.  Every positive degree among them is
therefore divisible by six.  None of the four pairs in (4.4) has both
degrees divisible by six.  This contradiction proves the promised exact
lemma:

```text
If the depressed cubic has the row's irreducible one-place sextic branch
and only ordinary affine nodes, then deg A<=2 and deg B<=3.              (5.6)
```

In particular the set of Davenport-extremal families compatible with the
row is empty.  The argument treats the two-variable pair globally; it does
not identify line-dependent normalizations.

## 6. Assembly: the strongest theorem proved

**Theorem (monogenic-resolvent no-go, conditional on the provisional row
data).**  Let `D=V(F)` be an irreducible reduced affine sextic whose affine
singularities are exactly three ordinary nodes and whose projective
normalization has one point over infinity with the charged
`Delta=(6,4,3)` / `(2,15)` germ (`mult=2`, intersection six).  No surjection
`phi:pi_1(A^2-D)->S_4` that takes smooth meridians to transpositions and, at
each node, takes the two local branch meridians to commuting transpositions
can have a globally monogenic cubic resolvent for the quotient
`q:S_4->S_3`.

The charged r2 report proves, without monogenicity, that this quotient gives
a connected normal finite-flat triple cover and that
`disc(B)=kappa F` with multiplicity one (lines 35--84).  Under the one extra
hypothesis, section 1 gives (1.4), and the cancellation lemma (5.6) now
proves rather than assumes

```text
deg a<=2,       deg b<=3.                              (6.1)
```

The completed charged contradiction can therefore be consumed at its exact
scope.  Its leading-form classification (r2 lines 167--222) and infinity
calculation (lines 224--270) show that the possible orders are
`2,3,4,6`, whereas the row's one `(2,15)` place requires order `5`.
Independently, its affine-node/Bezout argument (lines 272--365) forces the
`Z`-coefficient `a` to be constant and the sextic to factor.  Either is a
contradiction.

There is no longer a cancellation residual: sections 4--5 include the three
nonextremal bands omitted by the proposed dichotomy and all order-five
Stothers--Zannier extremals.  The sole remaining logical question is

```text
OPEN[PI1S4-(6,4)-TRIPLE-COVER-GLOBAL-MONOGENICITY-ROW]:
does every hypothetical charged S4 representation have a resolvent index
form representing a unit; equivalently, can the exact no-unit S4-resolvent
class (7.1) be excluded?                                               (6.2)
```

Under the provisional charged row hypotheses, any surviving `S_4`
representation is now proved to have a nonmonogenic resolvent.  Example
(2.1) shows that this conclusion is not formally impossible, but it does not
realize the row.

This is not an unconditional uniform kill of `(6,4)`.  For `(8,4)`, the
coordinator lines 70--92 record only a provisional target-automorphism
equivalence to the `(6,4)` survivor.  If an actual target automorphism carries
both the curve and representation, it preserves the complement and
monogenicity, so only the conditional theorem above transfers.  Hostile-review
lines 280--285 show that the shear does not transport a fixed `B_6` braid
tuple to a fixed `B_8` tuple.  Lines 289--342 confirm the fold only after
rewriting it as pullback-surjectivity for the complement of a two-component
preimage, not as a row-transfer theorem.  Neither route gives an
unconditional `(8,4)` kill.

## 7. Exact residual and status

Let `C_F` denote the charged surjections `phi` in the theorem, and retain
`q:S_4->S_3`.  With the twisted action
`I -> det(g)^(-1) I((S,T)g)`, the exact unresolved class is

```text
R_{F,S4}^nm = { (phi,[I]_*) : phi in C_F,
                B_I is isomorphic to B_{q o phi},
                disc(I)=-27 kappa F for some kappa in C^*,
                B_I is a normal domain,
                I(R^2) intersect C^* is empty } .      (7.1)
```

Since every complex unit has a cube root, the last condition is equivalent
to the affine equation `I(S,T)=1` having no `R`-point.  The actual resolvent
of a hypothetical charged `phi` is either monogenic, in which case section 6
gives a contradiction, or supplies a pair in (7.1).  The compatibility
`B_I isomorphic to B_{q o phi}` is essential: ambient nonmonogenic Miranda
covers need not lift to charged `S_4` representations.  No source or argument
here proves that the row's three nodes and one infinity place make (7.1)
empty.
