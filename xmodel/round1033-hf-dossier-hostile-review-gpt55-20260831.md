## 1. Reduced sequence and cable genus

AG-S sets `d_1=r_0`, `d_{k+1}=gcd(d_k,r_k)`, and
`e_k=d_k/d_{k+1}`. For `r=(6,4,9)`:

```text
d_1=6, d_2=gcd(6,4)=2, d_3=gcd(2,9)=1
e_1=3, e_2=2.
```

The free conditions hold:

```text
e_1 r_1 = 12 = 2*6 in <6>
e_2 r_2 = 18 = 3*6 in <6,4>.
```

The AG-S strict conditions also hold:

```text
r_1 d_1 > r_2 d_2:  4*6=24 > 9*2=18
r_0 > r_1 > d_2 > d_3:  6 > 4 > 2 > 1.
```

Thus `(6,4,9)` is a valid AG-S delta-sequence in the reduced sense used in
their Section 4. One terminology caution: AG-S use the word "telescopic"
for a free arrangement with increasing generators `r_0<...<r_h`; the
charged report's "free/telescopic" wording should be read as the displayed
free plus reduced-delta checks, not as AG-S's literal telescopic label.

Proposition 2 gives

```text
F = (e_1-1)r_1 + (e_2-1)r_2 - r_0 = 2*4 + 1*9 - 6 = 11
C = F+1 = 12.
```

The numerical semigroup is symmetric, so its genus is `C/2=6`, matching
the conductor-12 census row. The cable dictionary in the pinned census reads
the row as `C_(2,9)(T(2,3))`; the genus identity is

```text
g(T(2,3)) = 1
g(C_(2,9)(K)) = 2 g(K) + (2-1)(9-1)/2 = 2*1+4 = 6.
```

## 2. Associated affine curve and singularity completeness

For the associated curve

```text
f=(y^3-x^2)^2-x^3,   x=(t^3-1)^2,   y=t^4-t,
```

put `u=t^3-1`. Then `x=u^2`, `y=tu`, and

```text
y^3-x^2 = t^3 u^3 - u^4 = (u+1)u^3-u^4 = u^3,
```

so `(y^3-x^2)^2=u^6=x^3`. The parametrization lies on `f=0`.

At every cube root `zeta^3=1`, `x(zeta)=y(zeta)=0`. Also

```text
x'(t)=6t^2(t^3-1),   y'(t)=4t^3-1,
```

so at the three roots `x'=0` and `y'=3`; the three local branches are
smooth and have the same tangent line `x=0`. There are no ramification
cusps anywhere in the affine chart, since `x'=y'=0` has no solution.

The charged proof stopped there, which is not enough: nodes and worse
multi-preimage singularities require checking `x(t)=x(s), y(t)=y(s)`.
That check is exact. Let `u=t^3-1`, `v=s^3-1`. If two parameters have the
same image then `u^2=v^2`.

If `u=v` and `u!=0`, then `tu=su`, so `t=s`. If `u=-v` and `u!=0`, then
`tu=sv=-su`, hence `s=-t`; but then `v=s^3-1=-t^3-1` and `-u=1-t^3`,
forcing `-t^3-1=1-t^3`, impossible. If `u=0`, then also `v=0`, and both
parameters are cube roots of unity mapping to the origin. Thus the only
non-injective affine fibre is the triple fibre over `(0,0)`.

The local delta at the origin is also exact. On the branch with parameter
near `zeta`, `y` is a local coordinate and

```text
x = u^2 = y^2/t^2 = zeta^{-2} y^2 + O(y^3) = zeta y^2 + O(y^3).
```

For distinct cube roots the `y^2` coefficients are distinct, so every pair
of branches has intersection multiplicity exactly `2`. Three smooth branches
therefore give

```text
delta_0 = 2+2+2 = 6.
```

This closes the report's weakest step: its derivative-only proof was
incomplete, but the conclusion "one affine singular point, a three-smooth-
branch common-tangent point with local delta 6" is correct.

## 3. Infinity analysis

Homogenising the associated equation to degree 6 gives

```text
F=Y^6-2X^2Y^3Z+X^4Z^2-X^3Z^3.
```

At `Z=0`, `Y=0`, so the unique point at infinity is `[1:0:0]`. In the
chart `X=1`,

```text
g(Y,Z)=Y^6-2Y^3Z+Z^2-Z^3=(Y^3-Z)^2-Z^3.
```

For this associated curve the Puiseux calculation is valid as written:

```text
(Y^3-Z)^2=Z^3,
Y=t^2,  Z=t^6+alpha t^9+...,  alpha^2=1.
```

The two signs of `alpha` are conjugate under `t -> -t`, hence this is one
branch. Its value semigroup is generated by `v(Y)=2` and
`v(Z-Y^3)=9`, so

```text
S_infty=<2,9>,   gaps={1,3,5,7},   delta_infty=4.
```

The projective curve is rational: away from `u=0` the same fibre argument
above gives generic injectivity of the parametrization. Hence
`p_a=(6-1)(6-2)/2=10` and `delta_0+delta_infty=6+4=10`.

The report's universal Newton-face quantifier is not correct as written.
For the final AG-S `alpha_i` terms its weight bookkeeping is right:
`deg_y<3` and `fint=6a+4b<18` for a monomial `x^a y^b` imply, after
degree-6 homogenisation and with `wt(Y)=1`, `wt(Z)=3`,

```text
wt(Y^b Z^(6-a-b)) = 18-3a-2b > 9.
```

But AG-S also permits lower terms in the prefix curve for `(3,2)`. For
example a prefix `g=y^3-x^2+a xy` is allowed by the `h=1` construction
condition `3i+2j<6`, and the corresponding local square begins

```text
(Y^3-Z+aYZ)^2 + cZ^3 + ...
```

which contains the weight-7 term `2aYZ(Y^3-Z)`. Thus not every raw
`(Y,Z)` equation has first perturbation `cZ^3`. A row-level replacement
would have to formulate the Newton face in an approximate-root analytic
coordinate. This defect does not change the associated curve computation,
where the prefix lower terms are zero.

## 4. Licensing reads

BL 2014 works on curves in `CP^2`; its Section 2.1 defines singular
points of the projective curve and says a point is cuspidal/unibranched
when its local link is a knot. It then says, unless otherwise specified,
all singular points are assumed cuspidal. Theorem 5.4 starts "Let C be a
rational cuspidal curve of degree d"; Theorem 6.5 is its semigroup-counting
reformulation for rational cuspidal curves. Thus the hypothesis is on the
whole projective curve, including the point at infinity if singular. The
associated degree-6 curve fails this because the affine origin has three
branches.

BHL 2017 is likewise projective and cuspidal: it defines a cuspidal curve
as one whose singular points are all unibranched, then Theorem 1 assumes
such a cuspidal curve of genus `g` and degree `d`. Our curve has geometric
genus zero, so if it were cuspidal BL 2014 would already be the sharper
special case. It is not cuspidal.

BLZ 2024 Theorem 6.4 really is restricted, among noncuspidal points, to
singularities whose links are `T(2,2n)` and no other singularities. The
origin here has three local components; whatever name one assigns to its
algebraic link, it is not a two-component `T(2,2n)` link. Theorem 6.4 does
not apply.

FLMN's main rational-cuspidal distribution conjecture/theorems require
locally irreducible singularities. The associated curve is not in that
scope. There is, however, a weaker FLMN dimension-counting proposition
that applies to any irreducible degree-`d` plane curve at any selected
locally irreducible points. Applied only to `P_infty` with
`S_infty=<2,9>` and `d=6`, it gives no obstruction:

```text
l=1: #(<2,9> cap [0,6])  = 4  >= 3
l=2: #(<2,9> cap [0,12]) = 9  >= 6
l=3: #(<2,9> cap [0,18]) = 15 >= 10
l=4: #(<2,9> cap [0,24]) = 21 >= 15
l=5: #(<2,9> cap [0,30]) = 27 >= 21.
```

This is a licensed weak pass, but it ignores the multibranch affine
singularity and is not the BL/FLMN rational-cuspidal equality required for
candidate kill/pass in the charged protocol. It supplies neither KILL nor
`PASS_NECESSARY_ONLY`.

```text

```

The mutated-gap fail control also has the right count:

```text

```

One parenthetical reason in the charged report is off: the symmetry failure

plane-branch semigroup and fails the counting equality, is unchanged.

The `<2,7>` parenthetical is correct: it has gaps `{1,3,5}` and
`R(5)=#{0,2,4}=3`, so it still passes the `j=1`, `d=4` count.
