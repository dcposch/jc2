## 3. BLZ 2024 Theorem 6.4: equality case of the second inequality

Source: Borodzik–Liu–Zemke, arXiv:2104.13709v2, published as *Algebr. Geom. Topol.* 24 (2024) 4837–4889. Theorem numbering 6.4 is the same in the arXiv and in the published text (MSP/MPG PDF, pages 4882–4883). The second inequality is the second display of (6.5).

**What the paper proves, and from which inequality.** Let `N` be a tubular neighbourhood of `C` in `CP^2` and `Y=∂N`. Then `Y` is a `d^2`-surgery on a knotification `K̂ ⊂ #_ρ S^2×S^1` with `ρ=2g+η_+`. The complement `X=CP^2 \ int(N)` has trivial intersection form (Lemma 3.12). Levine–Ruberman [arXiv:1403.2464, Theorem 4.7] then gives, for every torsion `Spin^c` structure on `Y` that extends over `X`,

```text
d_bot(Y,s) ≥ -(ρ+e-1)/2,     d_top(Y,s) ≤ (ρ+e-1)/2
```

(BLZ Proposition 3.13; here `e=1` for an irreducible curve, so `b1(Y)=2g+η_+` and the bound is `±(g+η_+/2)`). The `V^{top}` and `V^{bot}` invariants of `K̂` are computed *exactly* from the infimal convolution `R` of the cuspidal semigroup counting functions together with the knotification staircases of the `T(2,2n)` points (Proposition 6.3(a,b)). Substituting `m=k-(d-3)/2` yields (6.5). The second inequality of (6.5) is therefore equivalent to sharpness of the *lower* bound `d_bot(Y, s_{md}) ≥ -η_+/2-g` at the `Spin^c` structures `s_{md}` that extend over `X`. For `d=9` those are `m=k-3`, hence `m=3` at `k=6` (`s_{27}`) and `m=4` at `k=7` (`s_{36}`).

**Does the paper characterize equality?** No. Theorem 6.4 is stated only as a pair of inequalities. The proof compares an exact Floer computation to a one-sided cobordism bound and stops. There is no “if and only if”, no rigidity of `R` at equality, and no classification of semigroups for which some slot saturates.

The nearest related statements in the same paper:

- Corollary 6.10 recovers Bodnár–Borodzik–Celoria–Golla–Hedden–Livingston: for a *cuspidal* curve of genus `g` (no `T(2,2n)` points) the two sides of (6.5) become a two-sided bound on `min/max_j (R(kd+1-2j)+j)`. At `g=0` that two-sided bound collapses to the BL 2014 identities `R(kd+1)=(k+1)(k+2)/2`. That collapse uses `κ_+=η_+=0` and `b1(Y)=0` (rational homology sphere bounding a rational homology ball, so `d=0` exactly). It does *not* apply to a curve with nodes.
- Subsection 6.3 compares (6.9) (rational, nodes allowed) with (6.11) (cuspidal, genus allowed). The extra inequality `R(kd)≤K` obstructs trading genus for a double point; equality is not characterized.
- Example 6.16 / Remark 6.17: Theorem 6.4 *does not obstruct* the Orevkov–Fibonacci genus-1 curves from being traded for a positive double point. That is a non-sharpness remark, not an equality case.
- Remark 7.5: for curves whose only singularities are `A_{2n}` and nodes, the second inequality of (6.5) is *vacuous* (it reduces to a bound already satisfied by a smooth plane curve of the same degree). Our candidate is not in that class: the unibranch point is `T(3,23)`, not `T(2,*)`, so the second inequality has content.

**Levine–Ruberman equality, checked and not consumed as extra rigidity.** LR Corollary 4.6 says: if a 4-manifold `X` with `∂X=Y` satisfies `b1(X)=b1(Y)` and `b2(X)=0`, then *every* extending torsion `Spin^c` has correction terms equal to those of `#_{b1(Y)} S^1×S^2`, i.e. both BLZ inequalities would be identities at every `k`. That fails here: the first inequality is strict at `k=1,...,6` (slack 1 at `k=6`). Therefore the hypotheses of LR 4.6 do not hold for this `X` (in particular one cannot upgrade the one-sided Proposition 3.13 to all-slot identities). LR Corollary 4.8 gives a diagonalizability criterion when `d_bot` equals `-b1/2+rank V`; BLZ never invokes it, and the intersection form on `X` is already trivial, so there is no extra lattice to rigidify.

**d-invariant rigidity in the cuspidal ancestors, not inherited.** BL 2014 Theorem 1.1 / Theorem 5.4: a rational unicuspidal curve forces *identities* on the Alexander coefficients, equivalently `R(kd+1)=(k+1)(k+2)/2` for all `k`, because `d(S^3_{d^2}(K),s_m)=0`. Bodnár–Celoria–Golla (arXiv:1409.3282, Theorem 1.1 and (5.4)) extend to unicuspidal arbitrary genus as a two-sided bound of width `g`; at large `d` their identities force `a+b=3d` for one Puiseux pair (Theorem 1.2), with `g=0` excluded and referred back to the FLMN/BL list. None of these characterizations applies to a curve with affine nodes.

Conclusion of this section: equality in the second inequality of Theorem 6.4 means precisely that `d_bot` achieves the Levine–Ruberman bound at the corresponding `s_{md}`. The paper does not convert that numerical equality into extra semigroup or singularity constraints.

## 5. Forced semigroup/singularity structure vs. the candidate pins

The saturations do not force extra semigroup data. They are identities of the reviewed pin class.

**Setup, used throughout this section.** Let `C` be a reduced irreducible plane curve of degree `d≥4` and geometric genus 0, with a single unibranch singular point whose semigroup is symmetric of delta `δ` (automatic for a plane branch: conductor `C_S=2δ`, Frobenius `F=2δ-1`), and with all remaining singularities of type `T(2,2n)`, so `κ_+=Δ_aff` and `δ+κ_+=p_a=(d-1)(d-2)/2`. Write `R` for the unibranch counting function. Then `R(m)=m-δ` for all `m≥2δ`.

**Lemma (k=d-2, second inequality is an identity).** For every such curve,

```text
min_{0≤j≤κ_+} (R((d-2)d+1-2j)+j) = d(d-1)/2 = RHS.
```

Proof. The smallest argument is `(d-1)^2-2κ_+`. This exceeds the conductor:

```text
(d-1)^2-2κ_+ - ((d-1)(d-2)-2κ_+) = d-1 ≥ 1,
```

so every `j=0,...,κ_+` is in the stable range. Then `R(kd+1-2j)+j=kd+1-δ-j`, strictly decreasing in `j`, and the minimum at `j=κ_+` is

```text
(d-2)d+1-δ-κ_+ = (d-1)^2-p_a = d(d-1)/2.
```

Unique argmin `j=κ_+`. For `d=9`, `κ_+=6` this is the k=7 identity of §2.

**Lemma (k=d-3, second inequality is an identity).** For every such curve,

```text
min_{0≤j≤κ_+} (R((d-3)d+1-2j)+j) = (d-1)(d-2)/2 = RHS.
```

Proof. Now `(d-3)d+1-2κ_+=2δ-1=F`. For `j≤κ_+-1` the arguments are `≥F+2=2δ+1`, hence stable, and `R+j=kd+1-δ-j` decreases to the value `p_a` at `j=κ_+-1`. At `j=κ_+` one has `R(F)+κ_+=δ+κ_+=p_a`. Argmin `{κ_+-1, κ_+}`. For `d=9` this is the k=6 identity of §2.

**First inequality at k=d-2 also saturates, both splits.** Split A (`η_+=κ_+`): `R((d-1)^2-κ_+)=(d-1)^2-κ_+-δ=d(d-1)/2`. Split B (`η_+=κ_+-1`): `min(R((d-1)^2-κ_++1), R((d-1)^2-κ_+-1)+1)=min(RHS+1, RHS)=RHS`. At k=d-3 the first inequality is *strict* of slack 1 for both splits (split A: `d(d-3)/2=RHS-1`; split B likewise 27 against 28 at `d=9`).

**What this does *not* force.** The identities use only `g=0`, symmetry of the unibranch semigroup (plane branch), `κ_+=Δ_aff`, and the genus split. They do not constrain the Puiseux pair of `S_∞` beyond `δ_∞=p_a-Δ_aff`, do not distinguish split A from split B, and do not force any affine germ to be a node rather than an `A_3`. They would *fail* (and kill) only if some `j` undershot `RHS`; in the stable range that cannot happen, and the single sub-conductor evaluation `R(F)=δ` is equivalent to `F=2δ-1`, i.e. to symmetry of the unibranch semigroup.

**Check against the pins.** The charged curve is of this class: `d=9`, `g=0`, `S_∞=⟨3,23⟩` a plane branch of delta 22, `κ_+=6`. The k=6,7 saturations are therefore predicted by the lemmas, and the explicit tables of §2 confirm them. No contradiction. The remaining-delta split remains unpinned, as in the hostile review.

**d-invariant reading, not extra structure.** Equality at `k=6,7` means `d_bot(Y,s_{27})=d_bot(Y,s_{36})=-η_+/2`. That is the content of the lemmas translated back through BLZ (6.6)–(6.7). It is already implied by the pins plus Proposition 6.3, not a new constraint on the curve.
