## Exact statements

Work over C. Let W=C<x,d | dx-xd=1> and D=Frac(W).

1. **Central denominator.** For every nonscalar P in W and U in D with
   UP=PU, there is a nonzero polynomial f in C[z] such that
   f(P)U belongs to W and commutes with P.
2. **Division centralizer of a commutator coordinate.** If P,Q in W satisfy
   [Q,P]=1, then C_D(P)=C(P).
3. **Rational indecomposability.** Under the same commutator hypothesis,
   if P=R(T) for T in D and a nonconstant rational R in C(z), then deg(R)=1.
   Here deg(R) is its degree as a map P1 to P1, after cancellation.
4. **Donor consequence.** For nonconstant R, form the rational Weyl pair
   P0=R(x), Q0=(1/R'(x))*d, with the coefficient ON THE LEFT. No
   C-algebra embedding sigma:D->D can send BOTH entries into W when
   deg(R)>1. This includes arbitrary division automorphisms, not only
   inner maps and extended polynomial Weyl automorphisms.

None of these statements proves JC2/DC1, classifies arbitrary rational
Weyl pairs, supplies a counterexample, or excludes every proper embedding
of D. The rational-decomposition premise in statement3 is essential to
this construction filter. Degree1 means Mobius, NOT necessarily affine.

## Lemma: proper nonzero left-ideal quotients have finite length

Let 0!=I be a left ideal of W, with I!=W. Choose 0!=a in I and let
m=deg_B(a)>0 for the total-degree Bernstein filtration. PBW gives
gr(W)=C[X,D]. Because degrees of nonzero products add, the associated
graded of the principal left ideal Wa is generated by the leading
symbol of a. Consequently W/Wa has a linear Hilbert polynomial, with
positive integer leading coefficient m. The quotient M=W/I has growth
at most linear and is finitely generated.

There is no nonzero finite-dimensional W-module: on such a module the
trace of [d,x]=1 would be 0=dim(M), impossible over C. Every nonzero
finitely generated subquotient of M therefore has a linear, not constant,
Hilbert polynomial and positive integer multiplicity. Submodules are
finitely generated because W is Noetherian; induced and quotient good
filtrations give additivity of this multiplicity in short exact sequences.
Any strict chain of submodules consumes at least one multiplicity unit
per nonzero factor, and its length is bounded by the multiplicity of M.
Thus M has finite composition length. The case I=W gives M=0 directly.

## Lemma: endomorphisms of these finite-length modules form a finite space

Every simple W-module S is cyclic and has at most countable C-dimension,
since the PBW monomials form a countable basis of W. We claim End_W(S)=C.
Schur's lemma makes this endomorphism ring a division ring. If an
endomorphism A were transcendental over C, the endomorphisms
(A-lambda)^-1, lambda in C, would be linearly independent: a finite
relation, multiplied by the commuting product of its denominators,
would be a nonzero polynomial relation in A. Evaluation at any fixed
nonzero s in S is injective on End_W(S), since a nonzero endomorphism of
a simple module is invertible. This would place an uncountable independent
set in S, a contradiction. Therefore A is algebraic over C; factoring
its annihilating polynomial over C in this division ring forces A to be
a scalar.

For finite-length modules M,N, successive short exact sequences and
left exactness of Hom bound dim_C Hom_W(M,N) by length(M)*length(N):
Hom between two simple factors is zero or one-dimensional by the result
just proved. In particular End_W(M) is finite-dimensional. Any one
endomorphism of M thus satisfies a nonzero polynomial over C.

## Proof of the central-denominator statement

Fix U commuting with nonscalar P and set

    I_U = {a in W : aU belongs to W}.

This is a left ideal and is nonzero: an Ore left-fraction expression for
U supplies a nonzero left denominator. It is stable under RIGHT
multiplication by P, because (aP)U=(aU)P. Therefore right multiplication
by P induces a W-linear endomorphism r_P of the LEFT module M=W/I_U.

If M=0, choose f=1. Otherwise the two lemmas supply a nonzero f in C[z]
with f(r_P)=0. Applying that identity to the class of1 gives f(P) in I_U.
Hence B=f(P)U belongs to W. Moreover B commutes with P, since both f(P)
and U do. Finally f(P)!=0: a nonscalar element of W has positive
Bernstein degree, so distinct powers have distinct degrees and cannot
satisfy a nonzero scalar polynomial relation. This proves statement1.

Crucially, I_U need not be a two-sided ideal or be stable under right Q.
No such stability was used; only right P is required.

## Remaining statements

For statement2, apply statement1 to U in C_D(P). The polynomial
centralizer theorem gives B=f(P)U in C[P]. Thus U=f(P)^-1 B belongs to
C(P). The reverse inclusion is immediate. There is no assumption that
W is finite over the subalgebra generated by P,Q.

For statement3, T commutes with P=R(T). Statement2 gives T in C(P).
Since P is nonscalar, T is transcendental over C. The two containments
P in C(T) and T in C(P) give C(T)=C(P)=C(R(T)). The degree formula for
a rational map of P1 now gives

    deg(R)=[C(T):C(R(T))]=1.

For statement4, the displayed donor satisfies [Q0,P0]=1 by the Ore
rule. If sigma sends it into W, the images P,Q still satisfy that
identity and P=R(sigma(x)). Statement3 then forces deg(R)=1.
No classification or generation theorem for Aut(D) is invoked.

## Desk controls and failed stronger readings

- R=x, sigma=id lands in W; the obstruction does not exclude degree1.
- R=1/x is NOT excluded here. The involution sigma(x)=x^-1,
  sigma(d)=-x^2*d of D sends its donor (x^-1,-x^2*d) to (x,d).
  Its commutator is1 and applying sigma twice fixes x,d. Thus replacing
  deg(R)=1 by 'R is affine' would be false for this enlarged scope.
- R=x^2 is excluded for every embedding in statement4, not just inner
  conjugations. This is a theorem about simultaneous polynomial landing,
  not an assertion that every proper division embedding has this form.
- Polynomial powers do NOT imply an element of D is in W. Following the
  rational-power mechanism in Makar-Limanov's
  [MPIM2019(65), printed pp4--5](https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/1789/1/preprint_2019_65.pdf),
  set t=xd and b=x*t*(t+2)/(t+1). The relation t*x=x*(t+1) gives
  b^2=x^2*t*(t+3) and b^3=x^3*t*(t+2)*(t+4), both in W. Yet b is not
  in W: its weight1 component would have to be in x*C[t], and the
  displayed rational coefficient has a genuine pole at t=-1.
  This does not refute statement1: b=b^3/b^2 is a ratio of commuting
  polynomial elements. It refutes the unnecessary stronger integrality
  shortcut. Only the cited rank-one statement and rational-power passages,
  plus the concluding question, were inspected in this second source;
  its later classification computations are not imported.
