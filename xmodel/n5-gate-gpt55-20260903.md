## Prop. 7.3 Source Read

`SOURCE-READ[XU-PROP-7.3]`.  Xu works over an algebraically closed field
`K` of characteristic zero.  In Section 7.3, `(f,g)` is a Jacobian pair, monic
in `y`, and `f` has two points at infinity.  Let `deg f=m`, `deg g=n`, and let
`T_0=g`, `T_1=f`, ..., `T_s` be the effective quasi-approximate roots.
Writing `d_i=gcd(-mu_0,...,-mu_{i-1})`, the principal minor roots of `f` have
multiplicity `m u_s/d_s`, with `u_s < v_s=d_s-u_s`.

The proposition states: if `sigma_1` is the order-1 `pi`-root for the
principal minor roots and

```text
T_i(sigma_1) = T_{i,sigma_1}(pi) t^((v_s-u_s)mu_i/d_s) + ...
T_s(sigma_1) = T_{s,sigma_1}(pi) t^((v_s-u_s)(mu_s+2)/d_s) + ...
```

then `T_{0,sigma_1}(pi),...,T_{s,sigma_1}(pi)` are powers of a common linear
polynomial.

The proof first obtains, using Moh Prop. 4.6 verbatim,

```text
T_{i,sigma_1}(pi) = C_i p(pi)^(-mu_i/d_s)        for i<s
deg p(pi) = u_s
T_{s,sigma_1}(pi) = C_s p(pi)^((-mu_s-2)/d_s)(pi-a)
```

with `p(pi) in K[pi]`, `C_i in K*`, and `a in K`.  It then proves
`p(pi)=(pi-a)^{u_s}` by a semigroup contradiction.  Prop. 7.3 itself contains
no Galois-action statement.

`SOURCE-READ[XU-SECTION-8]`.  In the `(99,66)` application Xu later uses the

`f(sigma)=p(pi)^6 t^{6(-8+3delta)}+...`,
`g(sigma)=p(pi)^9 t^{9(-8+3delta)}+...`, and explicitly writes
`deg p(pi)=u_3=3`.

`SOURCE-READ[MOH-P201]`.  Moh p.201 defines a different action: after an

`bar t^{L A_{r-1}}=t`; the automorphism `bar t -> omega bar t` restricts
`V_{r-1}`.  That is a major-disc denominator-increment device, not the
action asserted by Xu Prop. 7.3.

## Galois Audit

Set `delta=p/e` in lowest terms and write `t=tau^e`.  For the top
principal-minor packet, Moh p.194 and Xu Prop. 7.3 give a fixed lower jet
through order `1`, and the genuine split is at `delta>1`.  A root in the
packet has the form

```text
alpha = gamma(t) + c t^(p/e) + higher = gamma(tau^e) + c tau^p + higher,
```

where the lower center `gamma` is fixed by `Gal(K((tau))/K((t)))`.

(a) Movement.  Under `tau -> zeta tau`, the coefficient changes
`c -> zeta^p c`.  A term `c t^(r/e)` is fixed by the full `mu_e` action iff
`c=0` or `e | r`.  In lowest terms for `delta=p/e` and `e>1`, a nonzero split
coefficient is moved by every nonidentity element.

(b) Same leading polynomial.  Because `f` has coefficients in `K((t))`, the
conjugate of a root is again a root.  Since `gamma` is fixed and the contact
order remains `delta`, the conjugate root appears as another root of the same
leading polynomial `P_sigma(pi)` for the same packet:
`P_sigma(c)=0` implies `P_sigma(zeta^p c)=0`.

(c) Nonzero.  The center root `pi=0` can be fixed.  That does not collapse the
argument: if every distinct split coefficient were `0`, the leading polynomial
would have only one root and the `pi`-root would not be split.  A genuine split
has at least one nonzero root, and scaling a nonzero root never gives `0`.

(d) Exact orbit size.  If `zeta_1^p c=zeta_2^p c` with `c != 0`, then
`(zeta_1/zeta_2)^p=1`.  Since `gcd(p,e)=1`, `zeta_1=zeta_2`.  The nonzero
orbit has exactly `e` distinct roots.

(e) Subclusters.  If a child packet is fixed by a subgroup `H` of the Puiseux
Galois group and a later split has nontrivial character on `H`, the same orbit
count bounds the corresponding relative denominator increment by the child
leading-polynomial degree.  If the child is not Galois-stable, the orbit runs
through sibling packets; then the count applies to the orbit-union, not to an
arbitrary single child.  This is why the unqualified absolute statement is a
FALLACY-v2 risk.

Therefore, for the top principal packet, the nonzero `mu_e` orbit embeds in
the degree-`u_s` leading polynomial.  Hence `e <= u_s`.

## Sanity Cases

`CONFIRMED[(64,48)]`.  Moh p.202 lists the exceptional row and Moh p.207 says
Prop. 6.4 is applied to the first three exceptional cases, the `u_s=1` regime.
Thus the principal leading polynomial has degree `1`; no nontrivial orbit can
fit, so `den(delta)=1`.

`CONFIRMED[(75,50; V_2=3)]`.  Moh p.207 descends to
`(n,m,M_2,V_2)=(15,10,11,3)`.  Here `d_2=gcd(15,10)=5` and
`u=d_2-V_2=2`.  Moh p.210 states that in the major disc `D_2` there are three
subdiscs containing `2,2,6` roots of `f`, and the datum is `delta_1=1/2`.
So the denominator `2` is exactly the `u=2` packet size in the two minor
subpackets.  This is a descended major-disc sanity check, not the top
principal-minor N5 statement for the original `(75,50)` row.

`CONFIRMED[(99,66): delta=2]`.  Xu p.13 has `u_3=3` and the `delta=2` split
case.  Here `e=1 <= 3`; N5 imposes no obstruction.  The branch is governed by
the ODE face and Xu's displayed equation (8.2).

`CONFIRMED[(99,66): delta=5/2]`.  Here `e=2 <= 3`.  The action is
`pi -> -pi`, so the stable cubic split is `{0,a,-a}`, i.e.
`p(pi)=pi(pi^2-c)` with `c != 0`.  This confirms the inequality and refutes
the stronger divisibility claim.

`CONFIRMED[(99,66): delta=7/3]`.  Here `e=3 <= 3`, so N5 allows the order.
Xu excludes it by the separate ODE argument: p.13 says that when
`delta != 2` and `delta != 5/2`, equation (7.1) step-by-step gives
`q(pi)=p(pi)^13(pi-c)` and then cannot hold.  Concretely at `delta=7/3`,
the face equation

```text
9 a q p' - b q' p = 45 p^14,
a = 40 delta - 105 = -35/3,
b = 27 delta - 72 = -9
```

with `q=p^13(pi-c)` reduces to `(pi-c)p' = 3p`, whose degree-3 polynomial
solutions are linear cubes.  That is not a genuine split.  So `7/3` is killed
by the ODE, not by N5.
