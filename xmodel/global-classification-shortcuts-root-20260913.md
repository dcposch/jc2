## 3. Homogeneous classification: test the actual Keller frame

For this manual scope screen, let F=(f,g) be a hypothetical polynomial
Keller pair with J(f,g)=1. Translate the target so F(0)=0, and put

    X = g_y partial_x - g_x partial_y,
    Y = -f_y partial_x + f_x partial_y.

Then X(f)=1, X(g)=0, Y(f)=0, Y(g)=1, X wedge Y=partial_x wedge partial_y,
and [X,Y]=0. The last identity follows by applying the commutator to f,g:
their invertible Jacobian makes a derivation annihilating both identically
zero. These elementary identities attach the proposed classification to the
actual source, without a completeness assumption.

### The automatic grading field is not the required linear field

The polynomial field E=fX+gY satisfies

    E(f)=f, E(g)=g, [E,X]=-X, [E,Y]=-Y.

This does not make X,Y quasi-homogeneous under a LINEAR diagonalizable
source field. In fact, polynomial linearization of this particular E to
the ordinary Euler field is already an equivalent reformulation of
invertibility: if E=u partial_u+v partial_v in polynomial source coordinates,
then its polynomial eigenfunctions f,g of eigenvalue1 are linear in u,v.
Their constant nonzero Jacobian makes that linear map invertible. Conversely
an invertible F supplies precisely such coordinates u=f,v=g.

Local analytic coordinates f,g near a point of F^{-1}(0) do not furnish
this global polynomial conjugacy. No algebraic group integration or
polynomial linearization is supplied by the displayed commutators.

### Taking leading terms destroys the required independent frame

Write r=deg X and s=deg Y, using the maximum ordinary degree of coefficient
polynomials. If r+s>0, the degree-(r+s) part of the constant wedge identity
is

    X_r wedge Y_s = 0.

Thus ordinary leading terms are necessarily collinear. They cannot be
silently substituted into a non-collinear homogeneous classification.
This failure occurs even on an automorphism control, not only on an
unknown counterexample: take f=x+y^2 and g=y+f^2. Its inverse is
y=g-f^2, x=f-(g-f^2)^2, and J(f,g)=1. The fields are

    X=(1+4yf) partial_x-2f partial_y,
    Y=-2y partial_x+partial_y,

with leading parts 4y^3 partial_x and -2y partial_x. Their leading wedge
vanishes although the full wedge is1. No CAS was used for this calculation.
