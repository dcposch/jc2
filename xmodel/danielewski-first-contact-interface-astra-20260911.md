## 1. Exact full-identity dilation and the initial-commutation margin

    {f,g}=x^2(f_x*g_t-f_t*g_x).

    {delta_z f,delta_z g}=z^-1*delta_z{f,g}.                  (1)

For ANY finite-support regular H,G let A=hi(H), B=hi(G). The sums

    H_s=sum_m s^(A-m) H_m,   G_s=sum_n s^(B-n) G_n

    {H_s,G_s}=c*s^(A+B+1).                                 (2)

## 2. A literal failure of global normal-remainder detection

Let D_±={x=0,t=±1} and E_±={Z=0,t=±1}. They are four global prime divisors, with D_± and E_± meeting only at the corresponding origin. From the smooth chart coordinates, x has order 1 along each D and Z has order 1 along each E. Take

    R_0=x*Z^2,     F=x*Z.

Then

    div(R_0)=D_+ +D_- +2E_+ +2E_-,
    F^2=x*R_0,     R_0 does NOT divide F in O(T).             (5)

The last claim follows because F/R_0=1/Z has a pole on E_±. In particular R_0 is not a proper polynomial power: its D multiplicities are 1. So this is not just a removable repeated-power choice of a primitive reference.

The ring is the free C[x,Z]-module with basis 1,t, using t^2=1+x^2Z. Dividing its two coefficients by the monomial xZ^2 makes F=xZ an explicit nonzero NORMAL remainder modulo R_0. Nevertheless F restricts to zero on every D and E. Thus restriction of such normal remainders to the GLOBAL product of all reduced component fields is not injective. Restricting just to the simple components D_± also fails.

This is not a failure to find formal branches. At the generic point of each D_±, Z is a unit, t=±sqrt(1+x^2Z), and R_0=xZ^2 has x derivative Z^2. Both branches have an honest unit implicit coefficient over C(Z). The remainder is still invisible at z=0 on their product. Indeed on either branch it becomes F=z/Z: a locally rational reference multiple although not a globally regular reference multiple.

The exact missing source assertion is therefore a bound or normal-module property excluding remainders of this kind.
