## Reviewed statement
For a Keller map H, let N_H be its generic field degree and a_H(y)=N_H-#H^{-1}(y).
Let C_H be the effective affine curve cycle whose coefficient on D is the generic value of a_H along D; finite exceptional points are discarded.
Write B_H for the SUM OF COEFFICIENTS of C_H, not its projective degree or a valuation-weighted mass.
The parent identifies B_H=sum_i r_i delta_i for the omitted curves of the finite normalization, including addition when several omitted curves have the same image.
Its slack is S_H=N_H-1-B_H=sum_(i,x)(m_x-r_i t_x)>=0.
For any two whole-plane Keller maps F,G, the identities are
  C_(F o G)=N_G C_F+F_* C_G,  B_(F o G)=N_G B_F+B_G,
  S_(F o G)=N_G S_F+S_G.
Here F_* is pushforward of the indicated affine polynomial-curve cycles, with generic curve degree included in its definition.
For one F of generic degree N>1, the consequence is
  B_(F^n)=B_F (N^n-1)/(N-1),  S_(F^n)=S_F (N^n-1)/(N-1).
Thus all scalar inequalities B_(F^n)<=N^n-1 are equivalent to B_F<=N-1.

## 1. Actual affine curve transport
Let D be an irreducible affine curve whose normalization nu:A1->D is finite and birational.
Assume F is etale on the WHOLE affine plane. Then F(D) is again an affine polynomial curve and the induced map of A1 normalizations has degree one.
Proof: F o nu is a nonconstant polynomial map A1->A2. Nonconstancy follows from local invertibility of F and nonconstancy of nu.
Any nonconstant polynomial coordinate makes C[t] finite over the coordinate ring generated by the two component polynomials, so the image D' is closed and the map to D' is finite. Also C[D] is a finitely generated intermediate algebra between that image ring and C[t]; it is integral over the image ring, hence finite. Thus F|D is finite.
Its normalization is A1: extend the finite map to smooth projective completions. Riemann-Hurwitz for the map from P1 makes the target completion rational; each removed target point has a preimage among the single removed source point, so there is exactly one removed target point.
Equivalently this is the usual normalization property of an affine polynomial curve, already used for the parent's image curves. No smoothness of D or D' is assumed.
There is therefore a finite nonconstant polynomial g:A1->A1 with F o nu=nu' o g.
Fix t0. The normalization nu maps a sufficiently small analytic disc at t0 injectively onto the selected local branch of D; this includes a cusp or a branch at a multibranch point.
The analytic inverse function theorem makes F biholomorphic on a sufficiently small neighborhood of nu(t0).
Consequently F o nu is locally injective at t0. If g had local degree greater than one there, its composition with nu' would not be locally injective, a contradiction.
Thus g has no critical points. A nonconstant complex polynomial with derivative nowhere zero has degree one.
This argument uses actual ambient etaleness at every finite point. It DOES NOT assert delta_i=1 for an omitted-boundary curve under the finite map h:X->A2, where h can ramify.
It also does not assert that F|D is globally injective as a map to the singular image: distinct normalization parameters may still be identified by nu'.

## 2. Pointwise finite-fibre identity
Keller maps are quasi-finite, with finite fibres and generic degrees multiplying under composition.
For every y, direct counting gives
  #(F o G)^{-1}(y)=sum_(z in F^{-1}(y)) #G^{-1}(z).
Subtracting from N_F N_G yields the exact constructible-function identity
  a_(F o G)(y)=N_G a_F(y)+sum_(z in F^{-1}(y)) a_G(z).
The parent finite-flat normalization shows 0<=#H^{-1}(y)<=N_H and that the generic deficit over each omitted-curve image is sum_(i over D) r_i delta_i.
Away from the nonproper locus the deficit is zero. The deficit's exceptional zero-dimensional strata are finite and contribute no affine curve cycle.

## 3. Taking the curve part
Each curve in C_G is an affine polynomial curve with finite A1 normalization, at the parent's stated scope.
By section1, F is finite on that curve and its generic degree onto the image is one.
For a generic point of an image curve, its preimages on a given component D of C_G contribute the coefficient of D times this degree.
Finite exceptional sets, intersections of finitely many components, and singular parameter values affect only finitely many target points.
If several component images coincide, their coefficients ADD; none are cancelled or silently counted as distinct image components.
Taking the codimension-one part of the pointwise identity therefore proves C_(F o G)=N_G C_F+F_*C_G.
Section1 also shows that F_* preserves the SUM of coefficients on this cone of polynomial-curve cycles, even if components coalesce.
Hence B_(F o G)=N_G B_F+B_G. Since N_(F o G)=N_F N_G, subtraction gives S_(F o G)=N_G S_F+S_G.

## 4. Iteration and exact limit of the conclusion
Apply section3 with F fixed and G=F^(n-1): B_n=N^(n-1) B_1+B_(n-1), with B_0=0.
Induction gives B_n=B_1(1+N+...+N^(n-1)); the same calculation applies to S_n.
The positive geometric-series factor proves the claimed equivalence of all scalar budgets.
For N=1 the formal formula is B_n=n B_1, S_n=n S_1; the parent budget already gives B_1=S_1=0. No division by zero is used.
This closes ONLY amplification of this particular sum-of-coefficients inequality by iteration.
It supplies no valuation-weight bound, image-degree bound, uniform finite model, stabilization of omitted divisors, or control of how individual slack points redistribute.
It does not turn the inherited scalar budget into properness, surjectivity, N=1, a proof or a counterexample to JC2.
No novelty claim is made: frozen HQ/public searches were lexical and only selected previews were inspected; old general tower stops remain in force.
