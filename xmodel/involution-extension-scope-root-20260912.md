## Primary text and the exact mismatch

[Moskowicz, arXiv1410.7705v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.7705v1),
printed1, defines involutions as order-two polynomial automorphisms.
Lemma2.1 asserts that all are polynomially conjugate to coordinate exchange.
Section3, printed3–5, requires extension to the WHOLE polynomial ring;
its exchange on C[P,Q] swaps P and Q. It is not a rational transformation
fixing both. No automatic extension of a rational deck involution is proved.

The broad conjugacy lemma has an elementary counterexample. Let
alpha(x,y)=(y,x), gamma(x,y)=(-x,-y). Then J(alpha)=-1 and J(gamma)=1.
For any polynomial automorphism a, the chain rule and its nonzero constant
Jacobian give J(a^-1 alpha a)=-1. Thus gamma cannot equal a^-1 alpha a.
Independently, their fixed loci have dimensions one and zero respectively;
conjugacy preserves those loci. Both satisfy the paper's stated definition.
This falsifies that literal lemma, not every theorem or the exchange-specific
result. Proofs invoking the unrestricted lemma cannot be imported on that
basis. We do not claim a counterexample to any Keller conclusion.

The arXiv abstract page presently lists only v1. No journal-version or later
repair audit is claimed. This is not a novelty assertion about the error.

Consequently birational linearization and that exactness condition do not

not a new example or a closure of the pseudo-plane scalar-pair problem.

A deck involution of a degree-two first leg fixes both Keller coordinates,
so it acts trivially on C[f,g]. It cannot be substituted for an order-two
restriction on that ring which exchanges its generators. Changing the
target coordinates does not turn the identity restriction into an exchange.
This distinguishes the hypotheses even before the polynomiality issue.
