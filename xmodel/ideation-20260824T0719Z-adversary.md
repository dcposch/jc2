## 3. Hostile attacks by cluster

### 3.1 Hamiltonian \(\kappa\) and weight slices

The attractive part is exact, but it also exposes the restatement risk.  If
\(P_x,P_y\) generate the unit ideal, choose
\(V=A\partial_x+B\partial_y\) with \(V(P)=1\), and put

\[
  D_P=P_y\partial_x-P_x\partial_y,
  \qquad \kappa(P)=[\operatorname{div}V]
  \in \mathbb C[x,y]/D_P\mathbb C[x,y].
\]

Any two such \(V\)'s differ by \(hD_P\), and
\(\operatorname{div}(hD_P)=D_P(h)\), so the class is well defined.  Moreover,
\(\kappa(P)=0\) lets one subtract \(hD_P\) to make \(V\) divergence-free.
Polynomial de Rham exactness in two variables then writes
\(V=Q_y\partial_x-Q_x\partial_y\), giving \([P,Q]=1\).  The converse is
immediate.  Thus

\[
  \boxed{\kappa(P)=0\iff P\text{ has a polynomial Keller mate}.}
\]

That is a useful exact coordinate on the gap, but a universal theorem that
every atypical end pairs nontrivially with \(\kappa(P)\) is essentially the
missing theorem unless the pairing is independently computable on a proper
class.  The quotient is also close to classical Brieskorn/Petrov-module and
relative de Rham constructions, creating a serious **PRIORITY** risk.

The weight version is cheaper and can be honest if it freezes a proper
no-leak class.  One proposed deformation is invalid before weights enter:

\[
  P=x+x^2y+y^k.
\]

For every \(k\ge2\), \(P_x=1+2xy=0\) gives \(y=-1/(2x)\), and
\(P_y=x^2+ky^{k-1}=0\) becomes a nonzero equation
\(x^{k+1}=-k(-1)^{k-1}/2^{k-1}\), which always has a complex solution.
Hence this proposed “hostile deformation” is never unimodular.  The \(k=1\)
case also has common critical points.

**Adversarial disposition:** **REDESIGN, then launch one bounded gate.**  First
do a priority/equivalence freeze for \(\kappa\).  Then prove a complete weight
recurrence only for a certified unimodular family such as
\(P=x+x^ny\), with an explicit no-leak lemma.  Accept
`VALID-FAMILY-NO-GO`, `LEAK`, or `KNOWN-MODULE`; do not accept a universal
“residue should see every end” heuristic as progress.

### 3.2 Dual-pencil vanishing divisor

For a hypothetical Keller pair, every target-linear member
\(H_{[a:b]}=aP+bQ\) has no affine critical point.  The proposed next object,
however, is not yet a divisor.  An infinity-defect function in a one-parameter
pencil can have a positive generic value.  In that event its support is all
of \(\mathbb P^{1\vee}\), not a finite effective Cartier divisor.  Subtracting
the generic value may produce a jump divisor, but loses the claimed direct
positivity and changes the degree formula.

This is not a technicality: for a counterexample there is no prior reason
that a generic linear projection has zero infinity defect.  Establishing
generic vanishing is already very close to obtaining a coordinate member of
the pencil.  Likewise, `J=1` trivializes an affine two-form but does not by
itself force a degree-zero effective boundary divisor under GRR.  Any such
step must exhibit the missing determinant line and its sign, or it is the
same **COSTUME/RESTATEMENT** failure as the earlier raw boundary passports.
The surrounding theory of bifurcation values, polar curves, and vanishing
cycles at infinity is classical, so **PRIORITY** risk is high.

**Adversarial disposition:** run only the definition gate: prove that the
defect is generically zero, or replace it by a resolution-independent jump
cycle and derive its actual degree on controls.  `NOT-A-DIVISOR` is a complete
and likely outcome.  No GRR proof campaign is licensed before this gate.

### 3.6 Tate horizontal/mixed

The proposed “horizontal divisor of \(1-px^{p-1}\) in the Tate algebra” is
mislocated.  In the unit Tate algebra \(\mathbb Z_p\langle x,y\rangle\),
\(px^{p-1}\) is topologically nilpotent, so

\[
  (1-px^{p-1})^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}p^n x^{n(p-1)}
\]

converges.  The denominator is a unit and has no divisor in that affinoid.
Its algebraic zeros over \(\mathbb Q_p\) lie outside the closed unit disc.
Moving to a global rigid or algebraic compactification is possible, but then
the card's restricted-analytic pole obstruction is a different problem.

Moreover, one mixed ansatz modulo \(9\) and \(27\) cannot control an all-Witt
tower; the campaign's own alternate-lift control says low finite levels are
nonunique.  At \(p=3\), any genuinely bounded-degree inverse-limit success
would also fall in already excluded low-degree territory, so the experiment
can at best locate a gauge-specific obstruction.

**Adversarial disposition:** **TYPE-FAIL / STOP** as written.  A global annulus
or projective rigid formulation would be a new card and must first explain
