## 1. Primary-source audit and exact deductions

I read the full English primary text of S. I. Pinchuk,
*Quasi-polynomial mappings with constant Jacobian*, Izvestiya: Mathematics
85:3 (2021), 506--517, DOI `10.1070/IM9017`, rather than relying on the packet's
summary. The following distinction is load-bearing.

### 1.1 What the source actually defines and claims

- A q-polynomial is polynomial in `y` and may contain finitely many rational
  or negative powers of `x`.
- A q-elementary source change has the form
  `(x,y) -> (x,y+c*x^mu)` with rational `mu`.
- Q-equivalence is generated by right composition with such changes.
- A polynomial map `F` is called equivalent to a q-map `G` when some
  polynomially equivalent `H` is q-equivalent to `G`. This is not polynomial
  equivalence of `F` and `G`.
- Theorem 3.4 claims that a hypothetical noninvertible polynomial Keller map
  yields a reduced q-map whose leading pair for a weight `(-1/m,1)`, `m>1`,
  already has nonzero constant Jacobian.
- Theorem 4.1 states the equivalence, for coprime positive `k,r`,
  `deg p=kd`, `deg q=rd`, and `m=k+r+1`, of

  ```text
  r*p'*q-k*p*q' in k*,
  deg(p^r-q^k)=d*(k*r-k-r)+1,
  J(x^-k*p(x^m*y),x^-r*q(x^m*y)) in k*.
  ```

The elementary degree identity in Theorem 4.1 is independently checkable.
The much broader reduction in Theorem 3.4 needs a hostile audit of the exact
equivalence direction, the finite denominator extension, the decreasing
side-height/termination argument, and what information is retained when the
q-transform is reversed.

### 1.2 Polynomial-recovery firewall

This deduction is independent of the broad reduction theorem. Expand

```text
x^-k*p(x^m*y) = sum_i p_i*x^(m*i-k)*y^i,
x^-r*q(x^m*y) = sum_i q_i*x^(m*i-r)*y^i.
```

Because `m=k+r+1>k,r`, the only possible negative powers occur at `i=0`.
Thus both coordinates are polynomial only if `p(0)=q(0)=0`. But then

```text
(r*p'*q-k*p*q')(0)=0,
```

contradicting the nonzero constant in Theorem 4.1. Hence every equality model
of that displayed form has an unavoidable Laurent pole in at least one
coordinate. It can be an exact reduced-q control or a proof-side terminal
object; it is not by itself a polynomial Keller counterexample.

This also prevents a namespace error: the paper is unrelated to avenue 24's
real Pinchuk counterexample deformation.


