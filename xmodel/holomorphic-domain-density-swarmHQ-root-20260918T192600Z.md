## 1. Exact statement

Let F=(F_1,F_2):C^2 -> C^2 be a polynomial Keller map,
c=det JF a nonzero constant. Put

    g=F*(Euclidean metric),
    dmu=exp(-|F|^2) dvol_g
       =|c|^2 exp(-|F|^2) dV_z,
    H_F={h entire on C^2 : ||h||_mu<infinity}.

For J_ij=partial F_i/partial z_j, define

    D_i=sum_j (J^-1)_{ji} partial/partial z_j,
    E_F={h in H_F : D_1 h and D_2 h belong to H_F}.

The domain is the maximal common first-derivative domain inside the
holomorphic space, and density means the H_F norm, NOT graph-norm density.
The claim is

    closure_H_F(E_F)=H_F  if and only if  F is a polynomial automorphism.

More precisely, if F is noninvertible, there are a polynomial h_0 in H_F
and a continuous complex-linear functional ell on H_F such that

    ell(E_F)=0,       ell(h_0)!=0.

Thus the assertion is genuine nondensity, not merely that E_F is a proper
subset. It is conditional on a hypothetical noninvertible Keller map;
no such map is constructed or asserted to exist. The equivalence supplies
no proof of the density premise and therefore no solution of JC2.

## 3. Finite normalization and an omitted ramified divisor

Write A=C[F_1,F_2], R=C[z_1,z_2], L=Frac(R), and let S be the integral
closure of A in L. It is finite over A. Normality of R gives S subset R.
The morphism F is etale and quasi-finite; ZMT identifies

    U=Spec(R) --j(open)--> Y=Spec(S) --pi(finite)--> Spec(A)=A^2.

This is the ACTUAL source U, not an abstract isomorphic chart chosen
inside an unrelated finite cover.

If the field degree is one, S=A. The open immersion U into A^2 omits
no divisor: the equation of any omitted prime divisor would pull back
to a nonzero constant unit of R, contrary to dominance. Its complement
then has codimension at least two. Normal Hartogs extension identifies
Gamma(U,O)=A, so the open immersion between these affine schemes is an
isomorphism. Thus noninvertibility implies degree greater than one.

If pi had no height-one ramification, purity would make it etale.
A connected finite etale cover of complex A^2 is trivial, a contradiction.
Hence there is a prime ramification divisor E with index e>1. It is
omitted from U, since pi restricted to U is etale.

At a general closed point of E, Y and its branch curve are smooth,
the map of E to that curve is unramified, and no other boundary component
or exceptional bad locus meets the chosen point. Analytic coordinates
(t,s) upstairs and (u,v) downstairs then give

    pi(t,s)=(t^e,s),       E={t=0}.

Indeed, take v as the coordinate along E and a transverse defining
function u of the branch curve; pi*u=t^e times a holomorphic unit.
Absorb an analytic e-th root of that unit into t.
After shrinking, the entire punctured bidisc t!=0 belongs to U.
The original target coordinates are a biholomorphic function of (u,v).
Consequently

    dmu comparable to |t|^(2e-2) dA(t)dA(s)

on this bidisc, with two fixed positive comparison constants. The
Gaussian and the target coordinate-change Jacobian are bounded above
and below here. No global properness of F is assumed.

## 4. A globally L2 polynomial attaining the ramification pole

Normalize P^2 in L to obtain the projective finite extension Ybar of Y.
Choose a projective resolution r:M -> Ybar which is an isomorphism over
the smooth locus, in particular over U and a general point of E.
Set f=pi_bar composed r and Z=f^-1(A^2). Then
rho=r|_Z:Z -> Y is proper, Z is smooth, and Z contains the actual U.

The algebraic coherent sheaf G=rho_*omega_Z is generated by its global
sections on affine Y. Near a general smooth point p of E, rho is an
isomorphism, so G there is the canonical line bundle. Select a global
section whose value at p is nonzero. Its corresponding regular algebraic
two-form omega on Z has order zero along E.

Let Omega=F*(dw_1 wedge dw_2). On U this is c dz_1 wedge dz_2.
The quotient

    h_0=omega/Omega on U

is an algebraic regular function on the WHOLE affine plane U, hence a
polynomial in z_1,z_2. At E, Omega has order e-1, so

    v_E(h_0)=1-e.

The additional assertion needed here is h_0 in H_F. It follows without
asserting that all source polynomials have finite Gaussian moments:

Let H_infty be the target line at infinity and sigma its pulled-back
defining section of f*O(1) on M. The rational two-form omega is regular
on Z. Its poles on smooth M therefore lie in the support of div(sigma).
Choose m large enough that

    beta=sigma^m omega

has no divisorial poles. It is a global regular section of
K_M tensor f*O(m); smoothness removes the possible codimension-two issue.

Equip M with a smooth Hermitian metric, and f*O(1) with the pulled-back
Fubini--Study metric. On the target affine chart w,

    |sigma|^2=(1+|w|^2)^-1.

Compactness of M bounds the norm of beta. Hence on Z

    |omega|_M^2 <= C(1+|f|^2)^m.

The norm-density of a top-degree form is, up to a fixed convention
constant, omega wedge conjugate(omega), independent of the chosen
Hermitian metric. It follows that

    integral_Z exp(-|f|^2) |omega|_M^2 dvol_M
      <= C Vol(M) sup_{q>=0} (1+q)^m exp(-q) < infinity.

On U, omega=h_0 Omega, so this bounds a fixed positive constant times
||h_0||_mu^2. This is the promised global L2 witness.
Using regular forms on Z, not arbitrary reflexive forms on Y, avoids
any rational-singularity or Grauert--Riemenschneider vanishing assumption.

## 5. Domain elements have no negative Laurent coefficients

Any h in H_F is holomorphic on the punctured bidisc and has a Laurent
expansion

    h(t,s)=sum_{k in Z} a_k(s)t^k,

where each a_k is holomorphic in s. Weighted local L2 and circle
orthogonality imply a_k=0 for k<=-e; the allowed negative powers are
1-e,...,-1.

If h belongs to E_F, then its lifted derivative in the local target
u-direction belongs to local weighted L2: it is a linear combination
of D_1 h,D_2 h with bounded holomorphic target coefficients. In the chart,

    partial_u h=(1/e)t^(1-e) partial_t h.

The local weighted norm of this expression is comparable to

    integral |partial_t h|^2 dA(t)dA(s).

For any k<0, its k-th Laurent derivative term contributes the radial
factor integral_0^epsilon r^(2k-1) dr, which diverges. Orthogonality
prevents cancellation between distinct k. Fubini and holomorphicity
of a_k imply

    a_k(s)=0 for all k<0, for every h in E_F.

The k=0 term differentiates to zero; no assertion excludes constants.
This computation concerns the common derivative domain, since a
local normal target derivative may involve both original D_i.

## 6. A continuous obstruction, not just a strict domain inclusion

Choose the chart point and s_0 so that the leading coefficient of h_0
from Section4 satisfies a_(1-e)(s_0)!=0. Fix a t-circle of radius r_0
strictly inside the punctured bidisc and define

    ell(h)=a_(1-e)(s_0)
          =(1/(2*pi*i)) integral_{|t|=r_0} h(t,s_0)t^(e-2) dt.

The circle has a compact annular neighborhood in U, also compact in the
original source C^2 under the open-immersion inverse. On this compact
neighborhood the Gaussian density is smooth and strictly positive.
Local holomorphic L2 submean bounds and a finite cover of the circle give

    sup_{|t|=r_0} |h(t,s_0)| <= C ||h||_mu.

Cauchy's formula therefore gives |ell(h)|<=C' ||h||_mu. In particular,
ell is continuous on the FULL H_F norm; it is not merely graph-norm
continuous or an uncontrolled boundary evaluation at t=0.

Section5 gives ell(E_F)=0; Section4 gives ell(h_0)!=0. The proper closed
hyperplane ker(ell) contains the closure of E_F. Nondensity follows.

For completeness, H_F is a closed holomorphic subspace of L2(mu):
local L2 convergence passes the distributional dbar equation because
the weight is locally bounded above and below. The separating argument
does not require an assumed dense core or integration by parts.
