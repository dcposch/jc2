### 2.4 `LIPSHITZ-CODOMAIN` (mine) — refuted at its stated target (packet Q3)

**Exact fragment.** Lipshitz, *The Diophantine problem for addition and
divisibility*, TAMS 235 (1978), proves the **existential** theory of

it.** That is a precision failure in my own card, recorded.

**Can divisibility encode forbidden multiplication? Yes — and it kills the
card.** Julia Robinson (1949): multiplication is first-order definable in
`⟨N, +, |⟩`, so the full first-order theory is undecidable. Decidability
survives only in the `∃`-fragment. Now the card's headline promise was
"coverage becomes entailment; 'no cell escapes' becomes an unsatisfiability
check". Entailment `φ ⊨ ψ` between existential formulas is a `∀∃` sentence.
**`∀∃` is exactly outside the decidable fragment**, and is where undecidability
for addition-with-divisibility begins. The mechanism's headline application
leaves the fragment by construction.

**Exact salvage boundary.** Coverage-failure
`∃x (χ(x) ∧ ⋀_i ¬φ_i(x))` is existential **iff every record family `φ_i` is
quantifier-free**. AP families qualify: `ν ≡ a (mod b)` is the quantifier-free
atom `b | ν − a`. My own headline witness does not: the U2 one-P0 family
`ν = tK` with `t ≡ 5 (mod 6)` and both parameters free is
`∃t (t | ν ∧ 6 | t−5)` — genuinely existential, so its negation is universal and
coverage-failure becomes `∃∀`. **The single construct I cited as the reason to
adopt the fragment is the construct that ejects the application from it.** My
stop condition said "two or more"; the correct threshold for the coverage
application is one.

**Residual, honestly sized.** The `∃`-fragment still decides *nonemptiness /
membership* of an individual family record — real, but far smaller than
