## 1. The theorem

Let

```text
k = Q,
S = k[rho,X],
T = {u in k[rho] : u(0) != 0},
S_(rho) = T^(-1)S,
K = J:a1^infinity,
J0 = J|(rho=0).
```

Every variable in `X` has positive sigma weight, `a1` is one of those
variables, `rho` has weight zero, and `J` is sigma-homogeneous.  For the
frozen literal-total grade-through-19 source, `|X|=66`; `ez9` is a
positive-weight general-fibre variable and becomes a polynomial spectator
after `rho=0`.

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1. `K+(rho)=S`.
2. There are `N>=0` and `U(rho) in k[rho]` such that
   `U(0)=1` and `a1^N U(rho) in J`.
3. `a1 in sqrt(J S_(rho))`.
4. Both
   `a1 in sqrt(J k(rho)[X])` and `a1 in sqrt(J0)`.
5. Both dehomogenized ideals are units:

   ```text
   J|(a1=1)  = (1) in k(rho)[X\{a1}],
   J0|(a1=1) = (1) in k[X\{a1}].
   ```

If `J` is invariant under `rho -> -rho`, as the frozen source is, condition
2 may be required with `U(rho)=V(rho^2)`.

This is a theorem over the exact rings displayed above.  No algebraic
closure, point sampling, or generic-flatness heuristic is part of the
statement.

## 2. Proof, with the two review repairs

### 2.1 Saturation versus the local coefficient ring

The reviewed weight-zero projection proves `1 <=> 2`: from
`1=k0+rho*h`, `k0 in K`, take the sigma-weight-zero component.  Because
`rho` is the only weight-zero variable, this produces a polynomial
`U(rho) in K intersect k[rho]` with `U(0)=1`; saturation supplies `N`.
The reverse implication is immediate after reducing modulo `rho`.

Condition 2 is exactly condition 3: membership in the localized radical
means that some `u in T` and some `N` satisfy `u a1^N in J`; rationally
rescale `u` so that `u(0)=1`.

### 2.2 The only surviving coefficient primes

The hostile review's conclusion is correct, but its sentence classifying
all primes of `Q[rho]` as `(0)` or `(rho-c)` is not correct over `Q`: there
are also primes generated by irreducible polynomials of degree greater than
one.  The clean repair is direct.

Every nonzero prime of `Q[rho]` is `(p(rho))` for an irreducible `p`.  It is
disjoint from `T` exactly when `p(0)=0`, which by irreducibility is exactly
`p` associated to `rho`.  Hence the primes of `T^(-1)Q[rho]` are precisely
`(0)` and `(rho)`.  Therefore every prime over `J S_(rho)` lies either over
the generic point or over the closed special point, and

```text
a1 in sqrt(J S_(rho))
  <=> a1 in sqrt(J k(rho)[X]) and a1 in sqrt(J0).
```

This prime argument includes embedded primes and nilpotent thickenings.
Fibres over every nonzero algebraic value of `rho`, not merely rational
values, have been inverted.

### 2.3 Graded dehomogenization lemma

Let `F` be any field, `R=F[a,Y]` positively graded, `wt(a)=d>0`, and
`I subset R` homogeneous.  Then

```text
a in sqrt(I)  <=>  I|(a=1)=(1).
```

The forward direction is substitution.  Conversely, in `A=R/I`, the
right-hand side says that `a-1` is a unit.  Write a finite graded inverse
`u=sum u_n` with `(a-1)u=1`.  Degree comparison gives `u_0=-1`,
`u_n=a u_(n-d)` for `n>0`, and zero in nonmultiples of `d`.  If `u_md`
is its last nonzero homogeneous component, the next equation gives
`a u_md=0`, hence `a^(m+1)=0` in `A`.

This replaces the invalid shorthand that scaled `a1` to one over `Q` or
`Q(rho)` and invoked the Nullstellensatz there.  Those fields need not
contain the required fifth roots and are not algebraically closed.  The
graded proof works over the actual computation fields.

Applying the lemma with `F=k(rho)` and `F=k` proves `4 <=> 5`.

### 2.4 Even cofactor

If `J` is invariant under `rho -> -rho`, average a condition-2 identity
with its image under the involution.  The cofactor remains normalized at
zero and becomes even.  This parity is useful for the typed certificate but
is not a hypothesis of the two-fibre equivalence.

