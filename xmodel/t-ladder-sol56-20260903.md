### 1.2 Ring and variable map

Throughout the algebraic sections,

\[
R=k[\gamma,\pi],\qquad
J(F,G)=F_\gamma G_\pi-F_\pi G_\gamma,
\]

where \(k\) has characteristic zero.  In the drivers the declared map is
\(\gamma\mapsto x,\ \pi\mapsto y\), and coefficient comparison occurs in
\(k[\text{parameters}][x,y]\).  Moh's degree order is
\(n=\deg g>m=\deg f\); thus the task's high-degree \(P\) is Moh's \(g\), and
the lower-degree \(Q\) is Moh's \(f\); its sign is absorbed in \(C\).

Moh's Appendix II also names the remainder in
\(\beta^2=\alpha h+\gamma\) by the letter \(\gamma\).  That is not the
Prop.6.3 coordinate.  It is denoted \(\Gamma\) below.  Primes on
\(n',M_i',\delta_i'\) are labels, not derivatives.

### 1.3 Leading form: what it gives and what it does not

**PROVED-HERE / UNREVIEWED.**  Suppose

\[
\deg P=\deg_\pi P=n',\quad
\deg Q=\deg_\pi Q=m',\quad
J(P,Q)=c\gamma^k,\quad k<n'+m'-2.
\]

The degree-\(n'+m'-2\) part of the Jacobian is
\(J(L_P,L_Q)\), so it vanishes.  For homogeneous binary forms in
characteristic zero, Euler's identity and one-variable dehomogenization give
\(L_P^{m'}=\lambda L_Q^{n'}\).  Unique factorization then gives, after monic
normalization,

\[
L_P=H_0^p,\qquad L_Q=H_0^q,\qquad
d=\gcd(n',m'),\quad p=n'/d,\quad q=m'/d.
\]

This identifies a common *leading form* \(H_0\).  It does not determine a
full polynomial approximate root \(h\).  Once a monic \(h\) of
\(\pi\)-degree \(d\) is fixed, Euclidean division gives unique expansions

\[
P=\sum_{i=0}^{p}A_i h^i,qquad
Q=\sum_{j=0}^{q}B_j h^j,qquad
\deg_\pi A_i,\deg_\pi B_j<d.
\]

Without the remainder bounds the expansion is nonunique: a multiple of
\(T-h\) may be moved between adjacent digits.  Choosing \(h\) as a normalized
approximate root can kill one penultimate digit; it does not automatically
kill both.  Indeed replacing \(h\) by
\(h+A_{p-1}/p\) kills \(A_{p-1}\), while the other penultimate digit becomes
\(B_{q-1}-(q/p)A_{p-1}\).

## 2. The exact series rung

### 2.1 What Moh proves

**SOURCE-READ.**  Moh's Lemma 2.1, printed p.151, assumes a *constant*
Jacobian.  With

\[
g=\eta^{-n},\qquad
f=\eta^{-m}+\sum_{j>-m}f_j(x)\eta^j,
\]

it says that \(J(f,g)\in k^\times\) exactly when the derivatives of the
digits below \(n-1\) vanish and \(f'_{n-1}\) is a nonzero constant.  The
source does not state the monomial-Jacobian version and does not state an
\(h\)-adic version.

### 2.2 Monomial extension

**PROVED-HERE / UNREVIEWED.**  Let \(f,g\in k[\gamma,\pi]\) be monic in
\(\pi\), of \(\pi\)-degrees \(m,n\), put
\(\eta=g^{-1/n}=\pi^{-1}+O(\pi^{-2})\), and expand

\[
f=\eta^{-m}+\sum_{j>-m} f_j(\gamma)\eta^j.
\]

Then, for \(c\ne0\),

\[
J(f,g)=c\gamma^k
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
f_j\in k,&j<n-1,\\[2mm]
f_{n-1}=\dfrac{c}{n(k+1)}\gamma^{k+1}+a,&a\in k.
\end{cases}
\]

At fixed \(\eta\), the chain rule gives
\(J(f,g)=f_\gamma|_\eta\,g_\pi\), and \(g_\pi\) has
\(\eta\)-order \(-(n-1)\) and leading coefficient \(n\).  A nonzero
polynomial of \(\pi\)-degree \(D\) has \(\eta\)-order \(-D\).  This proves
the equivalence and its exact leading scalar.

In the dual convention \(\xi=f^{-1/m}\),

\[
g=\xi^{-n}+\sum g_j(\gamma)\xi^j,
\]

one obtains

\[
g_j\in k\ (j<m-1),\qquad
g_{m-1}=-\frac{c}{m(k+1)}\gamma^{k+1}+a.
\]

Thus the charged “degree \(k+1\)” iff is too weak: degree alone permits lower
\(\gamma\)-terms and the wrong leading scalar.  Moh p.210 is a useful
source control for \(k=2\): its first nonconstant displayed series digit is
at index \(m-1=9\) and contains \(x^3\).  It is not a proof that canonical
\(h\)-digits obey the same rule.

## 3. General \(h\)-adic Jacobian equations

### 3.1 Formal identity

**PROVED-HERE / UNREVIEWED.**  Put

\[
F(T)=\sum_i A_iT^i,\qquad G(T)=\sum_jB_jT^j,\qquad
\delta=[h,-].
\]

The typed evaluation map is

\[
\operatorname{ev}_h:R[T]\longrightarrow R,qquad T\longmapsto h,
\]

with \(T\) treated as independent while differentiating coefficients.  Then

\[
[P,Q]=\operatorname{ev}_h\bigl([F,G]_R+F_T\delta G-G_T\delta F\bigr).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
[P,Q]=\sum_{\ell\ge0}C_\ell h^\ell,
\]

where, with out-of-range digits set to zero,

\[
C_\ell=
\sum_{i+j=\ell}[A_i,B_j]
+\sum_{i+j=\ell+1}
\left(iA_i[h,B_j]-jB_j[h,A_i]\right).
\tag{H}
\]

For example,

\[
C_0=[A_0,B_0]+A_1[h,B_0]-B_1[h,A_0].
\]

These are *raw* coefficients in an independent symbol before \(T=h\).
Actual \(h\)-adic comparison needs carries.

### 3.2 Canonical carry recursion

Set \(K_0=0\).  Recursively perform monic Euclidean division in
\(k[\gamma][\pi]\):

\[
C_\ell+K_\ell=R_\ell+hK_{\ell+1},qquad
\deg_\pi R_\ell<d.
\tag{C}
\]

Continue through every terminal carry.  Then

\[
[P,Q]=\sum_{\ell\ge0}R_\ell h^\ell,
\]

and the exact level-by-level condition is

\[
J(P,Q)=c\gamma^k
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R_0=c\gamma^k,\qquad R_\ell=0\quad(\ell\geq1).
\tag{L}
\]

It is generally invalid to impose \(C_0=c\gamma^k\) and \(C_\ell=0\)
coefficientwise: products and brackets of digits can have
\(\pi\)-degree at least \(d\) and feed higher levels via (C).  Formally, the
Jacobian condition says that the independent-\(T\) expression differs from
\(c\gamma^k\) by a multiple of \(T-h\); it does not say its \(T\)-coefficients
vanish.

Even in an added no-carry model, the top-down equations usually control one
combination per rung.  Writing \(a_r=A_{p-r}\), \(b_r=B_{q-r}\), with
\(a_0=b_0=1\), the raw level \(p+q-r-1\) has the form

\[
C_{p+q-r-1}=
\sum_{a+b=r+1}[a_a,b_b]
+\sum_{a+b=r}
\bigl((p-a)a_a\delta b_b-(q-b)b_b\delta a_a\bigr).
\]

If \(a_1=0\) and \(b_1\in k\), it reduces to
\(\delta(pb_r-qa_r)+\Psi_r\), with \(\Psi_r\) involving earlier levels.
It does not separately make \(a_r\) and \(b_r\) constant.  A further
centralizer hypothesis such as
\(\ker[h,-]=k[h]\) would turn a lower-\(\pi\)-degree element in the kernel
into a constant, but that hypothesis is neither automatic nor sufficient to
separate the two digits.

### 3.3 Correction of the charged \((2,3)\) display

For

\[
f=h^2+2\beta,\qquad g=h^3+G_1h+G_0,
\]

direct Leibniz expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
J(f,g)={}&h^2\bigl(2J(h,G_1)+6J(\beta,h)\bigr)\\
&+h\bigl(2J(h,G_0)+2J(\beta,G_1)\bigr)\\
&+2G_1J(\beta,h)+2J(\beta,G_0).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2,3}
\]

The charged formula shifts \(6J(\beta,h)\) from the \(h^2\)-coefficient to
the \(h\)-coefficient and omits \(2J(\beta,G_1)\).  Actual minus charged is

\[
6(h^2-h)J(\beta,h)+2hJ(\beta,G_1).
\]

For canonical digits of \(\pi\)-degree \(<d\), \(d\geq2\), set

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_0&=2G_1J(\beta,h)+2J(\beta,G_0),\\
C_1&=2J(h,G_0)+2J(\beta,G_1),\\
C_2&=2J(h,G_1)+6J(\beta,h).
\end{aligned}
\]

The exact levels are

\[
C_0=R_0+hK_1,\quad C_1+K_1=R_1+hK_2,\quad
C_2+K_2=R_2+hK_3.
\]

Here \(\deg_\pi K_3\leq d-2<d\), so the possible fourth terminal digit is
\(R_3=K_3\).  Thus the target requires
\(R_0=c\gamma^k,\ R_1=R_2=K_3=0\); three raw equations do not suffice.

The exact control

\[
h=\pi,\quad\beta=\gamma/2,\quad G_1=3\gamma/2,\quad G_0=0
\]

has \(J(f,g)=3\gamma/2\), whereas the charged right side is
\(-3\pi^2+3\pi+3\gamma/2\).

In Moh's specialization \(G_1=3\beta,\ G_0=3\alpha/2\), the \(h^2\)-terms
cancel.  If \(\beta^2=\alpha h+\Gamma\), (2,3) simplifies further to

\[
J(f,g)=3\bigl(J(\beta,\alpha)-J(h,\Gamma)\bigr).
\]

Even this expression must be interpreted through exact polynomial identities,
not by treating unreduced powers of \(h\) as independent.

## 5. Uniform negative control

### 5.1 Construction

**PROVED-HERE / UNREVIEWED.**  Fix

\[
k\geq 0,\qquad p,r\geq 2,\qquad e>k+1,
\]

and an arbitrary monic polynomial

\[
\phi(T)=T^e+a_{e-1}T^{e-1}+\cdots+a_0.
\]

Set

\[
X=\gamma+\pi,qquad
Y=\frac{\gamma^{k+1}}{k+1},qquad
U=Y+\phi(X),qquad
H=X+U^r,
\]

and

\[
P=H^p+U,qquad Q=H.
\]

In the independent coordinates \(X,Y\),
\(J_{X,Y}(P,Q)=-1\), while
\(J_{\gamma,\pi}(X,Y)=-\gamma^k\).  Therefore

\[
J_{\gamma,\pi}(P,Q)=\gamma^k.
\]

Let \(d=re\).  Since \(e>k+1\) and \(\phi\) is monic,

\[
\deg H=\deg_\pi H=d,qquad
\deg P=\deg_\pi P=pd,qquad
\deg Q=\deg_\pi Q=d,
\]

and all three relevant leaders are monic in \(\pi\).  The canonical expansion
has reduced pair \((p,q)=(p,1)\):

\[
P=H^p+A_0,\quad A_0=U,\qquad Q=H,\qquad
\deg_\pi U=e<d.
\]

The \(H^{p-1}\) coefficient is zero, so \(H\) is the normalized \(p\)-th
approximate root of \(P\), not merely a convenient monic lift.

There is exactly one nonconstant outer digit, but

\[
\deg_\gamma A_0=e=d/r>k+1.
\]

This disproves the global degree clause for every \(k\), not only for Keller
pairs.

### 5.2 Keller case and growing data

At \(k=0\), \(Y=\gamma\), and every step is an elementary polynomial
automorphism.  An explicit inverse is

\[
U=P-Q^p,qquad X=Q-U^r,qquad
\gamma=U-\phi(X),qquad \pi=X-\gamma.
\]

Thus this is a Keller automorphism in Moh's monic
total-degree-\(=\pi\)-degree gauge for every \(e\).  It strengthens the
two-shear \((y+x^2,x+(y+x^2)^3)\) control: for that basic pair the
growing data can be hidden inside the chosen approximate root; the added
outer shear exposes it as \(A_0=U\).

The exact ladder output depends on what “coefficient” means:

- **Outer digits:** one nonconstant digit, independent of \(d\).  This part
  does not fail; it is the degree claim that fails.

- **Inner \(\pi\)-coefficient functions:** expanding
  
  \[
  U=\frac{\gamma^{k+1}}{k+1}+\phi(\gamma+\pi)
   =\sum_{b=0}^{e}u_b(\gamma)\pi^b,
  \]
  
  every \(u_b\) for \(b<e\) has nonzero leading term
  \(\binom eb\gamma^{e-b}\).  Hence \(e=\Theta(d)\) inner coefficients are
  nonconstant, their largest \(\gamma\)-degree is \(e\), and
  \(a_0,\ldots,a_{e-1}\) give \(e\) free scalar parameters.

This is the requested exact negative-control statement.  “Automorphisms
exist in every degree” alone would not refute a literal outer-index bound;
the displayed family identifies the unbounded degree and inner-data growth.

### 5.3 Boundary of the disproof

The counterfamily has \(q=1\).  It does **not** decide
`OPEN[T-LADDER-q>=2]`, in particular the \((3,2)\) and \((4,3)\) descended
strata.  Polynomial automorphisms naturally occupy this divisibility
boundary, so no conclusion for \(q\geq2\) is drawn by analogy.

There is nevertheless a second obstruction to the advertised “finite
computation.”  Even if only \(B=B(k,p,q)\) outer digits are nonconstant and
each satisfies \(\deg_\gamma\leq k+1\), the canonical condition only says
\(\deg_\pi<d\).  One such digit can contain up to
\((k+2)d\) scalar monomials.  A bounded number of such digits is still
\(\Theta(d)\) input, unless a separate theorem bounds or determines their
\(\pi\)-profiles.

## 6. Reading: consequences for theorem (T)

### 6.1 No degree-independent finite computation follows

The answer to the unrestricted reading question is **no**:

- on the outer-index reading the count is trivially bounded by \(p+q\), but
  the \(\gamma\)-degree clause is false;
- on the scalar/inner-coefficient reading the number grows as
  \(\Theta(d)\) in the explicit family;
- even the conjectured \(\gamma\)-degree bound would leave
  \(\Theta(d)\) scalar coefficients per bounded collection of exceptional

Therefore theorem (T) cannot currently be stated as one finite computation
independent of \(d\).  For the intended \(q\geq2\) descended rows the honest

### 6.2 Radii constrain the support of \(h\); they do not determine \(h\)

\(b_1,\ldots,b_4\) free in the \((16,12)\) approximate root.  P.211 leaves

\(h\).  The charged Opus statement that \(h\) is “determined” by
