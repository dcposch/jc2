is **not generic**. Two independent derivations:

*Fibre count.* Over `u = c`, the points of `D'` are `q(t)` with `r(t) = c`: three, since

lose all the structure of §3.)

## 3. Braid monodromy factorization constraints

### 3.1 The pullback theorem

**Theorem ZVK-PULLBACK (PROVED-HERE).** *Let `bm_D : π₁(C_x−Σ_x, x_*) → B₆` and
`bm_U : π₁(C_u−Σ_u, u_*) → B₆` be the braid monodromies of the `x`-projection of `D` and
the `u`-projection of `U_6`, with `x_* = u_*²` and the fibres identified by `ν`. Then*

```text
   bm_U = bm_D ∘ π_*  ,      π : C_u − Σ_u → C_x − Σ_x  the unbranched double cover u ↦ u².
```

*Proof.* `Σ_u = π^{-1}(Σ_x)` and `0 ∈ Σ_x`, so the branch point is deleted and `π` is an
unbranched double cover. The family of `6`-point configurations `U_6 ∩ {u=c}` is the
`π`-pullback of `D ∩ {x=c²}` — literally the same points, since `ν` is the identity on
`y`. Monodromy of a pullback is the pullback of the monodromy. `[]`

Write `B_D := Im(bm_D)`, `B_U := Im(bm_U)`. Since `π_*π₁(C_u−Σ_u)` is the kernel of
`π₁(C_x−Σ_x) → Z/2` (winding number around `x=0` mod 2),

```text
   B_U = bm_D( ker )  ⊆  B_D  ,     B_D = ⟨ B_U , β₀ ⟩ ,   β₀ := bm_D(γ₀) ,
```

`γ₀` a small loop about `x=0`. `π₁(C_x−Σ_x)` is free on `γ₀,…,γ₅`; the kernel is free of
rank `2·6−1 = 11` on

```text
   γ₀² ,     γ_k ,     γ₀γ_kγ₀^{-1}      (k = 1,…,5) .                     (3.1)
```

**The 11 discriminant values upstairs are exactly these 11 free generators.** ✓

By Zariski–van Kampen, for a curve which is an `n`-section, `π₁(C² − C) = F_n / N` with
`N` generated by `g_i^{-1}·(bm(γ)·g_i)` over all `γ` — i.e. `N` depends only on the
*image subgroup*. Hence, writing `β_k := bm_D(γ_k)`:

```text
   G := π₁(C²−D)   = ⟨ g₁..g₆ | β₀·T=T ,  β_k·T=T ⟩ ,
   Γ := π₁(C²−U_6) = ⟨ g₁..g₆ | β₀²·T=T , β_k·T=T , β₀β_kβ₀^{-1}·T=T ⟩ ,
```

`T = (g₁,…,g₆)` and `·` the Hurwitz action. `Γ ↠ G` (charged Proposition 3) is now
transparent: `B_U ⊆ B_D`.

### 3.4 Why the within-component collapse does not propagate

`π₁(C²−D') = Z` (charged §4.1, from promoted N-A) says: for any group `H`, every
`3`-tuple in `H³` fixed by the Hurwitz action of `bm_{D'}(π₁(C_u−Σ'))` has all three
entries **equal** — such a tuple is a homomorphism `π₁(C²−D') = Z → H`, and the three
meridians all map to the image of the generator. This is the "relations within a
component collapse" of the charge, and it is exact.

The obstruction to using it is precise and worth stating, because it is what the
successor must overcome. `D'`'s own ZvK uses the **3**-point fibre `C_y ∖ (D'∩{u=c})`, so
its conjugating words lie in `F(g_{1+},g_{2+},g_{3+})`. In `Γ` the same geometric paths
live in `C_y ∖` **6** points, and each time a path passes a `D'^-`-point it acquires a
letter `g_{j-}`. Thus `β_1,…,β_5 ∈ B₆` are *lifts* of `bm_{D'}`'s factors, and the
relation they impose is the `D'`-relation with extra `D'^-`-letters inserted. Setting

transposition-valued `ψ` cannot set them to `1`. **The collapse is available only if the
inserted letters are known — i.e. only with the factorization.**

### 3.5 A trap: `β₀` is *not* the tube-diagonal of the cable

It is tempting to write `β₀ = σ₁σ₃σ₅` in the *cable* basis of FIXED-TUPLE §2, where
`σ_{2i-1}` is the internal half-twist of tube `i`. That identification is **not
justified**, and for at least one row member it is false. The `x=0` matching pairs the
two points that collide at `x → 0` (the two roots of `r(·) = ±√x` near the same `τ_i`);
the `∞`-tube matching pairs the two points that are close as `x → ∞` (the `η ↔ −η`
pairs). Take the member `b = 0`, `c = 1`, so `r = t³+1`, and transport along the positive
real `u`-axis with a detour above the branch value `u = 1`. Writing
`Λ = (u−1)^{1/3}`, `M = (−u−1)^{1/3}` with `arg Λ(0) = arg M(0) = π/3`, one finds
`arg Λ(R) = 0` (the argument of `u−1` falls by `π`) while `arg M(R) = π/3` (the argument
of `−u−1` never moves). Consequently the pair that started together at the root `−1`
arrives as `(t,s) = (ωR^{1/3}, −R^{1/3})`, whereas the `∞`-tube containing `t = ωR^{1/3}`
is `{ωR^{1/3}, −ωR^{1/3}}`. All three transported pairs are likewise **transverse** to
the tubes, cyclically. So along this path `β₀`'s matching is not the tube matching.

Since a change of path conjugates `β₀` but fixes `ρ_∞`, the relative position of `β₀` and
the cable tubes is exactly the datum a Hurwitz-move search must supply. Typed
`GAP[BETA0-TUBE-POSITION]`; consumed nowhere below.

## 4. The `S₄` conditions and the fold-pullback constraints

### 4.1 The conditions carried by the fibre `u = 0` / `x = 0`

```text

 (D0)   t₁ = t₂ ,  t₃ = t₄ ,  t₅ = t₆        (downstairs) .
```

**(D0) is the fold-pullback condition, derived, not assumed.** Let `N_τ` be a cross-point

charged Proposition 3 — `ψ(μ_1) = ψ(μ_2)`. Consistency check: `ν|_{B∖U_6} → ν(B)∖D` is a
double cover branched along `L₀∖{pt}`, and `ν_* : Z² → Z` is `(a,b) ↦ a+b`, surjective,

cross-point is **equality**, not merely commutation — exactly (D0), matching the abstract
derivation via `β₀` in §3.2. Two derivations, one answer.

### 4.2 The condition at `Q'`

`Q' ∈ Ū_6` is a double point of two smooth branches with contact `13`
(charged §2.3, Bézout `16 = 3 + 13`; equivalently `deg_ζ Y_odd = −5` here). Its local

```text
   ψ(a), ψ(b) equal or disjoint ;   Π := ψ(a)ψ(b) ∈ {e} ∪ (V ∖ {e}) .
```

Under the fold pullback `Π = e` (both branches at `Q'` map to the single place of `D` at
`Q_D`), so the generic-fibre product of the six transpositions is `e`, `c(Π)=4`, and the

the downstairs `Π` is the monodromy of the *line* `x = c`, which meets `D̄` at `Q_D`

At each of the six self-nodes of `U_6`, the relation is a commutation, so the two

from the fold" of the charge; it is consumed at PROVISIONAL scope and is used only in the
Euler-characteristic check of §4.4, never in §5's enumeration.

## 5. The finite system and its exhaustive solution

### 5.1 The system

Unknowns: `T = (t_1,…,t_6)`, transpositions in `S₄`, indexed by the `β₀`-matching as in
§4. Equations:

```text
 (E0)  [t₁,t₂] = [t₃,t₄] = [t₅,t₆] = 1                              (β₀², upstairs)
 (E0') t₁ = t₂ , t₃ = t₄ , t₅ = t₆                                  (β₀, downstairs only)
 (E1)  W_1 t_{a₁} W_1^{-1} = t_{b₁} ,  W_2 t_{a₂} W_2^{-1} = t_{b₂}      (2 tangencies)
 (E1') W̄_1 t_{ā₁} W̄_1^{-1} = t_{b̄₁} ,  W̄_2 t_{ā₂} W̄_2^{-1} = t_{b̄₂}   (β₀-mirrors)
 (E2)  [ V_i t_{c_i} V_i^{-1} , t_{d_i} ] = 1 ,  i = 1,2,3               (3 self-nodes)
```

`W_i, V_i ∈ S₄` are `ψ`-images of words in `F₆` determined by the conjugating paths of
`β_1,…,β_5`; the barred data are the `β₀`-conjugates. Upstairs imposes
`(E0)+(E1)+(E1')+(E2)+(E2')+(E3)`; downstairs replaces `(E0)` by `(E0')`, after which
`(E1')`,`(E2')` become consequences of `(E1)`,`(E2)`.

**Reduction Theorem (PROVED-HERE).**
`{T : G-admissible} = {T : Γ-admissible and t₁=t₂, t₃=t₄, t₅=t₆}`.
*Proof.* `⊆`: `B_U ⊆ B_D` and `β₀·T = T` forces the pair equalities. `⊇`: `B_D =
⟨B_U,β₀⟩` (§3.1), and the pair equalities give `β₀·T = T`. `[]`

Hence: a NO on the upstairs system kills the row; a YES upstairs decides nothing unless
the witness has all three pairs equal (charged §3(i), re-derived and sharpened).

### 5.2 Exhaustive solution of the sub-system `(E0) + (E3)` and `(E0') + (E3)`

This is the part of the system that the fold supplies and that is complete at desk scale.
Two transpositions in `S₄` commute iff they are equal or complementary (disjoint), so each
pair `(t_{2i-1},t_{2i})` is `(f_i,f_i)` or `(f_i,\bar f_i)` — `6·2 = 12` options, `12³ =
1728` tuples satisfying `(E0)`. `(E3)` holds iff the edge set of `T` on `{1,2,3,4}` is
connected. Enumerate by `k :=` the number of complementary ("disjoint") pairs; a
complementary pair contributes a perfect matching `M(f)` of `K₄`, of which there are 3.

```text
 k=3 : 6³ = 216 ordered edge-triples; disconnected ⟺ all three matchings equal:
       3·2³ = 24 ;                                              192
 k=2 : position of the equal pair ×3 ; M(f_a)≠M(f_b): 24·6 = 144 ,
       M(f_a)=M(f_b): 12·4 = 48  ⟹ 192 per position ;           576
 k=1 : position ×3 ; 6·(36−4) = 192 per position ;              576
 k=0 : ordered triples of edges forming a spanning tree of K₄:
       16 trees × 3! = 96 ;                                      96
 ------------------------------------------------------------------
 total  (E0)+(E3) :                                            1440
 of which (E0')+(E3) (k = 0) :                                    96
```

**The enumeration is complete**: it is a partition of the `(E0)`-solution set by `k` and
each class is counted by an exhaustive case split on `K₄`'s 6 edges and 3 perfect
matchings; every tuple satisfying `(E0)` lies in exactly one class. So `1440` tuples
survive `(E0)+(E3)` upstairs, exactly `96` of them survive `(E0')+(E3)` downstairs, and
the ratio `15` is the full quantitative content of "what the fold buys".

### 5.3 What the remaining equations do, and what blocks them

`(E1)`–`(E2')` are ten equations in `S₄` whose *conjugators* `W_i, V_i, W̄_i, V̄_i` are not
determined by any charged input: they are the ZvK conjugating words of the five nonzero
factors `β_1,…,β_5`, i.e. precisely the content of `OPEN[PI1S4-(6,4)-FACTORIZATION]`.
Every individual equation of the form `W t_a W^{-1} = t_b` is satisfiable for arbitrary
transpositions `t_a,t_b` (all transpositions are conjugate), so **no subset of
`(E1)`–`(E2')` constrains `T` until the words are known**. This is not a shortfall of
effort: §3.4 identifies exactly why (the `D'`-collapse to `Z` is destroyed by the
`D'^-`-letters that the six-point fibre inserts), and §3.5 shows the one natural guess
that would have supplied them is false.

### 5.4 One unconditional consequence: the resolvent theorem

**Theorem ZVK-RESOLVENT (PROVED-HERE).** *If `π₁(C² − U_6) ↠ S₄` with all meridians
transpositions, then `π₁(C² − D) ↠ S₃` with all meridians transpositions.*

*Proof.* Compose with `S₄ → S₄/V = S₃`; transpositions go to transpositions, and
surjectivity is preserved. `S₃` has **no pair of disjoint transpositions**, so `(E0)`
becomes `\bar t₁ = \bar t₂`, `\bar t₃ = \bar t₄`, `\bar t₅ = \bar t₆`, i.e. `\bar T`
satisfies `(E0')` — it is fixed by `β₀`. It is fixed by `B_U` (the Hurwitz action commutes
with post-composition by a group homomorphism), hence by `B_D = ⟨B_U, β₀⟩`, hence defines
a homomorphism `G = F₆/N_D → S₃`. `[]`

Two corollaries, both new here. (i) A NO on `OPEN[PI1S4-(6,4)-TRIPLE-COVER]` — no
simply-branched `S₃`-cover of `C²` with branch curve `D` — kills **both** the row and the
upstairs `Γ ↠ S₄` question outright, without any braid data. (ii) The dichotomy: every
`Γ ↠ S₄` either has all three pairs equal, in which case it descends and `G ↠ S₄`
(the fold route is exhausted), or has some pair *disjoint*, in which case it is invisible
downstairs. There is no third case.
