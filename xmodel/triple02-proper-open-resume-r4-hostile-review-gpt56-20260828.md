## 7. Systemd emptiness and whole-cap enforcement: REPAIR

The advertised systemd empty-scope gate is:

```bash
if [[ -e "$cgroup_path/cgroup.procs" && -s "$cgroup_path/cgroup.procs" ]]; then
  systemd_empty_gate=0
fi
```

`-s` inspects a file's reported metadata size; it does not read the PID list.
`cgroup.procs` is a cgroupfs pseudo-file and cannot be audited this way.  The
gate must read and parse its content (and handle a collected/disappeared unit
under an explicit, separately verified inactive-unit rule).  This matters
because the systemd route passes `pgid=0` to the generic cleanup; its secondary
census is then job-tag-only and is not a substitute for enumerating the actual
cgroup.

The claimed 1,800-second supervisor bound also stops at an unbounded command:

```bash
wait "$launcher_pid"
```

After the monitor sends TERM and KILL there is no bounded reap loop or external
cap around this wait.  An uninterruptible launcher/scope can therefore keep the
supervisor alive indefinitely.  This failure is safer than publishing false
mathematics, but it violates the registered cap and prevents a complete
no-orphan terminal audit.

## 8. Terminal-manifest and archive replay: REPAIR

The terminal-manifest pipeline runs as a standalone command while the
supervisor deliberately lacks `set -e`:

```bash
(find ... | sort -z | xargs -0 sha256sum) > TERMINAL_MANIFEST.sha256
```

Its status is not captured.  A partial or failed pipeline therefore does not
force `archive_ready=0`.  The subsequent archive condition checks only:

- successful tar creation;
- successful `tar -tzf` listing;
- rename to the final archive name; and
- generation and checking of a new outer archive checksum.

There is no `sha256sum -c custody/TERMINAL_MANIFEST.sha256`, no extraction to a
fresh directory, and no post-extraction replay of that manifest.  The outer
hash proves that the just-created tar bytes remain unchanged during the next
command; it does not prove that the terminal manifest was complete or correct.
Thus the preflight report's “built, listed, hashed, and replayed terminal
archive” and the preregistration's “successfully replayed terminal archive” are
stronger than the implementation.

The public terminal separation itself is good: the archive contains the worker
candidate and gate evidence, not an already promoted mathematical marker, and
the final public marker is installed by one same-filesystem rename.  The defect
is the missing prerequisite replay, not multiple terminal authorities.
