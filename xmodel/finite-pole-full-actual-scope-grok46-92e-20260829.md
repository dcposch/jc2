## 2. Reconstruction of `a(P)`

Fix one fibre `f=a` and one physical component, the y-side unless noted.
Let `S={P_1,...,P_s}` be a finite nonempty set of g-pole places in that
component, with pole flags `Z_j=I_(P_j)(v_j)` (Notation 5.1 after the
promoted Proposition 5.1 repair; Notation 5.2).  Set

```text
U = union_(j=1..s) I_(P_j)([0, v_j]).
```

Definition 3.3 (printed p. 11) is the sole flag identification:

```text
(P,u) ~ (P*,u*)  iff  u=u* and u <= O(P,P*).
```

Notation 3.1: `I_P(u)=(P,u)_~`.  Unwinding gives the contact criterion

```text
I_P(t)=I_Q(t)  iff  t <= O(P,Q),
```

once `O(P,P):=infinity` is forced (`ladder/SIGRAY-AUDIT.md:19`).  The
predicate `t<=O(P,Q)` is downward-closed in `t` because `O(P,Q)` is one
height-independent value in `Q+ union {-1, infinity}` (Definition 3.2,
printed p. 10).  Therefore:

- rootward restriction: if two rays agree at height `t`, they agree at every
  smaller height;
- no remerging: if they differ at height `t`, they differ at every larger
  height;
- `{t: I_P(t)=I_Q(t)}` is a closed initial segment, empty iff `O=-1`.

Membership `I_P(t) in U` means: for some `j`, `t<=v_j` and
`I_P(t)=I_(P_j)(t)`, i.e. `t<=O(P,P_j)`.  Hence

```text
{t : I_P(t) in U} = [0, a(P)],
a(P) = max_(j=1..s) min(v_j, O(P,P_j)),
```

with the conventions `O(P,P_j)=infinity` when `P=P_j` and `[0,-1]=empty`.
This is an initial segment, so `U` is rootward closed.  Finite `s` makes the
maximum attained in `Q union {-1, infinity}`.

If `H=I_P(w)` lies outside `U`, then `w>a(P)` and the unique outermost
point of `rho_H cap U` is

```text
att_U(H) = I_P(a(P)).
```

The value `a(P)` is a pole height or a physical contact, hence a vertex
(Definition 3.4, printed p. 11), or the component root.  The flag
`I_P(a(P))` is unique because `I_P` is injective (`pi o I_P = id`).
Several indices `j` may attain the same maximum; they still determine one
flag on one ray.

The truncation `min(v_j,O)` is load-bearing.  At a pole endpoint one may
have `O(P,P_j)>v_j`; the relevant quantity is `v_j`, not the false assertion
`O=v_j`.  That is the selected-orbit endpoint defect, already closed at
two-pole scope, and it does not reappear when `s` grows.

## 3. Hostile attacks

### 3.1 Shared prefixes

`U` is a set of flags, not a multiset of path copies.  A trunk vertex lying
on every pole path from the root to a global meet contributes one element of
`U cap V_a`.  The formula `a(P)=max_j min(v_j,O)` already selects the
outermost contact among all prefixes.  No extra identification is required,
and nothing is charged once per incoming path.

### 3.2 Repeated endpoints

If the same pole place is listed twice, `U` is unchanged and the `max_j` is
redundant.  Distinct poles are distinct leaves of the chain tree (MP0:
poles have no chain predecessor).  Two poles cannot share a flag at their
endpoints: a common endpoint flag would force the two characteristic
sequences to remerge after splitting, contradicting Definition 3.3(ii).

### 3.3 Merge vertices, including `r>=3` and nested merges

A merge `G` is a vertex of `U` with at least two continuing pole
coefficients in

```text
Ch(G) = {Omega(P_j)_(pi G) : I_(P_j)(pi G)=G and v_j > pi G}.
```

For `s=2` one has `r(G)<=2`.  For `s>=3`, MP1 gives `sum (r-1)=s-1`, so
several merges, a star `r=s`, and nested inner/outer merges are all legal.
None of these changes the membership formula.  Continuing pole rays through
`G` are already quantified by the `max_j` and by `Ch(G)`.

Opus Theorem C1, preferred because it is grid-free, never uses `s=2`.  Let
`c in Ex(F)`: a realized coefficient at `F in U cap V_a` that is not in
`Ch(F)` and whose microchild is nearrow.  For every physical place `P`
through that child, `A_P=[0, pi(F)]`.  The inclusion `[0,pi(F)] subset A_P`
is rootward closure.  If some `t>pi(F)` lay in `A_P`, some pole index `k`
would satisfy `t<=v_k` and `t<=O(P,P_k)`, hence `Omega(P_k)` would agree
with `Omega(P)` at exponent `pi(F)`, the `k`-th pole ray would pass through
`F`, and `v_k>pi(F)`, i.e. `c in Ch(F)`, contradiction.  The quantifier is
“some `k`”, so it covers every finite number of continuing pole rays,
including nested topology.

Nested re-entry attempt, the only `s>=3`-specific diagram worth drawing.
Poles `P1,P2` merge at inner `G1`; that trunk meets `P3` at outer `G2`,
with `G2 < G1` in the rootward order:

```text
P1 -- ... -- G1 -- ... -- G2 -- ... -- (0,y)
P2 -- ... -- G1
P3 -- ... -- G2
```

An exit child at `G1` is outward from `G1`.  The third pole path lives on
the other branch from `G2`, which is rootward of `G1`.  Descendants of an
outward child travel away from the root; they cannot travel down through
`G1` to `G2` and up the third branch.  Unique rootward path (Corollary A4
of the Opus packet) forbids that.

### 3.4 Pole arrivals

### 3.6 Descendants that might re-enter `U`

Because `{t: I_P(t) in U}` is an initial segment, a ray that has left `U`
cannot re-enter it.  Equivalently: `U` is rootward closed and every point
has a unique rootward path, so `rho_H cap U` is a closed initial subsegment
of `rho_H`.  If `G_c` is an off-`U` microchild at height `u+1/K`, every
descendant has height `>u` and therefore lies outside `U`.  Identifying a
later descendant with a flag already in `U` would require
`I_Q(w)=I_P(w) in U` with `w>a(Q)`, contradicting the formula for `a(Q)`.

The MFE abstract countermodel (two distinct children, later one cv flag,
both incidences retained) is exactly the negation of Definition 3.3(ii).
It remains a valid proof that vertex-level MP0 does not discharge the
claim.  It is not a model of the available source.
