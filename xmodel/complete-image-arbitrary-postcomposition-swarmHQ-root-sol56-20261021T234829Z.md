## Statement and scope

Work over C. Let U,S be smooth integral algebraic surfaces and f:U->S a dominant
morphism. Let C0 be an irreducible positive-dimensional curve in U, and E a
complete irreducible reduced curve in S. Assume f(C0)=E set-theoretically and
f is etale at every point of C0.

**Claim FINITEPOST-COMPLETE.** There is no rational pair g=(p,q):S-->A2 such that
g composed f extends to a regular quasi-finite morphism H:U->A2.
Consequently, when U is an open subset of the source A2 containing C0, no such
composition extends to a polynomial Keller map on the entire source plane.

It suffices that every point of E have at least one etale preimage in C0.
There is no field-generation or birationality requirement on g, no chosen
two-form, no projectivity/rationality assumption on S, and no properness
assumption on f. The all-points etale-preimage hypothesis is essential to the
proof; it is an additional hypothesis compared with the earlier chart theorem.

## Proof

For y in E choose x in C0 with f(x)=y. Put A=O_(S,y), B=O_(U,x).
Etaleness makes A->B flat; a flat local homomorphism is faithfully flat.
Dominance embeds K(S) into K(U). For r=p or q write r=a/b in Frac(A), b!=0.
Its pullback equals the appropriate coordinate h of H in B, so a=bh in B.
Faithful flatness gives bB intersect A=bA. Thus a=bc for c in A and r=c in A.

To justify the contraction directly, A/(b)->B/(b) is injective under faithful
flatness. If a nonzero cyclic submodule generated by a mod b mapped to zero,
flatness would embed its tensor product in B/(b), while faithfulness would
make that tensor product nonzero, a contradiction.

Both p and q are therefore regular at every y in E. Their common regularity
locus contains an open neighborhood W of E. A regular function on a complete
irreducible reduced curve over C is constant, even if the curve is nodal:
one may pull it back to the complete normalization. Thus g(E) is a point.
On f^(-1)(W), H and g composed f agree on a dense open and hence everywhere,
by integrality and separatedness of A2. Consequently H contracts C0, contrary
to quasi-finiteness. A polynomial Keller map is etale and quasi-finite, giving
the stated consequence. QED.

## Dependencies and conditional application

The earlier [complete-image chart theorem](complete-image-chart-review-swarmHQ-root-sol56-20261021T112534Z.md)
excludes field-generating birational Darboux postpairs without assuming that
the donor is etale. Its proof uses a zero-free two-form and birational extension.
This new proof trades the additional etale-preimage hypothesis for freedom
from those restrictions on the postpair. Neither statement subsumes the other
without checking its hypotheses.

In the earlier report's conditional nodal application (lines97--114), a
component-rotating quotient is assumed finite etale near an entire relevant
fiber and degree one from each component to the normalization of the nodal
target curve. The chart deletes exactly one of the two node preimages.
The other preimage retains the node, so the chart curve covers the ENTIRE
complete nodal curve. Under precisely these assumptions, FINITEPOST-COMPLETE
excludes EVERY rational postpair making the composition polynomial Keller.
No such quotient, cyclic isogeny, or eligible configuration is constructed.

## Negative controls and limitations

- Dominance alone is insufficient for local descent: f(s,t)=(s,st) pulls
  Y/X back to the regular function t, although Y/X is not regular at the
  target origin. This f is not flat at (0,0).
- A dense A1 image in P1 is not set-surjectivity onto a complete curve;
  the omitted point can carry a pole.
- Without quasi-finiteness, a constant postmap yields no contradiction.
- The etale-preimage condition includes the node, not just the smooth locus.
  The ambient surface S is smooth; the reduced complete curve E may be singular.

The result concerns a fixed donor/source neighborhood. It asserts no arbitrary
finite source-substitution theorem, all-isogeny or all-donor exclusion, or
general pole-cancellation obstruction. An affine normalization Spec B contains
no complete curve: this is not an argument that B=R. Donor existence,
finite-normalization/surjectivity and outside-boundary descent remain open.
No parameter computation, degree bound, polynomial counterexample or JC2 proof
has been obtained.
