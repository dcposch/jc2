## 2. Normal form and total affine delta

Let the two coordinate functions supplied by the one-stage delta sequence
have degrees three and four.  Translate the affine parameter to kill the
quadratic term of the cubic, scale and translate the first target coordinate,
and obtain

```text

```

Scale and translate the second target coordinate, then subtract a scalar
multiple of `X` to kill its cubic term.  This gives (0.1).  All operations are
affine automorphisms of the normalization or target; they preserve the
normalization fibres and the charged topology.

The two pole orders at `t=infinity` are coprime, so
`C(X,Y)=C(t)` and the parametrization is birational.  Homogenizing to degree
four gives

```text
[T:S] |->
[T^3*S+a*T*S^3 : T^4+b*T^2*S^2+c*T*S^3 : S^4].       (2.1)
```

There is one point at infinity, `[0:1:0]`.  In the chart where the middle
coordinate is one and `r=S/T`, the first local coordinate is

```text

```


## 3. Exact coincidence scheme and the four `b1=1` strata

For `t!=u`, divide the two equal-coordinate equations by `t-u`:

```text

```

Put `s=t+u` and `p=tu`.  The first equation gives `p=s^2+a`; substitution in
the second gives (0.2).  The two endpoints of the resulting unordered pair




## 4. Why cubic ramification escapes the conductor

Let `{alpha,beta}` be the unique two-point normalization fibre.  Since the
two points have the same image, they have the same `X` value.  The derivative
of the cubic `X` has degree two.

If its two roots were the two distinct conductor endpoints, each endpoint
would have local degree at least two in the same cubic fibre, requiring
length at least `2+2>3`.  If `X'` has one double root at a conductor endpoint,
that endpoint has local degree three; its distinct conductor mate adds one,
again requiring `3+1>3`.  Thus some critical parameter

```text
gamma notin {alpha,beta}                               (4.1)
```

always exists.  Equation (3.5) also shows that `nu(gamma)` cannot share its
image with another normalization point.  It may nevertheless be a
point-bijective unibranch singularity.  This is why the proof must use the
charged local fibre table rather than an unsupported immersion claim.

For the charged `m=0,n4=0` packet, `nu(gamma)` is not the unique `(2,2)`
value and is therefore `(2,1,1)`, with complete local group `C2`.  A local
projection frame contains two meridians from the ramified cluster when the
local degree is two, or all three when it is three.  All nontrivial meridians
in that `C2` are the same transposition.  The local three-tuple consequently
has a duplicate.

Three transpositions generate a transitive subgroup of `S4` exactly when
their support edges form a spanning tree on four letters.  A duplicate leaves
at most two distinct edges and is intransitive.  Finally, an elementary
Hurwitz move

```text
(A,B) |-> (A*B*A^(-1),A)                               (4.2)
```

is a Nielsen transformation.  It and its inverse preserve the subgroup
generated by the whole tuple.  Therefore an arbitrary full braid tail between
the original based frame and the local frame cannot restore `S4`.  This is the
valid repair of the older false shortcut that tried to identify individual
original colors across different tails.

Nothing in this argument applies at a `(3,1)` point with local group `S3`.
There, three local meridians may map to overlapping transpositions and already
generate `S3`; the `m=1` horn survives this packet.
