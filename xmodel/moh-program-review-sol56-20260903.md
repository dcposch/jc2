### 1.3 Re-derivation of the p.188 identity

The derivation does not need the charged report.  In Prop.5.5 Moh writes on
p.187, with

\[
n=d_2n^*,\qquad -M_1=d_2m^*,\qquad U_2=d_2-V_2,
\]

the reduced fraction

\[
 \delta_1=
 { (n^*+m^*)U_2-1\over (n^*+m^*)V_2-1}={B\over A}.
\]

Because $B/A$ is reduced, its denominator divides the denominator before
reduction:

\[
 \boxed{A\mid(n^*+m^*)V_2-1}.                         \tag{1.1}
\]

For a longer tower the same identity for Moh's relative denominator $A_1$
comes from Lemma 5.2 (pp.177--179), rather than from pretending the $s=2$
fraction is unchanged.  Put $b=(n-M_1)/d_2=n^*+m^*$ and

\[
R=d_2\delta_s+\sum_{i=3}^sV_i{d_2\over d_i}
  (\delta_{i-1}-\delta_i)-V_2\delta_2.
\]

Every denominator in $R$ divides
$L_1=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{den}\delta_s,\ldots,
\operatorname{den}\delta_2)$.  Lemma 5.2 at $M_1$ gives

\[
b(R+V_2\delta_1)=-1+\delta_1,
\]

hence

\[
(bV_2-1)(L_1\delta_1)=-L_1-b(L_1R)\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Since $A_1$ is the reduced denominator of $L_1\delta_1$, (1.1) follows.
On p.188 Moh combines it with coprimality of
$g_\sigma,T^\psi_{1,\sigma}$ and of their derivatives.  Exactly one of their
degrees is $0\pmod {A_1}$ and the other is $1\pmod {A_1}$, yielding precisely
(12) or (13).  No third residue or second bottom clause appears.

## 3. Source investigation of OPEN[MOH-PROGRAM]

### 3.1 Proposition 5.3 and the bottom polynomial

#### Local degrees and what a selected path forgets

At a node $D_j$ of a tower, Props.4.6 and 5.3 (p.170(4)--(5),
pp.180--183) give

\[
 P_j=\deg p_j=V_{j+1}{d_j\over d_{j+1}},\qquad
 Q_j=\deg q_j=V_{j+1}{n-M_j\over d_{j+1}},             \tag{3.1}
\]

where $q_j$ is squarefree, every root of $p_j$ is a root of $q_j$,
$p_j$ is not a power of $q_j$, and at least one $p_j$-root has
multiplicity

\[
 u>{P_j\over Q_j}={d_j\over n-M_j}.                    \tag{3.2}
\]

Proposition 5.3 does not merely say that one may select one such $u$.  Its
proof applies to any factor above the threshold, and the summary theorem on
p.200(4) says every subdisc with more than $n/(n-M_j)$ roots of $g$ extends
the tower.  Consequently all major siblings must be followed.  A row
containing only one chosen sequence $(V_s,\ldots,V_2)$ forgets this universal
quantifier.

The p.201 conjugation makes this finite.  With $A=A_j$ and
$P=P_j$, an actual factorization has the form

\[
 P=b+A\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}u_\ell,                         \tag{3.3}
\]

where $b\ge0$ is the multiplicity at the fixed root $0$, and each $u_\ell$
is the per-root multiplicity of a nonzero $A$-orbit.  Thus
$b\equiv P\pmod A$.  Lemma 5.2 also gives

\[
 A\mid Q-1,                                             \tag{3.4}
\]

so $q$ has one fixed root and $(Q-1)/A$ nonzero root orbits; unused slots
are $q$-roots with $p$-multiplicity zero.  Equation (3.4) was asserted and
checked for every baseline row; it is automatic and by itself kills none.

The frozen driver tests (10)/(11) only for the selected $V_j$.  The complete
necessary test instead enumerates every (3.3), embeds the selected $V_j$ as
either $b$ or an $u_\ell$, and recursively checks every $b$ or $u_\ell$
satisfying (3.2).  Minor factors stop, exactly as p.200(5) says.  At $j=2$
every major child must satisfy (12) or (13).

#### A source theorem recovered from the construction

**WHOLE-MAJOR-TREE NECESSITY.**  If a nonreducible constant-Jacobian pair
realizes a path row, then at every node there exists an orbit partition
(3.3), within the $q$-root capacity (3.4), such that every factor above
(3.2) admits a complete lower major subtree, every bottom major leaf obeys
(12)/(13), and the recorded $V$-path is one path in that tree.

**SOURCE-DERIVED NECESSARY:** the implication is assembled from
Prop.4.6(1)--(5), pp.170--171; Prop.5.3, pp.180--183; Def.5.1, p.179; the
p.201 orbit action; and summary Theorem (4)--(7), p.200.  It is uniform in
$D$.  Moh does not state the assembled dynamic-program theorem, it is not a
sufficiency theorem for constructing $f,g$, and no source says that the CDC
program implemented this exact state space.

The two independent implementations agree on all 658 input rows.  The bare
source theorem leaves 60 rows / 40 groups / 12 $(n,m)$ classes, including all
six printed rows, and it already produces the exact $(75,50)$ discriminator.
Thus it accounts for 598 of the 652 excess rows.  It also explains why the
charged numerical `NOT-ALL-(11)` is too weak: it checks one possible path;
WHOLE-MAJOR-TREE checks all forced sibling paths.

#### The differential equation adds a real sibling condition

Appendix I Prop.A.3 (p.205) is a summary of the equation

\[
 D(P,Q,p,q)=Ppq'-Qqp'=cp,qquad c\ne0.                  \tag{3.5}
\]

At a simple $q$-root $a$ with $u=\operatorname{mult}_a p$, division by the
local power of $\pi-a$ gives

\[
 (P-Qu)q_a(a)=c,qquad q_a=q/(\pi-a).                  \tag{3.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{P-Qu\ne0\quad\hbox{for every }p\hbox{-root}.}  \tag{3.7}
\]

For the selected major root (3.7) follows already from strict (3.2), but it
can exclude a *minor sibling* of multiplicity exactly $P/Q$.  This is an
exact source consequence, not a guessed no-common-root rule.

For completeness I also implemented a stronger, externally motivated
passport necessity.  Put $z=\pi^A$, pad the nonzero-orbit multiplicities by
zeros to $S=(Q-1)/A$ slots, and define

\[
 W_0={P-Qb\over A},\qquad W_\ell=P-Qu_\ell.
\]

The logarithmic derivative of $p^Q/q^P$ shows that all $W$ are nonzero and,
after $g=\gcd(|W_i|)$ and
$d_+=\sum_{W_i>0}W_i$, a necessary reduced-passport inequality is

\[
 d_+/g\ge S.                                            \tag{3.8}
\]

This is the polynomial-abc/Davenport--Zannier degree obstruction for the
cyclic quotient; it is not stated by Moh and is kept typed as an external
candidate.  It leaves the same 20-row set at $n\le100$ as the local tests
when combined with the exploratory recenter rule below.  Hence even the
stronger root-position probe does not supply the missing exact discriminator.

### 3.6 Abhyankar--Moh semigroup candidates

The correct generators are not the literal $M_i$.  From p.150, set

\[
d_1=n,\quad n_i={d_i\over d_{i+1}},\quad
\mu_1=M_1=-m,\quad
\mu_i=n_{i-1}\mu_{i-1}+M_i-M_{i-1},\quad R_i=-\mu_i.
\]

Proposition 2.2 and p.154 identify $R_i$ with the $y$-degree of the
specialized approximate root.  Proposition 3.1, pp.157--159, gives the exact
bounded-digit condition: for each effective $r$ there are unique admissible
indices satisfying

\[
 jn+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}R_i\alpha_i=n_rR_r,qquad
 j\ge0,\quad0\le\alpha_i<n_i,\quad\alpha_r=0.           \tag{3.10}
\]

This is stronger than unbounded membership in
$\langle n,R_1,\ldots,R_{r-1}\rangle$.  It is
**PROVED-IN-SOURCE**, and printed condition (4)'s phrase “is ...

satisfy the same construction at the next level.  This applies to *every*
