## 5. Exact h-adic blocks and Kummer grading

### 5.1 The literal blocks

For the normal form above, define

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_0&=2[h,3\beta-A],\\
V_0&=2([A,\beta]-[h,B]),\\
W_0&=2(A[h,\beta]+[B,\beta]).
\end{aligned}
\]

Direct expansion (`PROVED-HERE`, checked by `block_audit.py`) gives

\[
[P,Q]=h^2U_0+hV_0+W_0. \tag{5.1}
\]

This is not yet a three-block answer, because the displayed coefficients can
still contain powers of the monic `h`. Perform exact Euclidean divisions

\[
U_0=u_1h+u_0,\quad V_0=v_1h+v_0,\quad
W_0=w_2h^2+w_1h+w_0,
\]

with every remainder of pi-degree below 6. The genuine blocks are

\[
C_3=u_1,\quad C_2=u_0+v_1+w_2,\quad
C_1=v_0+w_1,\quad C_0=w_0. \tag{5.2}
\]

Thus the **top two** equations are `C_3=C_2=0`, while the full Jacobian
requires

\[
C_3=C_2=C_1=0,\qquad C_0=c\gamma^3. \tag{5.3}
\]

Each `C_i` may then be uniquely divided in the degree-two root as
`C_i=C_{i,2} ell^2+C_{i,1} ell+C_{i,0}`.

There is a useful further normalization. Divide
`beta^2=alpha h+rho`, and, **if** Laurent support proves
`A=3 beta`, write `B=3 alpha/2+R`. Then (5.1) reduces to

\[
[P,Q]=-2h[h,R]+3([\alpha,\beta]+[h,\rho])+2[R,\beta]. \tag{5.4}
\]

This is the clean absorption receiver. But `A=3 beta` at this height is not
licensed by the printed height-two example. Even after imposing it, the
dense conditional system has
`27+63+99+1=190` unknowns.
