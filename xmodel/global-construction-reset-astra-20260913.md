## Cheapest whole-source discriminator

Put p=109 and g(z)=z-z^p. The known exact rational representative is

    F_rat(x,y) = (g(x), y/g'(x)),       g'(x)=1-p*x^(p-1).

Direct differentiation gives J(F_rat)=1. Its second coordinate is an integral restricted series on the p-adic unit bidisc, since 1/g'(x)=sum_{j>=0} p^j*x^{j(p-1)}. The displayed rational function is not a polynomial. These are the OLD cotangent-control facts, not a new construction.

Let K be ANY characteristic-zero field, and let a rational source substitution have coordinates h,k in K(u,v). Suppose the resulting pair

    P=g(h),       Q=k/g'(h)

were in K[u,v]^2 with a nonzero constant Jacobian. If h is constant, that Jacobian is zero, so h is nonconstant. Because

    h^p - h + P = 0,

h is integral over K[u,v]. It already lies in its fraction field; the polynomial ring is integrally closed. Hence h is a polynomial. The chain rule is now an identity in K[u,v]:

    J(P,Q) = (1-p*h^(p-1))*J(h,Q).

The first factor is a nonconstant polynomial, whereas a product equal to a nonzero constant requires both factors to be units. Contradiction. This argument permits arbitrary rational source substitutions; birationality and symplecticity are not needed once the resulting pair is assumed polynomial with nonzero constant Jacobian.

Nor can a polynomial target automorphism hide the obstruction. If tau composed with F_rat composed with the substitution were a polynomial Keller map, applying the polynomial inverse of tau would recover a polynomial pair of nonzero constant Jacobian of the displayed form. The same contradiction applies. No degree/support bound, prime sampling, coefficient enumeration, or assertion about generic branches entered the calculation.

Thus the tempting completion-to-rational-regularization arrow fails at a precise place: polynomiality of g(h) forces h back into the polynomial ring, after which g'(h) cannot be removed from the Jacobian. Merely clearing the denominator by k=g'(h)*Q does not fix this; it leaves exactly the same chain-rule factor.
