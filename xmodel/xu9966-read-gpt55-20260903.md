## 1. Xu's Definitions and Inequalities

`SOURCE-READ`, Xu pp.1--2 (`xu-layout.txt:41-51`): with `x=t^-1`, a
`pi`-root of `f` is

```text
sigma = sum_{j<delta_sigma} a_j t^j + pi t^delta_sigma
f(sigma) = f_sigma(pi) t^lambda_sigma + ...
deg_pi f_sigma > 0.
```

The multiplicity is `deg_pi f_sigma`.  `sigma` is split when `f_sigma(pi)` has
more than one root.  `delta_sigma` is the split order.  `sigma` is final when
`f_sigma(pi)` has no multiple roots and `deg_pi f_sigma(pi)>1`.

`SOURCE-READ`, Xu Lemma 4.2 and Definition 4.3 (`xu-layout.txt:203-220`):
for a root `alpha` of `f_xi=f-xi`, set
`delta=max{ord(alpha-beta) | g(beta)=0}` and
`sigma=sum_{j<delta} a_j t^j + pi t^delta`.  This related `sigma` is a final
`pi`-root.  The root `alpha` is major if `ord g(alpha)<0` and minor if
`ord g(alpha)=0`.  If the leading form at infinity of `f` is
`(y-a1 x)^u (y-a2 x)^v` with `u<v`, the roots
`alpha=a1 t^-1+...` are the principal minor roots.

`SOURCE-READ`, Xu Definition 4.6 (`xu-layout.txt:252-259`): for a final
`pi`-root `sigma`, `D_sigma^h` is the set of roots `alpha` of `h` with
`ord(sigma-alpha)=delta_sigma`.  Xu writes
`Pm={final minor pi-roots of f_xi}` and
`PM={final major pi-roots of f_xi}`.

`SOURCE-READ`, Xu Section 7.3 (`xu-layout.txt:491-501`): for a Jacobian pair
with two points at infinity, Xu uses effective quasi-approximate roots
`T_i(f,g) in K[f,g]`, with `T_0=g`, `T_1=f`, and `deg T_i=-mu_i`.  With
`d_i=gcd(-mu_0,...,-mu_{i-1})`, the normalized list
`(-mu_0/d_{s+1},...,-mu_s/d_{s+1})` is a characteristic delta-sequence.  If the
principal minor roots of `f` have multiplicity `m u_s/d_s` with
`u_s<v_s=d_s-u_s`, then the principal-minor multiplicities of
`T_0,...,T_{s-1},T_s` are

```text
(-mu_0)u_s/d_s, ..., (-mu_{s-1})u_s/d_s,
((-mu_s-2)u_s/d_s)+1.
```

`SOURCE-READ`, Xu Theorem 3.4 (`xu-layout.txt:176-187`): without using the
Jacobian condition,

```text
I(f_xi, f_y) = - sum_sigma ( e(f_sigma(pi)) - 1 ) lambda_sigma,
```

where `sigma` runs over all split `pi`-roots of `f_xi`, and `e(p)` is the
number of distinct roots of `p`.

`SOURCE-READ`, Xu Theorem 4.7 (`xu-layout.txt:260-322`): for a Jacobian pair,

```text
I(f_xi, f_y) <= deg_y f - 1
                + sum_{sigma in Pm} (|D_sigma^{f_xi}|-1)(delta_sigma-1)

I(f_xi, g) >= 1 + sum_{sigma in Pm} (delta_sigma-1).
```

If there are no final minor roots, Xu obtains `I(f, f_y)=deg_y f-1` and
`I(f,g)=1`.

`SOURCE-READ`, Xu Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.3 (`xu-layout.txt:342-397`):
final major roots compute `I(f_xi,g)`:

```text
I(f_xi,g) = IM(f,g)
          = - sum_{sigma in PM} |D_sigma^f| lambda_sigma^g
          = (deg_y g)/(deg_y f + deg_y g)
              sum_{sigma in PM} |D_sigma^f|(1-delta_sigma),

Im(f,g) = 1 + sum_{sigma in Pm}(delta_sigma-1),
IM(f,g) >= Im(f,g).
```

`SOURCE-READ`, Xu Corollary 5.2 (`xu-layout.txt:380-390`): for
`deg_y f>1` and `deg_y g>1`,
`[K(x,y):K(f,g)] < deg_y f deg_y g/(deg_y f+deg_y g)`.

`SOURCE-READ`, "not split before order 1": Xu records in the introduction
that Moh proved minor roots are not split before order `1`
(`xu-layout.txt:27-30`).  Xu's own final-root statement is Lemma 4.4:
related final major roots have `delta<1`, while related final minor roots have
`delta>1` (`xu-layout.txt:221-241`).  For principal minor roots of effective
quasi-approximate roots, Proposition 7.3 says the order-1 root `sigma_1`
has all leading polynomials `T_{0,sigma_1},...,T_{s,sigma_1}` equal to powers
of one common linear polynomial (`xu-layout.txt:502-525`); the proof rules out
any other root of the degree-`u_s` polynomial by an Abhyankar-Moh semigroup
contradiction (`xu-layout.txt:527-574`).  Corollary 7.5 adds that, if `u_s>1`,
a principal-minor `pi`-root of order
`delta < (v_s+1)/(u_s+1)` cannot split to `u_s` different roots
(`xu-layout.txt:577-616`).
