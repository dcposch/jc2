## 7. Adversarial scope audit of the best proposal (Card A)

Attack the claim “evaluate genuine saturated Rees/chart/routing equations
at K00, after splitting `rho`, and that decides the on-family receiver.”

1. **Completeness of the honest-equation list.** Simultaneous vanishing on
   a finite registered list does not prove that the saturated Rees chart
   is nonempty. It proves only that those equations do not exclude K00.
   Reporting a germ requires an explicit completeness statement
   (“these are all compiled Rees-kernel, bilinear, saturation, and
   routing generators at this presentation”). Absent that, the only legal
   positive reading is “no currently registered identity kills it.”
2. **Proj versus `V(I)`.** Points with all collision coordinates zero do
   not lie in any affine chart `D_+(f_i)`. Card A may be evaluating a
   *base-point* of `V(J1+J2)` rather than a point of `Proj(Rees(J1+J2))`.
   DVR arcs with all `f_i` of positive valuation live on the exceptional
   divisor, which the six charts cover; identically-zero series live at
   the base-point. Mixing these is the same class of error as treating
   fibre emptiness as a multiplicative `1+rho W` certificate. Repair:
   say which object is being tested, and do not quote the staged DVR
   lemma for a base-point unless a saturation identity is exhibited.
3. **“All-depth” is finite-tail.** The 569 tails are the frozen compiled
   source, not a complete formal germ. K00 vanishing is a theorem about
   that compilation. A different source family, or uncompiled connection
   jets, can still see the geometric section. The landing preflight
   already forbids replacing the owner family by `p=-2 rho^2`.
4. **Hypothesis dropping.** Promoted square theorems are scoped
   (`D(p k10)`, first-contact open, integral `Lambda`, delayed-load
   schedule, …). K00 has unit `k` and, on `D(rho)`, nonzero `p`. It need
   not lie on a first-contact open. An identity used outside its
   hypotheses is costume. The discriminator must emit a met/unmet bit
   per identity.
5. **Radical R1--R3 versus scheme.** The audit confirms R1--R3 at
   radical/field-point scope (`ee1^3` and `ec3^3` certificates, `aa0^4`
   after further combinations). Card A must not import those as
   scheme-theoretic generators of the honest Rees ideal. They remain
   descriptive raw algebra.
6. **`k` versus `k10`.** K00 uses the constant coefficient `k` as a unit.
   That is the named family. Do not identify `k` with the series `k10`,
   and do not drop the Rabinowitsch equation `1-v k`. Z00 must fail it.
7. **One piece is not the receiver.** A kill of `K00_ρ` leaves `K00_0`,
   and conversely. A kill of both named pieces still leaves other
   receiver supports (the audit's two raw leaves are *raw* and may
   acquire honest equations that cut or revive them). Do not close
   `V(J1+J2) ∩ D(k)` from two evaluations.
8. **False inversion.** Saturation may absorb an exceptional power;
   inverting `a1`, `rho`, or an unregistered jet is the error already
   made on `T-c0`/`T-c1` and refused by AS-TRI. Card A inherits that
   ban.
9. **Compute boundary.** “Desk-scale” becomes a Groebner the moment an
   unnamed Rees kernel is generated. The card as written is one-point
   evaluation of named polynomials. If an implementer starts a standard
   basis of a Rees kernel, that is a different, AWS-only, preregistered
   lane.

Surviving core after these attacks: K00 is a real on-family raw-zero
section; raw rows cannot empty it; the next test must be honest and must
split `rho`; any emptiness claim is only as wide as the named identity
and the piece on which it is a unit. That core is worth running. The
germ/algebraization reading is conditional on completeness and is
**SPECULATION** until the list is frozen.
