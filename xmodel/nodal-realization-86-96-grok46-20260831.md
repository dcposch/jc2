## 8. Obstruction toolkit (Plücker, AM, Orevkov, double-point scheme)

**AM / Galindo.** Every one of the six `Δ` satisfies Theorem 2.1 of Galindo–Monserrat (hashed in §0). Sathaye–Stenerson / Galindo converse realises each by a one-place curve of degree `d`; that is a local-plus-infinity existence theorem, not a genus-0 theorem, and not a nodal theorem (M-INF hostile review §3.2 already recorded this distinction for `(8,6,3)`). Semigroup genera match `δ_aff` and cannot kill a type that is already on the Galindo list. Primitive-generator uniqueness (FSY Lemma 3) distinguishes `δ`-sequences with the same semigroup (`⟨3,8⟩` as `{3,8}` versus `{8,6,3}`): it explains why `(t^3,t^8)` does not attain type `(8,6,3)`, but does not by itself forbid a different parametrization of degrees `(8,6)`.

**Class formula.** For a plane curve of degree `d`, the class is `m=d(d−1)−∑_p(μ_p+m_p−1)` (nodes contribute 2; a unibranch point contributes `μ+m_p−1`). On every `(8,6)` survivor this equals 13; on every `(9,6)` survivor it equals 14. Both positive. The identity is genus-tautological once the infinity type is unibranch of the stated `δ_∞` and the affine points are nodes filling `δ_aff`. It is a consistency check, not an obstruction. Inflection counts require a sourced Hessian multiplicity for `A_{2n}` (resp. for `⟨3,β⟩`); none was consumed, so no Klein/Hessian kill is claimed.

**Orevkov.** Theorem A of Orevkov, *On rational cuspidal curves I* (the bound `d<α(m+1)+1/√5` with `α=(3+√5)/2`) is for rational curves that are *cuspidal* (one analytic branch at each singular point). Nodes are two-branch. The bound is not applicable. Matsuoka–Sakai `d<3m_max` is likewise for the cuspidal setting. Applying either to the six types would be a substitution of a different singularity class.

**Double-point scheme.** For proper `(p,q)` of type `(d,n)` the scheme `V(Δp,Δq)` off the diagonal has length `δ_aff`. In-class attainment is reducedness plus distinct tangents plus no triple fibre. Two desk-visible degeneration mechanisms, both realised on closed loci of the coefficient space:

1. *Cover symmetry.* If `p,q∈ℂ[φ]` for `deg φ≥2`, the map is not proper of degree 1. For `(8,6)` this is the even locus `γ=b=0` (factors through `t^2`). For `(9,6)` this is the even-odd family of §6, a degree-3 cover of a `(3,2)` cusp. These loci are excluded by saturating against `gcd(p',q')` having a factor of degree `>1`, or against `gcd(S_aff)>1`.
2. *Forced pairs from an involution.* If an element of degree 2 (resp. a square structure `q=r^2`) is present, the Galois involution of that quadratic (resp. `r↦−r`) produces a 0-cycle of pairs that may be non-reduced at the ramification point or may collide with extra fibres of a higher polynomial in that element. This is the mechanism that produced an ordinary *triple* point on the banked `(6,4)` curve of the M-INF review, and that was *avoided* by the three-node witness of ROW-SWEEP §2. It is not forced on the mixed-term open set.

Neither mechanism, as a closed-locus phenomenon, kills a type. A type dies only if every component of the characteristic ideal lies on one of these loci or has non-reduced `I_{DP}`.

**Approximate-root job (FSY Lemma 1).** For a putative polynomial curve with `δ`-sequence `{δ_0,δ_1,δ_2}` the second approximate root `g_2` of the monic-in-`y` equation satisfies `deg_t g_2(x(t),y(t))=δ_2`. Vanishing of all higher `t`-coefficients is a polynomial system in the parametrization coefficients. This is the exact job that FSY ran for `{6,21,4}` and `{6,22,17}` (CAS, hours, weighted LEX). It is the same finite problem as the `h_k`/`k_i` ideals of §§2–7, written in approximate-root rather than leading-form coordinates.
