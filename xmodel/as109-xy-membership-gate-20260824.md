The proposed bridge points in the wrong direction.  The 109 Hensel sections
already give 109 pairwise distinct conjugates of `xy` on the target tube

```text
(P,Q)=(109S,1+109T).                                    (0.1)
```

Their residues are exactly `0,1,...,108`.  Consequently `xy` cannot lie in
the target field.  If the infinity bridge separately proves `d=109`, these
are all conjugates and `xy` generates the whole source field over the target
field.  A descended formal cycle moves, rather than fixes, this generator.

The repaired Moskowicz second case is therefore valid but cannot consume an
AS109 lift in the displayed source coordinates.  The audited first case and
the paper's headline prime-degree theorem are not used.

## 1. Setup and licensed inputs

Let `p=109` and assume conditionally that

```text
F=(P,Q) in Z_p[x,y]^2,
F mod p=(x-x^p,y),
det J_F=1.                                               (1.1)
```

Put

```text
K=Q_p,              M=K(P,Q),              L=K(x,y),
d=[L:M],             w=xy.                              (1.2)
```

The reviewed Hensel-to-degree gate gives `d>=p`.  The reviewed
`A_infinity` gate gives, over the formal target field, the conditional split

```text
L tensor_M E = E^p x A_infinity,
dim_E A_infinity=d-p.                                   (1.3)
```

This report consumes only those reviewed statements and the source audit's
repaired implication

```text
complex Keller + xy in C(P,Q)  =>  automorphism.        (1.4)
```

It does not consume the source's unsupported first-case implication or its
headline no-prime-degree claim.

## 2. The tube forces 109 distinct conjugates of `xy`

Take

```text
R=Z_p[[S,T]],                 E=Frac(R),
(U,V)=(pS,1+pT).                                        (2.1)
```

For each `a in F_p`, parameter Hensel gives one section

```text
(x_a,y_a) in R^2,
F(x_a,y_a)=(U,V),
x_a=a mod p,                 y_a=1 mod p.               (2.2)
```

As in the reviewed degree gate, substitution defines a distinct
`M`-embedding

```text
sigma_a:L -> E.                                         (2.3)
```

All these embeddings restrict to the same target substitution (2.1), but

```text
sigma_a(w)=x_a y_a = a mod p.                           (2.4)
```

Hence the `p` images of `w` are pairwise distinct.

For a finite separable extension, the number of distinct values of an
element under base-field embeddings equals the degree of that element's
minimal polynomial: every embedding of `M(w)` into an algebraic closure
extends to `L`, and the extensions repeat a given value exactly
`[L:M(w)]` times.  Equations (2.3)--(2.4) therefore prove

```text
[M(w):M] >= p=109.                                      (2.5)
```

In particular,

```text
xy notin K(P,Q),             xy notin K[P,Q].           (2.6)
```

No `A_infinity`, monodromy, deck, trace, or generic-support assumption is
needed for (2.5).  Choosing the nonzero target residue in (2.1) is
essential: on the tube centered at `V=0`, every product residue is zero and
does not distinguish the sections.

## 3. Nonmembership survives passage to complex constants

Let `k_0` be the characteristic-zero field generated by the finitely many
coefficients of `F`.  It embeds in `Q_p` and admits embeddings into `C`.
Membership in a rational subfield cannot first appear after extending the
constant field in this situation.

Indeed, if for a field extension `Omega/k_0` one had

```text
w=A(P,Q)/B(P,Q) in Omega(P,Q),                          (3.1)
```

clear denominators and freeze the finite supports of `A,B`.  Equation

```text
A(P,Q)-w B(P,Q)=0                                       (3.2)
```

is a finite homogeneous linear system in the coefficients of `A,B`, with
matrix entries in `k_0(x,y)`.  Its kernel after scalar extension is the
scalar extension of its `k_0`-kernel.  If an `Omega`-solution has a nonzero
`B` part, some `k_0`-solution does as well.  Thus (3.1) would imply
`w in k_0(P,Q)`, hence `w in Q_p(P,Q)`, contradicting (2.6).

Therefore every complex incarnation obtained by embedding the coefficient
field also has

```text
xy notin C(P,Q).                                        (3.3)
```

The antecedent of the repaired theorem (1.4) is not merely unproved for an
AS109 lift; it is impossible.

## 4. Sharp `A_infinity=0` consequence: `xy` is primitive

If the infinity bridge proves `A_infinity=0`, then (1.3) gives `d=p`.
Combining this with (2.5) and the tower law yields

```text
[M(w):M]=p,                  L=M(w).                    (4.1)
```

Thus `xy` is a primitive generator of the hypothetical degree-109 source
extension.  This is the exact opposite of target-field membership.

Let

```text
mu_w(Z)=Z^p+c_(p-1)Z^(p-1)+...+c_1 Z+c_0 in M[Z]       (4.2)
```

be its minimal polynomial.  Under the tube embedding `M->E`, all its roots
are the `p` integral elements `w_a=x_a y_a`.  Hence

```text
mu_w(Z)=product_(a in F_p)(Z-w_a) in R[Z],
mu_w(Z) mod p=product_(a in F_p)(Z-a)=Z^p-Z.            (4.3)
```

This gives the exact local coefficient ledger

```text
c_1=-1 mod p,            c_i=0 mod p for i!=1,p.       (4.4)
```

It is a useful univariate representation of the `A_infinity=0` client, not
a membership theorem.

## 5. Trace, norm, and discriminant stop

Equation (4.3) implies on the tube

```text
Tr_(L/M)(w)=0 mod p,
Nm_(L/M)(w)=0 mod p,
Disc(mu_w)=-1 mod 109.                                  (5.1)
```

The discriminant is a unit because the 109 residues are distinct.  It gives
no local obstruction to a square-discriminant condition:

```text
33^2=-1 mod 109.                                        (5.2)
```

Trace and norm are in `M` for every finite extension by definition; their
congruences in (5.1) are just the first and last elementary symmetric
functions of the split residue set.  The discriminant congruence is exactly
the same split-etale datum.  None forces `w` itself into `M`, constrains
`A_infinity`, or descends a deck transformation.

This stop is sharp.  The non-Keller polynomial control

```text
G_p=(g_p(x),y),             g_p(x)=x+(p-1)x^p           (5.3)
```

has all of the following:

```text
G_p mod p=(x-x^p,y),
generic degree p,
finite free formal fibre algebra of rank p,
A_infinity=0,
M(w)=L,
mu_w(W)=W^p+V^(p-1)/(p-1) W-U V^p/(p-1),
mu_w mod (p,U,V-1)=W^p-W.                               (5.4)
```

Its rational deck group is trivial, and

```text
det J_Gp=1+p(p-1)x^(p-1)                                (5.5)
```

is not constant.  Thus all local minimal-polynomial, trace, norm, and
discriminant data above occur without the global constant-Jacobian input
and without deck descent.

## 6. What a descended cycle would add

Suppose now, still conditionally on `A_infinity=0`, that a rational deck
automorphism `tau in Aut_M(L)` descends the formal transition

```text
a -> a+1.                                               (6.1)
```

It cannot fix `w`: on the tube,

```text
tau(w_a)=w_(a+1),
w_(a+1)-w_a=1 mod p.                                   (6.2)
```

Thus descent certifies nonmembership again rather than repairing it.

Because `L=M(w)`, the automorphism has a unique representation

```text
tau(w)=R_tau(w),               deg R_tau<p.             (6.3)
```

The unit Vandermonde of the roots in (4.3) makes the interpolation
coefficients integral on the tube, and (6.1) becomes the exact congruence

```text
R_tau(Z)=Z+1 mod (p,mu_w),
R_tau^[p](Z)=Z mod mu_w.                                (6.4)
```

Equations (6.3)--(6.4) are a finite **univariate deck-descent
discriminator**.  They do not follow from the symmetric coefficients of
`mu_w`; they are equivalent to supplying the missing root permutation.

A descended `p`-cycle also makes `L/M` cyclic Galois.  Since a `p`-cycle is
even for odd `p`, its action fixes the Vandermonde, so

```text
Disc(mu_w) is a square in M.                             (6.5)
```

This is a genuine conditional global coefficient constraint, but it is
compatible with (5.2) and supplies no contradiction by itself.  Moreover,
under `A_infinity=0` any nonidentity deck automorphism already makes the
prime-degree extension Galois; the reviewed Galois-Keller gate then closes
the lift without needing (1.4), trace, norm, or (6.5).

The cyclic field control

```text
L=K(s,v),             M=K(s^p,v),
x=s+v,                y=s+2v                            (6.6)
```

has deck generator `s->zeta s`, degree `p`, and square discriminant, yet

```text
xy=s^2+3sv+2v^2                                       (6.7)
```

is moved by every nonidentity deck element.  A deck group does not imply
membership of a chosen source monomial.
