## 0. Verdict

The exact cyclic complete-base controls used in

```text
xmodel/bd-a2-a1-p1-degree-controls-sol56-20260830.md
```

separate into two logically different conclusions.

1. They remain decisive countercontrols to any argument using only the first
   etale leg and its generic degree: cyclic `A1`-fiber spaces with
   multiplicities `(2,2)` and `(2,4)` admit affine pseudo-coverings from
   `A2` of degree two and four, respectively.
2. Neither of these two cyclic rows can have linearly trivial canonical
   class.  Consequently neither can admit an etale morphism to `A2`, or even
   to any smooth surface with trivial canonical bundle.

Thus the first-leg degree obstruction stays dead, but these two most concrete
external controls are now eliminated as models of the **full** etale
sandwich.  The proof is an exact two-fibre specialization of Miyanishi's
canonical and boundary-intersection calculation.  It does not assume the
potentially mistyped divisibility line in the lecture notes: the integral
torsion condition is derived directly from `Pic(X)`.

## 1. The precise theorem

Let `X` be a smooth affine surface with an `A1`-fibration

```text
rho:X -> P1
```

whose fibres are all irreducible and whose only multiple fibres are
`m_1F_1,m_2F_2`.  Use the adapted completion of Miyanishi, Section 2.5:
`V` is obtained from `Sigma_n`, the unique horizontal boundary maps to
`T~M+a*ell`, and the closure of each `F_i` is the unique `(-1)`-curve in its
completed fibre.  Then:

```text
(m_1,m_2)=(2,2) or (2,4)  =>  K_X is not linearly equivalent to zero.
```

In particular, no such `X` admits an etale morphism

```text
X -> Z
```

to a smooth surface `Z` with `K_Z~0`.  Indeed etaleness would give
`K_X=f^*K_Z~0`.

This theorem concerns the complete two-fibre cyclic controls only.  It does
not classify other multiplicity pairs, fibrations with reducible fibres, or
the internal F5 boundary rows.

## 2. Source inputs and a typo firewall

The primary input is M. Miyanishi, *Lectures on Geometry and Topology of
Polynomials -- Surrounding the Jacobian Conjecture*, arXiv:`1504.07179`,
Section 2.5, printed pp. 117--121.  In the source notation,

```text
K_V ~ -2M-(n+2)ell + sum_i(k_i F_i + boundary terms),
T ~ M+a ell,
K_X ~ ((2a-n-2)+sum_i k_i/m_i) ell       in Pic(X) tensor Q.   (2.1)
```

The source proves `k_i>m_i`.  When `T=M`, it also tabulates, for a completed
multiple fibre, the number `h` of blowups, its canonical coefficient `k`,
and the Schur-complement coefficient `alpha`:

```text
m=2:  k=h,     alpha=h/4.                                      (2.2)

m=4, one branching vertex and T' adjacent to the (-4)-curve:
      k=h,     alpha=h/16.                                     (2.3)

m=4, one branching vertex and T' not adjacent to the (-4)-curve:
      k=h+2,   alpha=(h+8)/16.                                 (2.4)

m=4, more than one branching vertex:
      s>=3, h>=s+2,
      k=h+s-1,
      alpha=((8s-3)(h-s)-(12s-5))/(16(2(h-s)-3)).              (2.5)
```

For three Platonic fibres, the printed text says that `K_X~0` includes a
condition transcribed literally as "`k_i` is divisible by `h_i`" and its
next displayed denominator is printed `m_1` rather than `m_i`.  Those lines
are not charged here.  The exact two-fibre Picard presentation below derives
the integral condition without choosing between a source typo and an
unexplained convention.

The other source input is the affineness criterion.  Write `alpha_i` for the
number in (2.2)--(2.5).  The vertical boundary blocks are negative definite.
Eliminating them from the intersection matrix leaves the one-by-one Schur
complement

```text
-n + alpha_1 + alpha_2.                                      (2.6)
```

An affine complement requires the boundary matrix to have a positive
eigenvalue, hence

```text
alpha_1+alpha_2 > n.                                         (2.7)
```

Miyanishi writes the same calculation for three fibres.  The block
elimination is unchanged for two fibres: one simply omits the third negative
definite vertical block.

## 3. Reduction to `T=M`

The Picard group has rational rank one, generated by the fibre class.  If
`T!=M`, then `a>0` and `a>=n`; using `k_i/m_i>1` in (2.1) gives

```text
I=(2a-n-2)+k_1/m_1+k_2/m_2 > 2a-n >= 0.
```

The inequality is strict, including when `n=0`.  Therefore `K_X` has a
nonzero free part.  Any row with `K_X~0` must have `T=M`, hence `a=0`.

On that row all omitted vertical components and `M` vanish in `Pic(X)`, so

```text
Pic(X)=<ell,F_1,F_2 | ell=m_1F_1=m_2F_2>,                    (3.1)
K_X=-(n+2)ell+k_1F_1+k_2F_2.                                (3.2)
```

Equation (3.1) is the required integral torsion ledger.

## 4. Exclusion of `(2,2)`

For two double fibres, set

```text
ell=2F_1,       F_2=F_1+t,       2t=0.
```

By (2.2), `k_i=h_i` and `alpha_i=h_i/4`.  Formula (3.2) becomes

```text
K_X=(h_1+h_2-2(n+2))F_1+h_2*t.
```

If `K_X~0`, then

```text
h_1+h_2=2(n+2),       h_2=0 mod 2.
```

The first equality then also makes `h_1` even.  Since `h_i=k_i>m_i=2`,

```text
h_1,h_2 >= 4,
```

and therefore `n>=2`.  On the other hand, the affineness inequality (2.7)
and the canonical equality give

```text
n < (h_1+h_2)/4 = (n+2)/2,
```

so `n<2`.  This is a contradiction.  At the numerical boundary `n=2` the
Schur complement is zero, not positive, and the complement is not affine.

## 5. Exclusion of `(2,4)`

For multiplicities two and four, write

```text
F_1=2F_2+t,       ell=4F_2,       2t=0.
```

Let `h_1` be the blowup number of the double fibre and `(h,k,alpha)` the data
of the quadruple fibre.  Equations (2.2) and (3.2) give

```text
K_X=(2h_1+k-4(n+2))F_2+h_1*t.                               (5.1)
```

Thus `K_X~0` forces

```text
h_1 even,
n=h_1/2+k/4-2.                                               (5.2)
```

In particular, `h_1>=4`; integrality of `n` forces `k=0 mod 4`; and `k>4`
forces `k>=8`.  Affineness would require

```text
h_1/4+alpha > h_1/2+k/4-2.                                  (5.3)
```

Every quadruple-fibre row instead satisfies

```text
alpha <= k/4-1.                                               (5.4)
```

Indeed:

* In (2.3), `alpha=k/16`, and (5.4) follows from `k>=8`.
* In (2.4), `alpha=(k+6)/16`; the difference
  `(k/4-1)-alpha=(3k-22)/16` is positive for `k>=8`.
* In (2.5), put `d=h-s>=2`, so `k=d+2s-1`.  After multiplying by the
  positive denominator `16(2d-3)`, the difference in (5.4) has numerator

  ```text
  Q(d,s)=8d^2+8sd-49d-12s+55.
  ```

  At `d=2`, `Q(2,s)=4s-11>0` for `s>=3`; moreover

  ```text
  Q(d+1,s)-Q(d,s)=16d+8s-41 >= 15.
  ```

  Hence (5.4) is strict throughout the multi-branch family.

Combining `h_1/4>=1` with (5.4) yields

```text
h_1/4+alpha <= h_1/4+k/4-1
              <= h_1/2+k/4-2 = n,
```

where the second inequality is equivalent to `h_1>=4`.  This contradicts
the strict affineness inequality (5.3).

## 6. Consequences and successor

The two external controls retain exactly their intended negative use:

```text

does not by itself exclude a P1-base A1-fibration.
```

But the canonical shadow of the second leg already separates those controls
from the actual block surface:

