## 1. One relation for the actual family; specialization

Set R=C[x,y], A=C[f,g], alpha=(x dy-y dx)/2 and dT=alpha-f dg. Choose the fixed coordinates with f_m(0,1)!=0 and the integer N>max(deg T,2). Write q=x^N, H_l=T+lq, and introduce an indeterminate l. Let F=C(f,g), L=C(x,y), d=[L:F], and

    S=C[l,l^-1],  R_S=S[x,y],  B_S=S[f,g,H_l].

The accepted leading-form argument works over S: the top forms are f_m and l*x^N, and l is a unit. Its bounded-degree generators therefore make R_S finite over B_S. The same radial field-generation proof over C(l), after the polynomial shear, gives Frac(B_S)=C(l)(x,y). Consequently the kernel of C[l,U,V,W] -> C[l,x,y] is generated by one primitive irreducible polynomial P(l,U,V,W), unique up to C*. It has W-degree d. This is a polynomial relation, not a monic-in-W integrality assertion over C[l,f,g].

In fact there are no extra nonzero-parameter specialization exceptions for this P. Fix a!=0. Since P is not divisible by l-a, P_a is nonzero. Finite surjectivity Spec(R_S)->Spec(B_S) survives base change to a, so the hypersurface P_a=0 is topologically the image of the irreducible plane. Thus P_a is a constant times a power of the actual irreducible relation p_a of (f,g,H_a). Every H_a is radial canonical in its own polynomial frame, so it generates L over F and deg_W(p_a)=d. Since deg_W(P_a)<=d, the power is one and the constant is nonzero. Hence P_a is the actual primitive relation up to a complex scalar; no extra factor in U,V is possible. The excluded parameter l=0 is not covered by this family finiteness proof; no statement there is needed. The fixed conditions on f_m and N are essential.

This also explains why arbitrary specialization of an unrelated primitive relation would be unsafe: both uniform finiteness and primitive field generation at every a were used.

## 2. Parameter differentiation is exactly conductor multiplication

Differentiate the literal identity P(l,f,g,T+lq)=0 in the polynomial source ring, holding x,y fixed:

    P_l(l,f,g,H_l)+c_l*q=0,
    c_l=P_W(l,f,g,H_l).

At a!=0, the first term belongs to B_a. The accepted exact conductor formula gives I_a=c_a R. Thus c_a*q in B_a was already automatic for every q in R, not just x^N. The source derivative supplies a particular expression for this automatic element; it supplies no inverse of c_a.

The precise unclosed division is visible in the B_a-module isomorphism

    R/B_a -> I_a/(c_a B_a),   [r] -> [c_a r].

It is bijective because I_a=c_a R and c_a is nonzero in the domain R. Consequently q belongs to B_a exactly when -P_l(a,f,g,H_a) belongs to c_a B_a, not merely to I_a or B_a. Cancelling c_a in Frac(R) proves only q is a rational element of B_a, already known. Calling the right-hand side divisible because it equals c_a*q in R assumes the desired B_a-divisibility.

The total derivation partial_l on R_S sends H_l to q. It need not preserve B_S. Differentiating the relation therefore cannot silently be treated as a derivation of B_S. This is the same missing membership, not another source hypothesis.

## 3. The varying Euler field and its cross coefficient

Put k=2N/(N-2). In the frame X=x, Y=y+k*l*x^(N-1), half the Euler field, expressed back on the fixed source, is

    E_l=(x/2)partial_x+(y/2-N*l*x^(N-1))partial_y
       =E_0+l*X_q,
    i_(X_q)(dx wedge dy)=dq,  X_q=-N*x^(N-1)partial_y.

Indeed E_l(Y)=Y/2, including the derivative of the shear term. Hence i_(E_l)omega=alpha+l*dq and dH_l=i_(E_l)omega-f dg. Replacing E_l by E_0 would omit precisely the term needed below.

Take two local inverse branches of the Keller map over the same target coordinates U,V; these are available locally, without assuming the map finite. Write T_i,q_i,H_(i,l)=T_i+l*q_i and Delta_l=H_(1,l)-H_(2,l)=Delta_T+l*Delta_q. With Omega=dU wedge dV, define X_h by i_(X_h)Omega=dh. The pushed-forward fields satisfy

    E_(1,l)-E_(2,l)=X_(Delta_l).

Thus (E_(1,l)-E_(2,l))(Delta_l)=0 identically. Its coefficient linear in l is exactly

    X_(Delta_T)(Delta_q)+X_(Delta_q)(Delta_T)=0,

the antisymmetry of the two-dimensional Hamiltonian bracket. The constant and quadratic coefficients are X_h(h)=0. No constancy, equality or vanishing of either Delta is forced.

At an ordinary double conductor pair, the actual local factorization is P=u*(W-H_1)*(W-H_2), with other factors in u. On Delta_l=0, the two restrictions of u agree and

    c_1=u_1*Delta_l,  c_2=-u_2*Delta_l,
    E_l(c)_1+E_l(c)_2=0.

The paired-Euler statement is therefore exactly the preceding automatic identity after multiplying by the common unit and restricting to the moving collision curve. Differentiating an identity valid only on Delta_l=0 while holding its points fixed discards the motion of that curve; comparing different parameters while dropping the variation of E_l also gives a spurious constraint. No assertion about arbitrary multiple conductor branches is needed.

## 4. Why two parameters or coprime exponents do not complete this step

For a!=b of the same exponent,

    B_a B_b=A[H_a,H_b]=A[T,x^N].

Here the product notation means the subring generated by both inside R, not their intersection. The difference H_b-H_a=(b-a)x^N places x^N in this larger ring. It does not place H_b or x^N in B_a. Even a common collision pair only gives Delta_T=Delta_q=0 on that pair; it does not make all collisions common to both graphs or kill either conductor divisor.

With two coprime exponents N,M, a ring already containing x^N and x^M has x in its fraction field and integral closure. Recovering x by integer Bezout exponents can require negative powers, so it does not prove polynomial-ring membership. The elementary control C[z^2,z^3] inside C[z] illustrates precisely this invalid inference; it is not a Keller-source example. In the actual situation the fraction field and normalization were already R's. The joint ring may genuinely be larger, but normality of that enlargement does not imply normality of either subring.

Similarly, the target derivations obey

    D_f H_b-D_f H_a=(b-a)D_f(x^N),
    D_g H_b-D_g H_a=(b-a)D_g(x^N).

All these are source polynomials. Neither summand is known to belong to B_a. Their conductor-multiplied versions, P_U+c_a D_f H_a=0 and P_V+c_a D_g H_a=0, have the same I_a/(c_a B_a) obstruction. Bezout combinations of conductors from different rings require coefficients and all summands in one specified ring; source-ring comaximality, even if separately proved, would not supply that condition. No such comaximality is asserted here.
