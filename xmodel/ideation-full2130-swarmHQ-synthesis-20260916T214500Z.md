## 1. Which field has degree one?

Write A=C[P,Q], R=C[x,y], B=A[H] for the canonical-potential graph, and
K=Frac(A), L=Frac(R). In the accepted finite-graph setup, Frac(B)=L and
Spec(R)->Spec(B) is a finite birational normalization. Its degree one is
over L, NOT over K. The Keller degree is still [L:K].

Trying to treat that normalization as the small-degree ambient tower in
the [connected-quotient theorem](connected-quotient-tower-swarmHQ-root-20260916T155300Z.md)

Spec(L) over K: localizing B=A[H] at the nonzero elements of A gives the
finite field K[H]=L. If d=[L:K]>1, characteristic zero gives

    L tensor_K algebraic_closure(K) = algebraic_closure(K)^d.

This is not a domain. The required geometric integrality of that endpoint
fiber therefore fails. Adding independent affine variables leaves the same
nontrivial algebraic constant-field extension. The theorem cannot be
applied to this diagram to erase a conductor defect.
